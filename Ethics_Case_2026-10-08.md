# Weekly Ethics Case Study — 8 October 2026

**GS Paper IV | Case Study | Setting: Environmental regulation (State Pollution Control Board)**

---

## The Case

You are the Regional Officer of the State Pollution Control Board (SPCB) in a district with a large textile-dyeing cluster. The cluster has 140 units, employs about 8,000 workers directly and many more indirectly, and is the district's main source of revenue. The units share a Common Effluent Treatment Plant (CETP) managed by the industry association.

Over the last six months, villagers in three downstream hamlets, mostly small farmers and landless labourers, have complained of discoloured river water, crop damage and skin ailments among children. A junior engineer in your office, Mr. Rao, submits a sampling report showing that the CETP's outlet breaches prescribed norms for colour, chemical oxygen demand and heavy metals on 5 of 8 sampling days. He tells you privately that two large units bypass the CETP at night. He is nervous and says the association president has already enquired about "who is taking samples."

The CETP is old, and upgrading it needs about Rs 40 crore. The association says it needs 18 months and warns that closure notices would force units to shut down or relocate to another state. The local MLA phones you and requests that you "go slow, give them time, jobs are at stake." The association president offers, as part of its CSR, to fund a primary health centre in the affected villages and to provide you "full cooperation" in future inspections. Your Chairman has not given any direction and has said, "Use your discretion, but avoid a crisis before the festival season."

Under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, you have powers to issue directions, impose closure or cut off utilities.

---

## Questions

**Marks: 20 | Suggested length: ~250–300 words**

1. Identify the ethical issues and the stakeholders involved in this case. **(5 marks)**
2. What are the options available to you? Evaluate the merits and demerits of each. **(8 marks)**
3. Which course of action would you adopt, and why? Substantiate with ethical reasoning. **(5 marks)**
4. Which values and principles of public service are at stake in this case, and how would you protect Mr. Rao? **(2 marks)**

## Model Approach (read ONLY after you have written your own answer)

### 1. Stakeholders

- **Downstream villagers, especially children**: health, livelihood and right to a clean environment (Art. 21).
- **Factory workers (8,000+)** and their dependants: jobs and income.
- **Industry owners and the association**: compliant and non-compliant units alike. Note that the law-abiding units are also harmed by the free-riders.
- **Mr. Rao (whistleblower)**: personal safety, career, trust in the institution.
- **The SPCB / the officer (you)**: institutional credibility and legal duty.
- **The local MLA and political class**: electoral and local-economy concerns.
- **District administration, state government and future generations**: sustainability, river ecosystem.

### 2. Core ethical dilemmas

- **Public health and environment vs economic interest and employment**: a classic short-term vs long-term and concentrated vs diffuse harm conflict.
- **Duty under law vs political/peer pressure**: rule of law vs "discretion" used as a euphemism for inaction.
- **Integrity vs inducement**: the CSR offer and "cooperation" is a conflict of interest and potential quid pro quo.
- **Protecting the whistleblower vs institutional comfort**: duty of care to a subordinate.
- **Rule vs compassion**: strict closure may punish compliant units and innocent workers.

### 3. Evaluation of options

| Option | Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Accept MLA/association request; go slow, take the CSR offer** | Avoids short-term conflict; keeps jobs and good relations; gets a PHC. | Abdicates statutory duty; ongoing harm to children; accepting CSR in this context is a conflict of interest and compromises independence; sets a precedent; betrays Mr. Rao and the villagers. Ethically and legally indefensible. |
| **B. Immediate closure of the entire cluster** | Strong deterrent; fully prioritises health; legally permissible. | Disproportionate; punishes compliant units; 8,000 workers lose income overnight; likely litigation and unrest; may push units to shift operations unregulated. Violates proportionality. |
| **C. Targeted, graded enforcement with a time-bound compliance plan** | Proportionate; consistent with the law; protects health urgently while giving a realistic path; keeps compliant units operating; allows the state to leverage funds. | Needs strong monitoring; some pressure and delay risk; villagers may see the timeline as too slow, so interim relief is essential. |
| **D. Escalate only, passing the file upward without acting** | Shares accountability. | Evasion of personal responsibility; delay hurts affected people. |

### 4. Recommended course of action (Option C, executed firmly)

1. **Verify and secure the evidence**: re-sample independently with proper chain of custody, in the presence of witnesses/a third-party lab, including surprise night inspections to confirm the bypass. Do not rely on a single report.
2. **Immediate action against the clear violators**: issue show-cause notices and then directions or closure and utility disconnection for the two units found bypassing the CETP. Impose environmental compensation (polluter-pays principle), and consider prosecution.
3. **Time-bound compliance order for the cluster**: a binding CETP upgrade schedule with milestones (for example interim treatment, tertiary treatment, real-time online effluent monitoring and Zero Liquid Discharge planning), backed by bank guarantees. Seek state/central scheme support for the Rs 40 crore with the association's co-funding. Warn that missing milestones triggers closure of the defaulting units, not a blanket shutdown.
4. **Interim relief for villagers**: arrange tanker water, medical camps through the District Health Officer, and a health survey. Seek compensation from the polluter fund. **Decline the CSR offer tied to the regulator** (any health facility can be pursued independently through the Collector/state with transparent funding and no link to enforcement).
5. **Handle political pressure transparently**: respond to the MLA in writing, explaining the legal mandate and the graduated plan that safeguards jobs. Brief the Chairman and the District Collector in writing, so the decision is on record.
6. **Protect Mr. Rao**: keep his identity confidential, record the report officially, assign him to a team rather than alone, and ensure no retaliation. Cite the Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014 principles and offer to escalate any threat to the police.
7. **Transparency**: put monitoring data in the public domain and hold a meeting with the Gram Sabha/affected villages (public participation).

**Justification**: this is the only option that satisfies legality, proportionality, the precautionary principle and the duty of care to the vulnerable, while recognising the livelihood concern. The aim is *compliance and restoration*, not punishment.

### 5. Values and thinkers to cite

- **Values**: integrity, objectivity, accountability, courage of conviction, compassion towards the weak, impartiality, transparency, public interest over private interest.
- **Kautilya (Arthashastra)**: the king's happiness lies in the happiness of his subjects; official must act with probity.
- **Gandhi's Talisman**: test every decision by its effect on the poorest and weakest person.
- **Max Weber's ethic of responsibility** (as opposed to mere conviction): weigh consequences, including jobs and health.
- **Constitutional and legal anchors**: Art. 21 (right to a healthy environment), Art. 48A and 51A(g), polluter-pays and precautionary principles, sustainable development, and the Nolan principles of public life.

### 6. Common mistakes to avoid

- Choosing an extreme (appease everyone or shut everything) without justifying proportionality.
- Treating the CSR offer as a harmless good deed, instead of flagging conflict of interest.
- Forgetting the whistleblower or the affected children.
- Writing only "I will consult my seniors," which is escalation without a decision.
- Ignoring the interim relief for villagers.
