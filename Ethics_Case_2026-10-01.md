## The Case Study

You are the Member Secretary of a State Pollution Control Board (SPCB). Your Board has to recommend, within 15 days, the renewal of the "Consent to Operate" for a large sponge-iron and captive power plant in an industrial district. The plant employs about 1,800 workers directly and nearly 4,000 through contractors and transport, and is the economic backbone of the area. It is also a major source of revenue for the State.

Your field team's recent inspection and a downstream water sample report show that the plant has been discharging inadequately treated effluent into a seasonal river, and that fly-ash is being dumped on open land adjoining three villages. Residents of these villages, mostly small farmers and landless labourers, have complained of crop damage, skin ailments and respiratory problems. A local NGO has filed a representation, and a regional newspaper has carried the story.

The plant management says the violations are "temporary" and due to equipment failure, and promises to install a new effluent treatment unit within eight months. The Chairman of the SPCB, a political appointee, privately tells you that "the State cannot afford a closure in an election year" and suggests renewing the consent with a "mild advisory." The local MLA, supported by the workers' union, has threatened a protest if the plant is shut. A senior colleague reminds you that the previous Member Secretary was transferred after taking a strict stand on a similar matter.

Meanwhile, you learn that a junior scientist in your Board, whose report documented the violations, is being pressured to "revise" his findings. He has come to you in distress, saying he will not alter his report but fears for his career.

---

## Questions

1. Identify the stakeholders in this case and the ethical issues involved. **(5 marks)**
2. What options are available to you? Evaluate the merits and demerits of each. **(6 marks)**
3. Which course of action would you adopt, and how would you handle the pressure from the Chairman, the political leadership and the union? Also state how you would protect the junior scientist. **(6 marks)**
4. Which values and principles of public administration guide your decision? **(3 marks)**

# Model Approach (read only after attempting)

## 1. Stakeholders

- **Affected villagers** (farmers, labourers, children, the elderly): their health, livelihood and right to a clean environment (Article 21).
- **Plant workers and contractors' employees:** their jobs and family income.
- **Plant management / owners:** profit, compliance cost, reputation.
- **The SPCB, the Member Secretary and the Chairman:** institutional credibility and statutory duty under the Water (Prevention and Control of Pollution) Act, 1974, and the Air Act, 1981.
- **The junior scientist and the field team:** professional integrity, job security.
- **Political leadership and the local MLA:** electoral and revenue concerns.
- **Local NGO and media:** public accountability.
- **Future generations and the river ecosystem:** intergenerational equity.
- **The State and society at large:** rule of law, sustainable development.

## 2. Core Ethical Dilemmas

- **Public interest vs. political pressure:** the statutory duty of an independent regulator against a superior's "suggestion."
- **Environment vs. employment (short-term livelihood vs. long-term health):** a conflict of two legitimate goods, not good vs. evil.
- **Rule vs. compassion:** strict closure vs. concern for workers.
- **Integrity vs. loyalty to the organisation and the Chairman.**
- **Truth and scientific objectivity vs. convenience:** the demand to "revise" a report.
- **Personal risk vs. duty:** fear of transfer.
- **Precautionary principle and "polluter pays"** vs. the promise of future compliance.

## 3. Evaluation of Options

**Option A: Renew the consent with a mild advisory (as the Chairman suggests).**
*Merits:* avoids immediate job loss and protest; keeps good relations with the superior and political leadership.
*Demerits:* a regulatory abdication; continued harm to villagers; the Board's credibility is destroyed; sets a precedent for other industries; makes you complicit in suppressing evidence; may attract action from the NGT or High Court, and personal liability.

**Option B: Refuse renewal and order immediate closure.**
*Merits:* strongest signal for deterrence; protects public health at once; fully in line with the precautionary principle.
*Demerits:* sudden loss of livelihood for thousands, unrest and law-and-order issues; possibly disproportionate when the violation is remediable; likely to invite litigation and stay orders; the wages of workers are not protected.

**Option C: Conditional / graded action (recommended).**
Issue a show-cause notice, then renew only a short-term, conditional consent, or issue directions under Section 33A of the Water Act, with stringent, time-bound, verifiable conditions: immediate stoppage of untreated discharge, safe fly-ash disposal and removal from open land, installation of the effluent treatment plant on a compressed timeline (not eight months), real-time online monitoring, a bank guarantee and environmental compensation under the polluter-pays principle, and automatic closure for non-compliance.
*Merits:* balances environment and employment; legally defensible; proportionate; keeps the plant accountable.
*Demerits:* the Chairman and political class may be displeased; requires strong monitoring capacity; the interim period still carries some risk to villagers unless the immediate measures are enforced.

**Option D: Refer the matter upward / seek an independent technical review or court direction.**
*Merits:* shares responsibility and gains legitimacy.
*Demerits:* may look like evasion if used to delay; the villagers continue to suffer meanwhile. This is best used as a supplement, not a substitute.

## 4. Recommended Course of Action

1. **Do not alter or dilute the evidence.** Record the findings formally. Get the water samples re-tested by an independent, NABL-accredited laboratory, so the facts are beyond dispute.
2. **Adopt Option C.** Issue a show-cause notice and a speaking, reasoned order with graded, time-bound conditions and a bank guarantee. Direct immediate cessation of untreated discharge and removal of fly-ash, with partial curtailment of the offending units if not complied with within a short period. Impose environmental compensation.
3. **Deal with the Chairman respectfully but firmly.** Explain in writing that the Board is bound by statute, that a "mild advisory" without basis would not survive judicial scrutiny, and that the conditional approach is also the best protection for the Board and the State against NGT action. Put the advice on file; do not rely on verbal instructions.
4. **Engage the workers and the union.** Explain that the aim is not closure but compliance, and that the conditional order protects their jobs in the long run. Ask the management to ensure wages are protected during any partial curtailment.
5. **Protect the junior scientist.** Assure him that his report stands, record it officially, instruct that no one may pressure him, shift him to a protected role if needed, and invoke the whistle-blower protection framework and the Board's vigilance mechanism. Document everything.
6. **Remedy for the affected villagers.** Arrange a health camp through the district health office, water and soil testing, and compensation from the polluter. Share the order with the community and the NGO to keep the process transparent.
7. **Escalate if needed.** If improper pressure continues, record it and bring it to the notice of the Chief Secretary or the appropriate authority; remain ready for the personal consequences of a principled stand.

## 5. Values and Principles at Stake

- **Integrity, objectivity and impartiality** (Nolan's principles of public life; Seven Principles).
- **Accountability and transparency**, particularly the duty to give reasons.
- **Courage of conviction / moral courage** in the face of political pressure.
- **Compassion and empathy** towards both villagers and workers.
- **Sustainable development, precautionary principle, polluter-pays and intergenerational equity.**
- **Rule of law and constitutional morality** (Article 21, Article 48A, Article 51A(g)).

## 6. Thinkers / Concepts to Cite (1-2)

- **Mahatma Gandhi:** "The Earth provides enough to satisfy every man's need but not every man's greed." This applies directly to the environment-vs-profit conflict. His Talisman also asks that every decision be tested against the welfare of the poorest.
- **Max Weber, the ethic of responsibility:** a public servant must weigh the consequences of an action, not merely follow rules mechanically or the superior's wishes. This supports the graded, proportionate approach.
- (Optional) **Kautilya:** the king's happiness lies in the happiness of his subjects; and the **Brundtland Report** definition of sustainable development.
