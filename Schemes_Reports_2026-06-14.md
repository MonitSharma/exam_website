## SCHEMES & PROGRAMMES

### 1. Delhi-NCR Old Truck & Bus Replacement Scheme (Support to NCRPB)
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Environment & pollution; GS2 — Centre-State coordination

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** National Capital Region Planning Board (NCRPB) under the **Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs (MoHUA)**; implemented with Ministry of Road Transport & Highways (MoRTH) and Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas (MoPNG).
- **Launched:** Approved by Union Cabinet, **3 June 2026** (two-year scheme).
- **Type:** Central Sector (with State contribution via tax concessions).
- **Objective:** Cut vehicular air pollution in Delhi-NCR by incentivising replacement of old, polluting commercial vehicles with cleaner ones.
- **Key features:**
  - Total outlay **₹9,585 crore** — ₹5,041 crore Central share + ~₹1,601 crore in State tax concessions; rest from incentives/levies.
  - Targets owners of trucks/buses meeting **BS-IV or earlier** norms, to replace with **BS-VI or stricter / EV** vehicles.
  - Centre: 5% interest subvention on loans (5 yrs), monthly fuel vouchers, lump-sum benefit for EV purchase; States: registration-fee waiver, motor-vehicle-tax concession, waiver of pending liabilities on old vehicles.
  - Covers participating States/UTs: **Delhi, Haryana, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh.**
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** ~**2.07 lakh** vehicle owners — **1.91 lakh trucks + 16,329 buses.**
- **Significance:** First large, incentive-led (rather than purely penal) fleet-modernisation push for NCR; complements GRAP and end-of-life-vehicle policy.
- **Criticism / issues:** Uptake depends on State follow-through on tax waivers; freight operators may resist switching costs; EV charging/fuelling infrastructure gaps remain.

### 2. BHAVYA — Bharat Audyogik Vikas Yojana (BHAVYA Portal launched)
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Industrial policy & infrastructure; GS2 — Cooperative/competitive federalism

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Department for Promotion of Industry & Internal Trade (DPIIT)**, Ministry of Commerce & Industry; Project Management Agency = **National Industrial Corridor Development Corporation (NICDC)**.
- **Launched:** Scheme approved March 2026; **BHAVYA Portal launched by Union Minister Piyush Goyal, c. 8–9 June 2026** (operationalisation).
- **Type:** Central Sector (Centre–State joint-venture model).
- **Objective:** Build **100 investment-ready, "plug-and-play" world-class industrial parks** over six years.
- **Key features:**
  - Outlay **₹33,660 crore** (~₹34,000 crore budgetary reference).
  - **51:49 Centre-State JV model** — States provide encumbrance-free land; Centre (via NICDC) matches equity for on-site infrastructure.
  - **Challenge-based, competitive selection:** States submit Detailed Project Reports through the BHAVYA Portal; appraisal on land availability, multimodal connectivity, investor interest, sectoral strength.
  - Parks to feature multimodal connectivity, reliable utilities, digital governance, worker-support facilities, and sustainability features.
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** States/UTs (competitive bidding); domestic and global investors, MSMEs, manufacturers in 100 parks.
- **Significance:** Shifts from ad-hoc land allotment to transparent, competitive federalism; aims to lift manufacturing's GDP share and integrate India into global supply chains.
- **Criticism / issues:** Land acquisition / encumbrance-free title remains the binding constraint at State level; execution speed and investor take-up of earlier industrial-park schemes (NIMZ, NICDP) has been uneven.

### 3. Indiramma Family Life Insurance Scheme (Telangana)
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Government welfare schemes; State social security

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Government of Telangana** (announced in 2026-27 State Budget).
- **Launched:** **2 June 2026** (Telangana Formation Day).
- **Type:** State scheme.
- **Objective:** Provide a universal life-insurance safety net against the sudden death of an earning family member.
- **Key features:**
  - **₹5 lakh** life-insurance cover **per family.**
  - Universal design — extended without income/social-status discrimination (poor, middle-class and affluent alike).
  - Budget allocation ~**₹4,000 crore** for implementation.
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** ~**1.15 crore families** across Telangana.
- **Significance:** Among the largest State-funded universal family-insurance schemes; under the "Indiramma" welfare brand.
- **Criticism / issues:** Fiscal sustainability of a universal (non-targeted) payout; claim-settlement and verification machinery yet to be tested at scale.

### 4. Annapurna Yojana / Annapurna Bhandar (West Bengal)
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Welfare of women; DBT-based income support

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Government of West Bengal.**
- **Launched:** Effective **1 June 2026**; first DBT transfers from **3 June 2026.**
- **Type:** State scheme.
- **Objective:** Assured monthly income support for women's socio-economic upliftment.
- **Key features:**
  - **₹3,000 / month** transferred directly to Aadhaar-linked bank accounts via **DBT.**
  - Eligibility: women aged **25–60**, not income-tax payers, not in permanent government employment / not drawing regular government salary or pension.
  - Existing **Lakshmir Bhandar** beneficiaries to be migrated to the new scheme.
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** Estimated ~**2 crore women.**
- **Significance:** Continues the trend of large, unconditional cash-transfer schemes for women shaping State politics and budgets.
- **Criticism / issues:** Heavy recurring fiscal burden; debate over unconditional cash transfers vs. targeted services; exclusion errors from the age/tax filters.

### 5. Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM) — 90-crore ABHA milestone
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Health governance & e-governance; GS3 — Digital public infrastructure

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **National Health Authority (NHA)** under the **Ministry of Health & Family Welfare.**
- **Launched:** ABDM launched 2021; **crossed 90 crore ABHA accounts — milestone reported c. 31 May–early June 2026** (PIB).
- **Type:** Central Sector.
- **Objective:** Build a connected, interoperable, citizen-centric digital health ecosystem linking citizens, doctors, hospitals, pharmacies and insurers.
- **Key features:**
  - **ABHA** = unique **14-digit** digital health ID for consent-based linking/sharing of health records.
  - Growth trajectory: 14.7 cr (2021) → 30.4 cr (2022) → 50.6 cr (2023) → 72.2 cr (2024) → 84.5 cr (2025) → **90 cr (2026).**
  - **Uttar Pradesh** leads with >15.3 crore ABHAs; Rajasthan and Maharashtra >7 crore each.
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** 90 crore citizens with digital health IDs nationwide.
- **Significance:** Among the world's largest digital health ecosystems; backbone for portable health records and scheme convergence (e.g., AB-PMJAY).
- **Criticism / issues:** Account *creation* far outpaces *active linkage of records*; data-privacy and consent-framework concerns; rural/elderly digital-access gaps.

## REPORTS & INDICES

### A. NITI Aayog — "School Education System in India: Temporal Analysis and Policy Roadmap for Quality Enhancement"
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Education / social-sector governance

- **Publishing body:** **NITI Aayog** (released c. early June 2026).
- **What it measures:** Decade-long analysis of school education across all **36 States/UTs** — access, infrastructure, equity, digital integration, learning outcomes.
- **India's standing / scale:** World's largest school system — **14.71 lakh schools, ~24.69 crore students.**
- **Key findings:**
  - Government-school enrolment share fell to **49.24% (2024-25)** from ~71% (2005) — a marked shift to private schools.
  - Persistent gaps in **foundational literacy & numeracy**, learning outcomes, teacher availability and equity.
  - **Punjab** ranked No.1 on school-education quality, ahead of Kerala/Maharashtra/Delhi; recommends a **"Whole-of-Society"** improvement approach.

### B. SIPRI Yearbook 2026
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Defence & security; GS2 — International relations / disarmament

- **Publishing body:** **Stockholm International Peace Research Institute (SIPRI)** — released **8 June 2026.**
- **What it measures:** Global armaments, disarmament and international security; annual estimates of nuclear arsenals.
- **India's standing / key findings:**
  - India's stockpile estimated at **~190 warheads** (Jan 2026), up from ~180 a year earlier; modernisation shifting toward **longer-range missiles** (China-focused deterrence).
  - SIPRI notes India "may have started to deploy a small number of warheads," including possible sea-based (SSBN) deterrence patrols.
  - Nine nuclear-armed states hold ~**12,187 warheads** total (~9,745 in military stockpiles); China's arsenal growing fastest.

### C. Global Peace Index (GPI) 2026
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Internal security; GS2 — International indices

- **Publishing body:** **Institute for Economics & Peace (IEP)** — released c. **11 June 2026.**
- **What it measures:** Relative peacefulness of **163 countries** across ongoing conflict, societal safety/security, and militarisation.
- **India's standing / key findings:**
  - India ranked **127th / 163**, score **2.409** — slipped from 115th (2025); peacefulness down ~2.9%.
  - Decline driven largely by the **"ongoing conflict"** domain (worsened ~9.2%) — internal-conflict deaths and strained relations with neighbours (Pakistan tensions, Myanmar-border instability).
  - *Caveat in commentary:* critics argue the GPI methodology can penalise large democracies actively countering terrorism.

### D. Report of the Committee on Datasets for State Finance Commissions (SFCs)
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Federalism, local government, fiscal devolution (Art. 243-I, 73rd/74th Amendments)

- **Publishing body:** **Ministry of Panchayati Raj**; released **8 June 2026** by **CEA Dr. V. Anantha Nageswaran**, with the Panchayati Raj Secretary and NIPFP (Dr. Manish Gupta).
- **What it measures / aims:** Identifies the essential datasets State Finance Commissions need and recommends standardized frameworks for **evidence-based fiscal devolution to Panchayati Raj Institutions.**
- **Key findings / recommendations:**
  - Origin: gap flagged at the Nov 2024 Finance Commissions' Conclave — difficulty accessing comprehensive cross-department datasets.
  - Recommends standardized SFC data systems, permanent State-level data cells, and reporting frameworks to strengthen local public finance.
  - Reinforces the constitutional mandate of SFCs (Art. 243-I) and timely, data-driven devolution to the third tier.
