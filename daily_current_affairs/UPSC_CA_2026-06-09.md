### 5. Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Ordinance, 2026 — strength 34 → 38  *(GS2 Polity — landmark)*
**Headline:** A Presidential Ordinance under **Article 123** raised the Supreme Court's sanctioned strength from **34 to 38** judges (i.e., **37 puisne judges + the CJI**), reviving the debate over using the ordinance route to create judicial posts before Parliament's nod.

**Exam angle:** Landmark Polity item — exactly the kind of "major development" worth a Mains question. Tests ordinance-making power, separation of powers, judicial independence, and the appointments process (collegium). Strong statement-based Prelims potential.

**Memorize:**
- Strength: **34 → 38** (Section 2 of the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act, 1956 now reads **"thirty-seven"** puisne judges, besides the CJI). Notified in the Gazette **16 May 2026**; Cabinet had cleared the corresponding Bill ~11 days earlier.
- **Article 123** = President's ordinance-making power; an ordinance must be **laid before both Houses** and **ceases to operate 6 weeks** after Parliament reassembles unless approved (max life if Parliament not approving). Original Constitution sanctioned strength was **7+1 (CJI)**; raised over decades to 34 (2019) and now 38.
- Rationale cited: case **pendency/backlog** and rising workload.

**Notebook:** GS2 Polity.
**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth → "Supreme Court" + "Ordinance-making Power of President (Art. 123)" chapters* — update the sanctioned strength to 38, log this as a fresh ordinance-route example, and cross-link to **Collegium system / NJAC (99th Amendment, struck down 2015)**.

### Mains Question (GS2 — Polity)

**Q.** *"Recourse to the ordinance route under Article 123 to expand the sanctioned strength of the Supreme Court tests the constitutional balance between executive efficiency and judicial independence."* **Critically examine.** (250 words / 15 marks)

**Model-answer skeleton:**
- **Intro / context:** The 2026 Ordinance raised SC strength 34→38 to ease pendency; note Article 123 is a legitimate but exceptional power meant for when Parliament is not in session.
- **The case for:** Genuine urgency (mounting case backlog, rising workload), constitutionally valid instrument, executive cannot await a session; precedent of periodic strength increases.
- **The concerns:** Posts created by a *temporary* executive instrument pending Parliamentary approval; risk to security of tenure and judicial independence; propriety of filling ordinance-created posts before ratification; separation of powers and the *D.C. Wadhwa (1987)* caution against re-promulgation/ordinance overuse.
- **Way forward:** Prompt Parliamentary ratification; transparent, time-bound collegium process; structural reforms (regional/Constitution benches, National Court of Appeal debate) so capacity expansion is durable rather than ad hoc. Conclude on balancing efficiency with institutional integrity.

## PART C — ANSWERS

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 2 only.** Surha Tal is India's 100th Ramsar site and India now leads Asia; but globally India is **3rd** (UK 176, Mexico 144), so statement 3 is wrong.

**Q2 — (b) Uttar Pradesh; Ganga basin.** Surha Tal lies in Ballia, UP — a freshwater wetland in the middle Ganga basin, formed from a Ganga meander.

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Strength raised 34→38 (37 puisne + CJI); promulgated under Article 123; ordinances must be laid before Parliament and lapse six weeks after it reassembles unless approved.

**Q4 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** June 2026 MPC held repo at 5.25% (Neutral); MPC = 6 members (3 RBI + 3 Govt). Statement 3 is wrong — repo is the rate at which RBI **lends** to banks (RBI borrowing is via reverse repo/SDF).

**Q5 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** G1 carries Vyommitra; Gaganyaan targets human spaceflight to LEO; the crew module re-enters and splashes down with recovery support.

**Q6 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct — Ramsar (1971, Iran), the Montreux Record, and MoEFCC as the nodal ministry in India.

**Q7 — (a) 2 only.** An ordinance has the same force as an Act (2 correct). It can be issued when **even one** House is not in session, not only when both are (1 wrong); the President's ordinance power is co-extensive with Parliament's legislative competence, so it does not ordinarily extend to State List subjects (3 wrong).
