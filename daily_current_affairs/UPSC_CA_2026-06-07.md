### 2. India crosses 100 Ramsar sites — Jai Prakash Narayan Bird Sanctuary (Environment)

**Headline:** On **World Environment Day (5 June 2026)**, the **Jai Prakash Narayan Bird Sanctuary (Surha Tal)** in **Ballia district, Uttar Pradesh** was designated India's **100th Ramsar site**.

**Exam angle:** Classic Prelims fact-magnet (number, state, "places in news") plus GS3 wetland-conservation Mains hook. India now has the **most Ramsar sites in Asia** and ranks **3rd globally**.

**Memorise:**
- 100th site: **Surha Tal / Jai Prakash Narayan Bird Sanctuary**, **Ballia, UP**; a **freshwater wetland (~34.32 sq km)** formed from a **meander of the Ganga**; **13th Ramsar site of UP**.
- Global ranking: **India 3rd** (after **UK – 176**, **Mexico – 144**); **1st in Asia**.
- Framework: **Ramsar Convention (1971, Iran)**; "wise use"; India's domestic law = **Wetlands (Conservation & Management) Rules, 2017**.

### 4. ISRO lines up GISAT-1A (EOS-05) geo-imaging satellite (Science & Tech)

**Headline:** ISRO is set to launch **GISAT-1A (EOS-05)** — a **geostationary Earth-observation (geo-imaging) satellite** — on a **GSLV Mk II (GSLV-F17)** from **Sriharikota**, scheduled for **end-June 2026**.

**Exam angle:** Tests the **GEO vs. polar Sun-synchronous (SSO)** distinction. Unlike most remote-sensing sats in low polar orbits, a geo-imaging satellite sits over a fixed point → **near-continuous, near-real-time imaging** of the subcontinent for **disaster/hazard monitoring**.

**Memorise:**
- **GISAT/EOS** = **geostationary** Earth-observation; images in **multispectral + hyperspectral** bands; revisit ~every **5–30 min** at coarse resolution.
- Launcher: **GSLV Mk II** (3-stage, cryogenic upper stage, 4 liquid strap-ons); site **Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota (AP)**.
- Context: 2026's first launch (**PSLV-C62**, Jan) failed (3rd-stage), losing DRDO's **Anvesha** hyperspectral sat — so this launch carries recovery significance.

### Mains-style Question (GS2/GS3)

**Q.** "Critical minerals have become a new theatre of strategic competition." In this light, **examine** the significance of the Quad Critical Minerals Initiative for India's economic security and its Indo-Pacific strategy. *(150 words / 10 marks)*

**Model-answer skeleton:**
1. **Frame the problem** — Critical minerals (Li, Co, REEs, graphite) underpin EVs, semiconductors, clean energy and defence; China dominates global processing → concentration risk and coercion leverage.
2. **Significance for India** — Access to up to $20 bn finance, technology and recycling; diversifies supply away from a single source; complements domestic push (National Critical Mineral Mission, MMDR 2023 amendment, KABIL, MSP membership).
3. **Indo-Pacific strategy** — Shifts the Quad toward economic statecraft beyond maritime security; positions India as a reliable supply-chain partner; strengthens collective resilience in the Indo-Pacific.
4. **Caveats & way forward** — India's limited reserves/processing capacity, financing and environmental-cost concerns, and the need to preserve strategic autonomy (e.g., Russia ties). Conclude: integrate Quad cooperation with domestic mineral-security capacity for genuine resilience.

## ANSWERS

**Q1 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** Surha Tal / Jai Prakash Narayan Bird Sanctuary is in **Ballia, Uttar Pradesh**, not Bihar (so 1 is wrong). India is now **1st in Asia** and **3rd globally** (after UK and Mexico) in Ramsar sites.

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Announced at the **New Delhi** Quad FMM (26 May 2026); members are India, Australia, Japan, USA; up to **$20 bn** intended for critical-mineral supply chains.

**Q3 — (c) Both 1 and 2.** The MPC kept the **repo rate at 5.25%** and retained a **'neutral'** stance (decision unanimous).

**Q4 — (b).** GISAT/EOS is a **geostationary geo-imaging** Earth-observation satellite, distinct from polar SSO remote-sensing sats; it enables near-continuous monitoring for disaster management.

**Q5 — (b) UGC.** Foreign branch campuses operate under the **UGC (Setting up & Operation of Campuses of Foreign HEIs in India) Regulations, 2023**, flowing from NEP 2020.

**Q6 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Montreux Record flags ecological-character change at Ramsar sites; India's listed sites are **Keoladeo (Rajasthan)** and **Loktak (Manipur)**; **Chilika** was delisted after restoration (only the 2nd site globally to be removed).

**Q7 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** MPC is statutory under the **RBI Act 1934 (2016 amendment)**, has **6 members (3 RBI + 3 govt)**, and the **Governor has a casting vote** on a tie.
