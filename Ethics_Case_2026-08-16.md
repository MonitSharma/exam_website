## Case Study

You are the Joint Drugs Controller in a state Food and Drug Administration, heading the team that clears manufacturing licences and inspects pharmaceutical units. During a routine inspection of "Vediksha Pharma," a mid-sized company, your team documents serious violations: two batches of a widely used paediatric antibiotic syrup fail sterility norms, temperature logs in the storage unit have been manually overwritten, and the in-house quality lab lacks a functioning calibration record for three months. Under the rules, this warrants immediate suspension of the licence and a public recall.

Vediksha Pharma is the district's largest employer, sustaining nearly 1,800 direct jobs and thousands of ancillary livelihoods. It is also a supplier to the state's own free-medicine scheme, and a sudden recall could disrupt drug availability in rural health centres for weeks.

That evening, you receive a call from a senior official in the Health Department, conveying that "the matter should be handled with restraint given the sensitivities," and hinting that the company has committed to fixing lapses within a fortnight if given a quiet warning instead of suspension. The company's managing director separately requests a meeting and, in it, offers to fund a "public-health awareness initiative" branded jointly with your department. Your junior inspector, who first flagged the violations, privately tells you he is worried about his transfer if the report is diluted.

You must decide how to record your findings and what regulatory action to recommend, knowing that a compromised batch of paediatric medicine could harm children, while an abrupt shutdown carries real economic and supply consequences.

---

## Questions

**(a)** Identify the ethical issues involved and the stakeholders affected in this case. **(5 marks)**

**(b)** What are the options available to you as the Joint Drugs Controller? Evaluate the merits and demerits of each. **(10 marks)**

**(c)** What course of action would you adopt and why? Justify with reference to the values a public regulator must uphold. **(10 marks)**

*Total: 25 marks | Suggested length: ~250–300 words*

## Model Approach

*(Read this only after writing your own answer.)*

### Stakeholders
- **Children and patients** relying on the antibiotic syrup — the most vulnerable, non-negotiable priority.
- **You (the regulator)** — custodian of public safety and legal duty.
- **The junior inspector** — subordinate acting with integrity, fearing reprisal.
- **Vediksha Pharma** — its management, and separately its 1,800 workers and dependent ancillary livelihoods.
- **The Health Department / senior official** — exerting informal pressure.
- **Rural health centres and the free-medicine scheme** — dependent on supply continuity.
- **The wider public and the credibility of the regulatory institution.**

### Core ethical dilemmas
- **Public safety (rule of law) vs. economic/livelihood consequences** — compassion for workers cannot override risk to children's lives.
- **Integrity and objectivity vs. political/hierarchical pressure** — the "handle with restraint" call is an attempt to subordinate a safety function to expediency.
- **Probity vs. conflict of interest** — the "jointly branded awareness initiative" is a disguised inducement; accepting it compromises independence.
- **Duty to protect a conscientious subordinate** vs. self-preservation.

### Evaluation of options
1. **Dilute the report and issue a quiet warning (as pressured).** Merit: avoids immediate economic disruption and pleases superiors. Demerit: falsifies an official record, risks children's lives, betrays the inspector, sets a precedent for regulatory capture — ethically indefensible and illegal.
2. **Full suspension and public recall, no mitigation.** Merit: legally correct, maximally protective, unambiguous. Demerit: does nothing to soften avoidable supply and livelihood harm; a rigid stance that ignores manageable second-order effects.
3. **Record findings truthfully and act proportionately (recommended).** Suspend the affected batches immediately, order recall of the failed paediatric syrup, and issue a show-cause notice for the licence — while simultaneously activating alternative supply for the free-medicine scheme and giving the firm a defined, monitored corrective window for non-safety-critical lapses. Decline the "awareness initiative" in writing and shield the inspector by recording his diligence formally. Merit: protects safety without gratuitous harm, upholds law and independence, is defensible on record. Demerit: demands courage against pressure and more coordination work.

### Recommended course of action
Adopt **Option 3**. The findings must be recorded exactly as observed — a public safety record cannot be negotiated. Take firm action on the safety-critical failures (batch suspension + recall) because no economic argument outweighs the risk of harming children. Address the economic dimension not by weakening enforcement but by proactively arranging alternative drug supply and offering the company a transparent, time-bound compliance pathway for remediable process gaps. Politely but firmly refuse the branded initiative and put your refusal on file. Formally commend the junior inspector to insulate him from reprisal, and escalate the informal pressure through proper channels if it persists.

### Values / thinkers to cite
- **Objectivity, integrity, and non-partisanship** in public service (Nolan Committee's Seven Principles of Public Life).
- **The public servant as a "trustee" of public interest** — echoing the Gandhian idea of trusteeship and the 2nd ARC's emphasis on *probity and public interest* over expediency.
- Kant's maxim that a person (here, a child) must be treated as an end, never merely as a means to economic or political convenience.
