## Case Study

**(Marks: 25 | Suggested length: ~250–300 words in total across the sub-answers)**

You are the Sub-Divisional Magistrate of a rural sub-division with a long history of caste-based social hierarchy. A young woman, 23, and a man, 26, from different castes, married three weeks ago under the Special Marriage Act. Both are adults; the marriage is legally valid. They approach you seeking police protection, saying the woman's family has threatened to "settle the matter."

The next morning, an assembly of village elders — not a statutory body, but the effective authority in social matters across forty villages — meets you. They demand that the woman be "restored" to her parents for counselling, warning that unless this happens, they will impose a social boycott on the man's community: no work, no shop credit, no access to the common pond. They are not, they insist, asking you to break any law, only to "avoid a disturbance."

Your Station House Officer, a local man, quietly advises that protection will "inflame things," and suggests you persuade the couple to leave the district instead. A sitting legislator telephones to say the elders are "reasonable people."

That afternoon, the woman is brought before you by her parents. In their presence, in a low voice, she says she wishes to go home. Her hands are shaking. Her parents demand you record this as her free statement and close the matter. A journalist is waiting outside for a comment, and the sub-division's weekly market — where both communities trade — opens in two days.

---

## Sub-Questions

**(a)** Identify the ethical issues involved in this case and list the stakeholders, indicating the interest of each. **(5 marks)**

**(b)** What options are available to you as the Sub-Divisional Magistrate? Evaluate the merits and demerits of each. **(10 marks)**

**(c)** What course of action would you adopt, and why? Justify your choice on grounds of legality, ethics and administrative prudence. **(5 marks)**

**(d)** Which values and principles, constitutional and personal, are at stake in your decision? **(5 marks)**

## Model Approach

*(Read only after writing your own answer.)*

### 1. Stakeholders and their interests

| Stakeholder | Interest at stake |
|---|---|
| The young woman | Life, liberty, bodily autonomy, freedom to choose a spouse; possibly under coercion |
| Her husband | Personal safety, dignity, livelihood; his community faces collective punishment |
| The woman's family | Perceived family and caste honour; emotional distress, genuine or performed |
| The village assembly / elders | Retention of traditional social authority |
| The man's community | Protection from social boycott — an illegal collective sanction |
| SHO and police staff | Local social embeddedness versus statutory duty |
| The legislator | Political constituency management |
| You, the SDM | Constitutional oath, rule of law, personal integrity, public order |
| The wider public / media | Precedent: whether law protects the vulnerable when custom objects |

### 2. Core ethical dilemmas

- **Constitutional morality vs social morality** — the elders' demand is socially legitimate in the locality but constitutionally indefensible. Article 21 liberty is not subject to majority sentiment.
- **Public order vs individual rights** — the classic administrative temptation to purchase peace by sacrificing one person's rights. Order obtained by surrendering a right is not order; it is deferred disorder.
- **Apparent consent vs genuine consent** — her statement, made in her parents' presence with visible distress, cannot be treated as free will. Recording it uncritically would be procedurally convenient and ethically dishonest.
- **Loyalty to subordinates/local wisdom vs duty** — the SHO's advice is practical but amounts to outsourcing the problem by exiling the victim.
- **Moral courage vs career prudence** — the legislator's call is an unstated cost signal.

### 3. Evaluation of options

**Option 1 — Accept her statement at face value, hand her to the parents, close the file.**
*Merits:* Immediate de-escalation; market and boycott threat defused; politically safe.
*Demerits:* Abdication of duty. Consent extracted under duress is no consent. Real risk to her life; if harm follows, the State is complicit. Signals that coercion works, inviting repetition. Legally indefensible before a court.

**Option 2 — Persuade the couple to leave the district.**
*Merits:* Removes the flashpoint; superficially protects them.
*Demerits:* Punishes the victims for exercising a legal right; rewards extra-legal authority; abandons the boycott-threatened community; an administrator's job is to make the law hold *here*, not to relocate its beneficiaries.

**Option 3 — Confront the elders immediately with prosecution and force.**
*Merits:* Unambiguous assertion of the law's primacy.
*Demerits:* Without first securing the woman, it risks violence with her still exposed; alienates every channel of communication; imprudent sequencing, not wrong principle.

**Option 4 (recommended) — Secure the individual first, ascertain consent lawfully, then deal with the collective threat firmly and separately.**
*Merits:* Legally sound, ethically consistent, operationally sequenced.
*Demerits:* Demands time, resources and personal courage; friction with the SHO and the legislator.

### 4. Recommended course of action

1. **Separate her from all pressure.** Have her statement recorded by a woman magistrate/officer, in camera, away from family, after a cooling-off period in a State-run shelter or safe custody, with a woman constable present. Follow the *Lata Singh* and *Shakti Vahini* line: an adult's choice of partner is protected, and it is the State's duty to shield, not to mediate.
2. **Grant protection immediately** to the couple — a security detail and a designated point of contact — irrespective of the pending statement.
3. **Treat the boycott threat as an offence, not a negotiating position.** Issue a written warning to the assembly under preventive provisions (Sections 163/126 BNSS-type preventive orders), invoke SC/ST (PoA) Act provisions on social boycott where attracted, and record the threat in writing so it cannot later be denied.
4. **Handle the SHO institutionally** — a written order on file, and if he is compromised by local ties, reassign the case to an officer from outside the sub-division. Do not humiliate him publicly; do not rely on him either.
5. **Communicate, do not bargain.** Meet the elders once — courteously, in office, on record — to state what the law permits and what it does not. Brief the press factually, protecting the couple's location and identity details. Deploy for the market day; confidence-building with both communities through peace committees.
6. **Report up the chain in writing** to the District Magistrate and SP, so the decision is institutional rather than personal, and so any political pressure meets a documented record.

### 5. Values and thinkers to cite

- **Constitutional morality (Dr B. R. Ambedkar)** — "constitutional morality is not a natural sentiment; it has to be cultivated." The administrator's role is precisely to hold the constitutional line where social sentiment will not.
- **Rule of law (A. V. Dicey) / equality before law** — no extra-constitutional body may impose sanctions.
- **Moral courage and impartiality** — two of the Second ARC's core civil service values (*Ethics in Governance*, 4th Report); also Nolan's "objectivity" and "leadership."
- **Kantian dignity** — the woman is an end in herself, not a means to communal peace. Contrast with a crude utilitarian calculus of "one couple versus forty villages" — which fails even on its own terms, since permitting coercion maximises future harm.
- **Gandhi's Talisman** — recall the face of the weakest person you have seen and ask whether your step will be of any use to them.
- **Empathy without capitulation** — the parents' distress is real and deserves patient hearing; it does not create a right over an adult daughter.

**One-line spine for the answer:** *Secure the person first, establish free consent lawfully, enforce the law against collective coercion, and keep every step on the record.*
