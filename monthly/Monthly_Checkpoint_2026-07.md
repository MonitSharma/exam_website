## 3. Revisit Plan (Pause & Consolidate)

- Consolidate Fundamental Rights article-by-article (14–32), especially Article 32 and the writs. Keep a one-line note on which FRs are available to citizens only versus all persons.
- Build the FR–DPSP contrast on one page: justiciable vs non-justiciable, and the key DPSP articles (39, 40, 44, 48, 50). Note the FR–DPSP conflict cases (Champakam Dorairajan → Golaknath → Kesavananda → Minerva Mills) as one continuous story.
- Cross-link the amendment procedure (Article 368) directly with Basic Structure — the two chapters are examined together. Keep the landmark-case ladder (Shankari Prasad → Golaknath → Kesavananda → Minerva Mills → Waman Rao) as a single timeline.
- Note the 42nd and 44th Amendments as recurring characters — they touch DPSP, Fundamental Duties, Preamble and Emergency all at once.

**Economy**
- Revise the national-income aggregates cleanly: GDP/GNP/NDP/NNP at market price vs factor cost, and how net indirect taxes and depreciation move between them. This is pure definition-recall and shows up often.
- Consolidate money and banking (Vivek Singh Ch2–3): the money-supply measures (M0–M3), CRR/SLR/Repo/Reverse Repo/MSF, and how each tool tightens or loosens liquidity. A one-page instruments table pays off repeatedly.
- Keep the pre-1991 baseline (from June) in view as you read the money-and-banking chapters — the contrast anchors the later reform material.

**Modern History** *(this month's mini-mock focus subject)*
- Treat the Revolt of 1857 as a structured block: causes (political/economic/military/social), major centres and their leaders (Kanpur–Nana Sahib, Lucknow–Hazrat Mahal, Jhansi–Rani Lakshmibai, Bihar–Kunwar Singh), and why it failed. This map recurs constantly.
- For the socio-religious reforms, build a grid: movement / founder / year / region / key idea (Brahmo, Prarthana, Arya Samaj, Ramakrishna Mission, Aligarh, Singh Sabha, etc.). The exam loves matching founders to movements.
- Lay the Congress story as one timeline: foundation (1885) → Moderate methods and demands → Partition of Bengal (1905) and Swadeshi → Surat Split (1907) → the rise of the Extremists. Keep Moderate vs Extremist methods on facing columns.
- Keep a short revolutionaries ledger (Anushilan Samiti, Alipore Bomb Case, the early Bengal/Maharashtra networks) — a skeleton now saves rework when the later revolutionary phase (Ch24 onward) arrives.
- Cross-link with Polity where useful: the constitutional-development thread (GoI Acts) runs parallel to this political narrative.

**Geography**

- Consolidate atmospheric structure (troposphere → thermosphere), the heat budget, and pressure belts/winds — these underpin the climatology questions.

**Environment**

- Fix the niche-vs-habitat distinction and the basic ecological terms (biome, biotic/abiotic components) — these are easy marks when clean.

**Science & Technology**

**Physics (optional)**

7. (a) — Lord Curzon carried out the Partition of Bengal in 1905.

9. (b) — The Alipore Bomb Case involved Aurobindo Ghosh and Barindra Kumar Ghosh.
10. (b) — Citizenship (Articles 5–11) is in Part II.
11. (c) — Article 32 gives the Right to Constitutional Remedies.
12. (b) — Article 44 provides for a Uniform Civil Code.
13. (b) — Fundamental Duties came on the Swaran Singh Committee's recommendation (42nd Amendment).

15. (c) — Article 352 requires war, external aggression or armed rebellion.

18. (b) — GST is a destination-based indirect tax on consumption.
