## 3. Revisit Plan — Pause & Consolidate

Go back over these before Phase 1B's material stacks on top. This is a consolidation pass; the aim is to lock in the cross-links while August is still fresh.

**Polity**
- Put the Emergency provisions on one page: Articles 352, 356 and 360 side by side — grounds, proclamation and approval procedure, duration, effect on Fundamental Rights (especially Articles 19, 20, 21 and 359), and revocation. Note precisely what the 38th, 42nd and 44th Amendments each changed.
- Consolidate Centre–State relations in three columns — legislative (Articles 245–255, including 249, 250, 252, 253), administrative (256–263, the Inter-State Council) and financial (268–293, Finance Commission). The exception Articles are the examinable part.
- Fix the President's powers by category (executive, legislative, financial, judicial, emergency) and keep veto types plus the ordinance power (Article 123) on a small card. Cross-link with Article 74 and the 42nd/44th Amendment tug-of-war over binding advice.
- For Parliament, drill the procedural detail: Money Bill vs Financial Bill vs Ordinary Bill (Articles 109–110, 117), joint sitting (Article 108) and when it cannot be used, and the devices — adjournment motion, no-confidence, cut motions, question hour. Add the parliamentary committees table (PAC, Estimates, Public Undertakings, DRSCs) — small and high-yield.
- Judiciary: Supreme Court jurisdictions (original, writ, appellate, advisory under Article 143), the collegium and the NJAC judgment, and the tenure/removal machinery. Keep a single comparison sheet of the constitutional bodies — creating Article, composition, tenure, removal, reporting — since they are examined by contrast.
- Cross-link back to July: the FR–DPSP–Basic Structure ladder now sits under the emergency and amendment material; the two are asked together.

**Economy** *(this month's mini-mock focus subject)*
- Definitions first: fiscal, revenue, primary and effective revenue deficit — write each as a formula and say aloud what it measures. This is the single most repeated Economy question type.
- Consolidate the budget documents (Annual Financial Statement, Demands for Grants, Finance Bill, FRBM statements) and the revenue/capital × receipts/expenditure 2×2 grid. Test yourself by classifying a dozen real line items.
- GST: Article 279A, the Council's composition and voting weightage, CGST/SGST/IGST and the destination principle, what stays outside GST (petroleum products, alcohol for human consumption, electricity), and the compensation mechanism.
- Growth and Industry: distinguish growth from development, and keep the industrial-policy sequence (1948 → 1956 → 1991) as one timeline. Note the composite MSME criteria and the current industrial-finance institutions.
- Agriculture and sectors: MSP and CACP's role (and the A2+FL / C2 cost concepts), procurement and PDS, and the shifting sectoral shares in GVA versus employment — the divergence between those two is itself a question.
- Infrastructure: the PPP models (BOT-Toll, BOT-Annuity, HAM, EPC) with who bears which risk. Draw the risk-allocation table once and it stays.
- Cross-link with Polity: the GST Council and Finance Commission belong to both syllabi; revise them once, in both frames.

**Modern History**
- Build the 1919–1922 block as a chain: Rowlatt Act → Jallianwala Bagh → Khilafat → Nagpur Session → Non-Cooperation → Chauri Chaura → withdrawal. Keep the reasons for withdrawal and the Swarajist–No-changer split immediately after it.
- Lay the constitutional-response thread as a timeline: Simon Commission (1927) → Nehru Report (1928) → Jinnah's Fourteen Points → Lahore Session and Purna Swaraj (1929) → Dandi (1930) → RTCs → Communal Award and Poona Pact (1932) → Government of India Act, 1935 → provincial ministries (1937–39).
- For Quit India and the INA, keep dates, places and slogans tight (Gowalia Tank, 8 August 1942; Azad Hind at Singapore, 1943; the Red Fort trials) and note the parallel governments at Ballia, Tamluk and Satara.
- For the endgame, sequence the missions and plans cleanly: Cripps (1942) → Wavell/Simla (1945) → Cabinet Mission (1946) → Direct Action Day → Mountbatten Plan (June 1947) → Indian Independence Act. Each has one distinguishing feature — write that feature, not a paragraph.
- Cross-link with Polity: the Government of India Acts of 1919 and 1935 are the same material read twice, once as history and once as constitutional development.

**Ancient History (new this month)**
- Indus Valley: town planning, site-to-feature pairs (Lothal–dockyard, Kalibangan–ploughed field, Dholavira–water management, Mohenjo-daro–Great Bath), the seals, and the decline debate. Keep it to a two-page skeleton for now.
- Vedic period: Early vs Later Vedic contrasts across polity, economy, society and religion; the literature hierarchy (Samhitas → Brahmanas → Aranyakas → Upanishads); and the geographic shift eastward.

**Geography**
- Redraw India's physiographic divisions and the drainage map from memory — Himalayan vs Peninsular river characteristics, and the major west-flowing rivers as a small separate list.
- Consolidate the Indian monsoon mechanism: the onset and withdrawal sequence, the two branches, the break in monsoon, and the role of the jet streams, ITCZ and El Niño/La Niña.
- Fix the soil types with their regions and crops, and the natural-vegetation belts against rainfall bands.

**Environment**
- Biodiversity hotspot criteria (endemism + threat) and India's four hotspots; also India's biogeographic zones and the difference between a biosphere reserve, a national park and a wildlife sanctuary in terms of legal rights and permitted activity.
- Keep a short conservation ledger: in-situ vs ex-situ, Project Tiger/Elephant, the Wildlife Protection Act schedules, and the Ramsar/CITES/CMS distinction (which convention does what).
- Climate change: the UNFCCC–Kyoto–Paris chain, common but differentiated responsibilities, India's NDCs, and the main greenhouse gases with their global warming potentials.

**International Relations**
- From NCERT 12, keep the Cold War architecture (alliances, NAM, the Cuban Missile Crisis) as background and note where India's non-alignment sits in it.
- For India–US and India–China, keep two one-page ledgers: pillars of cooperation, live friction points, and the institutional mechanisms (2+2, Quad, iCET on one side; the Special Representatives mechanism, border agreements of 1993/1996/2005, trade imbalance on the other).

**Physics (optional)**
- Re-derive rather than reread: Laplace's equation and the uniqueness theorems; the method of images for a point charge near a grounded plane and a sphere; the multipole expansion.
- For fields in matter: bound charges and the physical meaning of **D**, bound currents and **H**, and the boundary conditions for both — write them out cleanly.
- Magnetostatics: Biot–Savart, Ampère's law in both forms, and the vector potential.
- Then run the Maxwell's-equations assembly — displacement current, the wave equation in vacuum, Poynting's theorem — and work the corresponding PYQs from CSE Physics Optional Paper I on electrostatics and magnetostatics (these recur almost annually).

## Answers

**1. (c)** — Primary deficit = fiscal deficit minus interest payments; it shows the current year's borrowing need excluding the burden of past debt.  
*Economy · Types of deficit*

**2. (a)** — Effective Revenue Deficit = Revenue Deficit minus grants for creation of capital assets. The concept was introduced in the Union Budget 2011-12.  
*Economy · Effective revenue deficit*

**3. (a)** — Article 279A (101st Amendment) created the GST Council. Decisions need a three-fourths weighted majority, but the Centre's vote carries one-third weightage and all States together two-thirds, not one-half.  
*Economy · GST Council*

**4. (d)** — Capital expenditure either creates assets or reduces liabilities. Loans given to States are capital expenditure; interest, salaries and subsidies are revenue expenditure.  
*Economy · Budget classification*

**5. (b)** — Corporation tax and STT are direct taxes, where impact and incidence fall on the same person. GST is an indirect tax whose burden is shifted to the consumer.  
*Economy · Direct vs indirect taxes*

**6. (b)** — The 2020 revision replaced the investment-only test with a composite criterion of investment plus turnover, and removed the manufacturing/services distinction.  
*Economy · MSME classification*

**7. (c)** — CACP, an attached office of the Ministry of Agriculture, recommends MSPs; the final announcement is made by the Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs.  
*Economy · MSP mechanism*

**8. (d)** — HAM blends EPC and BOT: about 40 per cent of the bid cost is paid by the government in instalments during construction and the remaining 60 per cent as annuities over the concession period, with the government retaining toll risk.  
*Economy · PPP models*

**9. (a)** — The RBI held the repo rate at 5.25 per cent, revised FY27 growth up to 6.7 per cent and lowered its inflation projection to 5.0 per cent.  
*Economy · Repo rate*

**10. (d)** — The 44th Amendment narrowed the ground to 'armed rebellion' and required the Cabinet's written advice for a proclamation, reversing several 42nd Amendment changes.  
*Polity & Governance · Article 352*

**11. (d)** — All three are correct under Articles 109 and 110. The Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations, which the Lok Sabha may accept or reject.  
*Polity & Governance · Money Bill*

**12. (c)** — Nominated members and members of Legislative Councils do not vote. The election is by proportional representation with a single transferable vote and secret ballot.  
*Polity & Governance · Presidential election*

**13. (a)** — Article 249 allows such legislation for up to one year at a time. Article 250 applies during a National Emergency; Article 252 relates to two or more States requesting Parliament to legislate.  
*Polity & Governance · Legislative relations*

**14. (c)** — In the Fourth Judges Case the Court held that the NJAC compromised judicial independence and the primacy of the judiciary in appointments, both facets of the basic structure, restoring the collegium.  
*Polity & Governance · Judicial appointments*

**15. (b)** — The presiding officer decides, and in Kihoto Hollohan (1992) the Supreme Court held this decision is subject to judicial review.  
*Polity & Governance · Anti-defection law*

**16. (a)** — The Ali brothers, Shaukat Ali and Muhammad Ali, along with Maulana Azad and Hasrat Mohani led the Khilafat agitation, which Gandhi merged with Non-Cooperation in 1920.  
*Modern History · Khilafat and Non-Cooperation*

**17. (a)** — Drafted under Motilal Nehru in response to the all-white Simon Commission, it proposed dominion status, a bill of rights and joint electorates with reservation in certain cases.  
*Modern History · Simon Commission and Nehru Report*

**18. (a)** — Dandi March (March 1930), Gandhi-Irwin Pact (March 1931), Second Round Table Conference (September-December 1931), Poona Pact (September 1932).  
*Modern History · Civil Disobedience Movement*

**19. (c)** — The AICC adopted the resolution at Gowalia Tank Maidan, Bombay, on 8 August 1942, where Gandhi gave the 'Do or Die' call. The Working Committee had approved the draft earlier at Wardha.  
*Modern History · Quit India Movement*

**20. (d)** — All three are correct. The INA trials at the Red Fort, with Bhulabhai Desai among the defence counsel, triggered widespread public agitation.  
*Modern History · INA and Subhas Chandra Bose*

**21. (c)** — Kalibangan in Rajasthan yielded a pre-Harappan ploughed field with criss-cross furrows. Lothal is known for its dockyard and Dholavira for its water management and signboard.  
*Ancient & Medieval History · Harappan sites*

**22. (b)** — Thal Ghat and Bhor Ghat lie in Maharashtra, the Palghat gap separates the Nilgiris from the Anaimalais, and the Shencottah gap lies further south between the Anaimalai and Cardamom hills.  
*Geography · Western Ghats passes*

**23. (c)** — The study mapped 219 hanging glaciers in the Alaknanda basin, a class of ice masses that can detach and trigger downstream disasters.  
*Geography · Glacial hazards*

**24. (a)** — The two Myers criteria are endemism (at least 1,500 endemic vascular plants) and threat (at least 70 per cent of primary vegetation lost). There is no requirement of lying within the tropics.  
*Environment · Biodiversity hotspots*

**25. (a)** — Glaw Lake is Arunachal Pradesh's first Ramsar Site and India's 101st wetland of international importance.  
*Environment · Ramsar Sites*

**26. (d)** — Established in 2003, the Special Representatives dialogue is India and China's principal channel for the boundary question and wider bilateral ties.  
*International Relations · Special Representatives talks*

**27. (d)** — India and Uzbekistan became Comprehensive Strategic Partners during the late-August visit, which also opened a uranium supply line.  
*International Relations · India-Uzbekistan relations*

**28. (a)** — A receiver needs four satellites to solve for three position coordinates plus the receiver clock error. With only three operational satellites, NavIC could not offer standalone navigation.  
*Science & Technology · Satellite navigation (NavIC)*

**29. (a)** — The Rules establish one legally mandated time reference for the country, requiring synchronisation with IST disseminated by the national time-keeping infrastructure.  
*Science & Technology · Indian Standard Time*

**30. (b)** — India was placed 176th out of 177 countries in the EPI 2026, an index produced by Yale and Columbia University researchers.  
*Reports & Indices · Environmental Performance Index*
