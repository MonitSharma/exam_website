# Weekly Ethics Case Study — 12 November 2026

**Paper:** GS Paper IV (Ethics, Integrity and Aptitude) | **Theme this week:** Regulatory body / environmental clearance — public interest vs. development pressure vs. loyalty

---

## Case Study

You are the Member Secretary of a State Pollution Control Board (SPCB) in an industrially backward but mineral-rich district. A large private company has applied for consent to operate an expanded limestone-cement plant, promising an investment of ₹2,400 crore and 3,500 direct and indirect jobs in a region with high out-migration. The State Government has declared the project a "priority investment", and the Chief Secretary's office has sent two reminders asking for a quick decision.

Your technical team's inspection finds that the plant's existing unit has been discharging untreated effluent into a seasonal stream that feeds the drinking-water sources of nine villages, mostly tribal hamlets. Water samples show fluoride and heavy-metal levels above permissible limits. Local health workers report rising cases of skin disease and dental problems among children. The company's Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) report, prepared by an empanelled consultant, does not mention this stream.

The company's CEO meets you privately and assures you of "full compliance in the next phase". He hints that your senior colleague and mentor, the Board Chairman, who is due to retire next year and has backed your career, is "comfortable" with the project. The Chairman himself tells you informally, "Let's grant the consent with strict conditions. Closing the unit will anger the government, and these jobs matter more than a few villages' complaints."

A local NGO has meanwhile approached the media, and the public hearing scheduled next week is likely to turn hostile. A junior scientist in your office, who prepared the sampling report, privately tells you he fears being transferred if the report is "inconvenient".

**Questions**

1. Identify the ethical issues involved and the stakeholders in this case. **(10 marks, ~150 words)**
2. What are the options available to you? Evaluate the merits and demerits of each. **(10 marks, ~150 words)**
3. Which course of action would you adopt, and why? **(5 marks, ~100 words)**
4. Which values and principles of public administration are at stake in this case? **(5 marks, ~100 words)**

*Total: 30 marks (UPSC typically sets 20 to 25 marks; here it is split across four parts for fuller practice). Suggested length: about 250 to 300 words per full answer if attempted as a 20-25 mark question, or the word counts shown per part. Time: ~25 minutes.*

## Model Approach

### 1. Stakeholders

- **Nine downstream villages, especially tribal children:** right to clean water and health under Article 21.
- **The company, its shareholders and its employees:** legitimate business interest; jobs at stake.
- **The local unemployed youth and the district economy:** livelihood and development needs.
- **The Board Chairman:** mentor, superior and retiring official; a possible source of improper pressure.
- **The State Government:** investment goals; democratic mandate, but not above the law.
- **The junior scientist (potential whistleblower):** professional integrity and personal vulnerability.
- **The NGO and media:** public accountability.
- **You, as the quasi-judicial regulator**, and the institution (SPCB) whose credibility is at stake.
- **Future generations and the ecosystem:** intergenerational equity.

### 2. Core Ethical Dilemmas

1. **Public interest vs. investment and jobs:** short-term employment against long-term health and ecological damage.
2. **Duty vs. loyalty:** obligation to the statute (Water Act, 1974; EP Act, 1986) against gratitude and deference to a mentor and superior.
3. **Integrity vs. expediency:** pressure from the Chief Secretary's office, and a veiled inducement from the CEO.
4. **Transparency vs. institutional harmony:** disclosing the sampling data against keeping the peace.
5. **Protection of a subordinate vs. self-interest:** shielding the scientist from retaliation.
6. **Procedural flaw:** a deficient EIA that omits a material fact, which raises a question of fraud or suppression.

### 3. Evaluation of Options

| Option | Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Grant consent with conditions, as the Chairman suggests** | Saves jobs and investment; avoids conflict with superiors and government. | Rewards past violation; conditions are often unenforced; harms vulnerable villagers; makes the Board complicit; breaches the statute. Consent cannot lawfully be given on a deficient EIA. |
| **B. Reject the application outright** | Upholds the law; sends a deterrent message. | May be disproportionate and judicially challengeable if the company can remedy the problem; loses jobs; does not address the existing damage. A purely punitive response ignores the developmental need. |
| **C. Defer consent, issue a show-cause notice and direct remediation (recommended)** | Lawful, proportionate and fair (natural justice); protects health first; leaves room for a compliant expansion; creates a record. | Delay will irritate the government and the company; needs firmness and documentation. |
| **D. Quietly delay or "park" the file** | Avoids confrontation. | Evasion of duty; the harm continues; lacks accountability and moral courage. |
| **E. Leak or escalate publicly via the media** | Creates pressure. | Breaches service conduct rules; undermines the institution; should be a last resort, not the first step. |

### 4. Recommended Course of Action

Adopt **Option C**, in stages, with Option B held in reserve if the company does not comply:

1. **Decline the CEO's private overtures.** Record the meeting and the inducement hint in the file and report it to the appropriate authority. Do not take decisions through informal channels.
2. **Issue a show-cause notice and a closure/remediation direction** for the existing unit under Section 33A of the Water Act. Require immediate effluent treatment, an alternate safe drinking-water supply to the nine villages at the company's cost, and a health camp.
3. **Return the EIA for re-examination**, since it omitted the stream, and refer the matter to the Expert Appraisal Committee. Consider action against the consultant for the misrepresentation.
4. **Hold the public hearing transparently.** Share the sampling report and invite the villagers' views. This is the public participation that the process is meant to guarantee.
5. **Respectfully but firmly inform the Chairman** that you will decide on the merits and in writing, and ask for his written directions if he insists. A written direction can be examined; an informal nudge cannot. Gratitude to a mentor does not extend to breaking the law.
6. **Reply to the Chief Secretary's office in writing**, explaining that the pace is dictated by statutory requirements, and offering a time-bound, compliance-linked pathway so that investment is facilitated rather than blocked.
7. **Protect the scientist:** ensure the report is part of the official record, and prevent any victimisation or arbitrary transfer. Cite the Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014 where relevant.
8. **Consent for the expansion** may follow only after verified remediation, with continuous online monitoring, a third-party audit and a bank guarantee.

**Why this is right:** it treats development and environment as complementary under the principle of *sustainable development*, keeps the decision within the law, gives the company a fair chance to correct itself, and puts the lives of the most vulnerable first.

### 5. Values at Stake

Integrity, objectivity, impartiality, public service orientation, accountability, transparency, compassion, courage of conviction (moral courage), rule of law, and non-partisanship.

### 6. Thinkers and Concepts to Cite

- **Gandhi's Talisman:** "Recall the face of the poorest and weakest person you have seen, and ask whether the step you contemplate will be of any use to him." Here the tribal children downstream are that person.
- **Kant's categorical imperative / duty ethics:** the law must be followed as a duty irrespective of consequences or convenience; contrast with **utilitarianism** (jobs for many vs. harm to a few), and show how the greater good is served only if health costs and long-term damage are counted.
- **Nolan Committee's Seven Principles of Public Life** (selflessness, integrity, objectivity, accountability, openness, honesty, leadership) and the **Second ARC** on ethics in governance.
- **Precautionary principle and polluter-pays principle** (recognised by the Supreme Court as part of Indian environmental law, e.g. *Vellore Citizens* case): useful legal-ethical anchors.

### 7. Sample Answer Structure (for Q3, ~100 words)

Open with the decision in one line (defer consent; show-cause; remediation; transparent hearing). Give two to three reasons (legality, health of the vulnerable, long-term sustainability), address the Chairman and government pressure in one sentence, and close with the balanced message that lawful, compliant development will be supported.
