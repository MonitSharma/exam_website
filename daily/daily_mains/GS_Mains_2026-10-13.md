## MODEL ANSWER

**Introduction.** India imports nearly 85–88% of its crude, so every oil spike is an imported shock. With the import basket crossing ~$100/bbl in September and oil imports at $63.4 bn in Apr–Jul 2026 (vs $40.5 bn), the MPC hiked the repo rate to 5.50% on 7 Oct and moved to "calibrated tightening".

**How the shock transmits**

- **Inflation:** Direct effect via petrol, diesel, LPG and ATF; indirect effect via freight and input costs across manufacturing and food logistics. RBI's own study puts the pass-through at ~20 bps of headline CPI per 10% crude rise; the risk is **second-round effects** on expectations and wages.
- **External sector and rupee:** Each $10/bbl adds ~$13–14 bn to the import bill, widening the current account deficit; the rupee depreciates, which raises the cost of imports further — a feedback loop. Risk-off flows and firm global yields add to the pressure.
- **Fiscal:** Either the Centre cuts excise duty (revenue loss) or OMCs absorb under-recoveries and LPG subsidy rises — both erode the consolidation path (debt-to-GDP anchor of ~50% by 2030-31).
- **Growth:** Costlier energy squeezes margins and household real incomes; high rates add to the drag on private capex.

**Is a rate hike the right response?**

*For:* The MPC mandate is the 4% (±2%) CPI target; with projected inflation of 5.2% and the risk of de-anchoring expectations, acting early protects credibility and supports the rupee by narrowing rate differentials.

*Against:* Monetary policy cannot add a barrel of oil. A hike addresses demand, not supply, and costs growth. Standard doctrine is to **look through** a transitory supply shock unless it spills into core inflation or expectations.

**Assessment.** A calibrated, small hike is defensible — the shock is large, persistent and coincides with rising food pressures — but it must not be the *only* tool.

**Way forward.** (i) Targeted, temporary fiscal cushioning rather than blanket subsidies; (ii) draw on the Strategic Petroleum Reserves and diversify sourcing and local-currency settlement; (iii) accelerate EVs, ethanol blending, green hydrogen and nuclear (SHANTI Act); (iv) clear forward guidance by the RBI to anchor expectations. Policy must be coordinated, not leave the MPC to fight alone.

**Conclusion.** The way out of oil vulnerability is structural: lower energy intensity and import dependence. Monetary tightening buys time; energy security makes growth durable.

### Value-additions (for the exam)

1. **Legal anchor:** RBI Act, 1934 (Sec 45ZA onward) — 4% CPI target with ±2% band, retained for 1 Apr 2026–31 Mar 2031; failure = 3 consecutive quarters outside the band (Sec 45ZN, report to Government). Origin: **Urjit Patel Committee (2014)**.
2. **Data point:** RBI Bulletin (July 2025) — 10% crude rise ≈ +20 bps headline inflation; ~$13–14 bn extra import bill per $10/bbl rise (press estimate; verify).
3. **Concept:** "Look-through" doctrine for supply shocks, and the **Taylor-principle** logic of tightening if expectations de-anchor; contrast with 2022, when the RBI also faced a West Asia/Ukraine-linked oil shock and combined rate hikes with excise cuts.
