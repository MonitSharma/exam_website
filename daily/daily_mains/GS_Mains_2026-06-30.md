## MODEL ANSWER

**Introduction**

India completed a decade of flexible inflation targeting in 2016–2026, and in March 2026 the government renewed the 4% CPI target (±2% band) for 2026–31. Yet the dilemma persists: with CPI at 3.93% (May 2026) but the RBI holding the repo at 5.25% in June 2026 amid a West Asia oil shock, monetary policy is again caught between price stability and growth — much of it driven by forces it cannot control.

**The role monetary policy has played**

- **Credible nominal anchor:** A statutory 4% target (Section 45ZA, RBI Act) and a six-member MPC anchored expectations; average inflation fell from ~6.8% (2012–16) to ~4.9% under FIT.
- **Counter-cyclical support:** ~125 bps of repo cuts and a 100 bps CRR cut in 2025 (~₹2.5 lakh crore of liquidity) cushioned demand, helping FY26 growth near 7.4% — the fastest-growing major economy.
- **Better transmission:** The External Benchmark Lending Rate (2019) sharpened pass-through to borrowers.

**The limitations**

- **Supply-driven, global inflation:** Food (~46% of the CPI basket) and imported crude — as in the current West Asia conflict — lie outside the RBI's reach; raising rates curbs demand without fixing supply.
- **Blunt and lagged:** Long, variable transmission lags impose a growth/investment cost (the "sacrifice ratio").
- **Open-economy trilemma:** Capital flows and the rupee constrain policy autonomy (Mundell–Fleming).
- **Coordination gaps:** Risks of fiscal dominance, and the unresolved core-vs-headline targeting debate (RBI's 2025 paper).

**Conclusion**

Monetary policy is necessary but not sufficient. Durable price stability needs supply-side reform — agri-marketing, buffer stocks, a deeper strategic petroleum reserve — and genuine fiscal–monetary coordination. Renewing the 4%±2% target for 2026–31 signals welcome continuity; the task now is sharper transmission and complementary structural policy, so India can tame inflation without surrendering its growth dividend.

### Value additions to deploy (pick 2–3)

- **Urjit Patel Committee (2014):** recommended FIT and CPI as the nominal anchor; basis for the 2016 RBI Act amendment.
- **Statutory architecture:** Section 45ZA sets the target; the **MPC** (6 members — 3 RBI, 3 government-appointed; Governor has the casting vote) decides by majority. A **"failure"** is inflation outside the band for **three consecutive quarters**, triggering a report to government.
- **Impossible trinity (Mundell–Fleming):** a country cannot simultaneously have a fixed exchange rate, free capital flow and independent monetary policy — explains India's "managed-float + partial autonomy" mix.
- **Transmission tool:** **External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR), 2019** improved monetary transmission to retail and MSME loans.
- **Live data anchors:** repo **5.25%** (held, June 2026); CPI **3.93%** (May 2026); FY27 forecasts — growth **6.6%**, inflation **5.1%**; CRR **3.0%**.
