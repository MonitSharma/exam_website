## MODEL ANSWER

### Introduction
Gross NPAs of scheduled commercial banks peaked at about **11.2% in March 2018** (the "twin balance sheet" crisis). The RBI's **FSR (June 2026)** puts GNPA at **1.8%** and NNPA at **0.4%** in March 2026, with **CRAR 17.7%** and a provision coverage ratio of **75.6%**. The turnaround is real, but the same report cautions that resilience must be tested against new, non-traditional risks.

### Factors behind the clean-up (2015–26)
- **Recognition:** The **Asset Quality Review (2015)** ended forbearance and forced banks to recognise hidden stress.
- **Resolution architecture:** The **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016** shifted power from debtor to creditor and created a time-bound process; the **SARFAESI Act** and DRTs supported recovery.
- **Recapitalisation and consolidation:** Government capital infusion into PSBs under the **Indradhanush (2015)** "4R" approach; PSB mergers (2019–20) cut the number of public sector banks from 27 to 12.
- **Supervision and governance:** Prompt Corrective Action framework, risk-based supervision, tighter provisioning; **P.J. Nayak Committee (2014)** on bank governance.
- **Cyclical help:** A prolonged credit upcycle, corporate deleveraging and strong profitability allowed banks to build buffers.

### Emerging risks
- **Sectoral pockets:** Agriculture GNPA remains high at **5.1%**, contributing about **37% of total GNPA** (FSR 2026).
- **Stress-test sensitivity:** GNPA could rise to **3.8–4.1%** under adverse geopolitical/energy-price scenarios; CET1 falls to 13.9% even at baseline by March 2028.
- **Bank–NBFC interconnectedness:** Banks' lending to NBFCs creates a channel for contagion; the FSR notes some NBFCs could come under pressure in severe stress. *(Verify exact NBFC numbers in the FSR before quoting.)*
- **Margins and shift in credit mix:** Declining net interest margins; the FSR notes credit risk-weighted assets grew slower than credit for the first time in three years, indicating a move to lower-risk-weight lending — good for capital, but a sign of risk aversion and possible credit-supply gaps for MSMEs/infrastructure.
- **Technology and cyber risk:** RBI flags **AI-enabled cyberattacks** as the most significant near-term cyber risk; vendor dependence and digital fraud are rising.
- **Governance legacy:** Public ownership and the temptation toward directed or evergreening lending can return in the next upcycle.

### Conclusion and way forward
A low-NPA phase is when the next stress is seeded. India should combine **forward-looking provisioning (expected credit loss norms)**, stronger **stress-testing and interconnectedness monitoring** across banks and NBFCs, **cyber-resilience standards** for vendors, deeper **corporate bond markets** to reduce bank dependence, and continued **governance reform in PSBs** (including privatisation of select PSBs). Financial stability, in the spirit of the "Viksit Bharat" goal of growth with resilience, must be treated as a continuous process rather than a milestone achieved.

### Value additions (pick 2–3)
- **Data:** GNPA 11.2% (Mar 2018) → 1.8% (Mar 2026); CRAR 17.7%; stress scenarios 3.8–4.1% by Mar 2028 (FSR, June 2026).
- **Committees:** Narasimham Committee (1991, 1998) for the reform roadmap; P.J. Nayak Committee (2014) on governance.
- **Law/scheme:** IBC 2016; SARFAESI 2002; Indradhanush 2015 (4R: recognise, resolve, recapitalise, reform).
- **Thinker/idea:** Hyman Minsky's "stability breeds instability" — the logic for countercyclical buffers when NPAs are lowest.
