# GS Mains Answer — 2026-09-14 (GS2 — Polity & Governance)

*For: Monit · UPSC CSE 2027 · Monday = GS2 (Polity/Governance). One question to write today; the model answer is below the divider — attempt it on paper first.*

> **This week's hook:** On **10 September 2026**, Union Rural Development Minister **Shivraj Singh Chouhan** released the **Operational Guidelines for DILRMP 3.0** — the third phase of the **Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme** — a **Central Sector Scheme with an outlay of ₹565.50 crore for 2026–31**, **100% centrally funded** and rolled out State-by-State on a **performance-based** basis. The pitch is a shift from *digitising documents* to building an integrated, **GIS-enabled "Land Stack"**: every State to knit together its records-of-rights, cadastral maps, registration data and encumbrance information through **APIs**, with **full georeferencing** of cadastral maps and a **14-digit ULPIN ("Bhu-Aadhaar")** for every land parcel as the joining key.
>
> The striking thing is that, on paper, the job looks almost done. The Government's own figures put **99.90% of Records of Rights computerised, 97% of cadastral maps digitised and 99% of Sub-Registrar Offices computerised**; ULPIN has been rolled out across **28 States/UTs** with roughly **23 crore parcels** numbered (December 2024); **NGDRS** offers paperless registration; and **SVAMITVA** drones have surveyed lakhs of *abadi* villages. And yet **about 66% of all civil litigation in India is land- and property-related** (NITI Aayog), a land or real-estate dispute takes on average **close to two decades** to resolve, and the **Economic Survey 2025–26** describes Indian urban land — hemmed in by restrictive density norms and unclear titles — as **"dead capital"**, borrowing Hernando de Soto's phrase directly.
>
> That gap — *between a near-perfectly digitised record and a still-insecure title* — is today's GS2 Governance theme. It is deliberately distinct from this month's GS2 questions on **Article 142 and judicial power (7 September)** and **BRICS (11 September)**, from Sunday's **delimitation and federal representation** essay (13 September), from the Sunday essay on **urban governance and municipal finance (6 September)**, and from the GS4 case on **algorithmic welfare exclusion (12 September)** — though you may legitimately borrow *one* line from that case on digitisation freezing existing errors.

## QUESTION — 15 marks · 250 words

> India has computerised almost the entirety of its land records — over 99% of Records of Rights and Sub-Registrar Offices — yet land remains its most litigated and least bankable asset. **Critically examine** why the technological modernisation of land records has not translated into secure land titles, and assess whether the integrated "Land Stack" approach of DILRMP 3.0 can close that gap.

**(Directive: *critically examine* — do **not** write a descriptive "features and benefits of DILRMP" answer; that is a 4-mark answer. The marks lie in (a) naming the *legal* root of the problem — India registers transactions, not titles — rather than treating this as a technology-adoption failure; (b) a multidimensional diagnosis that includes the *constitutional* constraint (land is a State subject), the *survey* constraint, and the *equity* constraint (women, tenants, forest-dwellers, the urban informal); (c) an honest, two-sided assessment of what a DPI-style Land Stack can and cannot do; and (d) a forward-looking close that *sequences* the reform. 15 marks, ~250 words.)**

## MODEL ANSWER (~250 words)

### Introduction (definition + anchor)

A **land record** is an administrative statement of who holds a parcel; a **title** is a legally guaranteed claim to it. India has achieved the first and not the second. Under **DILRMP**, **99.90% of Records of Rights**, **97% of cadastral maps** and **99% of Sub-Registrar Offices** are computerised, and **ULPIN/Bhu-Aadhaar** covers **28 States/UTs**. Yet **~66% of civil litigation** concerns land, and the **Economic Survey 2025–26** calls unclear urban title a source of **"dead capital"**. **DILRMP 3.0**, launched **10 September 2026** (**₹565.50 crore**, 2026–31), attempts to bridge this through a GIS-based **Land Stack**.

### A. Why digitisation has not produced secure title

- **The law records transactions, not ownership.** The **Registration Act, 1908** registers the *deed*; it certifies that a document was executed, not that the seller owned the land. India therefore runs a **presumptive title** system in which a record is only *prima facie* evidence, rebuttable in court. Digitising such a record makes an uncertain claim faster to retrieve — not truer.
- **Fragmented custody of a single fact.** Textual records sit with the Revenue Department, maps with Survey & Settlement, deeds with Stamps & Registration, and urban property records with municipalities — historically four databases that never reconciled. DILRMP 3.0's API-linked Land Stack targets precisely this.
- **A stale survey base.** Many cadastral maps descend from colonial settlement operations and were never re-surveyed; without **georeferencing**, the digital map does not match the ground, so boundary disputes survive digitisation intact.
- **The constitutional constraint.** **Land, rights in land and land tenures (Entry 18)** and **land revenue and maintenance of land records (Entry 45)** are in the **State List**; only registration of deeds is Concurrent (**Entry 6**). The Union can fund, standardise and persuade — hence a *Model* Act — but cannot legislate titling. Adoption is therefore uneven.
- **Unrecorded transfers.** Inheritance, partition, oral sale and tenancy frequently never reach the mutation register. Restrictive tenancy laws push leasing underground; **women's coparcenary rights** under the **Hindu Succession (Amendment) Act, 2005** (affirmed in ***Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma*, 2020**) are rarely reflected in the Record of Rights.
- **Digitisation can harden injustice.** Where documentation is weakest — forest-dwellers awaiting **FRA, 2006** titles, sharecroppers, urban informal settlements — a clean database can quietly write out the undocumented. Accuracy and equity are not the same variable.

### B. What the Land Stack can — and cannot — do

**The case for it.**

- **Interoperability by design.** ULPIN acts as a unique key joining map, record and registry, replicating the **DPI logic** of Aadhaar and UPI: a bank or buyer could verify ownership and encumbrance in one query rather than across three offices.
- **Georeferencing fixes the map–ground mismatch**, attacking the single largest cause of boundary litigation.
- **Incentive-compatible design.** 100% central funding released on a **performance basis** gives States a reason to act in a domain the Centre cannot command.
- **Transparency dividends.** Machine-readable, spatially referenced records make benami holdings, double sale and fraudulent mortgage far harder, and make land acquisition compensation less arbitrary.

**The limits.**

- **A stack cannot supply a guarantee.** Conclusive title requires *law*: a title settlement and adjudication process, an appellate forum, and a **State-backed indemnity fund** — the architecture of the **Torrens system** and of **NITI Aayog's Model Conclusive Land Titling Act & Rules (2019)**. So far only **Rajasthan**, through the **Urban Land (Certification of Titles) Act, 2016**, has attempted even a limited State guarantee, confined to urban land.
- **Scale versus outlay.** ₹565.50 crore over five years across all States and UTs is modest against a task that ultimately requires re-survey.
- **The urban blind spot.** DILRMP is built on the rural revenue record; the **Economic Survey's** "dead capital" is largely *urban*, where municipal records, unapproved layouts and cooperative-society titles dominate.
- **Due process is not optional.** ***Kolkata Municipal Corporation v. Bimal Kumar Shah* (2024)** held that the right to property under **Article 300A** carries seven procedural sub-rights — notice, hearing, reasons, public purpose, fair compensation, timely conduct and conclusion. Any titling drive that extinguishes a claim by database entry, without notice or hearing, is constitutionally vulnerable.

### Conclusion (balanced, forward-looking)

Technology can make land records **accurate, spatially true and instantly accessible**; only legislation can make them **conclusive**. The sequence matters: re-survey and georeference → publish and invite objections with genuine notice and appeal → record tenants', women's and forest-dwellers' rights before the record is frozen → and only then legislate a State-guaranteed title backed by an indemnity fund, extended to urban land. Done in that order, the Land Stack converts India's largest household asset from a source of litigation into collateral, credit and revenue — the surest route from "dead capital" to living capital. Done in the wrong order, it will merely produce a very precise map of an unjust distribution.

## Examiner's note — what separates a 9 from a 12 here

1. **Find the legal root, not the technical one.** The weak answer says "implementation gaps, poor internet, low capacity." The strong answer says **India registers deeds, not titles** — and everything else follows from that one sentence. Write it early.
2. **Use the federal point.** Most candidates will miss that the Union *cannot* legislate land titling. Naming **Entry 18/Entry 45, State List** in one clause instantly raises the answer's register.
3. **Keep one paragraph for equity.** Digitisation questions are usually answered purely in efficiency terms. A short, specific line on women's unrecorded coparcenary rights, concealed tenancy and FRA claims shows multidimensionality and connects GS2 to GS1.
4. **Be two-sided about DPI.** Do not write an anti-technology answer — the Land Stack is a genuine advance. Credit it specifically (ULPIN as joining key, georeferencing, performance-linked funding) before stating its limits.
5. **Sequence the conclusion.** "Re-survey → notice and objection → record subordinate rights → statutory guarantee with indemnity fund" is a *reform pathway*, not a wish-list. Examiners reward sequencing over slogans.
