## ✅ MODEL ANSWER (≈250 words)

**Introduction**
Great Nicobar (GNI) sits near the Malacca Strait, one of the world's busiest sea lanes. The ₹72,000–80,000 crore project (Galathea Bay transshipment port, dual-use airport, power plant, township) aims to cut India's reliance on foreign transshipment hubs and strengthen the Indo-Pacific posture. But it requires ~130 sq km of forest diversion in a UNESCO-recognised biosphere reserve, home to the Shompen (PVTG). The NGT upheld clearance in Feb 2026, yet the debate remains open.

**Body**

*Strategic and economic case*
- Transshipment hub reduces cargo leakage to Colombo/Singapore; lowers logistics costs.
- Military presence near choke point; island infrastructure for HADR and surveillance.
- Tourism and jobs for an under-developed island.

*Ecological costs*
- ~1 million trees; loss of primary forest, coral and leatherback nesting beaches; threat to Nicobar megapode, saltwater crocodile, robber crab.
- Seismic zone — 2004 tsunami precedent; subsidence risk to port.
- Compensatory afforestation on the mainland (Haryana-type offsets) cannot replace island endemism.

*Tribal security*
- Shompen — limited contact, vulnerable to disease and displacement; Nicobarese ancestral lands. FRA 2006 consent and PVTG protection norms are central.

*Process concerns*
- Sealed-cover High-Powered Committee report; reliance on "strategic importance" by NGT; weakened forest scrutiny after the 2023 Forest (Conservation) Amendment (exempts security-related linear projects near borders).
- Fragmented clearance vs cumulative impact assessment.

*Adequacy of safeguards*
- NGT's binding conditions (turtle, coral, shoreline) are positive, but depend on monitoring capacity; track record of compliance is weak.

**Conclusion / Way forward**
Strategy and ecology are not binary: India needs both. Reduce footprint and phase the project (port first, review before airport/township), publish the HPC report, make independent third-party and tribal-community monitoring mandatory, create a real-time compliance dashboard, ring-fence Shompen areas, and build seismic-resilient design. As the SC reminded in *Vellore Citizens* and *Vanashakti*, sustainable development means precaution, not afterthought; "viksit Bharat" should also be "sustainable Bharat".

**Value additions (verify before quoting)**
1. **Precautionary principle & sustainable development** — *Vellore Citizens' Welfare Forum v. UoI (1996)*; *Vanashakti v. UoI (2025)* on ex-post-facto clearances (check status — judgment later reviewed).
2. **Legal anchors** — Art 21 / 48A / 51A(g); Forest Rights Act 2006 (consent); CRZ Notification 2019; Great Nicobar Biosphere Reserve (1989; UNESCO MAB 2013).
3. **Thinker / concept** — Gadgil-type "ecology of the poor" vs strategic ecology; Brundtland's definition of sustainable development.
