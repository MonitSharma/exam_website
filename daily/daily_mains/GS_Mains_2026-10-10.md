## QUESTION (Case Study) — 15 marks · 250 words

You are the **Secretary of a State Public Service Commission (SPSC)**. Tomorrow the Commission holds a recruitment examination for **800 posts**, with **6 lakh candidates**. Many have prepared for years; some are in their last attempt or close to the age limit.

Tonight, a **junior contractual employee** shows you photographs suggesting that a **sealed question-paper packet at the district treasury has been tampered with and re-sealed**. She is afraid and asks you to keep her name out of it. She says the packets were handled by the **printing press** that holds the Commission's contract. Your preliminary check can neither confirm nor rule out a leak.

Meanwhile:

- The **Chairman**, who appointed you, says: *"Don't create panic. Hold the exam. We will examine it quietly afterwards."* He adds that the press owner is a **close family friend**.
- The press head is your **former batchmate and friend**, and he calls to assure you that "everything is fine".
- A local **MLA** warns that cancelling the exam at the last minute will cause **unrest among youth**, and mentions that your "extension in service" is under consideration.
- A reporter has already asked whether "something is wrong with tomorrow's paper".
- Cancelling now would cost crores, disrupt travel and accommodation for 6 lakh candidates, and delay the recruitment by months.

**(a)** Identify the ethical issues and the stakeholders. **(b)** Evaluate the options available to you. **(c)** What course of action would you adopt, and why?

*Directive: Give a structured answer — issues → options (with merits/demerits) → decision with safeguards. Time: ~12 minutes.*

## MODEL ANSWER

### (a) Ethical issues and stakeholders

**Ethical issues**
- **Integrity vs institutional loyalty:** Your duty to the candidates and the Constitution (Art. 14 and 16, equality of opportunity in public employment) conflicts with loyalty to the Chairman who appointed you.
- **Conflict of interest:** The Chairman's friendship with the press owner and your own friendship with him compromise impartiality. Both of you should step back from decisions about the press.
- **Uncertainty and the precautionary duty:** The evidence is incomplete, but the harm from a leaked exam falls on 6 lakh people and on public trust.
- **Consequences vs principle:** Cancelling is costly. Holding a compromised exam would violate fairness, a principle that cannot be traded away for convenience.
- **Protection of the whistleblower:** She is junior, contractual and vulnerable. Her good-faith disclosure must be protected.
- **Pressure and inducement:** The MLA's reference to your extension is an implied inducement and should be refused.
- **Transparency vs panic:** The public's right to information must be balanced against the risk of rumour.

**Stakeholders:** candidates (especially those near the age limit), the whistleblower, the Chairman, the press owner, you as Secretary, the Commission as an institution, the government, taxpayers, the media and the public.

### (b) Options

1. **Hold the exam as the Chairman says.** *Merit:* no disruption, and the hierarchy is obeyed. *Demerit:* if a leak is proven, the exam is void later, causing larger loss; it violates fairness and exposes you to complicity.
2. **Cancel the exam at once.** *Merit:* it protects fairness. *Demerit:* on unconfirmed evidence it is disproportionate and costly, and it may reward a false alarm.
3. **Verify first, in a few hours, then decide.** *Merit:* proportionate and fact-based; it keeps both options open. *Demerit:* time is short and needs a credible, independent process.
4. **Partial or contingent measures:** replace the doubtful packets with a **reserve paper set** and, if available, change the printing source or the exam shift. *Merit:* it protects the exam without full cancellation. *Demerit:* it needs a ready alternative paper and depends on the Commission's own preparedness.

### (c) Course of action and reasoning

**Tonight**
- Secure the packets in question and seal the treasury room under video recording. Examine them jointly with a **neutral officer** (district magistrate or treasury officer) and a forensic expert, with the seal serial numbers and original handover records.
- Recuse myself and the Chairman from decisions involving the press, and record my friendship in writing. Ask for an independent person to verify the press records.
- Move to the **reserve paper set** if one exists, and decide **cancel, postpone or hold** on the basis of verified findings, not the Chairman's preference.
- Submit the Chairman a **written note** of facts and my recommendation, so that responsibility is clear and recorded.

**Handling pressure**
- Respectfully but firmly decline the Chairman's "hold quietly" instruction if the checks show tampering. An instruction that defeats the purpose of the institution does not bind my conscience; my oath is to the Constitution.
- Refuse the MLA's inducement without argument and, if it continues, report it to the competent authority.

**Whistleblower**
- Do not reveal her name. Keep her identity confidential and record her disclosure as a **protected complaint**. Brief a senior officer so that no retaliation, such as non-renewal of contract, can follow.

**Communication**
- Release a short, factual statement to the media and candidates: "the Commission is verifying the security of the question papers and will announce its decision by [time]". No speculation and no assurances that cannot be backed.

**If tampering is confirmed or cannot be ruled out:** postpone and re-hold the exam with fresh papers and a new printing arrangement, **register an FIR** under the Public Examinations Act, 2024, preserve evidence, and inform the government. Reimburse genuine candidate expenses where possible, and consider an age-relaxation for candidates affected.

**Why this course:** it applies the **precautionary principle** and proportionality, protects fairness (Kantian duty), and limits harm (utilitarian reasoning). Short-term pain in postponing is justified because the lasting damage of a compromised exam falls on the weakest candidates, those without coaching or connections. As the *Nolan principles* say, public office demands integrity, objectivity and accountability. As the *Gandhian talisman* says, ask whether the decision helps the last and weakest person affected.

### Conclusion
A public examination is a social contract: trust that merit alone decides. The right action is neither panic nor silence, but **fast verification, honest recorded reasoning, protection of the whistleblower and a fair outcome**, even at a personal cost. Systemically, the Commission should adopt **tamper-proof, tracked paper logistics, conflict-of-interest declarations for vendors and officers, and a standing whistleblower channel**.

### Value additions
- **Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024:** a central law against organised paper leaks. The 2026 Amendment Bill (reported 31 Jul 2026) raised the penalties and provides fast-track courts and a Special Task Force. *(Verify final text.)*
- **Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014:** enacted, but reported to be not yet operationalised, and it covers public servants; hence the need for a standing internal channel.
- **Second ARC (4th Report, "Ethics in Governance"):** recommends codes of conduct, disclosure of conflicts of interest and protection of those who report wrongdoing.
- **Radhakrishnan Committee (2024):** the expert committee on reforms of the National Testing Agency; relevant for secure, tracked exam logistics.
- **Thinkers:** Kant (duty, fairness as an end in itself); Gandhi's talisman; Nolan principles.
