## QUESTION — 15 marks · 250 words

> India has moved its medium-term fiscal anchor from the annual fiscal deficit to the debt-to-GDP ratio, targeting about 50±1% by 2030-31, even as interest payments absorb roughly two-fifths of the Centre's revenue receipts. **Critically examine** whether a debt anchor is a better guide to fiscal discipline than deficit targets, and the challenges in implementing it across the Union and the States. **(15 marks, 250 words)**

## MODEL ANSWER

**Introduction**
The FRBM Act, 2003 anchored discipline on the annual fiscal deficit. The N.K. Singh Committee (2017) had already recommended a **debt ceiling of 60% of GDP (40% Centre, 20% States)** with the deficit as an operating target. Union Budget 2026-27 now targets debt at **~50±1% of GDP by 2030-31** from **55.6%** in FY27, with the deficit at **4.3% of GDP**.

**Why a debt anchor is better**
- **Captures the stock, not a yearly flow:** a deficit target can be met while debt rises (through low nominal growth or a high interest bill).
- **Growth-sensitive:** debt falls if nominal growth exceeds the interest rate (the r–g gap), rewarding growth-enhancing spending rather than mere compression.
- **Counters "accounting" consolidation:** the deficit can be met by squeezing capex or shifting spending off-budget; a debt path exposes this.
- **Credibility with markets and rating agencies.**

**Limits and challenges**
- **Interest burden:** about **40% of revenue receipts** leaves little fiscal space; primary balance, not headline deficit, must do the work.
- **Vulnerability to denominator shocks:** a lower nominal GDP raises the ratio without any policy failure.
- **Multi-year target, weak enforcement:** no independent **Fiscal Council** (recommended in 2017) to certify numbers or trigger corrections.
- **Union–State coordination:** the **16th FC** sets States at **3% of GSDP** and wants **off-budget borrowings** counted; the combined debt is projected to fall only from **77.3% to 73.1%** by 2030-31, so the States' stock matters as much as the Centre's.
- **Contingent liabilities and quality:** guarantees, PSU borrowings and expenditure composition need transparency.

**Conclusion**
A debt anchor is the better compass, but a deficit path remains the operating instrument. Credibility will come from an independent fiscal council, consolidated Union–State (including off-budget) reporting, protected capex and a rules-based escape clause — so debt falls through growth, not austerity.

**Value additions**
1. **N.K. Singh FRBM Review Committee (2017):** 60% combined debt; fiscal council proposal.
2. **16th Finance Commission (2026-31):** 3.5% Centre deficit by 2030-31; States 3% of GSDP; off-budget borrowings on-budget.
3. **Domar condition / r–g gap:** debt stabilises when nominal growth exceeds the effective interest rate.
