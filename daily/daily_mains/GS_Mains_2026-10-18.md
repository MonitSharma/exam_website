## MODEL ANSWER

### Introduction
Food security (FAO) means physical and economic access to **sufficient, safe and nutritious** food for an active, healthy life. India has solved the first part: from a ship-to-mouth economy of the 1960s to a net food exporter with large buffer stocks, and the **NFSA 2013** now gives legal entitlement to subsidised grain to about 81 crore people, with **PMGKAY** making it free. Yet **NFHS-5** shows stunting near 35%, wasting near 19% and anaemia in over half of women, and India ranks 102/123 on GHI 2025. The statement therefore holds substantially, with caveats.

### Body

**1. Case for the statement: calories ≠ nutrition**
- *Cereal-centric basket.* Entitlements are rice/wheat; pulses, oils, milk, eggs, fruits are outside the PDS in most states, so protein and micronutrient gaps ("hidden hunger") persist.
- *Production incentives.* MSP and procurement favour paddy/wheat, skewing cropping away from pulses, millets and horticulture; the water-intensive Punjab-Haryana model has ecological costs.
- *Determinants beyond food.* Stunting reflects sanitation, safe water, maternal health, early marriage and anaemia, infant and young-child feeding practices, and women's status (the "Asian enigma" literature) — not just household food.
- *First 1,000 days.* Damage is largely irreversible by age two; school meals and PDS reach too late.
- *Delivery gaps.* Leakages in PDS, uneven Anganwadi quality, and limited dietary diversity data.

**2. Case against / caveats**
- *Progress is real:* NFHS-3 → NFHS-5 shows stunting falling from about 48% to about 35.5%; severe starvation deaths have virtually disappeared; the food net (PDS, PM POSHAN, ICDS/Saksham Anganwadi & Poshan 2.0) works as a floor and cushioned Covid.
- *Measurement dispute:* GHI weights child indicators heavily and its undernourishment estimate relies on a small opinion survey; a rank is not a verdict on policy.
- *Other policy moves:* fortified rice supply under PDS, Poshan Abhiyaan (2018) with convergence and tech monitoring, millet promotion (International Year of Millets 2023), Swachh Bharat and Jal Jeevan Mission as indirect nutrition investments.

**3. Way forward: from "food security" to "nutrition security"**
- Diversify entitlements: pulses, oils, millets, eggs; state experiments (e.g., Tamil Nadu's pulses/oil, eggs in school meals in several states) can scale.
- Reform procurement/MSP towards crop diversification and decentralised procurement of coarse grains.
- Concentrate on the first 1,000 days: maternal nutrition, breastfeeding, complementary feeding, anaemia control, with cash/DBT plus counselling (**PMMVY**).
- Convergence with WASH, health (POSHAN Tracker, ASHA-Anganwadi coordination), women's education and agency.
- Better data: timely NFHS/NSS consumption survey releases and independent audits, so debate is evidence-based rather than rank-based.

### Conclusion
India has built a robust food *floor*; the task now is a nutrition *ceiling-raiser*. Moving from "enough grain" to "enough of the right food, in the right body, in the first 1,000 days" is central to a demographic dividend and to **SDG 2 (Zero Hunger)** and **SDG 3**. As Dr Amartya Sen's entitlement approach reminds us, hunger is about access and capability, not merely availability.

### Value additions (use 2-3 in the exam)
- **Constitutional hook:** Article 47 (DPSP: raise level of nutrition and public health) and Article 21 as interpreted in **PUCL v. Union of India (2001, right to food case)** — mid-day meal and PDS entitlements became enforceable.
- **Thinker:** **Amartya Sen** — entitlement approach (*Poverty and Famines*, 1981): famine/hunger arises from failures of entitlement, not just food availability.
- **Scheme/mission:** **Poshan Abhiyaan / Poshan 2.0** and **NFSA 2013** — name the shift from grain to a nutrition-and-convergence approach.
- **Concept:** "Hidden hunger" (micronutrient deficiency), the **triple burden** of malnutrition (under-, over-nutrition and deficiencies), and **SDG 2**.
