## ❓ QUESTION (Case Study) — 15 marks · 250 words

You are the **Additional Commissioner (Engineering) of a municipal corporation in the NCR**. After three days of "severe" air, the Commission for Air Quality Management has invoked **GRAP Stage IV**, which **bans all construction activity**, public and private, until further orders.

Your corporation's **flagship flyover**, meant to relieve a notorious bottleneck, is 90% complete. The **Chief Minister is scheduled to inaugurate it in 12 days**, and the local **MLA**, who has staked political capital on it, calls you personally: *"Do not let the ban kill this project. Find a way."*

Meanwhile:

- The **contractor** — a politically connected firm — requests a written "exemption" citing "public-interest infrastructure". When you refuse, he suggests continuing **night shifts with covered barricading**, "where no one will see", and promises to pay workers full wages for the lost days.
- **200 workers** on site, mostly migrants, are idle. Many are **not registered** with the construction-workers' welfare board and are therefore **outside the state's ₹10,000 relief**. A group of them approaches you: *"Sir, let us work. Smog will not feed our children."*
- A **junior engineer** privately tells you he saw dust-generating work at night at another stretch of the project and was told by the site supervisor "not to put it in the log".
- Doctors at a nearby government hospital report a **spike in respiratory admissions**, particularly among children and the elderly.
- A **local news channel** has asked whether "government projects are quietly exempt from GRAP".

**(a)** Identify the ethical issues and stakeholders. **(b)** Evaluate the options before you. **(c)** What would you do, and why?

*Directive: Structured answer — issues → options (merits/demerits) → decision with safeguards. Time: ~12 minutes. Don't treat workers as villains or as props: they are both the most vulnerable stakeholders and the ones the rule hurts first.*

## ✅ MODEL ANSWER (≈250 words in the exam; the fuller version below shows the thinking)

### (a) Ethical issues and stakeholders

**Ethical issues**

- **Rule of law vs. political pressure:** the ban is a legal direction under the CAQM framework; the MLA's "find a way" is an implicit request to compromise it. Public servants owe fidelity to law and the Constitution, not to a patron.
- **Public health vs. project deadline (Art 21):** the right to clean air is part of the right to life. A symbolic inauguration cannot outweigh respiratory harm to children and the elderly.
- **Compassion for the worker vs. enforcement:** the ban's costs fall on the poorest. Their plea is morally weighty but cannot justify breaking a rule whose harm is borne by others, including other poor families breathing the air.
- **Integrity and honesty:** the "night shift" proposal is concealment, and the instruction "do not log it" is falsification of records. Both are corruption of process even if no money changes hands.
- **Conflict of interest:** a politically connected contractor with a ministerial-level stake pressing for an exemption.
- **Whistleblower protection:** the junior engineer has taken a risk to tell you.
- **Transparency vs. reputation:** the media query tests whether you will answer truthfully.
- **Justice/equity gap in relief:** unregistered workers are excluded — a design flaw I can partly fix within my own powers.

**Stakeholders:** idle workers (registered and unregistered) and their families; residents, especially children, the elderly and the sick; the contractor; the MLA and CM; the junior engineer; the corporation and CAQM; the media and the public; future commuters of the flyover.

### (b) Options

1. **Grant a quiet exemption / allow night work.** *Merit:* meets the deadline; workers earn. *Demerit:* illegal, harms health, creates a precedent that "connected projects are exempt", exposes me to personal liability and destroys the credibility of the ban.
2. **Refuse and do nothing more.** *Merit:* legally safe. *Demerit:* leaves 200 families without income and treats compliance as the whole of duty; ignores the junior engineer's disclosure.
3. **Refuse, enforce strictly, and pair enforcement with support:** stop work, document everything, investigate the night work, and use legitimate channels for the workers' relief and the project timeline.
4. **Ask CAQM / the court for a project-specific exemption.** *Merit:* lawful route. *Demerit:* unlikely to be justified for a nearly complete project; unprincipled if used only for the inauguration date.

### (c) Course of action and reasoning

**Decision: Option 3, with Option 4 only for genuinely essential, non-dust finishing work.**

- **Comply openly.** Tell the MLA and contractor, politely but in writing, that the ban applies to all projects and I cannot grant an exemption. Offer to **re-plan the schedule**: shift the inauguration, or inaugurate a safe, completed part after the ban lifts. The deadline is political; the ban is legal.
- **Stop and verify the night work.** Order a documented inspection of the reported stretch with an independent officer (e.g., the pollution control board). If violations are confirmed, issue a **show-cause notice and penalty** to the contractor under the contract and the relevant environment law; recommend action against the supervisor for the unlogged work.
- **Protect the junior engineer.** Record his disclosure on file, keep his identity confidential and make sure no adverse posting follows.
- **Care for the workers, lawfully.** Do not leave them to the market:
  - require the contractor to pay **minimum wages / a subsistence amount** for the idle days (the offer of full wages was good in itself — it was the night work that was wrong);
  - help unregistered workers **register with the welfare board** and push the labour department to include them;
  - arrange **safe shelters and masks**, and use available **BOCW welfare cess** funds.
- **Be transparent with the media.** Say that no project is exempt, that violations are being investigated and what relief is being given. Honesty is the best defence against rumour.
- **Reflect institutionally.** Recommend that future project timelines include a **smog-season buffer** and that contracts carry a clause on GRAP-related delays, so the pressure never recurs.

*Test of the decision:* (i) **Publicity test** — can I defend it before the media? (ii) **Universalisability (Kant)** — if every officer exempted "favoured" projects, the rule would collapse. (iii) **Rawls' veil of ignorance** — a rule I would accept without knowing whether I am the contractor, the worker or the asthmatic child.

### ✨ Value-additions to use

1. **Constitutional/legal:** Art 21 and *Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar* (clean environment part of right to life); Art 48A and 51A(g); *Vellore Citizens' Welfare Forum* (precautionary principle, polluter-pays).
2. **Thinker/ethics:** Gandhi's **Talisman** (think of the poorest person you have seen); **Rawls** on justice as fairness; Kant on duty vs. inclination; the **Nolan principles** (integrity, honesty, accountability).
3. **Institutional:** the **CAQM Act 2021**; the **BOCW Act 1996** welfare cess and boards; the **Whistle Blowers Protection Act 2014** (still not operationalised — see the 10 Oct case) as the principle behind protecting the junior engineer.

### 🧠 How to score on a GS4 case

- Show the **conflict** (law vs. compassion; project vs. health) in the first two lines.
- List **stakeholders** quickly, then spend your words on **options with merits/demerits**.
- Choose **one decision** and add **safeguards** (documentation, relief, transparency). UPSC rewards the *middle path that is lawful, humane and practical* over a heroic single move.
- Close with a systemic fix (contract clauses, buffers, registration).
