## MODEL ANSWER

**Introduction.** After the 2020 Galwan clash froze ties, the October 2024 patrolling arrangement and the Modi–Xi meetings that followed began a managed thaw. Delhi now speaks of engagement, yet insists that border peace is the "essential prerequisite" (EAM, Oct 2026) — engagement is calibrated, not trusting.

**Why the thaw (drivers)**
- *Border stabilisation:* disengagement at friction points and restored patrolling reduced the immediate risk of conflict.
- *Economic compulsion:* India needs Chinese inputs (electronics components, APIs, solar, rare-earth magnets, capital machinery) for its manufacturing push; a record deficit (~$99 bn in 2024-25) shows the dependence.
- *Strategic hedging:* an uncertain US tariff/trade environment and multipolar bargaining push both sides to keep channels open; BRICS and SCO give platforms.
- *People-level steps:* resumed flights, Kailash Mansarovar Yatra and visas — low-cost confidence-building measures.

**Why it remains tactical (limits)**
- *Unsettled boundary:* no agreed LAC; as the EAM put it, the two "do not have an agreed border". De-escalation (pulling back troops) has not followed disengagement; reports of Arunachal face-offs persist. China's renaming of places in Arunachal and its stand on Doklam/Bhutan keep the issue live.
- *Asymmetric trade:* a large deficit, with Chinese informal curbs on rare earths, fertilisers and specialist engineers/machinery showing that dependence can be weaponised. Press Note 3 (2020) and app bans signal Indian wariness.
- *Water and territory:* the Yarlung Tsangpo mega-dam and the absence of a binding water-sharing treaty (only hydrological-data MoUs).
- *Pakistan and the neighbourhood:* China's military/diplomatic support to Pakistan, CPEC through PoK, and its inroads in Nepal, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh and the Indian Ocean (string-of-pearls concerns).
- *Competing visions:* China sees India's alignment with the Quad and the US as containment; India sees BRI and Chinese primacy as hegemonic.

**Way forward**
- A *compartmentalised but conditional* approach: calibrated FDI opening (minority stakes, tech-transfer, local value addition, case-by-case under Press Note 3) in sectors where India lacks capability, while protecting critical sectors and data.
- Complete de-escalation and de-induction along the LAC, and institutionalised mechanisms (Special Representatives talks, WMCC, corps-commander talks) with timelines toward boundary delimitation.
- Reduce dependence through PLI, supply-chain diversification (Quad, IPEF, FTAs with EU/UK/others) and domestic rare-earth and component capacity.
- Seek a rules-based water-data and flood-warning mechanism on the Brahmaputra; strengthen neighbourhood and Indian Ocean presence.

**Conclusion.** India–China ties are best described as *competitive coexistence*: cooperate where interests converge, compete where they clash, and deter where core security is at stake. A durable normalisation needs verifiable border peace, a more balanced economic relationship and strategic autonomy — a "multi-alignment with a strong spine", as Jaishankar's "no blood shed 1975–2020" reminder suggests, rather than a return to 2019.

**Value additions (verify before using)**
1. **Agreements framework:** 1993 and 1996 border peace/CBM agreements, 2005 Political Parameters and Guiding Principles, and the 2013 BDCA — the architecture that Galwan tested.
2. **Concept:** Panchsheel (1954) vs. today's "Asian century" competition; Jaishankar's emphasis on "mutual respect, mutual sensitivity, mutual interest" as the three mutuals.
3. **Data/policy:** Record trade deficit ~$99.2 bn (2024-25); Press Note 3 (April 2020); PLI and the Rare-Earth Magnet initiative as de-risking tools.
