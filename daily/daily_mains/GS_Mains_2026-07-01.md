## MODEL ANSWER (~250 words)

**Introduction (context + anchor).**
India last counted caste for the whole population in the **1931 Census**; since Independence, enumeration was confined to Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes, leaving Other Backward Classes uncounted. By deciding (Cabinet Committee on Political Affairs, **30 April 2025**) to enumerate caste in **Census 2027**, the state proposes to fill a near-century-old data vacuum — but the move sits at the fault line between evidence-based justice and the fear of hardening identity.

**The case for a caste-enumerated Census.**

- **Evidence for equity:** Reservation and welfare rest on the *quantum* of backwardness, yet OBC policy still leans on the **Mandal Commission's (1980)** dated estimate of ~52%. Fresh data enables rational, targeted allocation.
- **Constitutional fit:** It operationalises **Articles 15(4), 16(4) and 340**, and answers courts' repeated demand for "quantifiable data" (*Indra Sawhney*, 1992; *M. Nagaraj*, 2006) before extending reservations.
- **Correcting invisibility:** It can expose intra-group inequality — a *creamy layer* cornering benefits while the most marginalised jatis stay excluded.

**The risks and limitations.**

- **Reifying caste:** Counting jati may *entrench* an identity a modern republic seeks to dissolve, and fuel competitive, zero-sum mobilisation.
- **Data quality:** The **SECC 2011** caste data was never released owing to ~46 lakh unclassifiable entries — self-enumeration risks a repeat.
- **Ceiling pressure:** Robust numbers may intensify demands to breach the **50% cap** (*Indra Sawhney*).

**Conclusion.**
Caste data is a *diagnostic tool, not a destination*. Handled with rigorous classification and transparency, and paired with a sunset vision for reservations, it can make social justice sharper without making caste permanent.

### Value-additions to weave in (data / committee / article / case law)

- **1931** — last full caste Census; **Census 2027** — first since then (Gazette notification **16 June 2025**; reference date **1 March 2027**).
- **Mandal Commission (1980)** — estimated OBCs at ~52%, basis of **27% OBC reservation** (implemented 1990).
- **Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992)** — upheld 27% OBC quota, capped total reservation at **50%**, mandated *creamy-layer* exclusion and *quantifiable data*.
- **SECC 2011** — caste data collected but never published (~46 lakh caste entries unclassifiable) — the cautionary precedent.
- **Constitutional hooks:** **Art. 340** (Commission for backward classes), **Arts. 15(4)/16(4)** (special provisions); **Art. 342A** (National/State Backward Classes lists, 102nd & 105th Amendments).
- **Recent trigger:** State surveys — **Bihar Caste Survey (2023)** — created political momentum for a national count.
- **Thinker's lens:** Ambedkar sought the *annihilation of caste*; the tension between *counting* caste and *abolishing* it is the essay's moral core.
