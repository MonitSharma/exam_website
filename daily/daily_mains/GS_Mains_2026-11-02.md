## The Question

**Q. "Parliamentary committees are often called 'mini-Parliaments', yet fewer Bills are being referred to them and Parliament's own working hours are shrinking." ## Model Answer

**Introduction**

Parliament cannot examine every Bill and budget line in detail on the floor. Committees, described as "mini-Parliaments", fill this gap: smaller, cross-party, less theatrical bodies working in closed sessions. India has Standing Committees (PAC, Estimates, COPU, 24 DRSCs) and ad hoc ones (Select/Joint Committees, JPCs). The 24 DRSCs were reconstituted for 2026-27 from 26 Sept 2026.

**Role in scrutiny and accountability**

- **Legislative scrutiny:** Technical vetting of Bills, hearings with experts and stakeholders, and amendments (e.g., the Joint Committee on the 2019 Data Protection Bill and the Select Committee on the Jan Vishwas Bill, 2022, both proposed changes).
- **Financial oversight:** The PAC examines CAG audit reports ex post; Estimates Committee suggests economy and efficiency; DRSCs examine Demands for Grants. These make the executive answerable for spending.
- **Policy continuity and expertise:** DRSCs work year-round, even when the House is not sitting, and develop domain knowledge.
- **Consensus-building:** Closed-door sittings and no whip-driven debate allow members to cross party lines.
- **Participation:** Brings in citizens' and experts' views, deepening deliberative democracy.

**Limitations**

- **Declining referral:** Referral is discretionary (Speaker/Chairman), and PRS data shows the share of Bills referred has fallen sharply since the 15th Lok Sabha. Several Bills pass within days.
- **Non-binding:** Recommendations are advisory; the government need not accept them, and DRSC reports are not debated on the floor.
- **Time and capacity:** One-year tenure, vacancies, frequent reshuffles, members on multiple committees, thin research support.
- **Partisanship:** Dissent notes, walkouts and chair-opposition friction weaken consensus, especially for JPCs on politically sensitive subjects.
- **Limited reach:** Subjects are chosen by ministries; Ministries' compliance on Action Taken Reports is uneven.

**Reforms**

1. Make **referral to DRSCs automatic** for all non-urgent Bills (or require written reasons for not referring), as recommended by the **NCRWC (Venkatachaliah Commission, 2002)** and discussed in Parliament repeatedly.
2. Extend committee **tenure to the Lok Sabha's term** or at least two years for continuity.
3. Provide **dedicated research staff**, AI/data tools (as the Speaker has suggested) and a **Parliamentary Budget Office**-like capacity.
4. Mandate **time-bound Action Taken Reports** and floor discussion of key committee reports.
5. Introduce **pre-legislative consultation** of draft Bills for public comment.
6. Protect members' independence through **rotation-based, convention-driven allotment of chairs**.

**Conclusion**

Committees are the quiet engine of parliamentary democracy. As Parliament sits for fewer days, their role becomes more, not less, important. Strengthening them is less about new law than about political will and convention. Robust committees do not weaken the government; they produce better laws, as the Speaker also acknowledges.

**Value-additions**

- **Constitutional/legal hooks:** Arts 105 and 118 (privileges, rule-making power); Rules of Procedure of Lok Sabha/Rajya Sabha (DRSCs under Rule 331C onward, LS — verify); PAC convention that an Opposition member chairs it.
- **Committee/report:** NCRWC (2002) on referral and committee strength; DRSCs are reconstituted annually.
- **Comparative:** US congressional committees are powerful (they can draft and amend Bills); UK Public Bill Committees scrutinise each Bill. India's are advisory by contrast.
- **Thinker:** Woodrow Wilson: "Congress in session is Congress on public exhibition; Congress in its committee-rooms is Congress at work."
