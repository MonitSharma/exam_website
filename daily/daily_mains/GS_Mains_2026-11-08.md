## MODEL ANSWER

**Introduction.** Platform work — delivery, ride-hailing, home services — is organised through algorithms and classified as "independent" work. NITI Aayog (2022) estimated ~7.7 million gig workers in 2020-21, projected to reach ~23.5 million by 2029-30. The question is whether "being your own boss" is freedom or risk-transfer with a friendly label.

**Body.**

*1. The case that flexibility is real*
- Low entry barriers: no formal degree, no hiring cycle; absorbs migrants, women, and youth who lack formal jobs.
- Choice of hours supplements income or fits caregiving; bridges unemployment.
- Platforms expand consumer services and MSME reach, and formalise some payment trails.

*2. The case that risk is shifted to the individual*
- **Income risk:** pay per task, unilateral changes in incentives and rates; the worker bears fuel, vehicle, data and EMI costs.
- **Safety/health risk:** accident, heat and pollution exposure; no assured leave, medical or insurance cover, no minimum wage.
- **Algorithmic control:** ratings, ID deactivation and allocation are opaque and grievance redress is weak — control without the responsibilities of an employer ("flexibility" is mostly the firm's).
- **Collective voice:** classification as "partners" blocks unionisation and bargaining.
- **Life-cycle risk:** no old-age security, and a young workforce faces limited upward mobility.

*3. The policy response so far*
- The **Code on Social Security, 2020** (in force since 21 Nov 2025) recognises gig and platform workers and aggregators for the first time and creates a social security fund financed by aggregator contributions (1–2% of turnover, capped at 5% of payments to workers). The **Social Security (Central) Rules, 2026** set eligibility at 90 days (one aggregator) / 120 days (multiple).
- **States:** Rajasthan (2023) and Karnataka (2025) legislated welfare boards and welfare fees; **e-Shram** registration and **Ayushman Bharat** health cover extension.
- **Gaps:** benefit schemes under Sec 114 yet to be defined; day-thresholds may exclude multi-app, intermittent workers; the employer–worker status question is unresolved; algorithmic transparency is not addressed.

*4. Reconciling flexibility with dignity*
- Portable, contribution-based benefits that follow the worker across platforms (not tied to one employer).
- Floor on earnings net of costs, transparent pay formula and human appeal against deactivation.
- Safety standards and accident/health cover, with rest/heat provisions.
- Legal clarity: a distinct "worker-lite" category with bargaining rights, rather than forcing a binary of employee vs contractor.
- Skilling and exit pathways so gig work is a ladder, not a trap.

**Conclusion.** Flexibility is valuable only when the worker can *choose* it without bearing all its costs. A forward-looking approach is to make benefits portable, the algorithm accountable and the floor guaranteed — so that the platform economy delivers both efficiency and *Sammaan* (dignity), consistent with Articles 21, 38 and 43 and the idea of *Viksit Bharat* built on decent work.
