It produced the grouping's most concrete economic deliverable yet: the **Quad Critical Minerals Initiative Framework**, which aims to mobilise **up to US$20 billion** in public and private support across **mining, processing and recycling** of rare earths and other critical minerals, organised around investment and project development, regulatory alignment, and recovery/recycling. ## QUESTION — 15 marks · 250 words

> The Quad has built an expanding agenda of functional cooperation — critical minerals, maritime domain awareness, connectivity — even as its leaders have not met since 2024. **Critically examine** the Quad's utility for India's Indo-Pacific strategy, in the light of India's simultaneous stabilisation of ties with China and its trade reset with the United States.

**(Directive: *critically examine* — investigate from both directions and end with a reasoned judgement. Do **not** write a descriptive "origins, pillars and achievements of the Quad" answer; that is a 4-mark answer. The marks lie in (a) identifying what the Quad *actually* delivers India — economic security and supply-chain insurance, maritime capacity, technology access, coalition leverage — rather than treating it as an alliance; (b) taking seriously the *structural* limits: no treaty obligation, no secretariat, four divergent China policies, leader-level fatigue, AUKUS/ASEAN frictions, and the **transactional turn in US policy** that tariffs exposed; (c) reconciling the apparent contradiction with **multi-alignment / strategic autonomy** rather than calling it hedging or drift; and (d) a forward-looking close on how India should position the Quad — *what it should be used for, and what it should not be asked to carry*. 15 marks, ~250 words.)**

## MODEL ANSWER (~250 words)

### Introduction (concept + anchor)

The Quad is best understood not as an alliance but as an **issue-based minilateral** — a coalition without treaty obligations, secretariat or collective-defence clause, whose currency is coordination rather than commitment. Its trajectory is now visibly two-speed: the **11th Foreign Ministers' Meeting (New Delhi, 26 May 2026)** launched a **Critical Minerals Initiative Framework mobilising up to $20 billion** and the **Indo-Pacific Maritime Surveillance Collaboration**, yet no Leaders' Summit has convened since **Wilmington (September 2024)**.

### A. What the Quad delivers India

1. **Economic security.** Critical-minerals, semiconductor and undersea-cable cooperation directly addresses India's chokepoint dependence — salient when bilateral trade with China touched **$151.1 bn** with a **$112.16 bn** deficit (FY to March 2026).
2. **Maritime capacity.** IPMDA and now IPMSC extend India's domain awareness across the Indian Ocean far beyond what national assets allow, complementing **MAHASAGAR** (Mauritius, March 2025) and the **IPOI**.
3. **Coalition leverage.** Membership raises the cost to Beijing of coercion on the LAC without India signing an alliance.
4. **Development diplomacy.** The **Fiji port** — the Quad's first joint infrastructure project — offers a credible non-BRI option in the Pacific.

### B. The structural limits

1. **No political ceiling-raiser.** Leader-level summitry has stalled on "scheduling", with India reportedly set to pass the chair to Australia — a signal of *priority*, not logistics.
2. **Four different China policies.** Australia and Japan trade heavily with China; the US alternates between confrontation and deal-making.
3. **Transactional risk.** Tariffs of **50%** on Indian goods (August 2025), cut to **18%** only in **February 2026**, showed that Quad membership buys no economic immunity.
4. **Regional reception.** ASEAN guards its **centrality**; AUKUS complicates the security narrative.

### C. Reconciling the apparent contradiction

India's simultaneous **Modi–Xi bilateral (12 September 2026)** on the "three mutuals" and its **10-year US Major Defence Partnership Framework (31 October 2025)** are not drift but **multi-alignment**: stabilising the border reduces coercive leverage over India, while capability partnerships accumulate options. Strategic autonomy is exercised *through* partnerships, not against them.

### Conclusion (forward-looking, balanced)

India should use the Quad for what minilaterals do well — **supply chains, standards, maritime domain awareness and public goods** — and avoid loading it with expectations of deterrence it was never designed to carry. Institutionalising the working groups so that delivery survives leader-level absence, and keeping the agenda **positive-sum and Global-South facing**, is how the Quad remains useful to India even in a transactional decade.

## COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Writing a history of the Quad (2004 tsunami core group → 2007 → 2017 revival → 2021 leaders' summit). Assume the examiner knows it; one clause at most.
- Calling the Quad an "Asian NATO" — factually wrong and analytically lazy; the absence of a mutual-defence clause is the whole point.
- Treating India's China outreach as a *contradiction* to be apologised for. Frame it as multi-alignment with a stated logic.
- Ending on "India must balance its interests carefully" — a non-conclusion. Say **what** the Quad should and should not be used for.
- Using undated figures. Every number you deploy should carry its month and year.
