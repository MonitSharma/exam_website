## MODEL ANSWER

**Introduction (context + data):**
Launched on 1 July 2017 under the **101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016**, GST subsumed a tangle of central and state levies into "**one nation, one tax**," pooling sovereignty in the **GST Council (Article 279A)**. Yet for eight years it was criticised as a "**good and simple tax**" that was neither — burdened by multiple slabs, classification disputes and an inverted duty structure. The **56th GST Council (Sept 2025)** answered with "**Next-Generation GST**": a move to **two principal slabs of 5% and 18%** (plus a 40% demerit rate), the most significant recast since 2017.

### How the two-slab structure fixes old problems

- **Simplicity & fewer disputes:** scrapping the **12% and 28%** slabs ends much of the "*roti vs. paratha*"-style classification litigation and rate arbitrage.
- **Consumption & equity boost:** cuts on essentials (soaps, toothpaste, packaged food) and on small cars (28%→18%) lower prices, aiding the **common man** and reviving demand.
- **MSME relief:** simpler registration, faster refunds and easier compliance reduce costs for **small firms and startups** — long the worst hit by GST complexity.
- **Correcting design drift:** the original RNR was pegged near **15–15.5%** (Arvind Subramanian Committee), but the weighted rate had slid to **~11.6%**; rationalisation aims at a cleaner, more buoyant structure.

### Fiscal-federal challenges that remain

- **Revenue risk to States:** rate cuts mean a near-term revenue hit, even as the **compensation cess regime (the 14% guarantee)** has lapsed — leaving States more fiscally exposed.
- **Erosion of fiscal autonomy:** with most rates pooled in the Council, States feel their **independent taxing space** is shrinking ("pooled but unequal" sovereignty).
- **Unfinished agenda:** **petroleum, electricity, real estate and alcohol** stay *outside* GST, fragmenting the value chain and breaking the input-tax-credit chain.
- **Buoyancy bet:** the reform stakes revenue on **higher consumption and compliance** offsetting rate cuts — credible given record collections, but not guaranteed in a slowdown.

### Conclusion (forward-looking)

The 2025 reform is a genuine course-correction — **simpler, more progressive, more growth-friendly** — but it completes only half the journey. Bringing **petroleum and electricity into GST**, building a **predictable, trust-based Centre–State compact** beyond the cess era, and strengthening **dispute resolution (the GSTAT)** are the next steps. Done well, GST can finally become the "**good and simple tax**" of cooperative federalism it was always meant to be.

## Value-additions (sprinkle 2–3, don't dump)

- **Constitutional anchors:** **101st Amendment Act, 2016**; **Article 246A** (concurrent power to tax goods & services), **Article 269A** (IGST on inter-state supply levied/apportioned by the Union), **Article 279A** (GST Council). In the Council, the **Centre holds 1/3 of votes and States 2/3, with decisions by a 3/4 majority** — the textbook example of **"pooled sovereignty."**
- **Data (verified):** **56th GST Council, 3 Sept 2025** → two slabs (5%, 18%) + 40% demerit rate, effective **22 Sept 2025**. **FY 2025-26 gross GST ₹22.27 lakh crore (+8.3%)**; **April 2026 ₹2.42 lakh crore (+8.7% YoY)** — evidence of buoyancy. Original **RNR ~15–15.5%** (Arvind Subramanian Committee, 2015) vs. actual weighted rate **~11.6%**.
- **Federalism case law / idea:** ***Union of India v. Mohit Minerals* (2022)** — the Supreme Court held GST Council recommendations are **persuasive, not binding**, affirming **cooperative federalism** and States' fiscal voice. Useful to nuance the "erosion of autonomy" point.
- **Cess context:** the **GST (Compensation to States) Act, 2017** guaranteed States **14% annual revenue growth for five years (to June 2022)**; the cess was then extended only to repay COVID-era back-to-back loans — its winding-down is why the post-2025 settlement matters.
- **Framing line:** "GST is less an *event* of 2017 than a *process* — and 2025 is its most important course-correction yet."
