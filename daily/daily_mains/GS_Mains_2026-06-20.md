## QUESTION (Case Study) — 15 marks · 250 words

You are **Anaya Rao, the newly posted Sub-Divisional Magistrate (SDM)** of a drought-prone sub-division. The IMD has issued a **red-alert heatwave** warning; daytime temperatures are touching 47°C in a year forecast to be a "super El Niño." Your state has **not notified heatwave as a disaster**, so dedicated relief funds are hard to release, and your Heat Action Plan is only partly funded.

Two pressures converge on the same afternoon:

1. A powerful local **MLA insists on holding a large open-air public rally at 1 p.m.**, against your written heat advisory. He offers to "personally arrange" water tankers and an ambulance — through a **contractor whose pending bills are currently on your desk for clearance**. Your District Magistrate, away on leave, has phoned to say *"don't antagonise him."*
2. Local **brick kilns and construction sites continue to employ migrant labour, including some adolescents,** in the open through peak afternoon heat. A journalist asks you, on record, whether the rally and the worksites are safe.

**Answer the following:**

(a) Identify the **ethical issues** and the **stakeholders** involved. *(4)*
(b) What **options** are available to you? Evaluate their merits and demerits. *(6)*
(c) What course of action will you adopt and **why**? Justify with ethical reasoning. *(5)*

## MODEL ANSWER

**Introduction (frame the dilemma):**
This case pits an administrator's **legal and moral duty to protect life** (Article 21) against **political pressure, a latent conflict of interest, and weak institutional backing**. As the field-level executive magistrate, my touchstone must be the **public interest and the safety of the most vulnerable** — migrant workers, children, and the rally crowd — not personal convenience or political comfort.

### (a) Ethical issues and stakeholders

**Core ethical issues:**

- **Right to life vs. political pressure:** Permitting a 1 p.m. rally in a red-alert heatwave risks heat-stroke deaths — a failure of the duty to protect.
- **Conflict of interest / probity:** Accepting tankers via a contractor whose bills I am clearing compromises **integrity and objectivity** and invites *quid pro quo*.
- **Crisis of conscience vs. obedience:** The DM's "don't antagonise him" tests **courage of conviction** against hierarchical compliance.
- **Justice and compassion for the invisible:** Adolescents and migrants in the heat raise **child-labour and humane-governance** concerns.
- **Accountability and transparency:** The journalist's question demands an honest, on-record answer.

**Stakeholders:** the rally crowd and general public; migrant and child workers; the MLA; the contractor; the DM and the administrative chain; the media/public conscience; and *me* — my integrity and the institution's credibility.

### (b) Options — evaluation

| Option | Merits | Demerits |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Allow the rally, accept the MLA's tankers** | Avoids confrontation; pleases superiors | Endangers lives; conflict of interest; sets a corrosive precedent; legally indefensible if deaths occur |
| **2. Refuse permission under heat-safety powers; arrange relief through official channels** | Protects life; upholds rule of law and probity; defensible | Political friction; strains thin resources |
| **3. Postpone/reschedule the rally to cooler hours with safeguards; act firmly on worksites** | Balances democratic right to assemble with safety; proportionate | Requires negotiation, monitoring capacity |
| **4. Stay neutral / pass the file upward and wait** | Avoids personal risk | Abdication; delay can be fatal; violates duty of care |

### (c) Course of action and justification

I would **adopt a calibrated version of Options 2 and 3.**

1. **Invoke my lawful powers** (heat-safety advisory / Sec. 163 BNSS provisions on public safety) to **deny the rally at 1 p.m.** and offer to **reschedule it to the evening (post-6 p.m.)** with mandatory shade, water, ORS and ambulance cover — respecting the *right to peaceful assembly* while honouring the *duty to protect life*.
2. **Decline the MLA's tankers and contractor-arranged relief**; route all relief through **official procurement / the District Disaster Management Authority and NGOs**, keeping the contractor's bills on a strictly **merit-based, arms-length** track to preserve probity.
3. **Issue immediate orders to brick kilns/sites** to halt open-air work during peak hours (12–4 p.m.), provide shaded rest and water, and refer suspected child labour to the **labour department/CWC**.
4. **Answer the journalist honestly** — state the advisory and the safeguards required — modelling **transparency and accountability**.
5. **Document everything in writing** and apprise the DM in writing, converting a verbal nudge into a record, so the decision rests on law and evidence, not on whim.

This path is justified because **no political or administrative pressure can override the constitutional duty to protect life**; integrity is non-negotiable; and a public servant's legitimacy flows from **impartial, courageous, and compassionate** service.

### Conclusion

The measure of an officer is what she does when the rulebook is thin and the pressure is high. By **firmly protecting the vulnerable, refusing tainted help, and being transparent**, I convert a crisis into a demonstration that **administration's first loyalty is to the citizen, not to power.** As Gandhi's talisman urges, the right act is the one that helps the weakest person I can picture — here, the labourer and the child in the 47°C sun.

## Value-additions (sprinkle 2–3, don't dump)

- **Nolan Committee (1995) — Seven Principles of Public Life:** *selflessness, integrity, objectivity, accountability, openness, honesty, leadership.* The conflict-of-interest and transparency strands map directly onto these.
- **2nd ARC, 4th Report — "Ethics in Governance":** stresses insulating administration from undue political pressure and managing conflicts of interest — quotable for the MLA/contractor strand.
- **Constitutional anchor:** **Article 21 (right to life)** — the State's duty to protect citizens from foreseeable harm; complements **Article 19(1)(b)** (right to peaceful assembly), which I balance rather than crush.
- **Policy/data fact (verified):** Heatwave is **NOT** among India's 12+ notified disasters under the Disaster Management Act, 2005 (the schedule lists cyclone, drought, earthquake, fire, flood, tsunami, hailstorm, landslide, avalanche, cloudburst, pest attack, frost and **cold wave** — *but not heatwave*). **NDMA + IMD** run Heat Action Plans across 23 states and 130+ cities/districts — useful to show systemic, not just personal, fixes.
- **Foundational values on display:** *compassion* (vulnerable workers/children), *integrity* (refusing tainted tankers), *courage of conviction* (resisting the MLA/DM), *objectivity* and *transparency* (the journalist).
