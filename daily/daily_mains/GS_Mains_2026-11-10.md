## MODEL ANSWER (≈250 words)

**Introduction.** A PMI measures *month-on-month momentum among surveyed firms*, not the sector's structural weight. October 2026's PMI of 54.7 signals healthy orders and rising exports, yet manufacturing has hovered around 15–17% of GVA for two decades despite the National Manufacturing Policy (2011) targeting 25%. Momentum is not transformation.

**Why the gap persists**

- **Measure vs structure:** PMI tracks diffusion (more firms reporting a rise), is skewed to larger formal firms, and says little about the informal MSME base that employs most workers.
- **Missing middle:** a dualistic structure of tiny units and a few large firms; small firms rarely scale due to labour-law, credit and compliance cliffs. Labour-intensive sectors (textiles, apparel, footwear, leather, toys) lost ground to Bangladesh and Vietnam.
- **Capital-intensive pivot:** incentives (PLI) have favoured capital- and import-intensive assembly (electronics, autos), where jobs per rupee invested are low and domestic value addition is thin.
- **Cost of doing business:** high logistics cost, power tariffs, costly land, inverted duties and tariffs on intermediates; limited integration into global value chains; skill mismatch.
- **Weak confidence:** the same PMI shows only ~18% of firms expecting higher output next year — investment hesitancy amid global tariff uncertainty.

**Critical assessment.** PLI (₹1.97 lakh crore across 14 sectors, 2021) and Make in India have lifted electronics and mobile exports, but benefits are concentrated, disbursals have lagged, and employment results are modest.

**Way forward**

- Labour-intensive export push: textile/apparel, footwear, toys — with trade deals (FTAs) and lower duties on inputs.
- MSME scaling: cluster development, credit guarantees, compliance simplification; operationalise the **Labour Codes** for formalisation.
- Cut logistics cost (PM Gati Shakti, National Logistics Policy); reliable cheap power; plug-and-play industrial parks.
- Skilling tied to demand; **National Manufacturing Mission** (Budget 2025-26) to bundle policy, ease of doing business and clean-tech manufacturing.

**Conclusion.** A good PMI is a thermometer, not a cure. By making jobs-per-rupee, MSME scaling and GVC integration the metrics — not just output — India can convert cyclical strength into the structural shift its demographic dividend demands.
