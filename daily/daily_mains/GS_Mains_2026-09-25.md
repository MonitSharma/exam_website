## QUESTION — 15 marks · 250 words

> Eighteen years of Intergovernmental Negotiations on Security Council reform have not yielded even a negotiating text, even as the Council's inability to act on major conflicts deepens. **Critically examine** the obstacles to reform of the UN Security Council, and suggest how India should recalibrate its strategy in pursuit of a permanent seat.

**(Directive: *critically examine* — probe the question from all sides and arrive at a reasoned judgement. The trap is writing "why India deserves a permanent seat" — population, economy, peacekeeping — which answers a question that wasn't asked. The marks lie in (a) diagnosing *why* reform is stuck — legal, political and procedural locks, and the split among reformers themselves — and (b) a **realistic, multi-track** strategy for India, not a restatement of the demand.)*

## MODEL ANSWER (~250 words)

### Introduction (concept + anchor)

The Security Council is the only UN organ whose decisions bind all members (Art. 25), yet its permanent membership still reflects **1945**, and it has been enlarged only once (11→15, effective **1965**). The **G4 ministerial of 22 September 2026** noted that the IGN has spent **18 years** without a negotiating text — while vetoes have left the Council largely sidelined on Ukraine and Gaza.

### A. Why reform is stuck

1. **Legal lock** — **Article 108** requires two-thirds of the GA *and* ratification by two-thirds of members **including all P5**: each permanent member holds a veto over its own dilution.
2. **Reformers divided** — G4 (six new permanent seats, 25–26 members, **January 2026 model**) vs **Uniting for Consensus** (Italy, Pakistan and others: only longer-term elected seats) vs **Africa's Ezulwini Consensus** (two permanent seats *with* veto). Regional rivals block each other.
3. **Veto question** — no agreement on extending, restricting or abolishing it.
4. **Procedural drift** — the IGN works by consensus with no text, so there is nothing to vote on; the co-chairs' **"fixed regional seats" bridging idea** satisfies neither camp.

### B. Why it still matters

Legitimacy deficit (Africa, with the most Council agenda items, has no permanent seat); Council paralysis driving action to the GA (**Res. 76/262** "veto initiative", 2022) and to minilaterals.

### C. Recalibrating India's strategy

1. **Push text-based negotiations** and build a **G4–Africa (C-10) bridge**, since the **Pact for the Future (2024)** prioritises redressing Africa's under-representation.
2. **Be flexible on the veto** — the G4 already accepts deferring it pending review.
3. **Earn it meanwhile** — the **2028–29 non-permanent term**, peacekeeping (**~2.86 lakh personnel since 1950**), and G20/Global South leadership.
4. **Hedge** through BRICS, Quad, SCO and minilaterals, so India's influence doesn't depend on the Charter.

### Conclusion (forward-looking, balanced)

Permanent membership will come, if at all, as the result of a coalition that includes Africa and outlasts P5 resistance — not as recognition of India's size. India should treat reform as a **long campaign of coalition-building and demonstrated responsibility**, while making sure its global role never waits on the Charter being amended.

## VALUE ADDITIONS (weave 2–3, do not list them)

- **G4 Ministerial, New York, 22 September 2026** — IGN "18 years" without substantive outcome; rejected the co-chairs' bridging proposal; next review at a DG-level meeting in **New Delhi**.
- **G4 model (IGN, 22 January 2026)** — Council of **25–26**; **six new permanent seats**: 2 Africa, 2 Asia-Pacific, 1 Latin America & Caribbean, 1 Western Europe & Others.
- **IGN co-chairs' Revised Elements Paper (GA80, 2026)** — convergence on a Council of **21–27**; majority support for at least **3 additional seats for Africa, 3 for Asia-Pacific, 2 for GRULAC**; bridging option of **"fixed regional seats"** with renewable terms and periodic review.
- **Legal anchors** — Arts. **23, 24, 25, 27(3)** (veto), **108/109** (amendment); **GA Res. 76/262 (April 2022)** — GA must debate within 10 working days after a veto is cast; **Ezulwini Consensus (2005)** and **Sirte Declaration**.
- **Pact for the Future (Summit of the Future, September 2024)** — commits to "redress the historical injustice against Africa" as a priority in Council reform.
- **UN80 retrenchment** — regular budget **$3.45 bn for 2026**, ~7% below 2025, **2,900 posts abolished** (GA, 30 December 2025) — shows how financial dependence on major contributors shapes the organisation.
- **India's 2028–29 bid** — launched by Jaishankar on **14 July 2026**; eight previous terms, most recently **2021–22**.

## COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

- Spending the whole answer on "India's credentials" — population, GDP, democracy, peacekeeping. One line, as a lever in Part C, is enough.
- Calling UfC or China "the only obstacle". The real lock is **Article 108 + divided reformers + the veto question** together.
- Getting the G4 model wrong: the G4 has said new permanent members would **not exercise the veto until a review** — don't claim India is demanding an immediate veto.
- Ignoring Africa. Any credible strategy runs through the **Common African Position**.
- Ending with "the UN must reform or become irrelevant". Say **what India should do** in the next 5–10 years.
