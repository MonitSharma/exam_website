## QUESTION (Case Study) — 15 marks · 250 words

You are **Vikram Rao, the Chief Executive Officer (CEO) of a Zilla Parishad**, responsible for implementing a flagship **DBT-based maternity-benefit scheme** for pregnant and lactating women in a poor, largely rural district.

A routine **social audit, backed by data analytics, flags a large block of "ghost" and duplicate beneficiaries** — Aadhaar seedings re-routed to a handful of accounts. Several thousand genuine poor mothers were never paid; the money was siphoned. The digital trail points to the scheme's **private IT vendor**, and — far more uncomfortably — to a **senior officer who was once your direct mentor**, who shielded you early in your career and is now months from retirement and in poor health.

Two further pressures land the same week:

1. The **young woman data-entry operator who first raised the red flag** is being intimidated, denied her contract renewal, and recommended for a punitive transfer.
2. A call from the **State Secretariat** advises you to "**close the audit quietly**" and not derail a high-profile scheme-launch event next month. A **journalist**, meanwhile, has the outline of the story and seeks your on-record comment.

**Answer the following:**

(a) Identify the core **ethical issues** and the **stakeholders** involved. *(4)*
(b) What **options** are available to you? Evaluate their merits and demerits. *(6)*
(c) What course of action will you adopt, and **why**? Justify with ethical reasoning. *(5)*

## MODEL ANSWER

**Introduction (frame the core conflict):**

The case sets a *fiduciary* duty — public money meant for the poorest mothers — against a deeply personal one: gratitude to a mentor. Public office is held in trust; the decisive test is whether private obligation, compassion, or institutional pressure may be allowed to dilute that trust. They may not.

---

### (a) Ethical issues and stakeholders *(4)*

**Ethical issues**

- **Integrity vs. gratitude/loyalty** — a personal debt to a mentor pulling against duty.
- **Conflict of interest & misuse of public funds** meant for a vulnerable group.
- **Whistle-blower protection** — a powerless junior punished for honesty.
- **Transparency & accountability vs. reputation management** ("close it quietly").
- **Courage of conviction** under political/institutional pressure.

**Stakeholders:** the *genuine poor mothers* denied dues (most vulnerable, voiceless); the *whistle-blower*; the *mentor* and the *IT vendor* (the accused); *you* as CEO; the *State government and taxpayers*; the *media/public*.

---

### (b) Options — merits and demerits *(6)*

**Option 1 — Soft-pedal the audit, shield the mentor, follow the Secretariat.**
*Merit:* short-term peace; repays a personal debt; career safety.
*Demerit:* betrays beneficiaries and your oath; complicity in fraud; abandons the whistle-blower; legal liability if exposed; normalises leakage.

**Option 2 — Act fully and by the book, with humanity.**
Complete and document the audit, freeze the leak, recover funds, recommend due process against *all* implicated, protect and reinstate the whistle-blower, brief superiors **in writing**, and respond to the press factually within rules.
*Merit:* upholds integrity, rule of law, and the rights of the poor and the whistle-blower.
*Demerit:* personal and emotional cost; antagonises the powerful.

**Option 3 — Middle path: fix the system but spare the mentor formal action.**
*Merit:* stops ongoing harm; shows compassion.
*Demerit:* selective justice; undermines accountability; legally untenable; unsustainable.

---

### (c) Course of action and justification *(5)*

I will adopt **Option 2**, executed firmly but humanely.

- **Stop the harm first:** freeze suspect disbursements, trigger re-verification so genuine mothers are paid.
- **Due process for all,** including the mentor — no cover-up, but also no pre-judgement; gratitude is repaid through honesty, not protection of wrongdoing.
- **Protect the whistle-blower:** stay the transfer, renew the contract, record the protection in writing.
- **Resist pressure on the record:** seek instructions in writing; respond to media truthfully and within rules.

**Why:** A public servant's higher loyalty is to the Constitution and the citizen, not to any individual. As Gandhi's *Talisman* urges — recall the face of the poorest mother this money was meant for. A cover-up fails Kant's universalisability test and the **Nolan principle of selflessness**. Compassion is shown not by hiding facts, but by ensuring fair process and care for the most vulnerable.

**Conclusion:** *Integrity with empathy.* Plug the leak, protect the honest, follow due process for the guilty — and let the office, not personal debts, decide.

### Value-additions (deploy 1–2, not all)

- **Scale = stakes:** PMMVY has disbursed **₹20,060 crore to 4.26 crore beneficiaries** via DBT (to 8 Jan 2026, MoWCD). At this scale, *data integrity and social audit* are frontline ethics — quote to show the public-money dimension.
- **Whistle-blower angle:** The **2nd ARC, 4th Report — "Ethics in Governance" (2007)** recommended statutory protection for informants; the **Whistle Blowers Protection Act, 2014** was passed but **remains un-operationalised** — so *administrative* protection of the whistle-blower is squarely the officer's duty here. (Also: **Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988** — duty to report.)
- **Principle/thinker hooks:** **Nolan Committee's Seven Principles of Public Life** (Selflessness, Integrity, Objectivity…); **Gandhi's Talisman**; **Kautilya's *Arthashastra*** — "in the happiness of his subjects lies the king's happiness." Use one as the spine of part (c).
