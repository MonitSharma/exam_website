## Model Answer

**Introduction**

GST (101st Amendment, 2016; launched 1 July 2017) is India's largest indirect-tax reform. October 2026 gross collections of ₹2.31 lakh crore (+22.9%) look strong, yet import IGST grew 42.2% against 15.8% for domestic revenue, and over April–October domestic gross grew only 7.4%. The headline therefore needs unpacking.

**What the surge indicates**

- **Formalisation and compliance:** Higher e-invoicing, analytics-based enforcement and a wider base keep buoyancy up.
- **Revenue capacity for the Centre and States:** Stronger collections ease fiscal consolidation and the pooled IGST settlement supports States.
- **Trade activity:** High import IGST reflects continued import demand; import GST is also the input-tax-credit pipeline for domestic producers.

**Why the headline can mislead**

- **Import-led, not demand-led:** Import IGST depends on import value, which rises with commodity (especially crude) prices and a weaker rupee — price effects, not volumes of consumption or growth. (*Analytical inference; verify.*)
- **Credit mechanics:** Import IGST is largely claimable as input credit, so it is partly a timing item; net domestic revenue grew only 6.0% over the seven months, and refunds rose 16.9%.
- **Base and rate effects:** After the September 2025 rate rationalisation (GST 2.0), year-on-year comparisons are distorted; a low base can flatter growth.

**Structural challenges**

- **Unfinished design:** Petroleum, alcohol and electricity remain outside GST; inverted duty structures persist; there is no GST Appellate Tribunal functioning at scale (statutorily provided, operationalisation recent/slow — verify).
- **Cooperative federalism:** States have lost fiscal autonomy and depend on the GST Council; compensation cess has ended, raising concerns for States' revenue assurance.
- **Compliance burden and disputes:** Multiple returns, notices and litigation, especially for MSMEs.

**Way forward and conclusion**

Track *net domestic* revenue and volume-adjusted indicators rather than gross totals; settle the tribunal, simplify returns, and rationalise slabs. Revenue strength is welcome, but a tax system's health lies in domestic consumption and compliance, not in an import bill.

**Value additions**

- **Article 279A** (GST Council) and **Article 246A** (concurrent taxing power); **101st Amendment Act, 2016**.
- **GST 2.0 (rate rationalisation, Sept 2025):** moved to two main slabs (5% and 18%) plus a 40% demerit rate — verify details with the GST Council's decision before quoting.
- **Supreme Court, Mohit Minerals (2022):** GST Council recommendations are persuasive, not binding on Union/States, underlining cooperative federalism.
