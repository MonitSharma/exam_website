## MODEL ANSWER

**Introduction.** A demographic dividend arises when the working-age share rises relative to dependants, but it yields growth only if those workers find productive jobs. The World Bank (Nov 2026) sees India's working-age share peaking around 2034, while the *India Ageing Report 2023* projects the 60+ share doubling from 10.8% (2022) to ~20.8% by ~2047. The window is real, but finite.

**Body.**

*1. Why the concern is valid*
- **Shrinking window:** the working-age share peaks within about a decade; the elderly are projected to outnumber children (0–14) by ~2046.
- **Quantity without quality of jobs:** low female labour-force participation, a large informal workforce, and weak formal job creation mean the dividend is under-used.
- **Ageing at lower income:** Europe and Japan aged after becoming rich; India will age at far lower per-capita income, with a thin fiscal cushion.
- **Care and pension gaps:** only ~21% of 50+ people with functional limitations get care; only ~13% of the informal workforce is covered by pension initiatives (World Bank, as reported).
- **Regional divergence:** southern and some western states are ageing early (e.g., Kerala, Tamil Nadu), while Bihar and UP remain young — migration, fiscal transfers and delimitation debates are tied to this.

*2. Why the picture is not wholly bleak*
- India's median age is still roughly 29, and its demographic advantage lasts longer than China's did.
- Fertility has fallen near/below replacement (TFR ~2.0 per NFHS-5), showing success in health and education; the ageing is partly a success story.
- Scenario modelling shows sizeable gains: closing the gender participation gap could lift GDP per person up to 19% by 2050 (World Bank scenario).
- Health, skills and digital public infrastructure are improving; the time is enough to act if reforms are front-loaded.

*3. Converting the dividend to productivity (act now)*
- **Jobs:** labour-intensive manufacturing and services (see PLI/ELI, textiles, tourism); MSME formalisation.
- **Women:** safe transport, childcare, flexible work to raise female participation.
- **Skills and health:** NEP 2020 skilling, apprenticeships; nutrition and adolescent health so that young workers are productive.
- **Urban and migration policy:** livable cities and portable entitlements for internal migrants.

*4. Preparing for an ageing society*
- Expand **pension and social security** coverage for informal workers (NPS, Atal Pension Yojana, Code on Social Security 2020).
- **Health and long-term care:** geriatric care, Ayushman Bharat coverage of the elderly (extended to 70+ in 2024), community care workforce.
- **Savings and fiscal planning:** build pension/health fiscal space now, while the dependency ratio is favourable.
- **Active ageing:** age-friendly workplaces, lifelong learning, and the National Policy for Senior Citizens' implementation; the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007.

**Conclusion.** India's task is to compress a sequence other countries spread over decades: create productive jobs and build ageing-ready systems at once. Treating the dividend as a window with a deadline, rather than a gift, and investing in women, skills and social protection can turn "old before rich" into "ageing with dignity", in line with Arts 21, 41 and 47 and the goal of *Viksit Bharat*.
