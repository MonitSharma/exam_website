## MODEL ANSWER

**Intro (context + anchor).**
"One Nation, One Election" (ONOE) proposes synchronising elections to the Lok Sabha, State Assemblies and, in a second phase, local bodies. India in fact held simultaneous polls from 1951–52 to 1967, until premature dissolutions broke the cycle. The idea has been revived through the **High-Level Committee under Ram Nath Kovind (report, 14 March 2024)** and the **Constitution (129th Amendment) Bill, 2024**, now before a **JPC (tenure extended to Winter Session 2026)**, targeting a 2029 rollout. The reform promises efficiency but touches the basic architecture of federalism and accountability.

**The case for ONOE.**

- **Governance continuity:** frequent polls keep the country in near-permanent election mode; the **Model Code of Conduct** repeatedly freezes new schemes and appointments, stalling development.
- **Cost and administrative load:** synchronised polls economise on expenditure, security deployment and the diversion of teachers, officials and security forces to repeated election duty.
- **Reduced polarising politics:** fewer election cycles can lower the constant communal/caste mobilisation and populist "freebie" competition that recurring polls encourage.
- **Voter convenience and turnout:** a single, predictable election and a **single electoral roll/EPIC** (proposed via amended Article 325) reduce duplication and voter fatigue.

**The constitutional and federal concerns.**

- **Federalism and the basic structure:** federalism is part of the **basic structure (*S.R. Bommai*, 1994)**; synchronisation via a new **Article 82A** could require curtailing or extending State Assembly terms to fit a national cycle, subordinating State mandates to the Union's calendar.
- **Accountability deficit:** fixed, synchronised terms weaken the discipline of mid-term verdicts; a government that loses confidence may be followed by President's/Governor's rule or a truncated "unexpired term" House, diluting responsible government.
- **Regional voice:** clubbing national and State polls risks **national issues overshadowing regional/local concerns**, disadvantaging smaller and regional parties — a democratic-diversity cost.
- **Practical–legal hurdles:** ratification by States (federal amendments), EVM/VVPAT logistics for a single mega-exercise, and handling **hung Assemblies or no-confidence motions** mid-cycle remain unresolved.

**Balanced conclusion.**
ONOE's efficiency logic is real, but efficiency cannot be purchased at the cost of federal balance and voter accountability. The prudent path is a **phased, consensus-based reform** — beginning with Lok Sabha and Assembly synchronisation, robust constitutional safeguards for mid-term instability, and genuine consultation with States and regional parties through the JPC — so that "one nation, one election" strengthens, rather than centralises, Indian democracy.

- **Thinker/principle:** invoke **B.R. Ambedkar's** caution that constitutional forms must serve *responsible* government — accountability is as vital as stability.
