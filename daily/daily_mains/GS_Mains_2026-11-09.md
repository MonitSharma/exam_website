## ✅ Model Answer (~250 words)

**Introduction**
Article 280 creates the Finance Commission (FC) to recommend the sharing of net tax proceeds (Art 270) and grants-in-aid (Art 275) between the Union and States. The 16th FC (2026-31) retained vertical devolution at **41%**, shifting its attention to horizontal criteria, grants and borrowing rules.

**Where it addresses state concerns**
- **Horizontal equity and efficiency:** replaces tax-effort with *contribution to GDP* (rewarding economic performers), keeps income-distance and demographic performance, and uses a weighted forest-cover measure — attempting to balance equity with incentives. ICRA estimates 14 of 28 states gain share.
- **Third tier:** local-body grants roughly double (₹3.9 trn → ₹7.9 trn), 60% untied-but-conditional, strengthening Art 243-I/243-Y-linked finance.
- **Capex and transparency:** 50-year interest-free capex loans; off-budget borrowings to be brought on-budget; disaster grants up ~27%.

**Where it falls short**
- **Vertical share unchanged** despite demands for 50%; more importantly, **cesses and surcharges** (outside the divisible pool under Art 270(1)/271) cut the pool from ~89% (2014-15) to ~74–80% of gross tax revenue — so the *effective* devolution is well below 41%. The "grand bargain" remains only a proposal.
- **Revenue-deficit grants ended**, hurting fiscally weak states (13 of 17 former recipients likely to gain less in total grants), widening north–south and rich–poor tension.
- **Tight borrowing room:** 3% of GSDP ceiling, no extra reform-linked borrowing, no carry-forward — while states bear welfare and capex burdens.
- **Conditionality** on local-body grants raises autonomy concerns.

**Conclusion**
The award is a *cautious recalibration* rather than a structural reset: it rewards performance but leaves the cess problem and the weaker states' needs unresolved. Durable cooperative federalism needs a statutory cap on cesses/surcharges, a standing GST Council–FC dialogue, and an equalisation-first approach so that "efficiency" does not erode "equity".

**Value additions**
- **Constitutional anchors:** Arts 270, 271, 275, 280, 281; Art 243-I/243-Y (State Finance Commissions).
- **Precedent:** the 14th FC raised the share from 32% to 42% — the high-water mark of devolution; the 15th FC (41%, after J&K's reorganisation) also introduced demographic performance.
- **Wider lens:** the Sarkaria (1988) and Punchhi (2010) Commission views on Centre–State finance; the **GST Council** as a shared-sovereignty forum; "grand bargain" on cesses as a policy fix.
