## MODEL ANSWER

**Introduction**
A free and fair election begins with an accurate roll. Article 326 guarantees adult suffrage to every citizen aged 18+, while Article 324 vests superintendence, direction and control of roll preparation and elections in the ECI. SIR — an intensive, house-to-house, document-linked re-verification — is the Commission's tool to remove duplicate, dead, shifted and ineligible entries.

**Legal basis**
- **Art. 324**: plenary, residuary power where law is silent (*Mohinder Singh Gill v. CEC, 1978*), but it must be exercised fairly, in good faith and subject to statute.
- **Representation of the People Act, 1950, s.21(3)**: allows the Commission to direct a special revision of rolls; **Registration of Electors Rules, 1960** prescribe the procedure.
- **Art. 326 / s.16 & s.19, RPA 1950**: only citizens, ordinarily resident, aged 18+ can be enrolled — so eligibility checks are legitimate.
- Precedent: eight intensive/special revisions between 1951 and 2004 (last 2002–04); the Supreme Court upheld the 2025–26 exercise as within ECI's mandate.

**Why it is needed**
- Migration, urbanisation and deaths leave stale entries; duplicate EPICs dent credibility.
- Clean rolls reduce impersonation disputes and improve turnout arithmetic and polling-booth planning.

**Concerns**
- **Disenfranchisement risk**: the poor, migrant workers, women who marry out, and the undocumented may lack papers; the burden shifts to the elector, whereas deletion historically needs notice and reason (*Lal Babu Hussein v. ERO, 1995* — no deletion of existing electors without notice and opportunity to prove citizenship).
- **Blurring of roles**: ECI verifying "citizenship" shades into adjudication, which belongs to the Union under the Citizenship Act, 1955. Aadhaar/EPIC prove identity, not citizenship.
- **Timing and capacity**: short windows near elections, BLO overload and weak remedies for wrongly deleted names.
- **Function creep**: SIR data being used for welfare/other purposes (the Court's clarification bars this).

**Safeguards / way forward**
1. Notice, reasoned order and hearing before any deletion; time-bound appeals to the designated authority.
2. Wider, graded list of acceptable documents; proactive door-to-door outreach for migrants, women and the elderly.
3. Publish deletion lists with reasons (booth-wise, machine-readable) for civil-society audit.
4. Any citizenship doubt to be referred to the competent authority, not decided by roll officers.
5. Schedule SIRs well away from poll dates; add digital, portable enrolment (Form 6, e-EPIC).

**Conclusion**
The Court's balance is instructive: ECI's power to clean rolls is valid, but the presumption must favour the elector. As *Anoop Baranwal (2023)* stressed the Commission's independence, trust in its rolls depends on transparent process. A roll should be as accurate as possible and as inclusive as possible — "no eligible voter left out, no ineligible voter kept in."

**Value additions**
- *Mohinder Singh Gill (1978)*, *Lal Babu Hussein (1995)*, *Anoop Baranwal (2023)* — case law above.
- Art. 324, 326; RPA 1950 s.21(3); Registration of Electors Rules 1960.
- ECI's own "SVEEP" and Form 6/7/8 framework; Law Commission/ECI reform ideas on linking rolls with birth/death registers (Registration of Births and Deaths data).
- Thinker: Dr Ambedkar in the Constituent Assembly on an independent, central electoral machinery.
