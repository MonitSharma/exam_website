## MODEL ANSWER

**Introduction (context + data):**
"Care" — cooking, cleaning, child-rearing, tending the elderly and the sick — is the invisible scaffolding on which every market economy stands, yet it appears in no ledger. The **Time Use Survey 2024 (MoSPI)** found women spend nearly **5 hours a day (289 minutes)** on unpaid domestic work against **88 minutes** for men, devoting **19.7%** of their day to unpaid domestic and care work versus **2.6%** for men. When the Supreme Court (June 2026) called homemakers **"nation builders,"** it gave law's voice to what statistics had begun to show: care is *work*, even if it is unwaged.

### Why care remains economically invisible

- **A measurement blind spot:** GDP counts only marketed output (the "System of National Accounts" production boundary), so non-market household labour is excluded by design.
- **Gendered social norms:** care is naturalised as women's "duty," not labour — so it is neither paid nor counted, reinforcing the *"she doesn't work"* fallacy.
- **No price, hence no power:** without monetary value, care work confers no income, pension, bargaining power or visibility in policy.
- **Time poverty → opportunity cost:** the unpaid-work burden depresses female labour-force participation, shrinking women's economic agency.

### What recognition would mean

- **Justice & dignity:** valuing care affirms the homemaker as an **equal economic contributor, not a dependent** — advancing substantive equality (Articles 14, 15, 21) and the SC's logic of notional income.
- **Growth:** counting and redistributing care can unlock women's paid participation; estimates value unpaid work at a *large share of GDP* (an SBI study put it near **7.5%**, others at **15–17%** by different methods) — a vast, untapped contribution.
- **Better policy:** recognition justifies investment in the **care infrastructure** (crèches, elder care, water/fuel access, parental leave) that *reduces* and *redistributes* drudgery.

### The balance — limits and cautions

Recognition must not *entrench* care as women's domain. Cash-only valuation risks "paying women to stay home"; the goal is the **3R framework — Recognise, Reduce, Redistribute** (UN/SDG 5.4) — so that men, the State and markets share the load, not merely price it.

### Conclusion

A republic that aspires to be a *developed Bharat by 2047* cannot build prosperity on labour it refuses to see. By **counting care, investing in care infrastructure, and redistributing it across genders and institutions**, India can convert invisible sacrifice into visible citizenship — making growth not only larger, but fairer and more humane.

## Value-additions (sprinkle 2–3, don't dump)

- **Case law (recent):** *SC, 11 June 2026* — created the head **"Loss of Domestic Care,"** fixed **₹30,000/month** national value, enhanced award **₹8.43 L → ₹62.77 L** (Justices Sanjay Karol & N. Kotiswar Singh).
- **Case law (precedent):** ***Kirti v. Oriental Insurance Co. Ltd.* (2021) 2 SCC 166** — SC held that fixing **notional income** (with future prospects) for a homemaker is "imperative for social equality"; valued a homemaker's work on par with an earning spouse's (Justices N.V. Ramana, S. Abdul Nazeer, Surya Kant).
- **Data:** **Time Use Survey 2024** — women 289 min/day unpaid domestic work vs men 88 min; women 19.7% of day on unpaid domestic + care work vs men 2.6%.
- **Economic value:** **SBI Research (2023)** estimated unpaid household work at ≈**₹22.7 lakh crore (~7.5% of GDP)**; other methods put women's unpaid care at **15–17% of GDP** — *cite as a range, since estimates vary by method.*
- **Framework / global anchor:** **SDG 5.4** — *Recognise, Reduce, Redistribute* unpaid care and domestic work (the "3R" approach). Links neatly to **care economy** as emerging employment sector.
- **Thinkers / lenses (essay flavour):** Amartya Sen's **capability approach** (development as expanding real freedoms — care work expands others' freedoms while constraining the carer's); feminist economist **Marilyn Waring's** critique *"If Women Counted"* — what GDP omits, society undervalues.
