## MODEL ANSWER

### Introduction
Since the 2005 *Next Steps in Strategic Partnership* and the 2008 civil nuclear deal, the US has become India's most consequential partner in defence, technology and the Indo-Pacific; yet the US is also the source of India's sharpest recent shocks — tariffs of up to 50% (Aug 2025), the Russian-oil penalty and pressure over Pakistan. The relationship is thus **convergent in strategy but contingent in transactions**.

### Where the convergence is structural
- **China balancing:** shared concern over Chinese assertiveness in the Indo-Pacific; the **Quad** (revived 2017), maritime domain awareness and logistics cooperation.
- **Defence plumbing:** the "foundational agreements" — **GSOMIA (2002), LEMOA (2016), COMCASA (2018), BECA (2020)** — plus a **10-year defence framework (Oct 2025)**.
- **Tech and supply chains:** iCET (2023), succeeded by the **TRUST** initiative (2025) on critical technologies; diaspora and education links.

### Why friction is also structural, not accidental
1. **Asymmetry of power and method:** Washington under Trump treats trade, migration and security as one transactional bundle; India treats the partnership as a long-term strategic hedge. Tariff-for-oil linkage is the sharpest example.
2. **Energy security vs. sanctions:** Russian crude has been a large share of India's imports since 2022; a secondary-tariff regime puts *sovereign energy choices* in conflict with US sanctions policy.
3. **The Pakistan variable:** US outreach to Pakistan and claims of brokering the May 2025 ceasefire collide with India's long-held position of **no third-party mediation** and its "terror and talks cannot go together" line.
4. **Trade-model divergence:** India's protected agriculture/dairy and data/digital rules vs. US demands for market access; the February 2026 framework still lacks a signed final text.
5. **Domestic politics on both sides:** US legislative and judicial volatility (e.g. tariff powers litigated in court) and Indian electoral sensitivities make commitments harder to lock in.

### Strategic autonomy — what it should and should not mean
- **Not** equidistance or neutrality: India's threat is on its land border with China; abandoning the US/Quad would reduce leverage, not raise it.
- **Not** a swing state: oscillating between Washington, Moscow and Beijing gains nothing from any of them; *issue-based convergence* (Jaishankar's "multi-alignment", *The India Way*, 2020) works only if India has capabilities to offer.
- **Yes** to *agency*: diversified energy and arms sourcing, rupee/local-currency trade settlement where feasible, and an independent line on Russia, Iran and Pakistan.

### Way forward
- **Compartmentalise:** lock in the defence/tech tracks (co-production, jet engines, semiconductors) and keep trade as a separate, time-bound negotiation.
- **Close the trade deal** on India's red lines (sensitive agriculture/dairy, data sovereignty) rather than under deadline pressure; diversify through the **EU, UK, EFTA and Gulf FTAs**.
- **Reduce vulnerability:** broaden crude sources, expand strategic reserves and renewables, so that sanctions threats lose leverage.
- **Build a coalition of the "middle":** coordinate with the EU, Japan, Australia, ASEAN and Gulf states on rules for tariffs and secondary sanctions at the WTO/G20.
- **Quiet diplomacy with the US Congress and business lobby**, where the India relationship has bipartisan support.

### Conclusion
The relationship will remain "convergent at the top, contested at the edges". The goal is not to choose between autonomy and alignment, but to build enough economic and military capability that India can say *yes* to the US on shared interests and *no* on coerced ones — what Jaishankar calls being a "leading power", not a balancing one.

### Value-additions
- **Concept:** *Nonalignment 2.0* (2012) — the policy document that argued for "strategic autonomy" through capability-building, not distance.
- **Doctrine:** Jaishankar's *The India Way* — multi-alignment, "many-sided engagement"; contrast with Cold-War non-alignment.
- **Treaty/Initiative:** Foundational agreements (GSOMIA, LEMOA, COMCASA, BECA) and TRUST (successor to iCET).
- **Cross-linkage (GS3):** energy security — India's high oil import dependence is the real source of its vulnerability to secondary tariffs.
