## MODEL ANSWER

**Introduction (context + data):**
"Sovereign AI" is a nation's capacity to build and control AI using its own compute, data, talent and models, rather than depending on foreign platforms. India's bid rests on the **IndiaAI Mission (₹10,371.92 crore, March 2024)** and gained global salience when the **India AI Impact Summit (Feb 2026)** produced the **New Delhi Declaration** (~92 endorsers) and pledges across the AI value chain. NASSCOM estimates AI could unlock **~$621 billion (≈18% of India's 2023 GDP)** — making the innovation-versus-governance balance a defining policy question.

**Key pillars of India's AI strategy:**

- **Compute as public infrastructure:** subsidised sovereign GPUs (≈34,000 at ~₹65/hour, target 100,000 by Dec 2026) democratise access for startups, academia and government.
- **Foundation models & data:** indigenous models (e.g., **Sarvam**, **BharatGen**) optimised for Indian languages, backed by curated datasets — reducing reliance on Western LLMs.
- **Skilling & application:** FutureSkills, sectoral "AI for All" deployment in health, agriculture and governance.
- **Light-touch governance:** the **India AI Governance Guidelines (Nov 2025)** rely on existing laws (IT Act, **DPDP Act 2023**) plus new coordinating bodies — an **AI Governance Group**, expert committee, and **AI Safety Institute** — rather than a single AI law.

**Challenges in balancing innovation with safety, equity and sovereignty:**

- **Hardware dependency:** sovereign compute still runs on imported (largely NVIDIA) chips — sovereignty in software, dependence in silicon.
- **Safety vs. speed:** "innovation over restraint" risks weak guardrails on deepfakes, algorithmic bias and misinformation; light-touch rules may under-deter harms.
- **Equity & jobs:** entry-level IT roles have fallen **20–25%** to automation; benefits may concentrate, widening a digital divide across language, gender and region.
- **Data & energy:** thin data-protection enforcement and the heavy water/energy footprint of data centres raise privacy and sustainability concerns.

**Conclusion (forward-looking, balanced):**
India's model — *capability-building at home, agile regulation, and global agenda-setting* — is well-suited to a fast-moving technology and to its development needs. But durable AI leadership requires pairing ambition with **domestic chip capacity, enforceable safety standards, reskilling at scale and credible data protection**, so that "AI for All" advances both growth and the public good.
