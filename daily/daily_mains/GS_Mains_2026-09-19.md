# GS Mains Answer — 2026-09-19 (GS4 — Ethics, Integrity & Aptitude)

*For: Monit · UPSC CSE 2027 · Saturday = GS4 (Ethics — short case-based question). One case to write today; the model answer is below the divider — attempt it on paper first.*

> **This week's hook:** The most instructive ethics case in Indian administration this year is not about corruption. It is about a failure in which almost nobody set out to do harm.
>
> In **September–October 2025**, children in **Chhindwara and other districts of Madhya Pradesh** began dying of acute kidney injury. The trail led to **"Coldrif"**, a paediatric cough syrup made by **Sresan Pharmaceuticals, Kancheepuram (Tamil Nadu)**. Tamil Nadu's testing found **diethylene glycol (DEG) at about 48.6%** of the formulation. DEG is not an ingredient at all — it is a toxic impurity, which pharmacopoeial standards permit only in trace amounts (a limit reported in the range of 0.01–0.1%); at 48.6% the syrup was, in effect, industrial solvent sold to children. The official toll crossed **20 children, most under five**; the **WHO issued a global medical product alert in October 2025**, and CDSCO/WHO identified two further contaminated products — **Respifresh (Rednex Pharmaceuticals)** and **ReLife (Shape Pharma)**. The firm's licence was **revoked** and the unit **sealed and permanently shut**; proprietor **G. Ranganathan was arrested**, and an **Enforcement Directorate probe reported that industrial-grade raw material had been used** and attached assets. A **prescribing doctor was also arrested**.
>
> What makes it a *GS4* case rather than a crime report is the second half of the record. **Two Tamil Nadu drug inspectors were suspended for dereliction**, and the ED found that departmental officials were in "frequent touch" with the proprietor while **the mandated annual inspections were simply not conducted**. That is not an aberration in the system; it *is* the system. The **Parliamentary Standing Committee on Chemicals & Fertilizers (chaired by Kirti Jha Azad)** recorded that **303 of 504 sanctioned drug inspector posts at CDSCO — roughly 60% — were vacant as of December 2023**, against **750+ districts**. The **Mashelkar Committee (2003)** had already recommended converting CDSCO into an *"empowered, independent and professionally managed"* **Central Drug Administration**; it remains substantially unimplemented, twenty-three years on.
>
> And the tragedy was foretold. Indian-made syrups were linked to the deaths of **over 140 children in The Gambia and Uzbekistan (2022–23)**. India's response then was to police the *export* channel — **DGFT made government-lab testing and a certificate of analysis mandatory for cough syrup exports from 1 June 2023** — while the domestic channel was left as it was. The children who died in 2025 were Indian. Since then the **Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission has amended IP 2022 to mandate DEG/EG testing of oral liquids at the finished-product stage**, **Revised Schedule M became binding on all manufacturers from 1 January 2026** (after MSME units with turnover up to ₹250 crore obtained a Form-A extension in 2025), and **CDSCO began stakeholder consultations on high-risk solvents such as propylene glycol in paediatric formulations**.
>
> That is today's case: *what does accountability mean when the harm is catastrophic, the culpability is distributed, and the pressure on you is to make it look like one bad man?* It is deliberately distinct from the last three Saturday GS4 cases — **algorithmic welfare exclusion and the Collector (12 September)**, the **DBT maternity-benefit fraud and the mentor (27 June)** and the **heatwave red-alert SDM dilemma (20 June)**. That 12 September case was about a decision no human consciously took; this one is about a duty many humans consciously skipped.

## QUESTION (Case Study) — 15 marks · 250 words

You are the **Drugs Controller** of a State with a large pharmaceutical cluster — roughly **400 small manufacturing units** supporting about **12,000 jobs** and a significant share of the State's exports.

Eleven children have died of acute kidney injury in a neighbouring State. Testing traces the cause to a paediatric syrup made by **a small unit in your cluster**. Your departmental enquiry establishes four facts:

- The unit procured **industrial-grade propylene glycol from an unlicensed trader** and performed **no finished-product test for diethylene glycol**, despite this being mandatory.
- The unit's **mandated annual inspection had not been conducted for three years**.
- The jurisdictional **drug inspector holds charge of four districts and over 400 units**. He had recorded, in writing and more than once, that inspections were falling due and that he had neither staff nor a vehicle. No one replied.
- Your **predecessor granted this unit a Revised Schedule M compliance extension on a desk review**, without any site visit. That file carries your office's seal.

Within forty-eight hours, four things reach you:

- The **Health Minister's office** wants your signed statement that this was "an isolated act of criminal adulteration by one manufacturer" — a cluster-wide audit, you are told, would "destroy 12,000 livelihoods over one criminal."
- The **industry association** offers to fund new testing equipment for your State drug laboratories as CSR, "so that this never happens again" — conveying, informally, that a cluster-wide audit would make such cooperation difficult.
- The **drug inspector**, now facing suspension and possible prosecution, comes to you in tears with copies of his unanswered warnings, and asks you to save him.
- The **parents of the dead children** have asked to meet you. Your department's counsel advises no contact whatsoever, since litigation is certain.

**Answer the following:**

**(a)** Identify the ethical issues involved in this case.
**(b)** Evaluate the options available to you and state, with justification, the course of action you would adopt.
**(c)** When an administrative failure is systemic, what does genuine accountability require — and what safeguards would you institutionalise?

**(Directive: this is a *case study* — do not write an essay on "ethics in drug regulation". The marks lie in (i) naming the *competing values* precisely rather than listing virtues; (ii) real, sequenced administrative options with honest merits and demerits, including the ones you reject and why; (iii) a decision that is lawful, defensible and implementable by a Drugs Controller *this week*; and (iv) a part (c) that refuses both scapegoating and the excuse that "the system failed". 15 marks, ~250 words.)**

## MODEL ANSWER (~250 words)

### Framing the dilemma

Eleven children are dead from a drug my department licensed and did not inspect. The pressure on me is not to *do* wrong but to *narrate* wrong — to convert a regulatory failure into one man's crime. The core conflict is between **the State's duty to protect life (Article 21; Article 47)** and **the institution's instinct to protect itself**.

### (a) Ethical issues

| Issue | Values in conflict |
|---|---|
| Suppressing the systemic cause | **Truth and transparency** v. institutional and political reputation |
| The cluster-wide audit | **Public health / precaution** v. livelihoods and economic interest |
| The CSR offer | **Objectivity and probity** v. resource pragmatism — a textbook **conflict of interest** and incipient **regulatory capture** |
| The junior inspector | **Justice** v. **compassion**; individual liability v. **the "problem of many hands"** (Dennis Thompson) — where many contribute, no one is answerable |
| My predecessor's desk-review file | **Institutional continuity of responsibility** v. personal self-protection |
| Refusing to meet the parents | **Empathy and accountability to citizens** v. legal caution |
| My own role | **Courage and constitutional loyalty** v. **obedience to the political executive** |

### (b) Options and course of action

**Option 1 — Sign the "isolated incident" statement.** *Merit:* protects jobs and the department; instant political goodwill. *Demerit:* a false certification by the one authority competent to know, which guarantees the next batch and the next funeral. Rejected.

**Option 2 — Suspend the inspector and close the file.** *Merit:* rapid, visible action; satisfies public anger. *Demerit:* punishes the only person who left a written warning; the vacancy and the desk-review approval survive untouched. Scapegoating is the *appearance* of accountability. Rejected as a *complete* response.

**Option 3 — Accept the CSR equipment.** *Merit:* my labs genuinely lack capacity. *Demerit:* accepting resources from the regulated while deciding whether to audit them destroys objectivity, whatever my intent. Rejected.

**Course of action adopted — sequenced:**

1. **Life first (0–48 hours).** Statewide recall and stop-sale of every batch of the implicated product; suspend the licence; seal the unit; alert **CDSCO, the neighbouring State and all State Drugs Controllers**; issue a vernacular public advisory through ASHA/ANM networks so illiterate households stop dosing children tonight.
2. **Contain the class of risk, not just the product.** Direct every unit using propylene glycol or glycerine to submit a **certificate of analysis** for each incoming consignment and to halt release pending **finished-product DEG/EG testing** — this is already mandatory under the amended **IP 2022**, so I am enforcing law, not inventing a burden. A **risk-based, prioritised audit** of the cluster, targeting paediatric-liquid units first, borrowing inspectors from other zones and requesting CDSCO assistance.
3. **Put the truth on file.** A written report to the Principal Secretary recording *all four* findings, including my predecessor's desk-review approval and my own department's vacancies. I will not sign the "isolated incident" statement; I record my reasons on file rather than in the press. **Dissent belongs in the file; loyalty belongs in the implementation** — if overruled by a lawful written order, I implement and my note survives.
4. **On the inspector — due process, not tears and not scapegoating.** Suspension is a procedural step, not a verdict; I place his unanswered warnings on the enquiry record and simultaneously state, in writing, that **command responsibility for an uninspected unit rests with this office**. Compassion cannot become favouritism; nor can rigour become cruelty.
5. **Decline the CSR offer in writing**, and instead move a costed proposal for lab strengthening through the budget — transparently.
6. **Meet the parents**, with counsel present. Regret is not admission. Facilitate ex-gratia and medical follow-up for surviving children. Refusing to face those one has failed is administrative cowardice dressed as legal prudence.

**Justification:** my oath runs to the Constitution, not to my department's reputation. **Kant's** test settles it — a child cannot be treated as an acceptable cost of industrial policy — and the consequentialist calculation points the same way: a false assurance buys weeks and costs the cluster its export credibility, as the **2022 Gambia and Uzbekistan alerts** showed.

### (c) What systemic failure demands

- **Accountability must be joint, not substituted.** The inspector, the licensing officer who approved on paper, and the head of department each answer for their own omission; naming the system is an explanation, never an alibi. *Nemo judex* also applies to me — I recuse from nothing, but I disclose everything.
- **Separate licensing from inspection**, and end desk-review approvals for GMP compliance — a Schedule M certificate without a site visit is a forged assurance.
- **Fill the cadre; publish the gap.** Where **60% of inspector posts lie vacant**, an inspection schedule is fiction. A **public, quarterly inspection dashboard** converts a hidden failure into a visible one — and the visible ones get funded.
- **Protect the officer who reports upward.** An inspector's written warning must generate a time-bound recorded response; institutionalised whistleblower protection is what converts a diary entry into a safeguard.
- **Statutory strict liability at the source** — vendor qualification for excipients, and mandatory DEG/EG testing of every paediatric oral liquid before release.

### Conclusion

A regulator's only real asset is credibility: spent in a day, rebuilt over years. Choosing the harder truth costs this department a season of embarrassment; choosing the comfortable fiction costs another district its children. **Sen's** distinction is exact — *niti*, the propriety of following the rulebook, is worthless if *nyaya*, justice actually realised, still ends in a small coffin.

## Value-additions to deploy (2–3, woven in — not listed)

1. **Mashelkar Committee (2003)** — recommended an *"empowered, independent and professionally managed"* **Central Drug Administration**; largely unimplemented, which is why 2022 (Gambia/Uzbekistan) repeated itself in 2025 (Chhindwara).
2. **Parliamentary Standing Committee on Chemicals & Fertilizers** — **303 of 504 CDSCO drug inspector posts vacant (≈60%), December 2023**, against 750+ districts.
3. **The "problem of many hands"** (Dennis Thompson) — the classic diagnosis of why distributed administrative failure produces no responsible official; the antidote is **joint and several accountability**, not collective absolution.
4. **Constitutional anchor** — **Article 21** (right to life, which includes safe medicines) read with **Article 47** (State's duty to improve public health); ***Vincent Panikurlangara v. Union of India* (1987)**, where the Supreme Court treated maintenance and improvement of public health, including control of injurious drugs, as an obligation of the State.
5. **Statutory teeth** — **Section 27(a), Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940**: manufacture or sale of an adulterated or spurious drug likely to cause death or grievous hurt attracts imprisonment of **not less than ten years, extendable to life**, and a fine of **not less than ₹10 lakh or three times the value of the drugs confiscated**, whichever is more — the fine being payable as compensation to the person who used the drug.
6. **Second ARC, 4th Report, *Ethics in Governance*** — on conflict of interest and the corrosive effect of regulated entities financing their regulator.
7. **Nolan's Seven Principles of Public Life** — **objectivity, accountability and openness** are precisely the three under pressure here.
8. **Gandhi's talisman** — recall the face of the weakest person you have seen, and ask whether your step will be of any use to him. Here the weakest person is four years old and cannot read a label.

## Common traps in this case

- **Writing an essay on drug regulation.** Part (c) invites systemic reform, but (a) and (b) must stay a *case*: named values, named options, a decision.
- **Total absolution of the inspector.** His overload is real and mitigating; it is not exculpatory. Examiners reward the candidate who can hold *both* — due process for him *and* accountability above him.
- **Grandstanding.** "I will resign" / "I will call a press conference" reads as theatre. The disciplined answer records dissent on file, acts within powers, and escalates lawfully.
- **Accepting the CSR offer with conditions.** Any variant of "I will accept it but remain impartial" loses the conflict-of-interest mark. Appearance of impartiality *is* impartiality in regulation.
- **Ignoring the predecessor's file.** Candidates routinely miss that the office's own seal is on the lapse. Institutional responsibility is continuous across officeholders — noticing that is a differentiator.
- **Forgetting the neighbouring State and CDSCO.** Containment beyond one's own jurisdiction is the single most administratively realistic point available, and most answers omit it.
