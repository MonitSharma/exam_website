## MODEL ANSWER

**Introduction (anchor + context):**
Dr. B.R. Ambedkar told the Constituent Assembly that the Governor is a constitutional head with "no functions which he can discharge by himself" — an apolitical link, not a parallel power centre. Yet with assent to Bills delayed for *years* in several Opposition-ruled States, the Supreme Court was drawn in twice in 2025 — the **Tamil Nadu verdict (8 April 2025)** and the **Presidential Reference opinion (20 November 2025)** — turning the Governor's office into the fault-line of Indian federalism.

### The constitutional scheme (Articles 153–167, 200–201)

- Under **Article 163**, the Governor acts on the **aid and advice** of the Council of Ministers, save in a narrow discretionary sphere.
- **Article 200** gives three options on a Bill — **assent, withhold** (return a non-money Bill with a message), or **reserve it for the President**; **Article 201** governs the President's decision on a reserved Bill.
- The Governor is a *constitutional head*, not an independent authority (**Shamsher Singh, 1974**; **Nabam Rebia, 2016** — discretion is limited and judicially reviewable).

### Why the office has become a flashpoint

- **Indefinite inaction** ("pocket veto") on Bills in Opposition-ruled States — Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Punjab, Telangana.
- **Partisan appointees:** Governors are seen as acting as the Centre's political agents — in university appointments, summoning sessions, MLA disqualifications.
- **No express timelines** in Articles 200–201, which invites delay and erodes the legislature's mandate.

### What the Court has settled — and left grey

- **TN v. Governor (Apr 2025):** the Governor has **no "pocket" or "absolute" veto**; using **Article 142**, the Bench "deemed" 10 Bills assented and prescribed timelines (1–3 months).
- **Presidential Reference (Nov 2025, CJI Gavai-led 5-judge Bench):** courts **cannot fix timelines** and there is **no "deemed assent"**; Article 142 cannot substitute the Governor/President — **BUT** the Governor **cannot stall indefinitely**, and prolonged, unexplained inaction is **judicially reviewable** (a writ can *compel action*, not dictate the decision).

### The balance — the unresolved tension

The two rulings leave a grey zone: the Governor *must act*, yet the enforceable remedy of judicial timelines is gone. Rigid timelines raise **separation-of-powers** concerns; their absence risks **reviving delay**. The deeper problem is **political (partisan appointments)**, not merely legal — so a purely judicial fix is incomplete.

### Conclusion (forward-looking)

The cure lies in **convention and reform, not litigation**: implement **Sarkaria/Punchhi** norms — appoint eminent, non-partisan persons after consulting the Chief Minister, give fixed tenure with removal only for cause, and require Governors to **record reasons and decide within a reasonable, self-disciplined period**. A revitalised **Inter-State Council** can rebuild cooperative-federal trust. Only then will the Governor be the *bridge* the Constitution intended — not a barricade.

## Value-additions (sprinkle 2–3, don't dump)

- **Case law (recent, verified):**
  - ***State of Tamil Nadu v. Governor of Tamil Nadu*** **(8 April 2025)** — Justices **J.B. Pardiwala & R. Mahadevan**; held Governor R.N. Ravi's withholding of **10 Bills** "illegal/erroneous," used **Article 142** to "deem" them assented, and laid down timelines.
  - ***In re: Assent, Withholding or Reservation of Bills by the Governor and the President*** **(Presidential Reference opinion, 20 Nov 2025)** — 5-judge Constitution Bench led by **CJI B.R. Gavai**: **no judicially-fixed timelines**, **no "deemed assent,"** Art 142 cannot replace the Governor/President; yet Governors **cannot withhold Bills indefinitely** and inaction is reviewable. (President referred **14 questions** under **Article 143(1)** on 13 May 2025.)
- **Precedents:** ***Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab* (1974)** — Governor bound by aid and advice; ***Nabam Rebia v. Deputy Speaker* (2016)** — discretion is limited; ***B.P. Singhal v. Union of India* (2010)** — the **Article 156 "doctrine of pleasure"** cannot be exercised arbitrarily, only in rare/exceptional cases for valid reasons.
- **Committees:** **Sarkaria Commission (1988)** — Governor should be an *eminent person from outside the State*, *not an active politician*, appointed **after consulting the CM**; reserved Bills disposed **within 6 months**. **Punchhi Commission (2010)** — **90-day limit** for the President on reserved Bills; provide for impeachment-like removal by the State legislature; delete the unqualified "pleasure" doctrine.
- **Quote/thinker:** **Ambedkar** — the Governor "has no functions which he can discharge by himself"; the office is meant to be a *constitutional, not political,* head — a useful framing line.
