## MODEL ANSWER (~250 words)

**Introduction**
The southwest monsoon delivers roughly three-quarters of India's annual rainfall and underpins kharif sowing, reservoirs and rural incomes. In 2026 it ended about **12.6% below normal**, with east/northeast India and the south far worse hit than the northwest and centre. The pattern shows how a global ocean signal can translate into local distress.

**Drivers: global and regional**
- **El Niño (ENSO):** Warm central-eastern Pacific waters weaken the **Walker circulation**, causing subsidence and suppressed convection over India. Most major droughts (e.g., 2009, 2014-15) coincided with El Niño, though not every El Niño year fails.
- **Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD):** A positive IOD can offset El Niño; a neutral or negative IOD removes that cushion.
- **Regional systems:** Northward shift of the monsoon trough, weak low-pressure systems in the Bay of Bengal, and **intra-seasonal oscillations (MJO)** produce long "breaks" and uneven spatial spread.
- **Other factors:** Eurasian snow cover, the Mascarene High and the Somali jet, plus warming and rising extremes (drier spells punctuated by intense bursts).

**Socio-economic consequences**
- Lower kharif output (rice, pulses, oilseeds, cotton); food-price pressure and inflation risk.
- Reservoir and groundwater stress, affecting rabi sowing and drinking water.
- Rural distress: lower incomes, indebtedness, distress migration and higher MGNREGS demand.
- Fiscal and hydropower impacts; stress on rain-fed regions and small farmers.

**Building resilience**
- Better forecasting: **Mission Mausam**, the Monsoon Mission, block-level advisories.
- Crop insurance (**PMFBY**), drought-proofing, micro-irrigation (**PMKSY - "per drop more crop"**), contingency crop plans.
- Groundwater recharge, watershed management, climate-resilient and short-duration varieties.
- Buffer stocks and calibrated trade policy; follow the **NDMA/MoA drought management manual** for early triggers.

**Conclusion**
The monsoon cannot be controlled, but its impact can be managed. Linking accurate forecasts to local, farmer-level action turns a meteorological shock into a manageable one.

### Value-additions (use 2–3 in the exam)
1. **Walker circulation / Sir Gilbert Walker:** Walker's early-20th-century work on the "Southern Oscillation" after the 1899 and 1918 failures laid the basis for ENSO linkage; India is a classic teleconnection case.
2. **IOD (Saji et al., 1999)** as the key counterweight: 1997 and 2019 saw strong positive IOD events moderating or reversing El Niño effects. Use as a "why not every El Niño is a drought" point.
3. **Institutions and schemes:** Monsoon Mission (MoES, 2012), **Mission Mausam** (2024), **PMFBY**, **PMKSY**, **MGNREGS** as a counter-cyclical safety net. *(Verify budgets/coverage before quoting numbers.)*
4. **Data point:** 2026: 759.4 mm vs 868.6 mm normal; NE India driest since 1901 (press reports of IMD).

### Common mistakes to avoid
- Treating El Niño as the *only* cause; examiners expect IOD, MJO, monsoon trough and a mention of intra-seasonal variation.
- Writing only "impacts on agriculture"; add food prices, water, fiscal and migration dimensions.
- Listing schemes with no link to forecasting-to-action; stress *last-mile* use of forecasts.
- Stating "El Niño = drought" as a rule; say "raises the probability of deficiency".
