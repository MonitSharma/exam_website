## MODEL ANSWER

**Intro (context + anchor):**
A clean electoral roll is the foundation of "free and fair elections," held by the Supreme Court to be part of the Constitution's **basic structure**. The roll is therefore where the franchise is *first* won or lost. The Election Commission's recent **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** — extended nationwide in 2026 — has sharpened a long-standing tension: the **purity** of the roll (no dead, shifted, duplicate or non-citizen entries) versus the **inclusiveness** of universal franchise. Upholding the exercise on 27 May 2026, the Court called Article 324 "not a dead letter," even as it insisted the power be used fairly.

**A. The power to revise rests on firm constitutional–statutory ground**

- **Article 324** vests "superintendence, direction and control" of the *preparation of electoral rolls* and the conduct of elections in the ECI — a plenary, reservoir power.
- **Article 326** mandates universal adult suffrage: every citizen aged 18+ is entitled to registration unless disqualified (non-citizenship, unsound mind, non-residence, corrupt/illegal practice).
- The **Representation of the People Act, 1950** and the **Registration of Electors Rules, 1960** empower the ECI to prepare and *intensively revise* rolls; keeping rolls accurate is not merely a power but a **constitutional duty**.
- A bloated or padded roll enables impersonation and erodes the "one person, one vote" principle — so periodic deep revision is legitimate and, at times, necessary.

**B. But "purity" can shade into wrongful exclusion**

- In Bihar, the draft roll omitted **~65 lakh electors**; the exercise initially **shifted the burden of proof onto the voter** and excluded common documents (Aadhaar, EPIC, ration card), squeezed into short timelines.
- The hardest-hit are the **least documented** — migrants, the poor, women, and marginalised communities — risking *silent disenfranchisement*.
- Determining *citizenship* is **not** the ECI's mandate; an over-zealous revision risks becoming a "back-door" citizenship test, conflating roll-cleaning with exclusion.

**C. Safeguards — make the process the guarantee**

- **Due process before deletion:** prior notice, opportunity to be heard, and a *speaking order* with reasons; a **presumption in favour of existing electors**.
- **Transparency:** publish booth-wise deletion lists with reasons, online and at BLO/panchayat offices (as the Court directed); accept Aadhaar/EPIC and a wide basket of documents; allow online + physical claims.
- **Adequate time and appeal:** realistic timelines and an effective, speedy appeal to ERO → DEO → courts.
- **Institutional neutrality:** an ECI seen as independent is essential to public trust in any revision (see the unresolved appointments debate below).

**Conclusion (balanced, forward-looking):**
Roll revision is a legitimate constitutional function, not an attack on democracy; its *legitimacy*, however, lives entirely in its *process*. Purity and inclusiveness are not rivals but the **twin guarantees** of a credible roll. The durable answer is a **permanent, continuously updated, technology-enabled and auditable** electoral roll built on due process — so that no eligible citizen is struck off in the name of accuracy, and no ineligible name survives in the name of inclusion.

### Value-additions (deploy 2–3, not all)

- **Case law:** *Mohinder Singh Gill v. CEC* (1978) — Article 324 is a "reservoir of power," but must be exercised in conformity with the **rule of law and natural justice**; *Association for Democratic Reforms v. ECI* (2026) — SIR upheld, "Article 324 is not a dead letter," yet subject to fairness; interim orders (Aug 2025) compelling published deletion lists + Aadhaar/EPIC.
- **Independence angle:** *Anoop Baranwal v. Union of India* (2 March 2023) prescribed a PM–LoP–CJI committee for appointing the CEC/ECs; the **CEC & Other ECs (Appointment…) Act, 2023** replaced the CJI with a Union Cabinet Minister — a live debate on the ECI's insulation from the executive.
- **Constitutional/legal hooks:** Articles **324, 325, 326**; RoPA **1950 & 1951**; Registration of Electors Rules, **1960**.
- **Committees/data:** **255th Law Commission Report (2015)** and the **Dinesh Goswami Committee (1990)** on electoral reforms; Phase-III SIR (14 May 2026) covering **~36 crore electors** — scale itself is an argument for built-in due process.
