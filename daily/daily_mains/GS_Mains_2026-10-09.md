## MODEL ANSWER

### Introduction
India invested about **US$3 billion** in Afghan reconstruction after 2001 (Zaranj–Delaram highway, Salma/"Afghan-India Friendship" Dam, Parliament building), but shut its embassy after the Taliban's August 2021 takeover. It restored a *technical team* in 2022 and **upgraded it to an embassy in October 2025**. This is *engagement without recognition*: realist interest balanced against normative and legal caution.

### Strategic rationale for engagement
1. **Avoiding strategic vacuum:** China, Russia, Iran, Central Asian states and Pakistan all engage Kabul; **Russia formally recognised the Taliban government in July 2025**. Staying away would cede influence and leave Indian assets without protection.
2. **Counter-terror assurance:** Taliban assurances that Afghan soil will not be used against India, and shared concern about **ISIS-K**, matter after Pahalgam (April 2025) and given past attacks on Indian missions in Kabul.
3. **Pakistan's strategic depth reversed:** the Kabul–Islamabad rift (TTP, the **Durand Line** dispute, border closures) lets India hedge Pakistan without direct involvement. Engagement also avoids the "encirclement" narrative being proved true.
4. **Connectivity and Central Asia:** Afghanistan is a gateway to Central Asia; the route runs via **Chabahar–Zaranj–Delaram**, since Pakistan blocks overland transit. Trade and an air-freight corridor sustain access.
5. **Soft power and the Afghan people:** wheat, medicines, vaccines, scholarships and health infrastructure keep goodwill that outlasts the regime.

### Risks and constraints
- **Legitimacy and norms:** no formal recognition; UNSC **1988 sanctions regime** listings of Taliban leaders; exclusion of women from education and work; **ICC arrest warrants (July 2025)** for the Taliban's supreme leader. Over-engagement damages India's rights-based credibility.
- **Security:** Haqqani links, safe havens for **LeT/JeM**, ISIS-K; assurances are verbal and unverifiable. Staff safety is a constant concern.
- **Pakistan's reaction:** Islamabad alleges India backs the TTP and Baloch groups; engagement raises the risk of proxy escalation on India's western front.
- **Access and sanctions:** the **Chabahar** port depends on shifting US sanctions waivers, and Iran-related sanctions risk makes the route uncertain.
- **Capability limits:** India has no land access, limited leverage and few instruments beyond aid; the Taliban can hedge between India and Pakistan too.
- **Regime fragility:** internal Taliban factions (Kandahar vs Haqqani) make commitments unreliable.

### Way forward
- **Calibrated, reversible ladder:** progress in steps, linked to verifiable counter-terror delivery; keep recognition tied to **inclusive governance and women's rights**, preferably in concert with UN, Russia, China, Central Asian and Gulf states (Moscow Format, Doha process).
- **People-first projects:** health, food, water and education delivered through UN/NGO channels.
- **Diversify access:** Chabahar plus INSTC, air corridor, Central Asian links; seek durable US/Iran arrangements.
- **Intelligence and border security upgrades**; maintain Pahalgam-style deterrence against Pakistan-based groups.

### Conclusion
India's approach is pragmatic rather than principled: engaging the *state structure* without endorsing the *regime*. Its success will depend on keeping the Afghan people, not any faction, as the constant in policy — what it can call a "civilisational" and "neighbourhood-first" interest rather than an ideological one.

### Value-additions
- **Doha Agreement (Feb 2020)** between the US and the Taliban, and the **Moscow Format** consultations; India's participation in Doha-era and Moscow-format talks marks its earlier shift.
- **Strategic depth** (Pakistan's doctrine) versus **"Neighbourhood First"** and **"Extended Neighbourhood"/Connect Central Asia (2012)** policies; **INSTC** and **Chabahar (IPGL, 2024 long-term contract)**.
- **Thinkers/concepts:** Kautilya's *mandala* theory (the neighbour's neighbour is a natural friend) — useful but state it as an analogy, not a doctrine.
- **Cross-link (GS3):** energy/connectivity and Central Asian trade; (GS4) *realism vs ethics* in foreign policy.
