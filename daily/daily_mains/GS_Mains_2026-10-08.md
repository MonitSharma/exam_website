> **This week's hook:** India is rewriting the rules of its civil nuclear sector.
>
> - **The Act.** The **SHANTI Act, 2025** (Sustainable Harnessing and Advancement of Nuclear Energy for Transforming India; assent **20 Dec 2025**) replaces the **Atomic Energy Act, 1962** and the **Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act (CLNDA), 2010**. It lets Indian companies/JVs build, own and operate nuclear plants, makes the **AERB** a statutory body, and keeps enrichment, spent-fuel management and heavy water with the Centre (Takshashila brief, Jan 2026).
> - **Liability.** Operator liability is tiered by reactor size (up to ₹3,000 crore for the largest class, up from ₹1,500 crore). The operator's statutory right of recourse against suppliers (old Sec 17(b)) and the CLNDA's Sec 46 are removed — critics call this a dilution of accountability.
> - **Draft SHANTI Rules, 2026** (Aug 2026): mandatory insurance/financial security, strict no-fault liability, an expert group to review liability limits, and uses beyond power (process heat, hydrogen, isotopes, data centres/AI). Target: **100 GW by 2047**.

## MODEL ANSWER

### Introduction
India's nuclear sector, held by the State since the Atomic Energy Act, 1962, has stayed small despite decades of effort. The **SHANTI Act, 2025** replaces that Act and the CLNDA 2010 with one law, allows Indian companies and JVs to build and operate reactors, and supports the **100 GW by 2047** aspiration. It promises firm clean power, but raises hard questions on liability and oversight.

### Body

**1. Why it matters (opportunity)**
- **Firm, low-carbon baseload:** solar and wind need a dispatchable partner; nuclear gives round-the-clock zero-emission power, supporting the net-zero-2070 pledge and 500 GW non-fossil target.
- **Capital and capability:** public entities alone (NPCIL) could not fund large build-out; private and JV capital, plus faster project execution, can widen the base.
- **Beyond electricity:** process heat for hard-to-abate industry, hydrogen, medical isotopes; Small Modular Reactors for captive use (the Union Budget 2025 announced a Nuclear Energy Mission for SMR R&D).
- **Unblocking supply chains:** clearer liability rules ease foreign-vendor hesitation that stalled projects like Jaitapur.

**2. Concerns (challenges)**
- **Liability dilution:** removal of operator recourse against suppliers (old Sec 17(b)) and of Sec 46 (remedies under other laws) narrows victims' routes; the cap remains modest against the scale of accidents like Fukushima.
- **Safety culture and regulator independence:** the AERB is now statutory, a gain, but it still needs financial, technical and functional autonomy from the promoter departments (the CAG has earlier flagged this conflict).
- **Economics:** high capital costs, long gestation, land and public-acceptance issues (Kudankulam, Jaitapur protests); private players may seek tariff support.
- **Waste and security:** spent-fuel management, decommissioning and fuel-supply security remain with the State; capacity to oversee many operators is untested.
- **Transparency:** Draft Rules need public consultation; the expert group on liability limits should be independent.

**3. Reconciling the two (way forward)**
- Notify rules with robust insurance pools, a periodically revised liability cap, and a strengthened Nuclear Liability Fund.
- Give the AERB statutory autonomy in budget and appointments; add independent safety audits, public disclosure and local-community consent.
- Phase the build-out: fleet-mode PHWRs, SMRs (Bharat Small Reactors) and thorium-based third stage, with domestic manufacturing.
- Back with uranium diversification and international fuel agreements.

### Conclusion
SHANTI can turn nuclear from a niche into a pillar of India's clean-energy mix, but only if opening up is matched by accountability. A credible regulator, adequate victim compensation and transparency are not costs on the programme — they are what make it politically and financially bankable. Safety, not speed, will decide success.

### Value-additions
- **Three-stage nuclear programme (Homi Bhabha):** PHWR → Fast Breeder → Thorium; the 500 MWe PFBR at Kalpakkam is the second-stage milestone.
- **Law/convention:** Convention on Supplementary Compensation for Nuclear Damage (CSC) — the Takshashila brief links the supplier-recourse change to alignment with it; also the polluter-pays and absolute-liability principle of **M.C. Mehta v. Union of India (1987)** as a benchmark for accountability.
- **Targets/scheme:** 100 GW by 2047; Budget 2025 Nuclear Energy Mission for SMRs; Bharat Small Reactors.
