## QUESTION (Case Study) — 15 marks · 250 words

You are the **Municipal Commissioner** of a mid-sized city. After a High Court direction to act against unauthorised construction, you must clear **~600 illegal structures** in *Nehru Basti*, an old settlement on a drain-side municipal plot. Facts:

- Most residents are daily-wage workers who bought plots from a local broker; many have **voter ID, ration cards and electricity connections** — some of which were issued by municipal staff who looked the other way for bribes.
- A structural audit finds **three buildings at risk of collapse**. Board exams of about 150 children begin in five months.
- The **ruling-party MLA** calls you: demolish the *rival-community market* nearby first, but "go slow" in Nehru Basti, which is his vote bank. A senior colleague hints that "cooperating" will help your next posting.
- An NGO threatens to approach the court and media alleging **selective demolition**; a builder lobby wants the cleared land.

**(a)** Identify the ethical issues and the stakeholders. **(b)** Evaluate the options available to you. **(c)** What course of action would you adopt, and why?

*Directive: Give a structured answer — issues → options (with merits/demerits) → decision with safeguards. Time: ~12 minutes.*

## MODEL ANSWER

### (a) Ethical issues and stakeholders

**Issues**
- **Rule of law vs compassion:** a judicial direction must be obeyed, yet demolition without due process or rehabilitation violates the right to shelter/livelihood under **Article 21**.
- **Impartiality vs political pressure:** the MLA's demand to target one community's market first is *selective enforcement* — a breach of **Article 14** and of non-partisanship, a core civil-service value.
- **Integrity vs career incentives:** a posting-linked inducement is a conflict between personal interest and public interest.
- **Institutional corruption:** residents are partly victims of official complicity; punishing only the poor while the guilty officials escape is unjust.
- **Safety:** three unsafe buildings create a duty to act without delay (**do no harm / duty of care**).
- **Distributive justice and children's welfare:** the poorest bear the cost; the exam year raises the stakes.

**Stakeholders:** residents (esp. children, women), the municipality and its staff, the MLA/political executive, the High Court, NGO and media, the builder lobby, the wider public (rule-of-law, public land), and myself as a public servant.

### (b) Options

| Option | Merit | Demerit |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Demolish all immediately, as per court order | Quick compliance; legally "safe" | Inhumane, ignores due process and rehabilitation; likely to face stay and unrest |
| 2. Yield to the MLA — sequence as he asks | Political peace; career safety | Unethical, illegal (arbitrary classification), invites contempt/litigation and erodes trust |
| 3. Delay / seek more time citing the situation | Avoids conflict | Contempt risk; leaves collapse risk and encourages further encroachment |
| 4. **Rule-based, phased and humane enforcement** | Legal, impartial, safe, defensible | Needs effort, coordination and political courage |

### (c) Course of action (Option 4)

1. **Immediate safety:** evacuate and secure the three unsafe buildings first, with temporary shelter — a ground that is neutral and defensible to everyone.
2. **Due process:** follow the Supreme Court's demolition guidelines — show-cause notice, time to respond, personal hearing, a reasoned final order, and time to vacate — and document everything. Verify entitlements (e.g. rehabilitation eligibility) through a transparent survey.
3. **Objective sequencing:** one published, criteria-based list (safety risk, court priority, date of construction) applied *uniformly* — including the rival market and any other unauthorised structure, whoever the beneficiary.
4. **Rehabilitation:** coordinate with the housing board/state for alternative housing or transit camps under existing schemes; schedule non-urgent demolition **after** the board exams or ensure schooling continuity.
5. **Handle the pressure:** respond to the MLA politely and in writing — "the sequence is court-monitored and rule-based"; brief the Chief Secretary/Divisional Commissioner; report any improper inducement through proper channels. A transfer is a risk I accept; integrity cannot be conditional.
6. **Accountability:** initiate departmental inquiry against staff who issued papers illegally; recover land from brokers and the builder lobby through lawful process; inform the High Court of progress by an affidavit.
7. **Transparency:** public notice, ward-level meetings, and engagement with the NGO — they become a partner in monitoring, not an adversary.

**Why:** this reconciles the *ethic of responsibility* (consequences for the poor) with the *ethic of conviction* (duty under law), satisfying the **"touchstone of public interest"** test — the action I would be comfortable defending in court and before my conscience.

### Value-additions
- **Supreme Court (13 Nov 2024), *In Re: Directions in the matter of demolition of structures*** — nationwide guidelines: prior show-cause notice (about 15 days), hearing, reasoned order, time to vacate; officials liable for violations. *(Verify exact details in your notes before quoting.)*
- ***Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation* (1985)** — right to livelihood is part of Article 21; eviction must follow a just procedure. Also *Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation v. Nawab Khan Gulab Khan* (1997) on rehabilitation of pavement dwellers (use with care).
- **Nolan Committee's Seven Principles of Public Life** — selflessness, integrity, objectivity, accountability, openness, honesty, leadership; the **2nd ARC (4th report, *Ethics in Governance*)** on codes of ethics and political neutrality.
- **Thinkers:** Gandhi's *talisman* (think of the poorest person before deciding); Max Weber's *ethic of responsibility*.

**Conclusion line:** "Law without compassion is tyranny; compassion without law is anarchy — the administrator's task is to enforce impartially, humanely and transparently."
