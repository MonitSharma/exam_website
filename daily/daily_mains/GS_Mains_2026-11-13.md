## MODEL ANSWER (~250 words)

**Introduction.** The India–EU FTA, concluded on 27 January 2026 after nearly two decades, links economies with about a quarter of world GDP and roughly €190 bn of annual trade in goods and services. With the US framework unsigned and tariff-prone, it is India's most significant hedge in a multipolar trade order.

**Strategic autonomy and diversification — the case for**
- **Hedging:** reduces over-dependence on one market; the EU is a large, rules-based, non-coercive buyer.
- **Market access:** 99%+ of Indian exports (by value) get preferential entry; labour-intensive textiles, leather, jewellery, gems gain competitiveness against Vietnam and Bangladesh.
- **Services and mobility:** access across 144 EU subsectors and a mobility framework help IT, education and professional services.
- **Supply chains:** complements China-plus-one, green hydrogen and clean-tech cooperation (EIB €2 bn climate-infrastructure commitment).
- **Reform lock-in:** tariff cuts on autos, machinery and wine discipline India's high-tariff regime and attract FDI.

**Limits and challenges**
- **CBAM:** a carbon border levy of an estimated $2–4 bn a year hits steel, aluminium and cement; the deal gives only a forward-looking MFN assurance, not an exemption.
- **Non-tariff barriers:** EU standards on deforestation, sustainability due diligence and food safety can neutralise tariff cuts, especially for MSMEs.
- **Domestic sensitivities:** the auto quota and wine/olive-oil cuts expose domestic industry; dairy and rice are excluded — a pointer to agriculture's political constraints.
- **Implementation lag:** signing in December 2026, but entry into force only around mid-2027, requiring ratification by European Parliament and Council.
- **Capacity:** gains depend on logistics, quality infrastructure and ease of doing business at home.

**Conclusion.** The FTA is a strategic hedge, not a panacea. India should pair it with CBAM negotiation, an MSME compliance support mechanism, a domestic carbon market and safeguards for sensitive sectors, so that autonomy is built on competitiveness rather than on market access alone.

### Value-additions (use 2–3 in the exam)
- **Data:** India–EU merchandise trade ~US$136.5 bn in FY25 (exports ~US$75.9 bn), per IBEF.
- **Regulatory instrument:** EU **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)** — a framework for the 'carbon-leakage' debate; also link to *common but differentiated responsibilities* (CBDR-RC) at the UNFCCC.
- **Institutional anchors (static; verify):** the India–EU **Trade and Technology Council (TTC), launched 2022**, and the **India–EU Connectivity Partnership (2021)**; **IMEC** (announced at the G20 New Delhi summit, 2023) as the connectivity extension.
- **Concept:** *Strategic autonomy / multi-alignment* — diversify dependencies without joining blocs.
