## 1. Ministry of Education: **Six-month Certificate (Bridge) Course in Primary Teacher Education launched — giving effect to the Supreme Court's *Devesh Sharma* direction**

**What it is.** Union Education Minister **Shri Pralhad Joshi** launched a **six-month Certificate (Bridge) Course in Primary Teacher Education (PTE)** for **in-service primary teachers who hold a B.Ed. degree** but not a D.El.Ed. The course has been designed as per the framework of the **National Council for Teacher Education (NCTE)** and will be delivered through the **National Institute of Open Schooling (NIOS)** in **Open and Distance Learning (ODL)** mode.

**Why it matters.** This is the administrative answer to a judicial problem, and that is exactly why it is examinable. In **Devesh Sharma v. Union of India (Civil Appeal No. 5068 of 2023, judgment dated 8 April 2024)**, the Supreme Court held that a B.Ed.-qualified person cannot teach at the primary stage unless a bridge course is completed, and **empowered NCTE to devise one**. The launch shows the chain **judicial direction → regulator (NCTE) framework → delivery institution (NIOS) → teacher certification**, and it protects the employment of teachers already appointed while upholding the child's right to age-appropriate pedagogy under the **RTE Act, 2009** (which makes NCTE the academic authority for laying down minimum teacher qualifications).

**Key facts and figures**

- **Duration:** six months. **Nomenclature:** Certificate (Bridge) Course in **Primary Teacher Education (PTE)**.
- **Designed by:** **NCTE**. **Implemented by:** **NIOS**. **Mode:** Open and Distance Learning.
- **Eligibility:** in-service primary teachers with a **B.Ed.**, appointed between **28 June 2018 and 11 August 2023**.
- **Course structure:** self-learning material plus **at least 10 days of online contact classes** and **at least 20 days of school-based experience**.
- **Medium:** instruction in **Hindi and English**; the public theory examination may be written in **any of the 22 scheduled languages** (Eighth Schedule).
- **Pedagogic focus:** child-centred, inclusive and developmentally appropriate practice at the primary stage, aligned to **NEP 2020** and the foundational-stage emphasis of **NCF-SE 2023**.
- **Legal anchor:** Supreme Court judgment of **8 April 2024** in *Devesh Sharma v. Union of India*.

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (education policy, statutory regulators, implementation of judicial directions) + GS2 Polity (RTE Act, Article 21A).

## 2. Ministry of Jal Shakti (DDWS) & Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs: **Swachhata Hi Seva 2026 launched — month-long national campaign, 17 September to 17 October**

**What it is.** The eighth edition of **Swachhata Hi Seva (SHS) 2026** was launched on **17 September 2026** and runs to **17 October 2026**. It is organised jointly by the **Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation (Ministry of Jal Shakti)** for rural areas and the **Department of Urban Development / MoHUA** for urban areas, and was reviewed with States and UTs by Union Minister **Shri Manohar Lal**. Theme for 2026: **"Swachhata Mein Sahbhag; Swachh Bharat, Viksit Bharat."**

**Why it matters.** SHS is the annual mass-mobilisation arm of the Swachh Bharat Mission and the clearest live example of **Jan Bhagidari as a delivery instrument** rather than a slogan. Two elements are new-generation and worth noting: the explicit **Swachh Paathshala** pillar puts schoolchildren at the centre of behaviour change, and the **Swachhata se Samriddhi** pillar formally routes the campaign into **circular-economy and waste-to-wealth** territory — connecting SBM to GS3 environment questions rather than leaving it purely in GS2 governance.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Dates:** 17 September – 17 October 2026 (culminating around **Gandhi Jayanti**, 2 October).
- **Theme:** "Swachhata Mein Sahbhag; Swachh Bharat, Viksit Bharat."
- **Five pillars:** (i) transformation of **Cleanliness Target Units (CTUs)** and high-footfall public places; (ii) **Swachh Paathshala — Yuva for Swachhata**; (iii) **SafaiMitra Suraksha Evam Samman Shivirs**; (iv) **Jan Bhagidari for Swachhata**; (v) **Swachhata se Samriddhi** (circular economy, waste-to-wealth).
- **Signature event:** nationwide collective shramdaan **"Ek Din, Ek Ghanta, Ek Saath"** on **1 October 2026**.
- **Citizen call:** clean one's own home on 17 September and light a **diya** in the evening — clean home, healthy family, sustained cleanliness.
- Sits alongside the **Swachh Seva Aankalan** Gram Panchayat self-assessment tool launched on 16 September (covered in yesterday's briefing) — assessment and mobilisation are the two halves of SBM-G 2.0's current push.

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (Jan Bhagidari, centrally sponsored scheme delivery) + GS3 Environment (solid waste management, circular economy).

## 3. Prime Minister's Office / Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology: **SEMICON India 2026 inaugurated; PIB backgrounder on the Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme — 106 projects, ₹69,548 crore approved investment** *(UPDATE — SEMICON was flagged as upcoming on 14 September; what is new is the inauguration, the ecosystem numbers and the ECMS backgrounder)*

**What it is.** The **Prime Minister inaugurated the fifth edition of SEMICON India 2026** at **Yashobhoomi (IICC), Dwarka, New Delhi**, on **17 September**, themed **"Silicon to Systems: Building the Ecosystem"** (17–19 September). Alongside, the PIB issued a backgrounder on the **Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme (ECMS)**, the component-layer complement to the Semicon India Programme.

**Why it matters.** The policy story has shifted from "can India fabricate chips" to "can India build the **whole stack**" — components, packaging, equipment, materials, talent. ECMS is the least glamorous but most examinable piece: passive and sub-assembly components are where India's electronics import bill actually sits, and the scheme is designed to attack **value-addition depth**, not headline assembly volume. Note also **Pax Silica**, which India joined at the **India AI Impact Summit 2026** — a supply-chain security grouping that is likely prelims material.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme (ECMS)**

- **Ministry:** Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY).
- **Objective:** build domestic capacity in **electronic components, sub-assemblies and capital equipment**, deepen value addition, and integrate Indian firms into global electronics supply chains — moving beyond final-product assembly.
- **Key features (4):** (i) support across **passive components, sub-assemblies and capital goods** for electronics manufacturing; (ii) **turnover- and employment-linked incentives**; (iii) coverage of **30 electronic-domain products**; (iv) designed to complement the **Semicon India Programme** (chips) and **PLI** (finished goods) rather than duplicate them.
- **Coverage/progress:** **106 projects approved** across **15 States**, approved investment **₹69,548 crore** — above the original **₹59,350 crore** target; projected production value **₹5,34,101 crore**; **74,628 direct jobs** expected plus roughly **2.5 lakh indirect**; **38 plants already manufacturing**, **16 more** at advanced construction/installation stage.
- **Significance:** shifts the India electronics story from assembly to component depth, the single biggest determinant of net import dependence.

**Semicon India context (prelims facts)**

- **Semicon India Programme** launched **2021**; **Semicon 1.0** outlay **₹76,000 crore**; **Semicon 2.0** approved **July 2026**, outlay **₹1,27,500 crore** — covering chip design, equipment/materials, fabrication, packaging, R&D and talent.
- **12 semiconductor projects** approved across **6 States**; investment commitments exceeding **₹1.64 lakh crore**; **3 facilities** in commercial production.
- **SEMICON India 2026 scale:** 600+ exhibitors, ~300 international companies, 15,000 sq m exhibition area, **6 country pavilions and 12 State pavilions**.
- **Pax Silica:** coalition India formally joined at the **India AI Impact Summit 2026**, aimed at securing the full "silicon stack" — critical minerals → fabrication → AI deployment infrastructure.
- **Persisting gap:** no domestic advanced-node fab comparable to leading foundries; continued dependence on imported photolithography equipment and specialty chemicals.

**Notebook:** GS3 Science & Technology (semiconductors, indigenisation) + GS3 Economy (industrial policy, supply-chain resilience).

## 4. Ministry of Defence / Indian Army: **22nd Exercise YUDH ABHYAS begins — India–US joint exercise at Auli and Mahajan Field Firing Range**

**What it is.** The **22nd edition of Exercise YUDH ABHYAS**, the annual India–United States joint military exercise, commenced at **Auli (Uttarakhand)** and the **Mahajan Field Firing Range (Rajasthan)**, with roughly **600 soldiers from each side**. The 2026 edition foregrounds **mountain warfare**, **drones and autonomous systems** for surveillance and reconnaissance, and joint employment of **Integrated Battle Groups (IBGs)** in high-altitude terrain.

**Why it matters.** Two-location design is the interesting bit: **Auli** is a high-altitude acclimatisation and mountain-warfare setting, **Mahajan** is desert/plains armoured and mechanised terrain — so the exercise tests **two very different operational environments in one iteration**. The drone and autonomous-systems emphasis reflects lessons from recent conflicts being absorbed into Indian doctrine. For mains, YUDH ABHYAS is the best single hook for the **India–US Major Defence Partnership** and interoperability arguments.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Edition:** 22nd. **Partner:** United States. **Service:** **Army** (bilateral).
- **Venues:** **Auli, Uttarakhand** and **Mahajan Field Firing Range, Rajasthan**. **Strength:** ~600 troops per side.
- **Focus:** mountain warfare, interoperability, **Integrated Battle Groups**, drones/autonomous systems, technology infusion.
- **Strategic frame:** India–US **Major Defence Partnership**; complements Quad and Indo-Pacific cooperation.
- **India–US exercise map (high-yield prelims table):** **Yudh Abhyas** — Army; **Vajra Prahar** — Special Forces; **Malabar** — Navy (Quad: India, US, Japan, Australia); **Cope India** — Air Force; **Tiger Triumph** — tri-services, amphibious/HADR.

**Notebook:** GS3 Internal Security / Defence (military exercises, defence diplomacy) + GS2 IR (India–US relations, Indo-Pacific).

## 5. Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports: **Ten-member Task Force constituted to assess the revival of Formula 1 in India; target of hosting a Grand Prix in 2028**

**What it is.** Union Minister **Shri Mansukh Mandaviya** announced a **10-member Task Force** to examine the challenges and feasibility of **reviving Formula 1 racing in India** and to prepare a roadmap for the **sustainable growth of motorsports**. It is headed by **Shri Yugal Kishore Joshi**, Programme Director and Adviser, **NITI Aayog**, and will explore hosting an **F1 Grand Prix in 2028**.

**Why it matters.** Beyond sport, this is a clean case study in **why a marquee event failed the first time**: the Indian Grand Prix (Buddh International Circuit, 2011–2013) collapsed largely on **taxation classification** — F1 was treated as entertainment rather than sport, attracting entertainment tax and customs complications. The Task Force's explicit mandate to examine taxation and **PPP models** makes it usable for answers on **sports economy, event-led infrastructure and regulatory predictability**. It also sits against the backdrop of India's 2036 Olympic bid ambitions.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Constituted by:** Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports (MYAS). **Size:** 10 members.
- **Chair:** **Yugal Kishore Joshi**, Programme Director & Adviser, **NITI Aayog**; includes the **Joint Secretary, Department of Sports**.
- **Representation:** Department of Commerce, Ministry of Tourism, **CBIC**, **CBDT**, Governments of **Uttar Pradesh** and **Maharashtra**, **Buddh International Circuit (BIC)**, **Madras International Circuit (MIC)**, **FMSCI** (Federation of Motor Sports Clubs of India) and an FMSCI-nominated technical expert.
- **Terms of reference:** examine **taxation-related challenges** behind F1's earlier exit; explore **PPP models**; assess economic, tourism, investment and international benefits; identify infrastructure and operational requirements.
- **Target:** feasibility of an **F1 Grand Prix in India in 2028**. Historical note: India hosted the **Indian Grand Prix at BIC, Greater Noida, in 2011, 2012 and 2013**.

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (inter-ministerial committees, sports administration) + GS3 Economy (event economy, PPP, taxation policy).

## 6. Ministry of Communications (Department of Posts): **Commemorative postage stamp released in honour of Sant Malukdas Ji**

**What it is.** Union Minister for Communications **Shri Jyotiraditya Scindia** released a **commemorative postage stamp** issued by the **Department of Posts** in honour of **Sant Malukdas Ji**, at a **Sant Samagam** held in the saint's memory.

**Why it matters.** Commemorative stamps are a low-effort, high-return prelims source — they force you to place a figure in the right movement and region. **Malukdas (c. 1574–1682)** was a **Nirguna Bhakti** poet-saint associated with **Kara (Kada), near Prayagraj**, in the Kabir–Dadu stream of north Indian sant poetry: devotion without ritual, vernacular expression, and a strong strand of trust in divine providence. He is the natural companion entry to Kabir, Dadu Dayal and Raidas in the GS1 Bhakti-movement table.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Issued by:** Department of Posts, Ministry of Communications. **Released at:** Sant Samagam in the saint's memory.
- **Sant Malukdas:** devotional poet-saint of the **Bhakti movement**, from **Prayagraj (Kara/Kada)**, Uttar Pradesh.
- **Best-known couplet:** *"Ajgar kare na chakri, panchhi kare na kaam / Das Maluka kah gaye, sabke data Ram"* — a statement on contentment and divine providence.
- **Works attributed:** *Gyan Bodh*, *Ratan Khan*, *Sukh Sagar*, *Gyan Parochi*, *Maluk Shatak* (101 couplets) and *Bhakt Bacchawali*.
- **Framing used:** "Vikas bhi, Virasat bhi" — heritage preservation alongside development, linked to the **Viksit Bharat 2047** vision.

**Notebook:** GS1 Art & Culture (Bhakti movement, Nirguna tradition, medieval sant literature).

## Answers

**Q1 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct. The six-month Certificate (Bridge) Course in Primary Teacher Education was designed as per the **NCTE** framework and is delivered by **NIOS** in Open and Distance Learning mode. It is meant for **in-service primary teachers holding a B.Ed.**, specifically those appointed between **28 June 2018 and 11 August 2023**. It operationalises the Supreme Court's judgment of **8 April 2024** in *Devesh Sharma v. Union of India* (Civil Appeal No. 5068 of 2023), which empowered NCTE to devise a bridge course for primary teachers lacking a D.El.Ed.

**Q2 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** Statement 1 is wrong — ECMS is administered by the **Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY)**, not Commerce and Industry; this ministry-swap is the standard trap. Statement 2 is correct: ECMS deliberately targets the **component, sub-assembly and capital-goods layer**, complementing the Semicon India Programme (chips) and PLI (finished goods). Statement 3 is correct: approved investment stands at **₹69,548 crore against an original target of ₹59,350 crore**, across **106 projects in 15 States**.

**Q3 — (b) United States — Army.** **YUDH ABHYAS** is the annual **India–US Army** exercise; the 22nd edition began at **Auli (Uttarakhand)** and **Mahajan Field Firing Range (Rajasthan)** with about 600 troops from each side. For the rest of the family: **Vajra Prahar** is India–US Special Forces, **Cope India** is India–US Air Force, **Tiger Triumph** is India–US tri-services amphibious/HADR, and **Malabar** is the naval exercise involving India, the US, Japan and Australia.
