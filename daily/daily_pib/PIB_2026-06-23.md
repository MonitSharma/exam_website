**Key facts to remember (mission-mode programmes):**

## 2. India becomes the world's No. 1 ship-recycling nation (2025)

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Ports, Shipping & Waterways (MoPSW), citing **UNCTAD** | **Date:** 22 June 2026 | **Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure) / Environment (circular economy)

Per UNCTAD, India's share of global ship recycling rose to **35.4% in 2025** (from 30.1% in 2024), making it the world's top ship-recycling nation and achieving the **Maritime India Vision (MIV) 2030** leadership target **five years early**. Why it matters: a rare case where environmental compliance and economic competitiveness reinforce each other.

**Key facts to remember:**

- India recycled **2.99 million GT in 2025** (up ~60% from 1.86 mn GT in 2024); **115 yards** made **Hong Kong Convention (HKC)**-compliant with ₹53.5 cr govt assistance.
- **Hong Kong International Convention (HKC):** an **IMO** treaty for safe & environmentally sound ship recycling; **India ratified it in 2019**. Domestic law: **Recycling of Ships Act, 2019**.
- **Alang (Gujarat):** world's largest ship-breaking yard; planned expansion targets **~9 million LDT** capacity (nearly double).
- India is pursuing inclusion in the **EU Ship Recycling Regulation (EUSRR)** approved list (key market-access lever for EU-flagged vessels).

### Scheme-in-focus: Ship-breaking Credit Note Scheme

- **Name / Ministry:** Ship-breaking Credit Note Scheme — Ministry of Ports, Shipping & Waterways.
- **Objective:** Incentivise environmentally sound ship recycling **and** domestic shipbuilding through a single linked lever.
- **Key features:** ship owners get a **credit note = 40% of scrap value** of a recycled ship; usable for **up to 5%** of the cost of a **new vessel built at an Indian shipyard**; works alongside HKC-compliance grants for yard modernisation.
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** ship owners, recyclers and Indian shipyards (esp. Alang–Sosiya, Gujarat).
- **Significance:** converts a recycling subsidy into shipbuilding demand — twin gains for the maritime economy.

## 5. Khurasani Imli (Mandu, Madhya Pradesh) gets a GI tag

**Issuing body:** Geographical Indications Registry (CGPDTM, DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce & Industry) | **Date:** 23 June 2026 | **Notebook:** GS1 — Art & Culture / GS3 — Economy (IPR)

The **Khurasani Imli** of **Mandu (Dhar district, MP)** received a **Geographical Indication (GI)** tag. Why it matters: a distinctive new GI with an unusual biology/heritage hook — useful prelims trivia and a tribal/regional-livelihood angle.

**Key facts to remember:**

- Despite its name ("foreign tamarind"), Khurasani Imli is the fruit of the **African Baobab tree (*Adansonia digitata*)** — **not** the tamarind plant; Mandu hosts **India's largest natural cluster of Baobab trees** (believed introduced in the Mughal/medieval era).
- Known for sweet-sour taste, **high Vitamin C** and traditional medicinal use.
- GI governed by the **Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999**; administered by **CGPDTM under DPIIT**; **GI Registry at Chennai**; validity 10 years (renewable). India's first GI: **Darjeeling Tea**.

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** NQM was approved in **April 2023** with an outlay of **₹6,003.65 crore** and has the four stated pillars, so statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong — NQM is steered by the **Department of Science & Technology (DST)**, not the MEA (the MEA runs the NEST Division for tech diplomacy).

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 3 only.** The HKC is an **IMO** treaty for safe and environmentally sound ship recycling, and India's domestic law is the **Recycling of Ships Act, 2019** — statements 1 and 3 correct. Statement 2 is wrong: **India ratified the HKC in 2019**.

**Q3 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The meeting was chaired by NSA **Ajit Doval** under India's 2026 BRICS Presidency, and the expanded **11-member** BRICS includes Indonesia, Iran, Egypt, Ethiopia, Saudi Arabia and the UAE — statements 1 and 2 correct. Statement 3 is wrong: the theme was **non-traditional security challenges** (cyber, AI, information warfare, terrorism), not conventional/territorial threats.
