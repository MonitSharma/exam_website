## 1. Ministry of Defence: **DAC accords AoN to acquisitions worth about ₹1,10,000 crore; ~98% from Indian industry**

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Defence — Defence Acquisition Council (DAC), chaired by Raksha Mantri Shri Rajnath Singh.

**What it is.** On 7 September 2026 the DAC accorded **Acceptance of Necessity (AoN)** — the in-principle administrative approval that begins a capital procurement — to proposals of the three Services at an estimated cost of **about ₹1.10 lakh crore**. Approximately **98% of the value is earmarked for procurement from Indian industry**, making this one of the largest single-sitting AoN packages routed almost entirely through the domestic defence industrial base.

**Why it matters.** AoN is the *first* gate in the Defence Acquisition Procedure (DAP) 2020 pipeline, not a contract — a distinction UPSC has tested. The composition of the package is the story: it is weighted towards **mobility, engineering support, survivability and electronic warfare** rather than headline platforms, and towards categories that Indian firms and DRDO/DPSUs can actually deliver.

**Key facts — what was cleared:**

- **Indian Army:** Chemical, Biological, Radiological and Nuclear (**CBRN**) Recce Vehicles — for detecting, identifying, monitoring and marking contaminated areas; **High Mobility Vehicles (HMV)**; **Self-Propelled Mechanical Mine Layers (MML)** for fast minelaying in difficult terrain; **Advanced Light Helicopters (ALH)** (for the Army and the IAF); **Trawl Tanks** and the **Sarvatra Bridge System**, both for composite crossings for fighting formations.
- **Indian Navy:** **Arudhra Radars**, to replace existing Air Route Surveillance Radars at Naval Air Stations; and **Design & Development plus subsequent procurement of Marine Gas Turbines (MGT)** — a warship propulsion system, explicitly aimed at cutting dependence on foreign vendors.
- **Indian Air Force:** proposals to enhance fighter, transport and helicopter war-fighting capability; a **Ground-Based Multi-Purpose Jammer (GBMPJ)** for jamming adversary radars; and the **Defence Forces Secure Access Card (DEFSAC)** system, replacing paper identity cards/passes/permits with interoperable **RFID-based smart cards**.

**Static hooks worth revising:** the DAC was constituted in **2001** following the recommendations of the Group of Ministers set up after the Kargil Review Committee; it is the apex body for capital acquisition approvals in the MoD and is chaired by the Defence Minister. Marine gas turbines and CBRN reconnaissance are both **strategic-import-dependency** areas — link this to the *Positive Indigenisation Lists* and the **iDEX/SRIJAN** ecosystem.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security & Defence (defence indigenisation); GS3 Economy (defence manufacturing, Atmanirbhar Bharat).

## 2. Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change: **Swachh Vayu Diwas 2026 — 5th Swachh Vayu Sarvekshan awards; NCAP Compendium of Best Management Practices released**

**Ministry/Body:** MoEFCC, with the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) and the Commission for Air Quality Management (CAQM). Chief Guest: **Justice Prakash Shrivastava, Chairperson, National Green Tribunal**. Also present: Secretary (EFCC) Shri Tanmay Kumar; Chairperson (CAQM) Shri Rajesh Verma; Chairperson (CPCB) Shri Amandeep Garg; DG (Forest) & Special Secretary Shri Sushil Kumar Awasthi.

**What it is.** MoEFCC observed **Swachh Vayu Diwas 2026** on **7 September**, the UN-designated **International Day of Clean Air for Blue Skies**, conferring the **5th Swachh Vayu Sarvekshan** awards on the best-performing cities and wards under the **National Clean Air Programme (NCAP)** and releasing a **Compendium of Best Management Practices under NCAP**.

**Why it matters.** This is the single most quotable annual data release on NCAP performance, and it converts a scheme into examinable numbers. It also usefully reframes air pollution as a **local-body implementation problem** — the NGT Chairperson's line was that urban and rural local bodies are the "frontline institutions", and that pollution "does not know political boundaries" — which is exactly the airshed-versus-city-boundary argument that Mains answers need.

**Key facts and figures:**

- **NCAP coverage:** **130 cities across 24 States/UTs** (the "non-attainment"/million-plus city set).
- **Funding:** a **performance-linked grant of ₹16,425 crore** has been provided to the 130 NCAP cities as critical-gap funding.
- **PM10 outcomes in 2025-26 against the FY 2017-18 baseline:**
  - **108 of 130 cities** showed improvement in PM10 levels;
  - **72 cities** achieved reductions of **more than 20%**;
  - **26 cities** recorded reductions **exceeding 40%**;
  - **14 cities** met the **National Ambient Air Quality Standards (NAAQS)**.
- **PRANA portal** tracks air-quality-management parameters for all 130 NCAP cities.
- **Swachh Vayu Sarvekshan framework** was released in **2022-23**. Cities are ranked in **three population groups** — below 3 lakh; 3–10 lakh; and above 10 lakh — on road dust, solid waste management, vehicular emissions, industrial pollution, C&D waste management, other emissions (DG sets, commercial establishments) and IEC activities.
- **Evaluation chain (a classic "arrange the sequence" MCQ):** self-assessment by cities → approval by the **State-level Monitoring Committee** → evaluation by **CPCB** → field verification by **Independent Committees** → selection of the 10 best-performing cities.
- **Compendium sectors:** road dust control, vehicular pollution, industrial pollution, solid and C&D waste management, control of open burning, greening, use of technology, and public outreach.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Environment (air pollution, NCAP, NAAQS, CPCB/CAQM); GS2 Governance (urban local bodies as implementing agencies, performance-linked grants).

## 3. Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare: **Action plan to strengthen Krishi Vigyan Kendras — 300 KVKs to be upgraded by March 2027, Incubation-cum-Skill Centres in all KVKs by December 2028**

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare. Meeting chaired by Union Minister **Shri Shivraj Singh Chouhan**, with MoS **Shri Bhagirath Choudhary**, and **Dr. Mangi Lal Jat**, Secretary (DARE) and Director General, ICAR.

**What it is.** A review of the **Action Plan for strengthening KVKs through enhanced State participation**, taking forward the KVK-related recommendations of the **5th Chief Secretaries' Conference 2025**. The core proposition is that KVKs are Central-ICAR institutions but cannot be scaled without States putting in money, land, staff and institutional support through their **State Agricultural Universities (SAUs)**.

**Why it matters.** KVKs are the last-mile extension arm of Indian agricultural research — the interface where lab-to-land actually happens. The two hard targets announced give this item a shelf life well beyond today.

**Key facts:**

- **Target 1:** upgrade infrastructure and logistics of **300 prioritised KVKs by March 2027**.
- **Target 2:** establish **Incubation-cum-Skill Centres in all KVKs by December 2028**, with KVKs mapped to agro-based enterprises, technical partner institutions and **district-specific agricultural opportunities**; KVK-wise incubation plans are being prepared for State/UT funding.
- **State progress:** **Maharashtra, Gujarat, Goa and Chhattisgarh** have received Detailed Project Reports (DPRs) from Universities/KVKs; other States are at earlier stages.
- **Personnel reforms:** proposed reforms on **pay, allowances and promotion** of KVK staff have been **approved by the Department of Expenditure**; States were asked to provide **NPS contributions and retirement benefits** as applicable, and to fill vacancies promptly.
- **Funds:** emphasis on **timely release of funds** to KVKs, particularly for **seasonal** agricultural programmes, so activities fall within the correct agricultural window.

**Static hooks:** the first KVK was set up in **1974 at Puducherry**; KVKs function under **ICAR**, which sits within the **Department of Agricultural Research and Education (DARE)** of this Ministry. Connect to **ATMA** (Support to State Extension Programmes), the **Agriculture Infrastructure Fund**, and the agri-startup incubation ecosystem (**RKVY-RAFTAAR** agribusiness incubators).

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (extension machinery, agri-entrepreneurship); GS2 Governance (Centre–State delivery, cooperative federalism in a Central Sector institution).

## 4. Ministry of Ayush: **11th Ayurveda Day 2026 — curtain raiser; main celebration on 23 September at Nagpur, theme "Ayurveda for a Healthier Tomorrow"**

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Ayush. Curtain-raiser addressed by **Shri Prataprao Jadhav**, Minister of State (Independent Charge) for Ayush and MoS for Health & Family Welfare, at the National Media Centre, New Delhi.

**What it is.** The launch of the nationwide campaign leading to the **11th Ayurveda Day**, whose main celebrations will be held on **23 September 2026 in Nagpur, Maharashtra**, under the theme **"Ayurveda for a Healthier Tomorrow" (स्वस्थ भविष्य के लिए आयुर्वेद)**.

**Why it matters.** Ayurveda Day is the Ministry's principal annual policy-signalling event, and this edition's framing — evidence, integration and regulation rather than heritage alone — is the direction UPSC prefers to test (traditional knowledge systems + public health + the global wellness economy). Note also that the observance is now pegged to a **fixed calendar date, 23 September**, rather than the moveable **Dhanteras / Dhanvantari Jayanti** to which earlier editions were tied — a small but frequently-asked change.

**Key facts — three campaign pillars announced:**

1. **Integrative Healthcare for Better Health Outcomes** — combining the strengths of different systems of medicine.
2. **Evidence, Research and Innovation in Ayurveda** — positioned as the precondition for wider global acceptance.
3. **Ayurveda for Community Wellbeing and Sustainable Living** — individual, family and community wellbeing plus sustainable living.

**Deliverables flagged for 23 September** (by **Prof. Sanjeev Sharma**, Vice-Chancellor, National Institute of Ayurveda, Jaipur):

- **National Dhanwantari Ayurveda Awards 2026**;
- **safety dossiers on medicinal plants**;
- **Standard Treatment Guidelines for skin diseases**;
- a **report on the Ayush Services Sector**;
- **Ayush Vision 2030**; and exchange of MoUs.

**Institutions to know:** Central Council for Research in Ayurvedic Sciences (**CCRAS**); All India Institute of Ayurveda (**AIIA**), New Delhi; National Institute of Ayurveda (**NIA**), Jaipur; Morarji Desai National Institute of Yoga (**MDNIY**). Adjacent static: the **National Commission for Indian System of Medicine (NCISM) Act, 2020**, and the WHO **Global Traditional Medicine Centre at Jamnagar**.

**Destination notebook:** GS1 Society/Culture (traditional knowledge systems); GS2 Governance (health policy, AYUSH integration into public health); GS3 Economy (wellness and medical value travel).

## 5. Ministry of Women and Child Development: **9th Rashtriya Poshan Maah 2026 to be launched at Varanasi on 9 September — theme "Hamari Anganwadi Hamari Jimmedari"**

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Women and Child Development. To be launched by Union Minister **Smt. Annpurna Devi** at the **Rudraksh International Cooperation and Convention Centre, Varanasi**, with UP Chief Minister **Shri Yogi Adityanath**, MoS **Smt. Savitri Thakur** and UP Minister **Smt. Baby Rani Maurya**.

**What it is.** The ninth edition of **Rashtriya Poshan Maah**, the month-long September nutrition *Jan Andolan* under **Poshan Abhiyaan** (launched 2018). The 2026 edition's theme — **"Hamari Anganwadi Hamari Jimmedari"** — shifts the campaign's centre of gravity from the beneficiary to the **Anganwadi Centre as a community-owned institution**.

**Why it matters.** This edition rides on a set of **substantive guideline changes** rather than being purely an awareness drive — which is what makes it examinable.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Mission Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0 — revised Supplementary Nutrition Programme (SNP) Guidelines**

- **Name / Ministry:** Supplementary Nutrition Programme under **Mission Saksham Anganwadi and Poshan 2.0**, Ministry of Women and Child Development.
- **Objective:** improve nutritional outcomes for children (0–6), pregnant women, lactating mothers and adolescent girls by standardising what is served at Anganwadi Centres and making entitlements visible and accountable.
- **Key features (new this cycle):**
  1. For the **first time**, the revised SNP Guidelines **define Hot Cooked Meals (HCM)**;
  2. They **mandate SNP Menu Boards** at every Anganwadi Centre, displaying the daily menu and entitlement;
  3. They issue **guidelines for the establishment of Anganwadi Management Committees (AMCs)** — the Ministry's AMC guidelines were issued on **25 June 2026**;
  4. States/UTs are to operationalise **weekly menu rotation** using **millets, pulses and seasonal vegetables**.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** all Anganwadi Centres nationwide; at least **one AMC meeting per Anganwadi Centre** during Poshan Maah, with AMCs and Mothers' Committees monitoring meal quality and menu adherence.
- **Significance:** converts nutrition delivery from a supply-side transfer into a **community-audited entitlement** — the transparency logic of social audit applied to the ICDS platform.

**The five themes of Poshan Maah 2026:**

1. **Dietary Diversity and Adequate Protein** — live recipe demonstrations with affordable local ingredients; Poshan Melas; a **"Protein-Rich Foods" campaign** (plant- and animal-based protein).
2. **Fresh Hot Cooked Meals at Anganwadi Centres** — weekly menu rotation, live HCM demonstrations, community tasting sessions with parents, Panchayats, SHGs and AMCs.
3. **Community Ownership and Accountability** — accelerated constitution and operationalisation of AMCs; SNP Menu Boards at every Centre.
4. **Nutrition, Early Stimulation and Education for Childhood Development** — for **0–3 years**: Home Visits guided by **Navchetana** and a revised Home Visit Scheduler, observation of **34 developmental milestones**, with fast-track referrals during **Village Health Sanitation and Nutrition Days (VHSNDs)** in convergence with the health department and **Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram (RBSK)**; for **3–6 years**: early learning through **Aadharshila**, an **ECCE Day** at every Centre, exposure visits to nearby government primary schools for 5–6 year-olds, and community drives for toys and learning materials.
5. **Ten Years of Pradhan Mantri Matru Vandana Yojana (PMMVY)** — a **100% saturation drive** to enrol all eligible pregnant and lactating women, expedited Direct Benefit Transfers, and a **PMMVY Toolkit** for State/UT localisation.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance & Social Justice (schemes for vulnerable sections, ICDS, health and nutrition); GS1 Society (child nutrition, maternal health).

## 6. Ministry of Electronics & Information Technology: **APNIC 62 in Mumbai — NIXI hosts the Asia Pacific internet number registry's flagship conference**

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), through the **National Internet Exchange of India (NIXI)**, co-hosted with the **Internet Service Providers Association of India (ISPAI)** and supported by the Government of Maharashtra. Organised by **APNIC — the Asia Pacific Network Information Centre**.

**What it is.** The **62nd APNIC Conference** brings **more than 600** internet experts, network operators, policymakers and technology leaders to **Mumbai** this September. APNIC is described in the release as "the region's **not-for-profit Internet number registry**" — i.e. the body that allocates and registers **IP addresses and Autonomous System Numbers** for the Asia-Pacific.

**Why it matters.** This is a rare, clean PIB entry point into **internet governance and critical internet infrastructure** — a topic UPSC has begun probing (data localisation, root servers, IPv6, routing security) but which rarely appears in government releases. It also positions India as a rule-shaper rather than only a market in the Asia-Pacific internet stack.

**Key facts:**

- **Agenda themes:** AI-powered services, **network automation**, cybersecurity, **routing security**, and internet governance issues — framed around AI and rising digital demand putting pressure on underlying internet infrastructure.
- **Quoted voices:** **Shri Sushil Pal**, Joint Secretary, MeitY ("secure, resilient and future-ready Internet infrastructure remains a key priority"); **Shri S. Krishnan**, Secretary, MeitY; **Shri Rajesh Aggarwal**, Chief Secretary, Government of Maharashtra; **Mr. Jia Rong Low**, Director General, APNIC; **Dr. Devesh Tyagi**, CEO, NIXI.
- **India's IPv6 record** was singled out by APNIC's Director General as showing "what is possible when technical capability, investment and collaboration align."

**Static hooks — learn these, they are the likely Prelims content:** **NIXI** is a **not-for-profit company (Section 8)** set up in **2003** under MeitY; it operates India's internet exchange points, manages the **.IN country-code domain registry**, and runs the **Indian Registry for Internet Names and Numbers (IRINN)** for IP allocation within India. **APNIC** is one of the **five Regional Internet Registries (RIRs)** worldwide — the others being ARIN, RIPE NCC, LACNIC and AFRINIC — all operating under **IANA/ICANN**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (internet infrastructure, cybersecurity); GS2 — International Relations (global internet governance, multi-stakeholder model).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the National Clean Air Programme (NCAP) and the Swachh Vayu Sarvekshan, consider the following statements:

1. The Swachh Vayu Sarvekshan ranks participating cities in three groups based on population size.
2. The final selection of best-performing cities follows a sequence of self-assessment by cities, approval by a State-level Monitoring Committee, evaluation by the Central Pollution Control Board and field verification by independent committees.
3. As announced on Swachh Vayu Diwas 2026, all 130 NCAP cities have met the National Ambient Air Quality Standards.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Consider the following statements regarding the National Internet Exchange of India (NIXI) and APNIC:

1. NIXI is a not-for-profit organisation functioning under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology.
2. APNIC is the Regional Internet Registry responsible for allocating Internet number resources in the Asia-Pacific region.
3. APNIC 62 was hosted in India for the first time by the Telecom Regulatory Authority of India.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** In the context of defence procurement in India, the term "Acceptance of Necessity (AoN)" refers to

(a) the final signing of a contract between the Ministry of Defence and a selected vendor
(b) in-principle administrative approval accorded to a capital acquisition proposal, marking the beginning of the procurement process
(c) the certification issued by the Directorate General of Quality Assurance after field trials of equipment
(d) the clearance granted by the Cabinet Committee on Security for the import of defence equipment above a specified value

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
Statement 1 is correct: the three groups are cities with population below 3 lakh, 3–10 lakh, and above 10 lakh. Statement 2 is correct — that is exactly the evaluation chain described by MoEFCC. Statement 3 is incorrect: as of the 5th Swachh Vayu Sarvekshan, **14 of the 130 cities** had met the NAAQS; 108 showed improvement in PM10 over the FY 2017-18 baseline, 72 achieved reductions above 20% and 26 above 40%.

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
NIXI is a not-for-profit company set up in 2003 under MeitY; it runs India's internet exchange points, the **.IN** registry and the Indian Registry for Internet Names and Numbers (IRINN). APNIC is one of the five Regional Internet Registries (with ARIN, RIPE NCC, LACNIC and AFRINIC) and allocates IP addresses and Autonomous System Numbers in the Asia-Pacific. Statement 3 is incorrect: **APNIC 62 in Mumbai was hosted by NIXI along with ISPAI**, supported by MeitY and the Government of Maharashtra — not by TRAI.

**Q3 — (b).**
AoN is the **in-principle administrative approval** accorded by the Defence Acquisition Council (or the competent financial authority) to a capital acquisition proposal; it initiates the procurement cycle under the Defence Acquisition Procedure and is followed by issue of the RFP, trials, contract negotiation and finally contract signature. On 7 September 2026 the DAC accorded AoN to proposals worth about **₹1.10 lakh crore**, with roughly **98%** of the value earmarked for Indian industry.
