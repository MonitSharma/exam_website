## 1. Ministry of Health and Family Welfare: **India crosses 80 lakh HPV vaccine doses under the National HPV Vaccination Campaign**

**Issued by:** Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, 11 September 2026 (Release ID 2309109)

**What it is.** India has administered more than 80 lakh (8 million) doses of the Human Papillomavirus (HPV) vaccine under the National HPV Vaccination Campaign launched by the Prime Minister in **February 2026**. The campaign gives **14-year-old girls a voluntary, free, single-dose HPV vaccination** with parental consent at government health facilities.

**Why it matters.** Cervical cancer is one of the largest cancer burdens among Indian women, and this is India's first nationwide, population-scale HPV immunisation push delivered through the public health system. It is a textbook case of *preventive* (rather than curative) public health — the theme UPSC repeatedly returns to in GS2 health governance questions — and of digital public infrastructure being used for programme monitoring.

**Key facts and figures**

- Annual target cohort: about **1.2 crore girls** aged 14 (Registrar General of India, 2021 estimates).
- **100% coverage** of identified target populations: **Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Mizoram**.
- **Above 90%:** Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, Assam. **Above 85%:** Karnataka. **Above 60%:** Chhattisgarh, Sikkim, Kerala, Telangana, Odisha.
- **Uttar Pradesh** has the highest absolute number — over **22 lakh** adolescent girls vaccinated.
- Monitoring runs on the **U-WIN platform**, used to track district-level coverage and identify missed beneficiaries.
- All sessions are supervised by trained Medical Officers, with facilities linked to **24×7 arrangements for management of rare AEFI** (Adverse Events Following Immunization).
- Global context cited: over **800 million** HPV vaccine doses distributed worldwide; HPV vaccines are now in the National Immunization Programmes of **87% of upper-middle-income, 78% of lower-middle-income and 54% of low-income countries**.
- The release notes a strong emphasis on countering **misinformation** through paediatricians, gynaecologists, AIIMS experts, teachers, ASHAs and ANMs.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 Governance (Health) → *Health & Social Sector*; also GS3 Science & Technology (biotechnology/vaccines).

## 2. Ministry of Finance: **Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 2026 to come into force from 1 October 2026 — the 1891 Act is replaced**

**Issued by:** Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Services), 11 September 2026 (Release ID 2309122)

**What it is.** A notification dated **10 September 2026** appoints **1 October 2026** as the commencement date for the **Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 2026**, which received the **President's assent on 13 August 2026**. It replaces the **Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891** — a 135-year-old colonial-era statute — and modernises how banking records are admitted as evidence in legal proceedings.

**Why it matters.** This belongs to the government's continuing programme of repealing and rewriting colonial-era laws (alongside the three criminal law codes, the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, and the Jan Vishwas decriminalisation exercise). For UPSC it sits at the intersection of GS2 (law-making, ease of doing business, judicial process) and GS3 (banking sector reform, digital economy, financial fraud investigation).

**Key features**

1. **Technology-neutral definition of records** — recognises banking records kept in **physical, electronic, digital, virtual, cloud-based and other contemporary forms**.
2. **Simplified, standardised certification** of banking records, including through **manual, digital or electronic signatures**.
3. **Clarity on summoning bank officials** where the bank is *not* a party to the proceedings — the Court must record a **"special cause" in writing** before doing so.
4. **Enabling extension** — the Central Government may extend the Act's provisions to specified **financial sector entities or classes of entities**, so the framework can track an evolving financial landscape.

**Exam angle.** Note the distinction: the *Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023* is the general law of evidence; the Bankers' Books Evidence Act is a *special* statute governing a specific category of documentary evidence. Also note the sequence — assent (13 Aug 2026) ≠ commencement (1 Oct 2026), a standard prelims trap.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 Polity & Governance (laws, colonial-law repeal); GS3 Economy (banking).

## 3. Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs: **9th CSMC clears 29,000+ more houses; PMAY-U 2.0 sanctions reach 18.77 lakh**

**Issued by:** Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs, 11 September 2026 (Release ID 2309343)

**What it is.** The **9th meeting of the Central Sanctioning and Monitoring Committee (CSMC)** under PMAY-Urban 2.0, chaired by **Shri Satendra Singh, Secretary, Department of Urban Development**, was held in New Delhi on **10 September 2026**. It approved **over 29,000 houses** under the **BLC and AHP** verticals across **six States — Assam, Bihar, Gujarat, Telangana, Tripura and Uttar Pradesh** — taking cumulative PMAY-U 2.0 sanctions to **18.77 lakh**.

**Why it matters.** Urban housing is a recurring GS2/GS3 theme (urbanisation, Housing for All, women's asset ownership). The equity data in this release is unusually quotable for Mains. The Secretary also announced a **procedural reform**: CSMC meetings will now be convened in the **second week of every month**, with States expected to submit approved projects by the end of each month — a small but examinable governance/monitoring point.

**Key facts and figures**

- Cumulative PMAY-U 2.0 sanctions: **18.77 lakh houses**.
- **17.19 lakh (92%)** allotted to **women** (sole or joint ownership).
- **1.80 lakh** to senior citizens; **3.25 lakh** SC, **1.02 lakh** ST, **8.22 lakh** OBC beneficiaries.
- PMAY-U and PMAY-U 2.0 combined: **more than 1.25 crore houses sanctioned**, **over 1 crore completed and delivered**.
- Beneficiary income ceiling under the scheme: annual household income up to **₹9 lakh**.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana – Urban 2.0 (PMAY-U 2.0)**

- **Ministry:** Housing & Urban Affairs. **Launched:** 2024, as the successor to PMAY-U (2015).
- **Objective:** "Housing for All" in urban areas — assistance to **1 crore** urban **EWS/LIG/MIG** families over five years.
- **Outlay:** overall investment of about **₹10 lakh crore**, with Central assistance of about **₹1.60 lakh crore**.
- **Four verticals:** **BLC** (Beneficiary-Led Construction), **AHP** (Affordable Housing in Partnership), **ARH** (Affordable Rental Housing), **ISS** (Interest Subsidy Scheme).
- **Income slabs:** EWS up to ₹3 lakh; LIG ₹3–6 lakh; MIG ₹6–9 lakh per annum.
- **Carpet-area caps:** up to 45 sqm (BLC/AHP), 60 sqm (ARH), 120 sqm (ISS).
- **Significance:** women-centric ownership norm; shifts the mission from pure construction targets to monthly sanction-and-monitoring discipline.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 Governance (welfare schemes, urban governance); GS1 Society (urbanisation, women's asset ownership).

## 4. Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare (ICAR): **First Badri cattle calf born through OPU-IVF — a first for Uttarakhand's indigenous breed**

**Issued by:** Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, 12 September 2026 (Release ID 2309386)

**What it is.** A healthy **Badri female calf** was born on **11 September 2026** at the Academic Dairy Farm through **Ovum Pick-Up and In Vitro Fertilization (OPU-IVF)** — a **Badri embryo transferred into a Sahiwal recipient cow**. **Badri cattle** are an indigenous cattle genetic resource of Uttarakhand and the **State Animal of Uttarakhand**.

**Why it matters.** Indigenous breed conservation (Rashtriya Gokul Mission, National Livestock Mission) plus advanced reproductive biotechnology is a high-probability GS3 Sci-Tech + Animal Husbandry crossover. Conventional breeding multiplies elite female germplasm slowly; OPU-IVF and cloning break that bottleneck.

**Key facts**

- **Collaboration:** **ICAR–National Dairy Research Institute (ICAR-NDRI), Karnal** and **G. B. Pant University of Agriculture and Technology, Pantnagar**, funded by the **Uttarakhand Biotechnology Council, Haldi**.
- **Process:** oocytes collected from genetically valuable Badri donor cows via **ultrasound-guided ovum pick-up** → matured and fertilised *in vitro* using semen from a superior Badri bull (via the **Uttarakhand Livestock Development Board**) → embryos cultured → transferred to synchronised recipient cows.
- Research programme began in **2023**; a second synchronised crossbred recipient is expected to deliver a Badri calf within 5–7 days.
- The team has also generated **cloned Badri embryos**; the next phase will transfer OPU-IVF- and cloning-derived embryos into Sahiwal, crossbred and Badri recipients.

**Prelims hooks:** Badri = State Animal of Uttarakhand; Sahiwal = indigenous *Bos indicus* dairy breed (Punjab/Haryana tract) used here as the recipient/surrogate; ICAR-NDRI is headquartered at **Karnal, Haryana**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 Science & Technology (biotechnology) and GS3 Agriculture (animal husbandry, indigenous breeds).

## 5. Ministry of Defence: **Colombo Security Conclave Table Top Exercise concludes at Eastern Naval Command, Visakhapatnam**

**Issued by:** Ministry of Defence, 12 September 2026 (Release ID 2309385)

**What it is.** A **three-day Table Top Exercise (TTX)** under the aegis of the **Colombo Security Conclave (CSC)** ran from **9 to 11 September 2026** at **Eastern Naval Command, Visakhapatnam**, on the theme **"Coordinated Response to Illicit Maritime Activities in the Indian Ocean Region."** It opened with an address by **RAdm Manoj Jha, Chief Staff Officer (Operations), ENC**.

**Why it matters.** The CSC is India's principal *minilateral* security platform in its immediate maritime neighbourhood, and this exercise is explicitly aligned with **MAHASAGAR** — *Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions*, the framing that succeeded **SAGAR** as India's Indian Ocean doctrine. Expect questions linking CSC, MAHASAGAR, IFC-IOR and Maritime Domain Awareness.

**Key facts**

- Delegations and maritime security experts from **CSC member states and observer nations** worked through **over 72 hours** of simulated operational scenarios.
- Focus areas: **maritime terrorism, narcotics smuggling, human trafficking, and illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing** — i.e. **non-traditional** security threats.
- Participants reviewed existing **Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)** and streamlined **information-sharing** to strengthen collective **Maritime Domain Awareness (MDA)**.
- Aligned with the **MAHASAGAR** vision.

**CSC background (for the notebook).** Begun in 2011 as a trilateral NSA-level maritime security dialogue among **India, Sri Lanka and the Maldives**; **Mauritius** joined in 2022, **Bangladesh** in 2024, and **Seychelles** became a full member in 2026 — a six-member grouping. Its **Secretariat is at Colombo, Sri Lanka**, established under a CSC Charter. It works across five pillars: maritime safety and security; countering terrorism and radicalisation; combating trafficking and transnational organised crime; cyber security and protection of critical infrastructure; and humanitarian assistance and disaster relief.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 International Relations (India's neighbourhood, minilaterals); GS3 Internal Security (maritime security, non-traditional threats).

## 6. PIB Backgrounder (Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare): **Seven years of the Pradhan Mantri Kisan Maandhan Yojana — "Securing Farmers' Future with Dignity"**

**Issued by:** PIB Research Unit, 11 September 2026 (Release ID 2309204), ahead of the scheme's seventh anniversary on **12 September 2026**

**What it is.** A Backgrounder marking seven years of **PM-KMY**, the old-age pension scheme for small and marginal farmers launched on **12 September 2019**.

**Why it matters.** Old-age income security for the unorganised and agricultural workforce is one of the weakest links in India's social protection architecture. PM-KMY belongs to the **Maandhan family** of contributory pension schemes (with PM-SYM for unorganised workers and PM-LVM for small traders), and the enrolment numbers below are the sort of underwhelming figure that makes for a good Mains critique.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Pradhan Mantri Kisan Maandhan Yojana (PM-KMY)**

- **Name and type:** Pradhan Mantri Kisan Maandhan Yojana — a **Central Sector Scheme**, voluntary and **contributory**.
- **Ministry / implementing partner:** Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, **Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare**; pension fund managed by the **Life Insurance Corporation of India (LIC)**.
- **Objective:** assured old-age income security for **landholding Small and Marginal Farmers (SMFs)**, male and female.
- **Key features (4):**
  1. **Minimum assured pension of ₹3,000 per month** on attaining **60 years** of age.
  2. **Family pension of 50% (₹1,500/month)** to the spouse on the death of a pensioner — spouse only, and only if not already a PM-KMY beneficiary.
  3. If the subscriber dies **before 60**, the spouse may continue the scheme with regular contributions or exit as per withdrawal provisions.
  4. Government **matches** the subscriber's contribution (the Maandhan-family 50:50 co-contribution design).
- **Eligibility / coverage:** SMFs with **cultivable land up to 2 hectares**, aged **18–40 years** at entry, names appearing in State/UT land records **as on 1 August 2019**.
- **Exclusions:** subscribers of **NPS, ESIC, EPFO**; beneficiaries of **PM-SYM** and **PM-LVM**; institutional landholders; present and former holders of constitutional posts; Ministers/MPs/MLAs/MLCs, Mayors, District Panchayat Chairpersons; serving and retired Central/State government and PSE employees (**MTS/Class IV/Group D are exempt from this exclusion and remain eligible**); **income-tax payers** in the last assessment year; and practising registered professionals.
- **Numbers as reported:** **24,96,252 enrolments** as on **6 February 2026**. **Haryana** leads with about **5.75 lakh**, followed by **Bihar** with over **3.46 lakh**; **Jharkhand** and **Uttar Pradesh** have each crossed 2.5 lakh and **Chhattisgarh** over 2 lakh. **₹540.66 crore** utilised since 2019 (as of February 2026).
- **Significance:** extends contributory social security to a group historically outside formal pension systems; but ~25 lakh enrolments against a small-and-marginal-farmer population of well over 10 crore is itself the critique worth writing in Mains.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 Governance (social security, welfare schemes); GS3 Agriculture (farmer welfare).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the **Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 2026**, consider the following statements:

1. It replaces the Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891.
2. It adopts a technology-neutral approach, recognising banking records maintained in electronic, digital, virtual and cloud-based forms.
3. It came into force immediately upon receiving the assent of the President in August 2026.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** With reference to the **Pradhan Mantri Kisan Maandhan Yojana (PM-KMY)**, consider the following statements:

1. It is a Central Sector Scheme of the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare, implemented in partnership with the Life Insurance Corporation of India.
2. It is a voluntary and contributory scheme providing a minimum assured pension of ₹3,000 per month on attaining 60 years of age.
3. Enrolment is open to small and marginal farmers with cultivable landholding up to two hectares who are between 18 and 40 years of age.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** The **Colombo Security Conclave**, in the news recently, is best described as:

(a) A regional maritime and non-traditional security grouping of Indian Ocean states, with its Secretariat at Colombo, in which India is a founding member
(b) A trade and connectivity initiative of BIMSTEC member states headquartered at Colombo
(c) A United Nations-mandated counter-piracy task force operating in the Gulf of Aden
(d) A grouping of Bay of Bengal littoral states created under the Indian Ocean Rim Association to coordinate disaster relief

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
The Act replaces the 135-year-old Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891, and is explicitly technology-neutral in recognising physical, electronic, digital, virtual and cloud-based records. Statement 3 is wrong: it received the President's assent on 13 August 2026, but a separate notification dated 10 September 2026 appointed **1 October 2026** as the commencement date — assent and commencement are distinct.

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.**
All three are correct. PM-KMY is a Central Sector Scheme of the Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare with LIC as the pension fund manager; it is voluntary and contributory with a minimum assured pension of ₹3,000 per month from age 60; and eligibility is confined to small and marginal farmers holding up to 2 hectares of cultivable land, aged 18–40 at entry, whose names appear in State/UT land records as on 1 August 2019.

**Q3 — (a).**
The CSC began in 2011 as a trilateral NSA-level maritime security dialogue among India, Sri Lanka and the Maldives, later adding Mauritius (2022), Bangladesh (2024) and Seychelles (2026); its Secretariat is at Colombo. It is not a BIMSTEC, UN or IORA body. Its five pillars cover maritime safety and security, counter-terrorism and de-radicalisation, trafficking and transnational crime, cyber security and critical infrastructure, and HADR — which is why the Visakhapatnam Table Top Exercise addressed maritime terrorism, narcotics, human trafficking and IUU fishing.
