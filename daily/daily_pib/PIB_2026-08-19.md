## 1. Government approves the **Incentive Scheme for Promotion of Domestic PNG Connections**

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas (MoPNG)

The Government has approved an **Incentive Scheme for Promotion of Domestic PNG Connections**, effective **1 September 2026**. The design is unusual: instead of a cash subsidy, the incentive is paid **in kind — as extra allocation of cheap domestic APM gas** to City Gas Distribution (CGD) entities that actually add *billed* household connections. It targets the sector's real bottleneck, which is not pipeline laying but the large stock of **connections that are laid but never billed**.

**Key facts**

- **Trigger:** for every **incremental billed domestic PNG connection** above a threshold fixed for each Geographical Area (GA), the CGD entity gets an additional allocation of **200 standard cubic metres (SCM)** of **domestically produced APM gas**.
- **APM gas** = gas under the **Administered Price Mechanism**, priced by the Government (linked to the Indian Crude Basket with a cap) and cheaper than imported LNG. Domestic PNG and CNG already enjoy **100% priority** in APM allocation.
- **Effect on economics:** the payback period on capex for a domestic PNG connection is expected to fall from roughly **10 years to about 3 years**.
- **Rollout:** in **two tranches over six months**.
- **Base:** India currently has about **1.74 crore domestic PNG connections**.

**Why it matters:** This is the clean-cooking-fuel story after Ujjwala — LPG cylinders reach households but PNG is cheaper per unit, safer and needs no logistics chain. The scheme also converts a *regulatory* lever (APM allocation priority) into a *performance* lever, a design worth remembering for GS3 answers on incentive architecture. Notebook: **GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure, Energy Security)**; overlaps GS2 (welfare delivery) and GS3 Environment (household air pollution).

## 2. CCEA approves **four railway multitracking projects** worth ₹9,450 crore — about **410 km** of new track

**Ministry/Body:** Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) / Ministry of Railways

The CCEA, chaired by the Prime Minister, cleared **four multitracking projects** on the eastern and southeastern trunk routes. All four sit on the congested **Kolkata–Chennai corridor** — the spine that carries coal, steel and port traffic from the Odisha–Jharkhand mineral belt to eastern and southern ports.

**Key facts**

- **Total cost:** **₹9,450 crore**; **completion target 2030-31**.
- **Coverage:** **eight districts** across **West Bengal, Odisha, Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh**; adds ~**410 km** to the existing network.
- The four projects:
  1. **Kharagpur–Bhadrak (Ranital) 4th line — 173 km** (West Bengal, Odisha)
  2. **Bhadrak–Haridaspur 4th line — 75 km** (Odisha)
  3. **Gummidipundi–Gudur 3rd and 4th lines — 90 km** (Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh)
  4. **Cuttack–Paradeep (Badabandha) 3rd and 4th lines — 72 km** (Odisha)
- Expected to improve connectivity for about **6,448 villages** with a population of roughly **60 lakh**.

**Why it matters:** Multitracking is the cheapest way to add capacity where land is already acquired — it is the standard answer to "how do you decongest saturated rail corridors?" The **Cuttack–Paradeep** line is specifically a **port-connectivity** link, tying into PM Gati Shakti's multimodal logic. Notebook: **GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure: Railways, Logistics)**.

## 3. CCEA approves 4-laning of the **Muzaffarpur–Sitamarhi–Sonbarsa section of NH-22** in Bihar

**Ministry/Body:** CCEA / Ministry of Road Transport & Highways (MoRTH)

**Key facts**

- **Length: 82.578 km**; **cost: ₹3,590.73 crore**; **mode: Hybrid Annuity Mode (HAM)**.
- The corridor is a **feeder to the India–Nepal border at Sonbarsa**, linking it to **Muzaffarpur on NH-27 (East–West Corridor)**.
- Designed for **100 kmph** (average 80 kmph), with **no at-grade median openings** — i.e. access-controlled in effect.
- Bypasses congestion in Muzaffarpur, Muksudpur, Runni Saidpur, Thumma, Dumra, Bhutahi and Sonbarsa.

**HAM in one line (frequently asked):** a PPP variant where the **government funds 40% of project cost during construction** in five instalments linked to milestones, and the **concessionaire funds the remaining 60%**, recovered as **annuities plus interest** over the operations period; **the government, not the developer, collects toll** — so traffic risk stays with the public authority while construction risk stays with the private partner.

**Why it matters:** Sonbarsa is a live **India–Nepal trade and transit point**; upgrading the approach corridor is as much a **border-connectivity and internal-security** measure as an economic one. Notebook: **GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure, PPP models)**; overlaps **GS2 — IR (India–Nepal connectivity)**.

## 4. Union Home Minister to chair the **31st meeting of the Southern Zonal Council** at Mamallapuram

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Home Affairs (Inter-State Council Secretariat)

The **31st Southern Zonal Council** meeting is being held at **Mamallapuram, Tamil Nadu on 20 August 2026**, chaired by the **Union Home Minister**, with Chief Ministers and Chief Secretaries of member States and UTs attending.

**Key facts — the Zonal Council architecture (high prelims value)**

- Zonal Councils are **statutory, not constitutional** — created under **Part III of the States Reorganisation Act, 1956**.
- There are **five Zonal Councils**: Northern, Central, Eastern, Western and Southern.
- The **Union Home Minister is the Chairman of all five**; the **Chief Minister of the host State serves as Vice-Chairman by annual rotation**.
- They are **advisory/deliberative bodies** — they discuss and recommend on inter-State disputes, border and linguistic-minority issues, and matters of common interest; recommendations are **not binding**.
- The **North Eastern Council is NOT a Zonal Council** — it is a separate body under the **North Eastern Council Act, 1971**.
- **Southern Zonal Council members:** Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, and the UTs of **Puducherry** and the **Andaman & Nicobar Islands**.

**Why it matters:** A textbook instance of **cooperative federalism through non-constitutional machinery** — contrast with the **Inter-State Council under Article 263**, which *is* constitutional. Notebook: **GS2 — Polity & Governance (Federalism, Centre–State relations)**.

## Answers

**Q1 — (c) 2 and 3 only.** The incentive is deliberately **in kind, not cash**: for every incremental *billed* domestic PNG connection above a Geographical Area-wise threshold, the CGD entity gets an **extra 200 SCM of domestically produced APM gas**, which is cheaper than imported LNG. Statement 1 is therefore wrong. The resulting saving is expected to compress the capex payback period from roughly **10 years to about 3 years**. The scheme takes effect from **1 September 2026** and runs in two tranches over six months, on a base of about **1.74 crore** domestic PNG connections.

**Q2 — (b) 2 and 4 only.** Zonal Councils are **statutory, not constitutional** — created under **Part III of the States Reorganisation Act, 1956** — so statement 1 is wrong. The **Union Home Minister chairs all five** Councils (Northern, Central, Eastern, Western, Southern), with the host State's Chief Minister as Vice-Chairman on annual rotation. The **North Eastern Council is not a Zonal Council**; it was set up under the separate **North Eastern Council Act, 1971** — statement 3 is wrong. The **Southern Zonal Council** covers Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Telangana and the UTs of **Puducherry** and the **Andaman & Nicobar Islands**, so statement 4 is correct.

**Q3 — (d) 1 and 3 only.** Under HAM the **government funds about 40% of project cost during construction** in milestone-linked instalments and the concessionaire funds the remaining 60%, recovered through **annuity payments with interest** during operations. **Tolling rights remain with the government/NHAI, not the developer** — traffic risk is not transferred — so statement 2 is wrong. The approved project upgrades the **82.578 km Muzaffarpur–Sitamarhi–Sonbarsa section of NH-22** at **₹3,590.73 crore**, and **Sonbarsa is an India–Nepal border point**, connected via this corridor to **Muzaffarpur on NH-27 (East–West Corridor)**.
