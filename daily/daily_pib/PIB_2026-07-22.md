## 1. Ministry of Coal releases AAROH — India's first Annual Report on Mine Closure; signs India–Germany (GIZ) pact on scientific mine closure

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Coal (event in New Delhi, 22 July 2026)

- **What it is:** The Ministry of Coal released **AAROH — the Annual Report on Mine Closure**, the first-ever such compilation since Independence. For the first time, **42 coal mines have been scientifically closed** in accordance with approved mine-closure plans, and the report documents their reclamation, ecological restoration and post-mining land reuse. The Ministry also signed an **Implementation Agreement with GIZ (Germany)** for technical cooperation on "to-be-closed" coal mines, and inaugurated **Coal NEER** plants (mine-water treatment for community use).
- **Why it matters:** Scientific mine closure is a **just-transition and environmental-governance** theme — it links coal phase-down, land restoration, water security and community rehabilitation. India–Germany technical cooperation (GIZ) is a recurring model of development partnership.
- **Key facts:** 42 coal mines scientifically closed (first time). AAROH = *Annual Report on Mine Closure*. Partner: **GIZ** (Deutsche Gesellschaft für Internationale Zusammenarbeit). Chief Guest: Union Minister of Coal & Mines G. Kishan Reddy; special guests Bhupender Yadav (Environment) and Dr Jitendra Singh (S&T). **Coal NEER** = mine-water treatment initiative. Statutory backdrop: mine-closure plans and escrow-account mechanism are mandated under coal-block allocation rules.
- **Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Economy (mineral resources, just transition).

## 2. Ministry of Textiles releases "White Gold: India's Cotton Story — From Seed to Shirt"; showcases Kasturi Cotton Bharat

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Textiles (feature/report, 20–21 July 2026)

- **What it is:** A Ministry of Textiles feature titled **"White Gold: India's Cotton Story – From Seed to Shirt"** presenting the full cotton value chain and the flagship **Kasturi Cotton Bharat** traceability-and-branding programme. Cotton is called **"White Gold"** for its dual role as an agricultural commodity and industrial raw material.
- **Why it matters:** Highlights India's structural **cotton paradox** — 1st in acreage but a supply gap — and the use of **QR-code + blockchain traceability** to raise the value of Indian cotton. Relevant to agriculture, textiles-value-addition and export competitiveness.
- **Key facts:** India ranks **1st in cotton area, 2nd in production and consumption** globally. Production **290.91 lakh bales (2025–26)** falls short of domestic consumption **~328 lakh bales** → structural supply gap. Kasturi Cotton Bharat launched **7 October 2022 (World Cotton Day)**; outlay **₹30 crore** (₹15 cr Govt + ₹15 cr trade/industry); implemented via **Cotton Corporation of India (CCI)**. As of 30 June 2026: **3,29,550 bales certified** (3,22,400 from CCI). Blockchain + microsite QR verification for end-to-end traceability.
- **Notebook:** GS3 — Economy / Agriculture (cash crops, value chains).

### Scheme-in-focus: Kasturi Cotton Bharat
- **Ministry:** Textiles (via Cotton Corporation of India).
- **Objective:** Establish a self-reliant traceability, certification and branding ecosystem for Indian cotton to build a premium global identity.
- **Key features:** (i) QR-based certification with **blockchain traceability** from farm to garment; (ii) coverage of Long-Staple (≥28 mm) and Extra-Long-Staple (≥35 mm) cotton; (iii) collaboration between Government (CCI), trade bodies and industry with matching ₹15 cr + ₹15 cr funding; (iv) registered ginning/pressing and testing units.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** Cotton farmers, ginners, spinners and textile exporters; 3.29 lakh bales certified so far.
- **Significance:** Traceability tackles adulteration/contamination and raises realisations, supporting the textile sector's raw-material security.

## 3. EPFO launches "VISHWAS, 2026" — one-time scheme to settle damages/penalty disputes

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Labour & Employment / EPFO

- **What it is:** A **one-time dispute-resolution scheme** for amicable settlement of disputes over **levy of penalty/damages** under **Section 14B** of the EPF & MP Act, 1952 and **Section 128** of the Code on Social Security, 2020. It came into effect on **29 June 2026** and remains open for **six months**.
- **Why it matters:** Aims to **reduce litigation, promote voluntary compliance** and clear long-pending recovery disputes — a governance/ease-of-doing-business measure that also protects workers' interests. (Distinct from the July 2026 EPFO *Amnesty Scheme* on exempted PF trusts — VISHWAS targets penalty/damages disputes.)
- **Key facts:** VISHWAS = *Vivadon ka Samadhan* (dispute settlement). Fully **digital, time-bound**; applications via EPFO Employer Portal using DSC/e-Sign. For defaults **before 14 June 2024**, damages recalculated at lower rates: **0.25%/month** (≤2 months), **0.50%/month** (2–4 months), **1%/month** (>4 months). Covers cases under judicial challenge, pending/partial recoveries (RCC cases) and un-issued notices.
- **Notebook:** GS2 — Governance / GS3 — Economy (labour, social security).

## 4. NITI Aayog's Bioeconomy Roadmap — India targeting a USD 691 billion bioeconomy by 2035

**Issuing body:** NITI Aayog (unveiled by Dr Jitendra Singh)

- **What it is:** A roadmap, **"Building India as a Leading Bioeconomy Powerhouse,"** to grow India's bioeconomy to **USD 691 billion by 2035** and place India among the world's top three biotech powers. It proposes **six National BioMissions** and a **₹50,000-crore BioEconomy Growth Fund** (2026–2035) to bridge lab research and commercial-scale manufacturing.
- **Why it matters:** Frames biotechnology as a growth engine (jobs, GDP, self-reliance) and complements the **BioE3 policy** (Biotechnology for Economy, Environment and Employment). Ties into agriculture, health, energy and the blue economy.
- **Key facts:** Bioeconomy target **USD 691 bn by 2035** (→ USD 2.6 trillion by 2047, ~8–10% of GDP); **30 million jobs** projected. India's bioeconomy grew **~16-fold in a decade to USD 195.3 bn (4.8% of GDP)**. Six BioMissions: **gene & cell therapies, climate-resilient agriculture, synthetic biology, disease surveillance, marine biotechnology, next-gen biopharmaceuticals**.
- **Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology / Economy (biotech, R&D).

## 5. MoHUA approves ₹2,719.80 crore for Ahmedabad under the Urban Challenge Fund

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs (MoHUA)

- **What it is:** MoHUA approved **six urban projects worth ₹2,719.80 crore for Ahmedabad** under the **Urban Challenge Fund (UCF)** — a ₹1 lakh crore central-assistance vehicle for reimagining cities as growth hubs, city revitalisation, and water & sanitation.
- **Why it matters:** Demonstrates the UCF's **50% ceiling on central/state assistance with the rest raised via municipal bonds/loans/PPP** — pushing cities toward **market-based financing** and creditworthiness (a key urban-finance reform theme).
- **Key facts:** Six Ahmedabad projects (sewerage rehabilitation in west/east/old areas, Intelligent Water Management System ₹215 cr, 125-TPD Integrated Sludge Management Facility ₹479.25 cr, Integrated Transit Management System ₹331.03 cr). **Funding split: 25% Centre + 25% Gujarat + 50% via municipal bonds/loans/PPP.** UCF corpus: **₹1 lakh crore** central assistance (approved Feb 2026).
- **Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (urban local bodies) / GS3 — Infrastructure & urban finance.

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to **"AAROH"** released by the Ministry of Coal in July 2026, consider the following statements:
1. It is the first-ever Annual Report on Mine Closure documenting scientifically closed coal mines.
2. It was released alongside an implementation agreement with Germany's GIZ on scientific mine closure.
3. The escrow-account mechanism for mine closure is provided under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.

How many of the statements given above are correct?
(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) All three
(d) None

**Q2.** Consider the following statements about the **Kasturi Cotton Bharat** programme:
1. It is a traceability, certification and branding initiative of the Ministry of Textiles.
2. It uses QR-based certification with blockchain traceability across the cotton value chain.
3. It is implemented on the ground through the Cotton Corporation of India.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q3.** With reference to the **Bioeconomy Roadmap** unveiled by NITI Aayog in 2026, consider the following:
1. It targets a bioeconomy of USD 691 billion by 2035.
2. It proposes the creation of six National BioMissions.
3. It recommends a dedicated BioEconomy Growth Fund to support scale-up of biotech manufacturing.

How many of the above statements are correct?
(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) All three
(d) None

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (b) Only two.** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: AAROH is the first Annual Report on Mine Closure and was released with an India–Germany (GIZ) implementation agreement. Statement 3 is wrong — the mine-closure **escrow-account** mechanism flows from coal-mining/mine-closure guidelines and coal-block allocation rules, **not** the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Kasturi Cotton Bharat is a Ministry of Textiles branding/traceability programme, uses QR + blockchain traceability, and is implemented via the Cotton Corporation of India.

**Q3 — (c) All three.** The roadmap targets **USD 691 billion by 2035**, proposes **six National BioMissions**, and recommends a **₹50,000-crore BioEconomy Growth Fund** for scale-up.
