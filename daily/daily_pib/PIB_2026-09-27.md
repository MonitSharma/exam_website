## 1. Election Commission of India: **Full Commission decides on SIR document collection, ECINet audit and new transparency rules; claims deadlines extended for Delhi and Maharashtra**

On **26 September 2026**, the full Commission met: CEC **Gyanesh Kumar** and Election Commissioners **S.S. Sandhu** and **Vivek Joshi**. It issued a set of decisions on the ongoing **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** of electoral rolls. The meeting followed press reports of dissent inside the Commission. The ECI says every SIR decision, starting with the **24 June 2025** order that began the exercise, was **unanimous**.

**Why it matters:** This is a live case study in how a multi-member constitutional body governs itself under **Article 324**. It connects to voter-roll integrity, the risk of exclusion, and the ECI's own internal procedures. All three are standard GS2 Mains themes.

**Key decisions**
- **Doorstep collection:** For "unmapped" electors, **BLOs will collect documents at home** and upload them to **ECINet** for the ERO to decide. A hearing will be held only in exceptional cases, **preferably online**, and a family member can represent the elector.
- **Inclusion:** DEOs must set up **help desks and special camps** for the homeless, labourers and economically weaker voters.
- **Form 6:** The Commission noted that the Supreme Court has upheld the Form 6 declaration used during SIR. Outside SIR periods, the regular forms under the **Registration of Electors Rules, 1960** apply.
- **ECINet audit:** A committee headed by the **Senior Deputy Election Commissioner**, with an **independent IIT/IIIT expert**, will check whether ECINet complies with electoral law and rules.
- **Deadlines extended:** **Delhi**: claims and objections until **30 October 2026**, disposal until **30 November**. **Maharashtra**: claims and objections until **12 October**, disposal until **10 November**.
- **Internal governance:** Agendas will be circulated before Commission meetings and minutes issued afterwards. APARs must be completed by **31 December** each year, and officers need prior approval for foreign trips.
- According to the release, **SIR has been completed in 20 States/UTs**.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Polity (ECI, Article 324, electoral rolls, RP Act 1950).

## 2. Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation: **PAIMANA infrastructure monitoring report for August 2026 — 1,731 central projects worth ₹33.60 lakh crore**

On **25 September 2026**, MoSPI released its monthly report on **central sector infrastructure projects costing ₹150 crore and above**, which are tracked on the **PAIMANA** portal. PAIMANA is MoSPI's project-monitoring platform. Its **CRISP** module (Central Repository of Infrastructure Projects), launched in July 2026, now receives about 80% of its data through API integration with ministries.

**Why it matters:** Capex quality is a core Economy theme, covering time and cost overruns, how much has actually been spent against sanctioned cost, and which sectors dominate. This report is the government's own dashboard on that question.

**Key facts**
- **1,731 projects**, with a **revised cost of ₹33.60 lakh crore**, across **17 ministries/departments**. Cumulative spending is **₹16.33 lakh crore (48.59%)**.
- **647 projects (38%)** have passed **80% physical progress**. **312 projects (18%)** have passed 80% financial progress.
- By size: **715 "large" projects** (₹1,000 crore and above, worth ₹25.89 lakh crore) and **1,016 "major" projects** (₹150–1,000 crore).
- By sector: **Transport & Logistics** has **1,191 projects (69%)** worth ₹16.05 lakh crore. **Energy** has 223 projects worth ₹10.95 lakh crore.
- By ministry: **MoRTH** leads with **982 projects (57%)** worth ₹9.52 lakh crore, followed by Railways (147) and Coal (122). **Power** has the second-largest value at **₹6.18 lakh crore**.
- **37 new projects** were added in August, including the **WR–ER inter-regional transmission expansion (₹6,444 crore)**. The September report is due on **26 October 2026**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (infrastructure, public investment, project monitoring).

## 3. Ministry of Cooperation: **First national consultation on the National Cooperative Training Framework (NCTF) 2026**

On **24 September 2026**, the Ministry of Cooperation held a national consultative meeting at **Atal Akshay Urja Bhawan, New Delhi**, to set the agenda for the **National Cooperative Training Framework (NCTF) 2026**. MoS (Cooperation) **Krishan Pal Gurjar** said cooperative education should be judged by **quality and outcomes, not by how many programmes are run**. The meeting brought together officials of the Ministries of Cooperation and Skill Development, State cooperative unions, training institutes and **Tribhuvan Sahkari University**.

**Why it matters:** The government wants cooperatives to run PACS computerisation, multipurpose PACS, grain storage and the new national cooperatives for exports, seeds and organics. That requires trained professionals, and NCTF is the planned framework for that training. It links to the **"Sahkar se Samriddhi"** vision and to the **National Cooperation Policy**.

**Key facts**
- This is the **first of two national consultations**. It will be followed by **five regional meetings** covering the North, South, East, West and North-East.
- The focus is on the institutional capacity and professional competence of the cooperative workforce.
- *Static link:* **Tribhuvan Sahkari University** at **Anand, Gujarat** was created by the **Tribhuvan Sahkari University Act, 2025** as India's first national cooperative university. The **NCCT** (National Council for Cooperative Training) runs the existing network of cooperative training institutes. Cooperative societies are covered by **Part IXB** of the Constitution (97th Amendment, 2011).

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance (cooperatives, capacity building); cross-tag GS3 Agriculture (PACS).

## 4. Ministry of Commerce & Industry: **Interest-free working capital of ₹50,000 per barn for FCV tobacco farmers in Andhra Pradesh**

On **25 September 2026**, the Centre approved an **Interest-Free Working Capital Assistance Scheme** for **Flue-Cured Virginia (FCV) tobacco** growers in **Andhra Pradesh**. The government linked the move to weaker global demand, geopolitical developments and shifts in international trade. About **100 million kg** of FCV tobacco had been auctioned in the **2025-26 season**, which opened on **25 March 2026**.

**Why it matters:** This is a textbook conflict for Mains. Farm-income distress in a crop whose exports earn foreign exchange sits against India's public-health commitments under the **WHO FCTC**. Article 17 of the FCTC asks parties to promote economically viable alternatives to tobacco growing.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Interest-Free Working Capital Assistance Scheme (FCV tobacco, Andhra Pradesh)**
- **Ministry:** Commerce & Industry (Department of Commerce). Tobacco is a commercial crop regulated by the **Tobacco Board** (a statutory body under the Tobacco Board Act, 1975, headquartered in **Guntur**).
- **Objective:** Give immediate liquidity to growers hit by slow sales in a weak market.
- **Key features:** (i) **₹50,000 per curing barn**, **interest-free**; (ii) paid by **DBT** into farmers' accounts; (iii) meant for repaying institutional loans, household needs and next-season inputs; (iv) aims to cut dependence on private moneylenders.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** About **44,000 farmers**, with a total outlay of about **₹220 crore**.
- **Significance:** A targeted, time-bound liquidity measure rather than a price support. Useful as an example of how trade shocks pass through to farm incomes.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (commercial crops, farm credit); cross-tag GS2 Health (FCTC).

## 5. Ministry of Ayush (AIIA) with the Territorial Army: **10,000 Ashwagandha saplings planted in the Aravalli ridge under an AIIA–Eco Task Force partnership**

On **24 September 2026**, the **All India Institute of Ayurveda (AIIA)** and **132 Infantry Battalion (Territorial Army) ECO, Rajput** started planting **10,000 Ashwagandha (*Withania somnifera*) saplings**. The site is the **Terriers Herbal Garden, Bhatti Mines**, in Delhi's Southern Ridge, which already holds about **200 medicinal plant species**. The plants will be scientifically monitored and documented.

**Why it matters:** The project combines medicinal-plant research with restoring degraded mining land in the **Aravallis**. It also brings in a lesser-known Prelims fact: the **Ecological Task Force** battalions of the **Territorial Army**, which carry out afforestation in degraded areas.

**Key facts**
- The 132 ETF has worked on plantation and restoration on the **mining-affected Southern Ridge** (Bhatti Mines).
- It is tied to the **National Medicinal Plants Board's** campaign "Ashwagandha – A Health Promoter".
- *Static link:* Ashwagandha belongs to the family **Solanaceae**. AIIA is an autonomous body under the Ministry of Ayush.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Environment (restoration of degraded land, Aravallis); cross-tag GS1/Culture (traditional medicine).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** Consider the following statements about the PAIMANA portal:

1. It is administered by NITI Aayog to monitor centrally sponsored schemes.
2. It monitors central sector infrastructure projects costing ₹150 crore and above.
3. By number of projects, the Ministry of Road Transport & Highways has the largest share of projects monitored on it.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Under the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960, "Form 6" is used for:

(a) Objecting to the inclusion of a name in the electoral roll
(b) Correcting entries in the electoral roll
(c) Applying for inclusion of name as a new elector in the electoral roll
(d) Requesting transposition of an entry within the same constituency

---

**Q3.** Consider the following statements:

1. Ecological Task Force battalions, such as the one partnering AIIA in a recent Ashwagandha plantation drive, are units of the Territorial Army.
2. Ashwagandha (*Withania somnifera*) belongs to the family Solanaceae.
3. The All India Institute of Ayurveda is a statutory body created by an Act of Parliament.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** PAIMANA is run by **MoSPI**, not NITI Aayog, and it tracks central sector infrastructure projects worth ₹150 crore or more. In the August 2026 report, MoRTH accounts for 982 of the 1,731 projects (57%).

**Q2 — (c).** Form 6 is the application for inclusion of a new elector. Form 7 is used for objections or deletions, and Form 8 for corrections, shifting of residence and replacement EPIC.

**Q3 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Ecological Task Force battalions belong to the Territorial Army, and Ashwagandha is in the Solanaceae (nightshade) family. AIIA is an autonomous institute under the Ministry of Ayush registered as a society, not a statutory body.
