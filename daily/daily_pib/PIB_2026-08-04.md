## 1. PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana crosses 50 lakh rooftop solar installations

**Ministry:** New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) | **Date:** 04 Aug 2026

India's flagship rooftop solar scheme has crossed **50.06 lakh benefitted households** — achieved in just two years, against **7.94 lakh** rooftop solar installations in the preceding *ten* years. It is described as the world's largest rooftop solar programme, with an outlay of **₹75,021 crore**.

**Why it matters:** It is the clearest live example of *decentralised, citizen-led* clean energy transition — a favourite GS3 theme linking energy security, DBT plumbing, discom finances and household income. Note the demand-side design: subsidy + concessional credit + digital net metering, not just capital grants.

**Key facts and figures**

- Pace: ~**1 lakh households every six days**; installations rose **3.2×** in nine months — from 5,038/day (Oct 2025) to ~**16,328/day (July 2026)**.
- **July 2026 alone: 5.06 lakh households** — highest monthly figure since launch.
- **₹28,024 crore** subsidy released directly via DBT; ~**19 lakh households now have zero electricity bills**.
- In FY 2024-25, over 12 lakh households earned **₹421 crore** from surplus power sold to the grid (~₹3,500/year per exporting household).
- Cumulative rooftop capacity commissioned under the scheme: **14.8 GW**.
- **Utility-Led Aggregation (ULA) model:** 1.6 lakh installations for PMAY / BPL / SC-ST households in 4 States; approved for rollout in **12 States**.
- Credit: **21.87 lakh** applicants sanctioned loans at a concessional **5.75%** (benchmark = repo + 50 bps); 17.5 lakh installations already loan-financed, integrated with the **Jan Samarth portal**.
- Ecosystem: **34,219 registered vendors** (29,469 active); 2.32 lakh persons trained; ~23,000 third-party quality inspections.
- Enablers: subsidy release within 15 days via **PFMS**; API integration with **80+ DISCOMs**; **32 States/UTs** have removed application and net-metering charges; ₹3,807.6 crore incentives to DISCOMs and ~₹104 crore to ULBs; **City Accelerator Programme in 46 cities**; DCR portal integration for domestic content compliance.
- Government buildings: **1.06 lakh** government buildings solarised so far — 25,255 Central government buildings (872 MW) out of 41,542 identified (1,418 MW potential), and 81,329 State buildings (1,450 MW) out of ~3.2 lakh identified (4,400 MW potential). A **payment security mechanism** has been introduced to de-risk RESCO bidders.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure/Energy) & Environment (Climate action)

## 2. India releases a dedicated Conservation Action Plan for the Clouded Leopard

**Ministry:** Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) | **Date:** 04 Aug 2026 (International Clouded Leopard Day)

India has notified a **Clouded Leopard Conservation Action Plan (CAP)** — prepared under a **Government of India–GEF–UNDP** initiative and released during the **91st meeting of the Standing Committee of the National Board for Wildlife (NBWL)** held at **Coimbatore in the second week of July 2026**.

**Why it matters:** The clouded leopard is one of Asia's rarest and least-studied wild cats, and its Indian range is almost entirely **North-East India**. The CAP is a good case study of the shift from single-species charisma (tiger, elephant) to *landscape-and-corridor* conservation for lesser-known species — and it explicitly ties conservation to transboundary cooperation and community livelihoods.

**Key features of the CAP**

- Identifies **14 priority conservation areas** in North-East India, with landscape-level habitat restoration and **corridor conservation**.
- The species has been brought under the **Species Recovery Programme of the Integrated Development of Wildlife Habitats (IDWH)** scheme — the institutional/funding anchor.
- **Standardised scientific monitoring**: camera traps, occupancy surveys, genetics and **environmental DNA (eDNA)**.
- Strengthened **anti-poaching and wildlife crime prevention**, with frontline capacity building using the **M-STrIPES SMART** application.
- **Community-based conservation** and sustainable livelihood support; **transboundary cooperation** with neighbouring countries across the species' range; and integration of **climate resilience and adaptive management**.
- The CAP document is a combined plan covering the **Caracal, Fishing Cat and Clouded Leopard** (hosted by the Wildlife Institute of India).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Biodiversity & Conservation)

## 3. MHA: 274 fugitive offenders brought back from 36 countries between 2019 and 2026

**Ministry:** Home Affairs | **Date:** 04 Aug 2026

MHA released a consolidated account of India's extradition/deportation record, positioning the return of fugitives as a matter of **sovereignty, economic stability, law and order and national security**, pursued through three pillars — *global operations, strong coordination and smart diplomacy*.

**Why it matters:** This is a ready-made GS2 (India and its neighbourhood / international treaties) + GS3 (internal security, money laundering) answer bank, and the institutional detail — BharatPol, MAC Standing Focus Group, BNSS trial in absentia — is exactly the kind of specifics Prelims tests.

**Key facts and figures**

- **274 fugitives** returned from **36 countries**, 2019 – July 2026. Year-wise: 9 (2019), 7 (2020), 23 (2021), 40 (2022), 37 (2023), 43 (2024), **70 (2025)**, 45 (2026 till July).
- Largest categories: murder/dacoity/violent crime **62**; sexual offences/POCSO **53**; organised crime/gangsters/extortion **42**; terrorism/anti-national/narco-terror **17**; fraud/financial crime **9**.
- Baseline for contrast: pre-2014 India had extradition treaties with only **37 countries**; 2004–2013 averaged about **4 extraditions a year** against ~110 pending requests.
- **BharatPol**, launched **January 2025**, links CBI, State and district police headquarters — **1,400+ unit offices** — to INTERPOL; information-sharing response time cut to **3–10 days** in some cases (10–20 days typically).
- **Red Corner Notices** issued: 40 (2022), 100 (2023), 107 (2024), 112 (2025), **182 in 2026 so far** — 401 in the last three years.
- **Operation Trishul** used satellite, surveillance and digital-footprint profiling to geo-locate fugitives living under changed identities abroad.
- Legal architecture: **Fugitive Economic Offenders Act**; NIA (Amendment) Act and UAPA (Amendment) Act, 2019; and, for the first time, **'trial in absentia' under Sections 355 and 356 of the BNSS** (three new criminal laws, 2024).
- **PMLA** enforcement: **₹17,874 crore** of fugitives' assets attached and **₹18,762 crore** restituted, 2019–2026.
- Institutions: CBI's **Global Operations Centre**; INTERPOL liaison officers in every State and central agency; a **Standing Focus Group under IB's Multi Agency Centre (MAC)** set up in **January 2026**. India hosted the **90th INTERPOL General Assembly in 2022**.
- Cited illustration: extradition of **Tahawwur Hussain Rana** from the USA.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security; GS2 — International Relations (extradition, treaties)

## 4. Second meeting of the BRICS Anti-Corruption Working Group opens in Hyderabad

**Ministry:** Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions (DoPT) | **Date:** 04 Aug 2026

The **2nd meeting of the BRICS Anti-Corruption Working Group (ACWG)** began in **Hyderabad on 4–5 August 2026**, held alongside the **2nd meeting of the BRICS Expert Network on Asset Recovery**. **Ms. Rachna Shah, Secretary, DoPT**, was chief guest; **Shri J. Ashok Kumar**, Chair of the BRICS ACWG, delivered the opening address.

**Why it matters:** Pair this directly with item 3 — the same policy problem (fugitive economic offenders, stolen assets, illicit financial flows) approached through a plurilateral forum. Useful for GS2 questions on "groupings and agreements involving India."

**Key takeaways**

- Core call: no jurisdiction should become a **safe haven** for those who profit from crime; deeper cooperation on **tracing fugitives, extradition assistance, and recovery of stolen assets**.
- Operationalisation of the **BRICS Expert Network on Asset Recovery** — a **standardised format for informal information exchange**, plus a directory of focal points and experts, to speed up law-enforcement-to-law-enforcement cooperation.
- Progress noted on the **BRICS network of law-enforcement experts** for informal cooperation in locating and extraditing corruption fugitives.
- Emphasis on **preventive vigilance and technology-driven governance** — digital identity, e-service delivery, digital payments, transparent procurement. India cited **GeM (the national public procurement portal)** and **DBT**, plus faceless and paperless taxation systems.
- Additional sessions on **ethical governance through innovation**, and on **emerging risks from FinTech and Virtual Digital Assets (VDAs)** — their misuse to conceal proceeds of corruption and launder money.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (BRICS) & Governance (transparency, anti-corruption)

## 5. Panchayat Development Index is now the Panchayat Advancement Index (PAI); PAI 2.0 covers 2.59 lakh Gram Panchayats

**Ministry:** Panchayati Raj (Lok Sabha written reply by Shri Rajiv Ranjan Singh alias Lalan Singh) | **Date:** 04 Aug 2026

The Ministry clarified that the index earlier known as the **Panchayat Development Index (PDI)** is now the **Panchayat Advancement Index (PAI)** — a composite assessment framework measuring incremental performance of Gram Panchayats on the **nine themes of Localisation of Sustainable Development Goals (LSDG)**.

**Why it matters:** PAI is India's main instrument for taking SDG measurement down to the *panchayat* level. The grade distribution is the striking bit — and a likely source of a data-based question.

**Key facts and figures**

- **PAI 2.0 (for FY 2023-24)** onboarded **2,59,867 Gram Panchayats / equivalents across 33 States/UTs**.
- Grade distribution: **A+ — 0**; **A — 3,635**; **B — 1,18,824**; **C — 1,23,719**; **D — 13,689**. *No Gram Panchayat in the country attained an A+ grade.*
- **Tripura** stands out — 943 of its 1,176 GPs graded A. Uttar Pradesh has the largest GP base (57,678) and the largest D-grade count (6,124).
- PAI delivers theme-wise and aggregate scores through **scorecards and dashboards**.
- The Ministry stated there is **no special incentive fund in the 2026-27 Budget** for "Front-runner" panchayats under any LSDG theme, and **no collaboration with UNDP** under PAI.
- Related: under the **revamped Rashtriya Gram Swaraj Abhiyan (RGSA)** — a Centrally Sponsored Scheme running since FY 2022-23 for capacity building and training — **36,81,750** elected representatives and stakeholders have been trained on LSDG themes (FY 2022-23 to FY 2026-27, as on 10 July 2026).
- **GPDP for FY 2026-27** (as on 29.07.2026): **1,56,019 GPs** have resolved to prepare Gram Panchayat Development Plans; **31,789** chose *Theme 4: Water Sufficient Panchayat* and **32,806** chose *Theme 5: Clean and Green Panchayat* as focus areas ("Sankalp").

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (local government, SDG localisation); GS3 — Reports & Indices

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the **PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana**, consider the following statements:

1. It is implemented under the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy.
2. The scheme has a total outlay of about ₹75,021 crore.
3. Cumulative rooftop solar capacity commissioned under the scheme has crossed 14 GW.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** With reference to the **Clouded Leopard Conservation Action Plan** released in 2026, consider the following statements:

1. It was prepared under a Government of India–GEF–UNDP initiative.
2. The clouded leopard has been included under the Species Recovery Programme of the Integrated Development of Wildlife Habitats (IDWH) scheme.
3. The Plan identifies 14 priority conservation areas located in the Western Ghats.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 3 only
(b) 1 and 2 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** Consider the following statements:

1. BharatPol, launched in January 2025, links over 1,400 unit offices of central and state law-enforcement agencies with INTERPOL.
2. The second meeting of the BRICS Anti-Corruption Working Group was held in Hyderabad in August 2026.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 2 only
(c) Both 1 and 2
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** PM Surya Ghar is MNRE's flagship rooftop solar scheme with a ₹75,021 crore outlay; 14.8 GW of rooftop capacity has been commissioned and 50.06 lakh households benefitted as on 4 August 2026.

**Q2 — (b) 1 and 2 only.** Statement 3 is incorrect — the 14 priority conservation areas are in **North-East India**, not the Western Ghats; the clouded leopard's Indian range lies in the North-East. The CAP was prepared under a GoI–GEF–UNDP initiative and released at the 91st Standing Committee meeting of the NBWL at Coimbatore.

**Q3 — (c) Both 1 and 2.** BharatPol was launched in January 2025 and connects 1,400+ unit offices to INTERPOL, cutting information-sharing time to as little as 3–10 days. The 2nd BRICS ACWG meeting, along with the 2nd meeting of the BRICS Expert Network on Asset Recovery, was held in Hyderabad on 4–5 August 2026.
