## 1. Hul Diwas 2026 — nation pays tribute on the 171st anniversary of the Santhal Hul

**Issuing body:** Rashtrapati Bhavan / Ministry of Tribal Affairs (commemorative) · **Date:** 30 June 2026

**What it is & why it matters:** President Droupadi Murmu, Lok Sabha Speaker, and the Home Minister led national tributes on *Hul Diwas*, marking 171 years since the Santhal Hul (rebellion) erupted on 30 June 1855. For UPSC this is a high-frequency modern-history + tribal-society anchor: the Hul is often described as one of India's earliest **organised** mass uprisings against colonial authority, predating the 1857 Revolt by two years, and is central to the government's wider tribal-pride (Janjatiya Gaurav) narrative.

**Key facts:**
- Led by brothers **Sidhu and Kanhu Murmu**, with brothers **Chand and Bhairav** and sisters **Phulo and Jhano**.
- Began at **Bhognadih** village (Sahebganj district, present-day **Jharkhand**); around 10,000 Santhals mobilised.
- Directed against British colonial rule and exploitation by **zamindars, moneylenders (mahajans/"dikus")** and the *Permanent Settlement* revenue machinery.
- Aftermath: the colonial state carved out **Santhal Pargana** as a separate administrative district (1855) and later enacted protective tenancy laws for the region.

**Destination notebook:** GS1 (Modern History — tribal/peasant movements) · Art & Culture / Society.

## 2. Index of Eight Core Industries (ICI) — May 2026 growth slows to 0.5%

**Issuing body:** Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT, **Ministry of Commerce & Industry** · **Released:** end-June 2026

**What it is & why it matters:** The provisional ICI rose just **0.5%** in May 2026 (year-on-year) — a subdued print signalling soft industrial momentum, with five of the eight sectors contracting. As a lead indicator for the Index of Industrial Production (IIP), the ICI is a staple of both Prelims (composition/weights) and Mains (industrial-slowdown analysis).

**Key facts:**
- **Base year: 2011-12 = 100.** The eight industries together carry **40.27%** weight in the IIP.
- The eight (in descending weight): **Petroleum Refinery Products (28.04% — highest), Electricity, Steel, Coal, Crude Oil, Natural Gas, Cement, Fertilizers.**
- May 2026 gainers: **Cement (+8.4%), Electricity (+8.7%), Steel (+5%)**; decliners: **Coal (−9.3%), Refinery Products (−8.7%), Natural Gas (−4.9%), Crude Oil (−4.6%)**.
- Cumulative April–May FY27 growth: about **1.1%**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 (Economy — industry, indices & indicators).

## 3. Haryana–Rajasthan sign MoU to operationalise the 1994 Upper Yamuna River Board Agreement

**Issuing body:** **Ministry of Jal Shakti** (inter-state); MoU exchanged in New Delhi in the presence of the Union Home Minister · **Date:** 29 June 2026

**What it is & why it matters:** The two states signed an MoU to finally implement Rajasthan's allocated share under the 1994 Yamuna-waters accord — letting Rajasthan draw water (chiefly in the monsoon) from **Hathnikund Barrage** in Haryana through an underground pipeline linked to the **Western Yamuna Canal**. It is a rare example of cooperative, negotiated federalism on river water (contrast with adversarial tribunals like Cauvery/Krishna), and updates the long-running inter-state water theme.

**Key facts:**
- The **1994 MoU** on sharing the surface flow of the Yamuna was signed by five basin entities: **Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan and NCT of Delhi**.
- The **Upper Yamuna River Board (UYRB)** was constituted in **1995** under this MoU to regulate/monitor allocations (functions under the Ministry of Jal Shakti).
- Beneficiary areas: drinking water for **Sikar, Churu, Jhunjhunu** (Rajasthan) and parts of **Bhiwani, Fatehabad** (Haryana).

**Destination notebook:** GS2 (Polity & Governance — federalism, inter-state relations, water disputes) · GS1 (Geography — drainage).

## 4. Indian Army to operationalise its first Integrated Battle Groups (IBGs)

**Issuing body:** **Ministry of Defence / Indian Army** · **Target:** by 1 July 2026

**What it is & why it matters:** The Army is set to raise its first **Integrated Battle Groups** — leaner, self-contained, mission-specific combat formations that fuse infantry, armour, artillery, air-defence, signals and logistics under a single commander for rapid mobilisation. It is a landmark in India's force-restructuring/"theaterisation" debate and a likely Prelims-cum-Mains item on defence modernisation.

**Key facts:**
- First IBGs to be raised from the Panagarh-based **XVII Corps (Mountain Strike Corps)** — a formation raised in 2013, oriented to the northern/eastern (China) border.
- Each IBG is to be **brigade-plus** sized, tailored to terrain and task, enabling faster offensive/defensive response than the traditional division-centric structure.
- Concept was field-tested via exercises from around 2018-19; this is the move from pilot to operational raising.
- Sits alongside the broader reform agenda (CDS, Department of Military Affairs, theatre commands).

**Destination notebook:** GS3 (Internal Security / Defence — security forces and their mandate, military reform).

## 5. AI-enabled Rural Internal Audit Portal launched at Rashtriya Gramin Vikas Sammelan

**Issuing body:** **Ministry of Rural Development** · **Date:** 28 June 2026, Pusa Campus, New Delhi

**What it is & why it matters:** An **AI-driven internal-audit portal** was launched to strengthen financial transparency and accountability across rural-development schemes (MGNREGA, PMAY-G, PMGSY, etc.). It reflects the governance push to embed technology and real-time auditing into welfare delivery and reduce leakages — useful as a GS2 governance example.

**Key facts:**
- Launched at the **Rashtriya Gramin Vikas Sammelan**, the ministry's annual flagship review conference for rural programmes.
- Uses AI tools for risk-based audit flagging of scheme expenditure at the gram-panchayat/block level.
- Complements existing accountability mechanisms (social audits, PFMS/DBT) under rural schemes.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 (Governance — transparency, accountability, e-governance, welfare delivery).

## Prelims MCQs

**Q1.** With reference to the *Santhal Hul*, commemorated as **Hul Diwas**, consider the following statements:
1. It was an 1855 uprising led by Sidhu and Kanhu Murmu against British rule and exploitation by moneylenders.
2. It began at Bhognadih village in present-day Jharkhand.
3. In its aftermath, the colonial government created the Santhal Pargana as a separate administrative district.

How many of the statements given above are correct?
(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) All three
(d) None

**Q2.** With reference to the **Index of Eight Core Industries (ICI)**, consider the following statements:
1. It is compiled and released by the Office of the Economic Adviser in the Ministry of Commerce & Industry.
2. The eight core industries together account for about 40% of the weight of items included in the Index of Industrial Production (IIP).
3. Petroleum Refinery Products has the highest weight among the eight core industries.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q3.** The **Upper Yamuna River Board (UYRB)** was recently in the news. Which one of the following is **NOT** a signatory to the 1994 Memorandum of Understanding under which the Board functions?
(a) Himachal Pradesh
(b) Rajasthan
(c) Madhya Pradesh
(d) NCT of Delhi

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (c) All three.** The Santhal Hul began on 30 June 1855 at Bhognadih (Sahebganj, present-day Jharkhand) under Sidhu and Kanhu Murmu against the British and the zamindar–moneylender nexus; its suppression led the colonial state to create the Santhal Pargana district.

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The ICI (base 2011-12) is released by the Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT (Ministry of Commerce & Industry); the eight industries carry 40.27% weight in the IIP; Petroleum Refinery Products has the single largest weight (~28%).

**Q3 — (c) Madhya Pradesh.** The 1994 Yamuna MoU was signed by Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan and NCT of Delhi (UYRB constituted 1995). Madhya Pradesh is not a party.
