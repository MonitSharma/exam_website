## 1. Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026 introduced in Lok Sabha

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions — Department of Personnel & Training (DoPT); moved by MoS (IC) **Dr Jitendra Singh**, 27 July 2026.

The Bill amending the **Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024** was formally introduced in the Lok Sabha today. It is the legislative follow-through on the Union Cabinet's approval earlier this week (covered on 25 July) — *what is new today is the actual introduction, the seven-amendment structure and the fast-track court design now on the table.* It responds directly to the NEET-UG 2026 paper-leak controversy.

**Key facts**

- **Seven amendments** proposed, built around time-bound investigation, fast-track trial, enhanced punishment and a stronger enforcement framework.
- Minimum imprisonment for paper-leak offences raised to **5–10 years** for individuals (earlier 3–5 years with fine up to ₹10 lakh); maximum punishment retained at **10 years**.
- Fine for **organised** examination fraud raised to **up to ₹10 crore** (earlier a minimum of ₹1 crore).
- States/UT administrations empowered to **designate any Court of Session as a Special Fast Track Court** for offences under the Act; investigation and trial expected to conclude within about **three months**.
- Business Advisory Committee allotted roughly **four hours** for discussion.

**Static hooks:** The parent Act of 2024 covers public examinations conducted by UPSC, SSC, RRBs, IBPS, NTA and Central Government departments; offences are **cognizable, non-bailable and non-compoundable**; it does *not* penalise candidates, only organised offenders and service providers.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (statutory bodies, criminal law amendments, education governance).

## 2. High-Powered Task Force on Examination Reforms constituted under Nandan Nilekani

**Ministry/Body:** Prime Minister's Office; announced by PM Narendra Modi on **26 July 2026**.

An executive-side complement to the Bill above: a high-powered task force to make the examination system — particularly **NTA-conducted examinations** — cleaner, more transparent and technology-driven. It follows the resignation of the Education Minister and the paper-leak protests.

**Key facts**

- **Chairperson:** Nandan Nilekani (co-founder, Infosys; architect of Aadhaar).
- **Members reported:** S. Somanath (former ISRO Chairman), Tapan Deka (former Director, Intelligence Bureau), V. Kamakoti (Director, IIT Madras), Anita Karwal (former Secretary, School Education) and Amrit Lal Meena (senior IAS officer, logistics).
- **Mandate:** structural reform of the examination system and maximum use of technology to eliminate irregularities; specific focus on revamping NTA examinations.

**Why it matters:** A classic UPSC governance case — executive committee + legislative amendment + enforcement machinery (the Delhi Police STF of 26 July) acting as three parallel prongs on the same problem. Useful for Mains answers on *"institutional trust deficit in public examinations."*

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (regulatory reform, statutory/executive bodies, education).

## 4. ICAR-CMFRI launches **DeepDATA** — mobile app for digital documentation of deep-sea fishes

**Ministry/Body:** ICAR–Central Marine Fisheries Research Institute (CMFRI), Kochi — developed under the **Deep Ocean Mission** with funding support from the **Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES)**. ([PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2288322&reg=48&lang=2))

A citizen-science platform that gives verified information on India's deep-sea fish species and lets users contribute field observations to a **national digital repository**.

**Key facts**

- Provides **taxonomy, distinguishing characteristics, distribution and conservation status**, with photographs; available on the Google Play Store.
- **Citizen-science model:** public submissions are verified by CMFRI experts before entering the national database.
- Developed by the IT Unit of ICAR-CMFRI; supports biodiversity assessment, conservation planning and sustainable fisheries management.
- **Deep Ocean Mission** — approved 2021, ~₹4,077 crore, nodal ministry **MoES**; six pillars including the **Matsya-6000** manned submersible (Samudrayaan), deep-sea mineral exploration and ocean biodiversity conservation.
- **ICAR-CMFRI** — established 1947, headquartered at **Kochi, Kerala**, under ICAR (Department of Agricultural Research & Education, Ministry of Agriculture).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Biodiversity / Science & Technology (marine biodiversity, Deep Ocean Mission, digital public goods in science).

## 6. FDI allowed in the inventory-based e-commerce model — but only for exports

**Ministry/Body:** Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), Ministry of Commerce & Industry.

DPIIT has carved out an exception to a long-standing prohibition: **restrictions on the inventory-based model of e-commerce will not apply to exports of goods manufactured or produced in India**. Foreign-funded platforms may now own, warehouse and directly export Indian-made goods.

**Key facts**

- **Earlier position:** 100% FDI under automatic route permitted only in **B2B e-commerce and the marketplace model**; FDI barred in **inventory-based/B2C** e-commerce, where the online retailer owns the inventory.
- **What changes:** the carve-out applies **exclusively to exports of domestically manufactured/produced goods**.
- **What does not change:** FDI remains **prohibited** in inventory-based B2C e-commerce for the **domestic market** — protection for small retailers is retained.
- **Implementation:** takes effect once amendments are notified under **FEMA, 1999** through the **Foreign Exchange Management (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules, 2019**.
- Rationale: expand outbound shipments and give Indian MSME manufacturers direct access to global consumers.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (FDI policy, external sector, e-commerce regulation).

1. It empowers State Governments and Union Territory Administrations to designate any Court of Session as a Special Fast Track Court for offences under the parent Act.

1. Foreign direct investment in the inventory-based model of e-commerce has been permitted exclusively for the export of goods manufactured or produced in India.
2. Foreign direct investment continues to be prohibited in inventory-based business-to-consumer e-commerce for the domestic market.
3. The revision is administered by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade and will take effect through amendments under the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

## Answers

**Q1 — (a).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. The Bill's seven amendments include empowering States/UTs to designate **any Court of Session as a Special Fast Track Court**, and raising the fine for organised paper-leak offences to **up to ₹10 crore** (minimum imprisonment for individuals also rises to 5–10 years). Statement 3 is wrong — the Bill was introduced by **Dr Jitendra Singh**, MoS (IC) for Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions (DoPT), not the Education Minister.

**Q2 — (b).** **DeepDATA** was launched by **ICAR-CMFRI, Kochi** under the **Deep Ocean Mission** with support from the **Ministry of Earth Sciences**. It provides verified taxonomy, distribution and conservation-status information on deep-sea fishes and allows the public to submit field observations, which CMFRI experts verify before adding to a national database.

**Q3 — (d).** All three are correct. DPIIT has carved out the inventory-based model **only for exports of domestically manufactured/produced goods**; the prohibition on FDI in inventory-based **B2C for the domestic market** stands (protecting small retailers); and the change operates through amendments under **FEMA, 1999** via the Foreign Exchange Management (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules, 2019.
