## 1. Ministry of Coal: **First sector-wide Comprehensive CSR Framework for Indian Coal Companies launched**

**What it is.** On 9 September 2026 the Ministry of Coal, with Coal India Limited (CIL), released a Comprehensive Corporate Social Responsibility Framework specifically for coal companies — described as the first sector-specific CSR framework for the coal sector since CSR became statutory under Section 135 of the Companies Act, 2013. It was prepared by the **Indian Institute of Corporate Affairs (IICA)**, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs' think-tank and capacity-building body on CSR.

**Why it matters.** Coal PSUs are among India's largest CSR spenders, but their spending has been criticised as scattered and input-driven. The framework pushes CSR in coalfield districts towards *outcome*-oriented, need-assessed, monitored projects — directly relevant to the "just transition" debate in coal-bearing states (Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Telangana, MP) where mining-affected communities bear the social and environmental costs.

**Key facts.**

- Launched in New Delhi in the presence of Union Minister for Coal and Mines **G. Kishan Reddy**, MoS **Satish Chandra Dubey**, and Coal Secretary **Vikram Dev Dutt**.
- Emphasis: local community need assessment, better project planning, monitoring, and measurable social outcomes.
- Released alongside milestones under CIL's health schemes — **Thalassemia Bal Sewa Yojana (TBSY)**, which has supported **over 1,050 bone marrow transplants** with assistance of up to **₹10 lakh per patient**, and **Nanha Sa Dil** (paediatric heart surgeries).

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (CSR, PSU accountability) · GS3 Economy (coal sector, just transition).

## 2. Department of Consumer Affairs: **Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026 notified — dark-pattern audits and price-history disclosure become mandatory**

**What it is.** The Department of Consumer Affairs (Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution) has notified amendments to the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020, framed under the **Consumer Protection Act, 2019**. The amended framework takes effect **1 January 2027**.

**Why it matters.** This is the first hard, rules-based enforcement of the **Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023**, which until now operated largely as advisory guidance backed by CCPA advisories. It converts self-regulation into a compliance obligation with an auditable paper trail — a standard GS2 "regulation of the digital economy" case study.

**Key features.**

- **Annual self-audit** by every e-commerce entity to certify its interface is free of dark patterns, with the **certificate displayed prominently** on the platform.
- **Prior price disclosure / price-history** requirements, aimed at fake-discount and inflated-MRP practices.
- **Search neutrality and sponsored-listing labelling** — paid placements must be identified as advertisements, and ranking parameters disclosed.
- **Consent safeguards** on consumer data, plus integration with public grievance redressal.
- Dark patterns covered include **drip pricing, basket sneaking, confirm shaming, subscription traps, false urgency and interface interference**.

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (regulation, consumer rights) · GS3 Economy (digital markets, e-commerce).

## 3. Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying: **PMMSY completes six years; record ₹2,500 crore allocation for 2026–27**

**What it is.** The **Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY)**, launched in 2020 as the flagship umbrella scheme for the fisheries sector, completed **six years on 10 September 2026**. Budget Estimates for 2026–27 carry **₹2,500 crore** — the highest annual allocation under the scheme so far.

**Why it matters.** India is among the world's largest fish producers and the second-largest aquaculture producer; fisheries is one of the fastest-growing sub-sectors of agriculture and a major source of rural livelihood and export earnings. The scheme is the standard anchor for any GS3 question on "blue revolution", allied-sector income diversification, or doubling farmer incomes.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY)**

- **Ministry:** Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying (Department of Fisheries).
- **Objective:** Sustainable, responsible and inclusive development of the fisheries sector — modernising the value chain from production to post-harvest and marketing, and raising the socio-economic welfare of fishers and fish farmers.
- **Outlay and period:** **₹20,750 crore**, for 2020–21 to 2026–27; implemented in **all States and UTs**.
- **Key features:** (i) enhancing fish production and productivity; (ii) post-harvest infrastructure, cold chain and market linkages to cut losses; (iii) traceability, insurance and livelihood support for fishers; (iv) a Central Sector sub-scheme, **PM-MKSSY** (Pradhan Mantri Matsya Kisan Samridhi Sah-Yojana), aimed at formalising fisheries micro and small enterprises.
- **Beneficiaries:** fishers, fish farmers, fish workers and vendors, FFPOs, and fisheries MSMEs.
- **Significance:** links India's blue-economy ambitions to nutrition security, rural employment and marine exports.

**Notebook:** GS3 Agriculture (allied sectors, blue economy) · GS3 Economy (schemes).

## 4. Ministry of External Affairs: **India hosts the 18th BRICS Summit at Bharat Mandapam, 12–13 September 2026** *(UPDATE — hosting was flagged on 28 August; what's new is the theme, agenda and scale of India's Chairship year)*

**What it is.** India, which assumed the **BRICS Chairship on 1 January 2026** (succeeding Brazil), hosts the **18th BRICS Summit** in New Delhi on **12–13 September 2026** at Bharat Mandapam. The Chairship theme is **"Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability."**

**Why it matters.** This is India's first BRICS Summit since the grouping's expansion, and it comes in a year of contested global-governance reform debate. It is a high-probability GS2 IR question — on plurilateral groupings, Global South representation, and reform of multilateral institutions.

**Key facts.**

- India's Chairship year has run **over 350 meetings across 25 cities**.
- Agenda priorities: **supply chains, cross-border payments, technology and AI, energy, climate action, digital cooperation, and reform of global governance**, with the Global South as the framing theme.
- BRICS expanded to **11 members** across 2024–25, adding **Iran, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Egypt, Ethiopia and Indonesia** to the original five.
- Leaders expected include Chinese President **Xi Jinping**, Russian President **Vladimir Putin** and Iranian President **Masoud Pezeshkian**.

**Notebook:** GS2 International Relations (groupings involving India, global governance reform).

## 5. Ministry of Education: **PM SHRI crosses 13,000 schools — about 90% of the envisaged target selected**

**What it is.** A PIB backgrounder on **PM SHRI (Pradhan Mantri Schools for Rising India)**, the Centrally Sponsored Scheme launched on **7 September 2022** to develop selected existing government schools into exemplar institutions demonstrating **NEP 2020** in practice, and to act as mentor schools for others in their neighbourhood.

**Why it matters.** PM SHRI is the clearest operational vehicle for NEP 2020 at school level, and also a live centre–state fiscal-federalism issue, since several states' participation has been tied to the scheme's conditionalities. Good material for GS2 (education governance, centre–state relations).

**Key facts (as of July 2026).**

- **13,092 schools selected** — about **90%** of the envisaged target of **14,500+** — across **725 districts** and **8,340 blocks/ULBs**.
- Expected to directly benefit **more than 20 lakh students**.
- Composition: **1,305 primary, 3,102 upper primary, 3,141 secondary, 5,544 higher secondary**, plus **80 KGBVs, 913 KVS, 620 NVS** and **4 NCERT** schools.
- Focus areas: competency-based and experiential learning, holistic progress cards, green-school practices, and integration with Vidyanjali and other NEP interventions.

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (education policy, NEP 2020, centre–state relations) · GS1/GS2 Society (human development).

## 6. Ministry of Commerce & Industry: **Five-point plan to make India a global medical value travel hub — target of 10 million medical tourists a year**

**What it is.** Speaking at the inauguration of **Sanjeevani 2026** at the **Bharat Health Global Expo**, New Delhi (7 September 2026), Union Commerce and Industry Minister **Piyush Goyal** set out a five-point action plan to build India's medical value travel (MVT) ecosystem around the **'Heal in India'** gateway.

**Why it matters.** MVT sits at the intersection of services exports, healthcare capacity and soft power. It is also a useful counterpoint in ethics/GS2 discussion — expanding international patient flows while domestic public-health capacity remains uneven.

**Key facts.**

- **Target: 10 million international medical value tourists a year**, against roughly **3.5 million patients** served over the last seven years.
- Action points include: training and **certifying caregivers** to international standards; bringing **more hospitals onto the 'Heal in India' gateway** with **transparent, published treatment packages**; and widening the offering beyond pharmaceuticals to hospitals, medical devices, diagnostics and traditional medicine.
- Positions India's advantage as cost plus a skilled pool of doctors, nurses and technicians, with AYUSH/traditional medicine as a differentiator.

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (services exports, healthcare industry) · GS2 Governance (health).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026, consider the following statements:

1. They are framed under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.
2. They require every e-commerce entity to conduct an annual self-audit certifying that its interface is free of dark patterns, and to display the certificate.
3. They come into force with immediate effect from the date of notification.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Consider the following statements about the Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY):

1. It is implemented by the Department of Fisheries under the Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying.
2. Pradhan Mantri Matsya Kisan Samridhi Sah-Yojana (PM-MKSSY) is a sub-scheme under it.
3. It has a total outlay of about ₹20,750 crore and is implemented in all States and Union Territories.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** With reference to the 18th BRICS Summit held in 2026, consider the following statements:

1. India assumed the BRICS Chairship in 2026, succeeding Brazil.
2. The Chairship theme is "Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability".
3. Indonesia is among the countries that joined BRICS during its expansion in 2024–25.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: the amended rules take effect from **1 January 2027**, not immediately on notification — the lead time is deliberate, to let platforms redesign interfaces and set up audit systems.

**Q2 — (d).** All three are correct. PMMSY is a Department of Fisheries umbrella scheme with an outlay of about ₹20,750 crore covering all States/UTs, and PM-MKSSY is a Central Sector sub-scheme under it aimed at formalising fisheries micro and small enterprises.

**Q3 — (d).** All three are correct. India took over the Chairship from Brazil on 1 January 2026; the theme is "Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability"; and Indonesia joined during the 2024–25 expansion that took BRICS to 11 members (along with Iran, the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Egypt and Ethiopia).
