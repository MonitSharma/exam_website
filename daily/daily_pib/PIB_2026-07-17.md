## 1. MoPSW launches "Seafarer-First" initiative — real-time tracking of every Indian seafarer in the West Asia conflict zone

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Ports, Shipping & Waterways (Union Minister Sarbananda Sonowal) / Directorate General of Shipping (DGS) · **Date:** 15–16 July 2026 · **Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (West Asia, diaspora/citizen protection) · GS3 — Internal Security (maritime security, SLOC/chokepoints) · Economy (energy & trade security)

Following attacks on the merchant vessels **MT Al Bahiyah** and **MT Mombasa** in the **Strait of Hormuz** — whose crews included **30 Indian seafarers out of 46** — the Ministry launched a "Seafarer-First" response to safeguard Indians operating in the **Persian Gulf, Strait of Hormuz and Gulf of Oman**. The DGS will run a **real-time, vessel-by-vessel operational dashboard tracking every Indian seafarer irrespective of the vessel's flag** — position, ownership, cargo, crew strength, threat assessment, intended voyage and next port of call — with dedicated liaison officers and coordination with Indian Missions in **Iran, Oman and the UAE**. A **24×7 helpline** for sailors and families has been set up (toll-free 1800-889-7768 in India; international toll-free and WhatsApp channels). In a parallel order, Indian seafarers are being kept off Hormuz-linked routes for now.

**Why it matters:** A textbook case study connecting India's ~larger-than-most seafaring workforce, chokepoint vulnerability (Hormuz — the same axis as the LPG/crude disruption covered on 4 July), and State responsibility for citizens abroad. Note the regulator: **DGS, the maritime administration under MoPSW** — a frequent prelims confusion with the Indian Navy/Coast Guard (who do escorting, not seafarer welfare).

## 2. India at CAC49 — seven Codex standards developed under Indian leadership adopted in Geneva

**Issuing body:** Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI), Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (delegation led by CEO Rajit Punhani, with the Spices Board) · **Date:** CAC49 held 6–10 July 2026, Geneva; PIB release 14 July 2026 · **Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (international institutions, standard-setting diplomacy) · GS3 — Agriculture/Economy (agri-exports, spices) · Science & Technology (food safety)

At the **49th Session of the Codex Alimentarius Commission (CAC49)** — the joint **FAO–WHO** international food-standards body — the Commission adopted **seven standards/guidelines developed under India's chairmanship or co-chairmanship**, including Codex standards for **dried coriander seeds** and **fresh curry leaves** (piloted by India), standards for **vanilla** and **large cardamom**, annexes to guidelines on **safe use and reuse of water** in food processing, guidelines on controlling **Campylobacter and Salmonella in chicken meat**, and labelling provisions for **multipack formats**.

**Key facts:** India has **chaired the Codex Committee on Spices and Culinary Herbs (CCSCH) since 2014** — the committee India hosts — and earlier steered global standards for millets and fresh dates. Codex standards are the WTO-recognised (SPS Agreement) benchmark for food-trade disputes, so India-led standards directly ease its spice and agri-export interests.

## 3. Railways expands "Reform Express" — eight more structural reforms to modernise freight operations

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Railways (Union Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw) · **Date:** 14 July 2026 · **Notebook:** GS3 — Economy/Infrastructure (logistics, ease of doing business)

The Railway Minister unveiled **eight new structural reforms** under the **"Reform Express"** initiative — taking the tally to **17 of a targeted "52 reforms in 52 weeks"**. Highlights: a **containerised fly-ash transportation** system using ISO-standard containers; a **unified pan-India Container Train Operator (CTO) licence** replacing the earlier four-category system; a simplified **per-tonne-per-kilometre freight rate** for foodgrains, flour and pulses (replacing slab-based rates) with containerised movement allowed; **liberalised petroleum transport** (oil companies may procure/lease specialised tank wagons); and a comprehensive **artisan-skilling policy** for railway works. Separately on 15 July, the **Union Cabinet approved two railway multitracking projects** across four districts of Odisha and Jharkhand.

**Why it matters:** Feeds directly into the GS3 logistics-cost narrative (National Logistics Policy target of cutting logistics cost, raising rail freight modal share). Good mains fodder on "process reform over budget outlay."

## 4. NABARD and NSDC launch "Gramodyam" — 4,000 rural entrepreneurs in three years

**Issuing body:** NABARD (launched on its 45th Foundation Day) with the National Skill Development Corporation (NSDC), Ministry of Skill Development & Entrepreneurship; executed by the Institute for Industrial Development · **Date:** 13 July 2026 · **Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture/Economy (rural credit, entrepreneurship, skilling) · GS2 — Governance (institutional convergence)

### Scheme-in-focus: Gramodyam

- **Ministry/Body:** NABARD + NSDC (under MSDE); execution by Institute for Industrial Development.
- **Objective:** Identify, train and hand-hold **4,000 young rural entrepreneurs over three years** to establish sustainable enterprises in villages and small towns.
- **Key features:** (1) **Digital-first hybrid delivery** — online learning plus field-based mentoring; (2) **psychometric assessment and counselling** before entrepreneurship development training; (3) industry-specific skill programmes with **credit facilitation and market linkages**; (4) end-to-end support from business planning to enterprise setup.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** Aspiring young entrepreneurs in rural India, pan-India rollout.
- **Significance:** Converges NABARD's rural-credit mandate with NSDC's skilling network — aligned to Atmanirbhar Bharat, Skill India, Startup India and Viksit Bharat 2047. Distinct from the Agriculture Ministry's PRAGATI agri-entrepreneur cadre (covered 12–13 July): Gramodyam is enterprise-generic and NABARD-led, not regenerative-agriculture-specific.

## 5. Q1 FY27 trade data — total exports $232.73 billion, up 11.37%; deficit widens to $37.42 billion

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Commerce & Industry · **Date:** released 13 July 2026 · **Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (external sector) · Reports & Indices

India's **total exports (goods + services) in Q1 FY27 (April–June 2026) rose 11.37% to USD 232.73 billion** (from $208.98 bn in Q1 FY26). **Merchandise exports grew 15.92% to $129.32 bn**, led by engineering goods and gems & jewellery; **non-petroleum exports rose 12.44% to $106.30 bn**, signalling basket diversification. But **imports grew faster — 17.55% to $270.15 bn** — widening the **trade deficit to $37.42 bn**. The government retains a **$1-trillion total-exports target for FY27**.

**Update on an earlier item:** the **India–UK CETA formally entered into force on 15 July 2026** (its Customs Tariff origin rules were covered in the 7 July briefing) — remember the pair: CETA + Double Contribution Convention (social security).

## Answers

**A1 — (b).** Statements 1 and 3 are correct: MoPSW (Minister Sarbananda Sonowal) launched it after the MT Al Bahiyah and MT Mombasa attacks in the Strait of Hormuz. Statement 2 is wrong — the DGS dashboard tracks every Indian seafarer **irrespective of the vessel's flag**.

**A2 — (d).** All three are correct: Codex is the joint FAO–WHO food-standards body; India has chaired the CCSCH since 2014; and CAC49 (Geneva, 6–10 July 2026) adopted seven India-led standards including dried coriander seeds and fresh curry leaves.

**A3 — (b).** Gramodyam is a NABARD (45th Foundation Day) + NSDC three-year programme for 4,000 rural entrepreneurs with a digital-first hybrid model. Option (a) describes PRAGATI; (c) describes LokOS; (d) is a distractor.
