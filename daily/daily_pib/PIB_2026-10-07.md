## 1. Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports: **India finishes 4th at the 20th Asian Games, Aichi-Nagoya, with 85 medals (21 gold, 27 silver, 37 bronze)** (19 Sep – 4 Oct 2026)

**What / why it matters:** The Games closed on 4 October. India's haul is below Hangzhou 2023 (106 medals, 28 gold) but still a top-four finish; the PIB-based sports summary uses it to highlight the policy ecosystem behind sport. Useful for Prelims "sports events/ policy" and a GS2 (governance of sport) hook.

**Key facts:**
- Host: Aichi Prefecture and Nagoya, Japan; dates 19 Sep – 4 Oct 2026.
- India's rank: 4th, behind China, Japan and South Korea.
- Gold sources included archery, shooting, boxing, cricket, hockey and kabaddi (women's cricket, women's hockey and both kabaddi events were among the golds).
- Policy anchors cited in the PIB-based summary (single secondary source, figures not independently verified): Khelo Bharat Niti (July 2025), National Sports Governance Act 2025, revamped Khelo India outlay for 2026-31, Sports Ministry BE 2026-27 about ₹4,480 crore.
- Next: 5th Asian Para Games, 18–24 October 2026.

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (sports policy) / Prelims current affairs.

## 2. Ministry of Defence (DRDO): **Military Combat Parachute System (MCPS) — indigenous high-altitude system proven at 22,000 ft in Ladakh** (trial 26 Aug 2026; revisited in 7 Oct PIB-linked summaries)

**What / why it matters:** Developed by ADRDE, Agra (DRDO) with DEBEL, Bengaluru; a test jump at Nyoma-Mudh Drop Zone, Leh district, Ladakh. Relevant to self-reliance in defence (Aatmanirbhar Bharat) and high-altitude operations. *Not a fresh release — the trial is six weeks old.*

**Key facts:**
- Exit at 22,000 ft above MSL, parachute deployed ~20,000 ft, landing at ~13,700 ft; about −15°C.
- System components: para computer, oxygen apparatus, thermal jumpsuits, NavIC integration.
- Reported as tested up to 32,000 ft.
- Separate item reported 7 Oct: an "Advanced High-Altitude Parachute (AHAP)" static-line system for mass paratroop drops (ADRDE/Ordnance Parachute Factory Kanpur, CEMILAC-certified) — **could not be verified from independent sources; treat as unconfirmed.**

**Notebook:** GS3 Internal Security / Defence technology.

## 3 Prelims MCQs

**Q1.** With reference to India's performance at the 20th Asian Games (2026), consider the statements:
1. The Games were hosted by Aichi Prefecture and Nagoya in Japan.
2. India finished 4th in the medal tally with 85 medals, including 21 golds.
3. India improved on its Hangzhou 2023 medal total.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only (b) 2 and 3 only (c) 1 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q2.** Consider the following statements about the Press Council of India:
1. It is a statutory quasi-judicial authority established under the Press Council Act, 1978.
2. Its Chairman is traditionally a retired judge of the Supreme Court.
3. It is a constitutional body under Article 19 of the Constitution.
Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q3.** The Military Combat Parachute System (MCPS), recently in the news, was developed by:
(a) Gas Turbine Research Establishment, Bengaluru
(b) Aerial Delivery Research and Development Establishment, Agra
(c) Defence Research and Development Laboratory, Hyderabad
(d) Combat Vehicles Research and Development Establishment, Chennai

## Answers
1. **(a)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: Hangzhou 2023 gave India 106 medals (28 gold), so 85 is a decline.
2. **(b)** — It is statutory (not constitutional); Article 19(1)(a) press freedom is what it seeks to preserve. Statements 1 and 2 are correct.
3. **(b)** — ADRDE, Agra (DRDO) developed MCPS, with DEBEL Bengaluru contributing physiological components.
