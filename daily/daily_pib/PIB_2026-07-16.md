## 1. Union Cabinet clears "Semicon 2.0" and Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme in a ₹2.19 lakh crore package

**Issuing body:** Union Cabinet / Ministry of Electronics & IT (MeitY) · **Date:** approved 15 July 2026 · **Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (manufacturing, PLI-type incentives) · Science & Technology (electronics/semiconductors)

The Cabinet approved a slate of decisions with a combined outlay of about **₹2,19,353 crore**, headlined by the **Semicon 2.0 programme (₹1,27,500 crore)** — the second phase of the India Semiconductor Mission to deepen the domestic chip-fabrication and design ecosystem — and a **Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme (MPMS) of ₹62,500 crore** to sustain India's electronics-export momentum after the earlier PLI for large-scale electronics. Why it matters: it signals continuity of India's "self-reliance in electronics" push — moving from assembly toward fabrication, components and design — and is directly relevant to supply-chain security and the electronics trade balance.

**Key facts:** Semicon 2.0 outlay ₹1,27,500 cr; MPMS ₹62,500 cr; total Cabinet package ~₹2,19,353 cr. The same meeting cleared the National Investment Policy for Urea (below) and railway projects — the doubling of the **Paradeep–Haridaspur** line (₹2,542 cr) and a fourth line between **Dangoaposi–Rajkharsawan** (₹1,365 cr) — plus elevated corridors along the Varuna and Ganga rivers in Varanasi.

## 2. Cabinet approves National Investment Policy for Urea (NIPU-2026) — targeting self-sufficiency

**Issuing body:** Union Cabinet / Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers (Department of Fertilizers) · **Date:** approved 15 July 2026 · **Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (fertilizer security, input subsidy) · Economy (import substitution)

**NIPU-2026** aims to add **10 million tonnes** of fresh domestic urea capacity through **8–9 new gas-based plants**, closing the country's structural import gap. India produces roughly **30 million tonnes** against demand of about **40 million tonnes**, with the ~10 MT shortfall currently met by imports; six new plants added over the last decade already cut import dependence. Why it matters: urea is India's most-consumed fertilizer and a major subsidy and forex outgo — domestic self-sufficiency improves food-security resilience and reduces exposure to global price/supply shocks.

### Scheme-in-focus: National Investment Policy for Urea (NIPU-2026)
- **Ministry:** Chemicals & Fertilizers (Department of Fertilizers).
- **Objective:** Make India self-reliant in urea by incentivising fresh gas-based capacity.
- **Key features:** (i) target of ~10 MT new capacity via 8–9 plants; (ii) separation of fixed and variable costs for transparency; (iii) a return-on-equity band of **12–16%**; (iv) forex-risk mitigation by converting fixed costs into rupees after four years.
- **Coverage/benefit:** Estimated savings of **over ₹250 crore per plant** versus the earlier 2012 policy; strengthens fertilizer supply for farmers nationwide.
- **Significance:** Reduces import dependence for the single largest fertilizer, supporting both fiscal (subsidy) and agricultural resilience.

## 3. BRCP Phase-III launched — ₹1,500 crore DBT–Wellcome Trust biomedical research programme

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Science & Technology (Department of Biotechnology) with the Wellcome Trust, UK · **Date:** launched 15 July 2026 (Union Minister Dr Jitendra Singh) · **Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (research ecosystem, health R&D)

Phase-III of the **Biomedical Research Career Programme (BRCP)** carries a total outlay of **₹1,500 crore — ₹1,000 crore from DBT and ₹500 crore from the Wellcome Trust** — delivered through the **DBT–Wellcome Trust India Alliance**. The programme funds scientists across career stages (early-career to senior fellowships) to build India's biomedical research base. Why it matters: it is a flagship public–philanthropic research partnership (running since 2008-09; Phase-II from 2018-19) and reflects the push to grow India's bioeconomy — which the government cites as rising from ~US$10 bn (2014) to over US$195 bn (2025), targeting US$300 bn by 2030.

**Key facts:** Total ₹1,500 cr (DBT ₹1,000 cr + Wellcome Trust ₹500 cr); implemented via India Alliance; Cabinet-approved before launch.

## 4. Centre of Excellence on Human–Wildlife Conflict inaugurated at WII-SACON, Coimbatore; National Portal launched

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Environment, Forest & Climate Change (Union Minister Bhupender Yadav) · **Date:** inaugurated 10 July 2026 · **Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (biodiversity, conservation, human-wildlife conflict)

The Minister inaugurated a **Centre of Excellence (CoE) on Human–Wildlife Conflict** at the **Wildlife Institute of India–Sálim Ali Centre for Ornithology and Natural History (WII-SACON)**, Coimbatore, and launched a **National Human-Wildlife Conflict Portal** plus the first edition of *Current Status of Human-Wildlife Conflict in India: An Overview*. The CoE is envisaged as a national hub for research, policy support, capacity-building and best-practice sharing on conflict involving elephants, leopards, tigers and bears. Why it matters: rising human-wildlife conflict is a key conservation and rural-livelihood challenge; a data-driven national portal and dedicated institution strengthen evidence-based mitigation.

**Key facts:** Hosted at WII-SACON, Coimbatore (Tamil Nadu); National Human-Wildlife Conflict Portal for data/knowledge sharing; followed by a National Workshop on Human–Wildlife Conflict.

## 5. NAMASTE Day 2026 marks profiling and safety milestones for sanitation workers

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment (with Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs) · **Date:** NAMASTE Day observed 14 July 2026, Kolkata · **Notebook:** GS1 — Society (dignity of labour, social justice) · GS2 — Governance (welfare delivery)

**NAMASTE (National Action for Mechanised Sanitation Ecosystem)** — launched 2023 to end hazardous manual cleaning of sewers and septic tanks — reported cumulative milestones on its second NAMASTE Day: about **89,248 sewer and septic-tank workers profiled**, roughly **87,000 PPE kits distributed**, and over **75,000 workers linked to health insurance** (AB-PMJAY). Why it matters: the scheme operationalises the goal of "zero fatalities" in sanitation work through mechanisation, occupational-safety training, capital subsidy for equipment, and formalisation via occupational identity cards.

**Key facts:** Profiling covers Sewer/Septic Tank Workers (SSWs); components include PPE kits, safety devices for Emergency Response Sanitation Units (ERSUs), health-insurance linkage under AB-PMJAY, and capital subsidy for sanitation vehicles/equipment; national event held in Kolkata, West Bengal.

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the recently approved **"Semicon 2.0"** programme, consider the following:
1. It is implemented under the Ministry of Electronics & Information Technology.
2. It is the second phase of the India Semiconductor Mission.
3. It was approved by the Union Cabinet along with a Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q2.** With reference to the **National Investment Policy for Urea (NIPU-2026)**, consider the following statements:
1. It aims to add about 10 million tonnes of fresh domestic urea capacity through new gas-based plants.
2. It is administered by the Department of Fertilizers under the Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers.
3. India currently exports urea and NIPU-2026 seeks to raise these exports.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q3.** The **Centre of Excellence on Human–Wildlife Conflict**, inaugurated in July 2026, is hosted at which of the following institutions?
(a) Wildlife Institute of India – Sálim Ali Centre for Ornithology and Natural History (WII-SACON), Coimbatore
(b) Indian Institute of Science, Bengaluru
(c) Bombay Natural History Society, Mumbai
(d) National Centre for Biological Sciences, Bengaluru

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Semicon 2.0 (₹1,27,500 cr) is MeitY's second phase of the India Semiconductor Mission, approved by the Cabinet on 15 July 2026 alongside the Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme (₹62,500 cr).

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** NIPU-2026 targets ~10 MT of new gas-based urea capacity (8–9 plants) under the Department of Fertilizers. Statement 3 is wrong — India is a urea *importer* (produces ~30 MT vs ~40 MT demand); the policy aims at import substitution, not exports.

**Q3 — (a) WII-SACON, Coimbatore.** The Environment Ministry inaugurated the CoE at the Wildlife Institute of India–Sálim Ali Centre for Ornithology and Natural History and launched the National Human-Wildlife Conflict Portal.
