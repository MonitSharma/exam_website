## 1. Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation (NSO): **First-ever district-level estimates for the unincorporated non-agricultural sector released**

**What it is.** MoSPI's National Statistics Office has, for the first time, disseminated **district-level estimates from a large-scale nationwide enterprise survey**, drawing on the **Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises (ASUSE) 2025**. Until now such data existed only at State/UT level (and, recently, for million-plus cities). It is a significant step towards **granular sub-state statistics** for evidence-based, area-specific policymaking.

**Why it matters.** The unincorporated sector — proprietary and partnership establishments outside the Companies Act — is the informal backbone of employment. District-level GVA and workforce numbers let planners target interventions rather than average them away across a State.

**Key facts and figures**

- Estimates cover **757 of the 770 districts** in the ASUSE 2025 sampling frame. Separate estimates are **not** given for Lakshadweep and Chandigarh (single-district UTs, already in the UT-level report) or for **Delhi** (rural and urban strata were pooled State-wide).
- The **top 10 districts** by number of establishments account for about **one-tenth** of all establishments, workers and GVA; the **top 50 districts** account for **nearly one-third**.
- The top 10 districts are spread across **four States — Gujarat, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal**; the top 50 span **twelve States**.
- Roughly **one-third of districts** have more than **1 lakh** unincorporated establishments; about **9%** have fewer than 10,000. Only **16 districts** exceed **5 lakh** establishments (8 in West Bengal, 3 in Maharashtra, 2 in Gujarat, 1 each in Karnataka, Telangana and Uttar Pradesh).
- Women make up **one-third of the workforce in 237 districts**. **GVA per worker** lies between ₹1–1.5 lakh in about **331 districts** and exceeds the all-India average in **280 districts**.

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (informal sector, employment) / Reports & Indices.

## 2. Prime Minister's Office / Department of Space: **PM addresses the first International Space Summit, Paris, by video conference**

**What it is.** The Prime Minister addressed the **first International Space Summit** hosted in **Paris, France**, convened on the initiative of **President Emmanuel Macron**. India's stated position: a **safe, secure, peaceful and sustainable space domain anchored in international law, dialogue and multilateral cooperation**.

**Why it matters.** Space governance is the live frontier of international law — orbital crowding, debris, resource use and dual-use technology. India is positioning itself as a bridge-builder ("cooperation over confrontation, sustainability over short-term gain, inclusion over exclusion") and framing its approach through **Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam**.

**Key facts and figures**

- India has launched **more than 430 payloads from 34 countries** aboard domestic rockets.
- The PM stressed that **scientific data must serve the common good, with openness and integrity**, and pointed to satellite applications in weather forecasting, disaster management and navigation reaching farmers and fishermen.
- ISRO was highlighted for excellence and **cost-effective missions**.

**Notebook:** GS2 International Relations (multilateral space governance) / GS3 Science & Technology.

## 3. Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare: **9th ASEAN–India Ministerial Meeting on Agriculture and Forestry (AIMMAF), New Delhi**

**What it is.** The **9th AIMMAF** was held in New Delhi on **9 September 2026**, co-chaired by India's Agriculture Minister **Shri Shivraj Singh Chouhan** and the **Philippines'** Agriculture Secretary (representing the ASEAN AMAF chair). The Meeting looked forward to adopting the **ASEAN–India Medium-Term Plan of Action for Cooperation in Agriculture and Forestry, 2026–2030**, succeeding the 2021–2025 plan.

**Why it matters.** Agriculture and forestry are a "practical and mutually beneficial" limb of the **ASEAN–India Comprehensive Strategic Partnership**. The framing — input-supply resilience (fertilizers, seeds, pesticides), transboundary pests and animal disease, low-emission rice — maps directly onto food-security and climate questions in the GS3 syllabus.

**Key facts and figures**

- The new **MTPA 2026–2030** has **four areas**: (i) policy dialogue, food security, trade, investment and resilient value chains; (ii) institutional networking, information exchange and digital innovation; (iii) capacity building, technology cooperation and joint R&D; (iv) human-resource development, farmer exchanges and inclusive rural enterprise.
- The Meeting welcomed **ASEAN 2045: Our Shared Future**, the **ASEAN Community Vision 2045**, and the **ASEAN Food, Agriculture and Forestry Sectoral Plan 2026–2030**.
- Achievements recalled from 2021–25: the **ASEAN–India Joint Leaders' Statement on Strengthening Food Security and Nutrition in Response to Crises**; **ASEAN–India Millets Festivals** (Jakarta and New Delhi, 2023); and the **ASEAN–India Fellowship for Higher Education in Agriculture and Allied Sciences** launched in 2024 — **50 Master's fellowships over five years** for ASEAN students.
- Priority themes for the new plan include **low-emission rice and water productivity**, AI/IoT in precision agriculture, soil health and bio-inputs, alternatives to **open crop-residue burning**, circular bioeconomy, agroforestry and forest-landscape restoration.
- The **10th AIMMAF** will be convened in **2028**.

**Notebook:** GS2 International Relations (India–ASEAN) / GS3 Agriculture.

## 4. Ministry of Communications (DoT): **Digital Bharat Nidhi signs Jharkhand agreement for the Amended BharatNet Program — ₹1,684 crore**

**What it is.** **Digital Bharat Nidhi (DBN)** under the Department of Telecommunications signed an agreement at Sanchar Bhawan with the **Government of Jharkhand**, the newly created SPV **Jharkhand Digital Infrastructure Corporation Limited (JDICL)**, **BSNL** and **Jharkhand Communication Network Limited (JCNL)** to implement the **Amended BharatNet Program (ABP)** in Jharkhand under the **State-led model**.

**Why it matters.** ABP is the rural broadband backbone; the State-led model and the shift from **linear to ring topology** are the substantive points — ring topology gives network resilience (a single fibre cut no longer isolates a Gram Panchayat).

**Key facts and figures**

- Central financial support of **₹1,684 crore** for Jharkhand.
- Scope: **4,395 Gram Panchayats** — **4,387 upgraded from linear to ring topology** plus **8 newly created GPs**; **26,777 villages** to get connectivity **on demand**.
- Expected to enable **over 4 lakh rural home fibre connections** with central subsidy support.
- The **Union Cabinet approved the Amended BharatNet Program on 4 August 2023**.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Digital Bharat Nidhi (DBN)**

- **Ministry/body:** Department of Telecommunications, Ministry of Communications (DBN is an **attached office** of DoT).
- **Objective:** Support **universal service** — promoting access to and delivery of telecom services in **underserved rural, remote and urban areas**.
- **Lineage:** The **Universal Service Obligation Fund (USOF)** was established under the **Indian Telegraph (Amendment) Act, 2003**, effective **1 April 2002**; under **The Telecommunications Act, 2023**, USOF **became the Digital Bharat Nidhi**.
- **Projects under DBN:** BharatNet; **4G Saturation Project**; mobile service in uncovered areas of **Aspirational Districts**; mobile services in **Left Wing Extremism-affected areas**, **Himalayan and border areas**, **islands**, and the **North-East**.

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (digital infrastructure, Centre–State delivery models) / GS3 Economy (infrastructure).

## 5. Ministry of New & Renewable Energy: **Small Hydro Power pitched as a distinct pillar of the clean-energy transition; ₹2,584.60 crore SHP Development Scheme proposed**

**What it is.** MoS (New & Renewable Energy and Power) **Shri Shripad Yesso Naik** addressed the curtain-raiser for the dedicated **Small Hydro Power (SHP)** session at the **Bharat Renewable Energy Summit & Expo 2026** (to be held **2–5 November 2026** at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi).

**Why it matters.** SHP is the underused corner of India's renewable portfolio: **run-of-river** designs need relatively little land and avoid the large-scale submergence and displacement of big reservoir projects, and they supply **decentralised** power close to consumption in geographically difficult regions.

**Key facts and figures**

- India's **assessed SHP potential is about 21 GW across more than 7,100 identified sites**; only about **5.2 GW** has been harnessed.
- India has crossed **300 GW of installed non-fossil-fuel capacity**.
- **Proposed Small Hydro Power Development Scheme, 2026-27 to 2030-31:** outlay **₹2,584.60 crore**, target of about **1,500 MW** of new SHP capacity, plus incentives to States to prepare **DPRs for about 200 projects**.
- MNRE Secretary **Shri Santosh Kumar Sarangi** flagged **long lead times** — delays in forest, environment and right-of-way clearances — as the sector's key execution bottleneck.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment / Economy (energy transition, infrastructure).

## 6. Ministry of Science & Technology (DST–BSIP): **Bakhira Lake sediments yield a 25,000-year monsoon record for the Central Ganga Plain**

**What it is.** Researchers at the **Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences (BSIP)**, an autonomous institute of the **Department of Science and Technology**, used **environmental magnetic properties** of sediments beneath **Bakhira Lake** (Sant Kabir Nagar, Uttar Pradesh) — combined with grain-size, geochemical and clay-mineralogical data — to reconstruct **Indian Summer Monsoon (ISM)** variability since the **Last Glacial Maximum**.

**Why it matters.** Long, high-resolution palaeoclimate records from the **Central Ganga Plain** are scarce and mostly confined to the Holocene. A well-dated 25,000-year archive improves **monsoon models** and, downstream, **water-resource management** in an intensely agricultural region.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Bakhira Lake** lies in the **Ghaghara–Rapti alluvial system** and is a perennial **oxbow/floodplain wetland** formed by the migration and cut-off of the **Rapti River**. One of **Uttar Pradesh's largest wetlands**, it was designated a **Ramsar Site in 2022**.
- The sequence extends to about **25 ka**, constrained by **seven AMS radiocarbon dates**.
- Climatic intervals identified: **Last Glacial Maximum / Heinrich Stadial 1** (~25.3–18.1 cal ka BP, cold-dry, weak ISM); **Bølling–Allerød** (~15.3–12.8 cal ka BP, warm-humid, enhanced ISM); **Younger Dryas** (~12.8–11.1 cal ka BP, cooling and weakened monsoon); **Holocene Climatic Optimum** (~9.2–4 cal ka BP, warm-wet, enhanced precipitation); **late Holocene droughts** (~4–2 cal ka BP).
- Published in *Palaeogeography, Palaeoclimatology, Palaeoecology*.

**Notebook:** GS1 Geography (monsoon, fluvial geomorphology) / GS3 Environment (wetlands, climate).

## Answers

**Q1 — (a).** DBN is the erstwhile USOF (created under the Indian Telegraph (Amendment) Act, 2003, effective 1 April 2002), renamed under the Telecommunications Act, 2023, and is an attached office of DoT. Statement 3 is wrong: besides BharatNet, DBN covers the 4G Saturation Project and mobile services in Aspirational Districts, LWE-affected areas, Himalayan and border areas, islands and the North-East.

**Q2 — (d).** All three are correct. Bakhira, one of UP's largest wetlands, is a perennial oxbow/floodplain wetland formed by the cut-off of the Rapti River, was declared a Ramsar Site in 2022, and the 25,000-year monsoon reconstruction was done by the Birbal Sahni Institute of Palaeosciences (BSIP), an autonomous institute of DST.

**Q3 — (d).** All three are correct. Of the ~21 GW assessed potential across 7,100+ sites, about 5.2 GW is harnessed — close to one-quarter; the proposed 2026-27 to 2030-31 scheme carries a ₹2,584.60 crore outlay with a ~1,500 MW target (plus DPR support for ~200 projects); and India has crossed 300 GW of non-fossil installed capacity.
