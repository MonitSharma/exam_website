## 1. Department of Space / PMO: **GSLV-F17 places EOS-05 in orbit — India's first imaging satellite from geosynchronous orbit**

**What it is.** ISRO successfully launched **GSLV-F17** carrying **EOS-05**, a state-of-the-art Earth observation spacecraft, from the **Second Launch Pad at Satish Dhawan Space Centre, SHAR (Sriharikota)**. The PM lauded the mission on 4 September, calling EOS-05 "India's first-ever imaging satellite from Geosynchronous orbit."

**Why it matters.** Almost all Indian remote-sensing satellites so far have flown in **low Earth / sun-synchronous polar orbits**, which give high resolution but revisit a given spot only once every few days. A geosynchronous imaging platform sits ~36,000 km up and stares **continuously at the same region** — persistent, near-real-time observation of the Indian subcontinent. That is a qualitative change for flood, cyclone, forest-fire and border monitoring, and for agriculture and water-resource assessment.

**Key facts.**

- GSLV-F17 is the **19th flight of the GSLV**; payload fairing was the **4-metre-diameter Ogive (composite)** version.
- EOS-05 was injected into a **Sub-Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit (Sub-GTO)** — *not* directly into a circular geostationary orbit; the satellite raises itself to its final slot using onboard propulsion.
- The PM also flagged the deepening **ISRO–Indian industry partnership** as adding "new strength and scale" to the space programme.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Space).

## 2. Ministry of Home Affairs: **28th meeting of the Western Zonal Council to be chaired by Amit Shah in Panaji on 7 September**

**What it is.** The Union Home Minister and Minister of Cooperation will chair the **28th Western Zonal Council** meeting in **Panaji, Goa**, on Monday, 7 September 2026. Organised by the **Inter-State Council Secretariat, MHA**, and hosted by the Government of Goa.

**Why it matters.** This is the cleanest current-affairs peg you will get for the **Zonal Councils** — a favourite Prelims/GS2 topic on federal coordination machinery. Note carefully that Zonal Councils are **statutory** (not constitutional) bodies, and their role is **advisory**, not binding.

**Key facts.**

- **Composition of the Western Zonal Council:** Goa, Gujarat, Maharashtra + the UT of **Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu**.
- **Statutory basis:** Five Zonal Councils established under **Sections 15 to 22 of the States Reorganisation Act, 1956**. (The **North Eastern Council** is separate — it is a creation of the North Eastern Council Act, 1971.)
- **Chairman:** the Union Home Minister for all five Councils. **Vice-Chairman:** a Chief Minister of a member State, rotating annually — here the CM of Goa.
- **Members:** the Chief Ministers of each member State plus **two senior Ministers from each State**.
- A **Standing Committee of Chief Secretaries** filters issues first; only unresolved ones go up to the Council.
- **70 meetings** of Zonal Councils and their Standing Committees have been held **since June 2014**.
- Recent agenda themes: speeding up investigation of rape cases against women and children, roll-out of **Fast Track Special Courts (FTSCs)**, and the **Emergency Response Support System**.
- Framing used: "TEAM BHARAT", cooperative *and* competitive federalism.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Centre–State relations, federal coordination bodies).

## 3. PIB Backgrounder (Ministry of Finance / NCGTC): **Emergency Credit Line Guarantee Scheme (ECLGS) 5.0**

**What it is.** A PIB Research Unit backgrounder issued 5 September on **ECLGS 5.0**, approved by the Government on **5 May 2026** to shield businesses from liquidity stress caused by external/geopolitical disruptions to supply chains and logistics costs.

**Why it matters.** ECLGS was a COVID-era instrument; its revival in 2026 for a *trade-shock* context is a live example of the State using **credit guarantees rather than direct subsidy** to keep working capital flowing. Expect questions on the guarantee-versus-subsidy design and on which sectors are excluded.

### Scheme-in-focus: **ECLGS 5.0**

- **Name & ministry:** Emergency Credit Line Guarantee Scheme 5.0; Ministry of Finance, implemented by the **National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company (NCGTC)**.
- **Objective:** Government-backed credit guarantees to **Member Lending Institutions (MLIs)** so they can extend *additional working capital* to eligible borrowers, targeting an **additional credit flow of up to ₹2.55 lakh crore**.
- **Validity:** operational until **31 March 2027**, or until guarantees of ₹2.55 lakh crore are issued — whichever is earlier.
- **Who is covered:** MSMEs (all sectors), eligible **non-MSME** business borrowers, and **scheduled passenger airline companies**. Borrowers must have had working-capital facilities as on **31 March 2026**, with repayments not overdue beyond **60 days**.
- **Guarantee cover:** **100%** for eligible MSMEs; **90%** for eligible non-MSMEs and for scheduled passenger airlines. **No guarantee fee** payable by MLIs.
- **Quantum:** MSMEs/non-MSMEs — up to **20%** of peak fund-based working-capital outstanding in Q4 FY 2025-26, ceiling **₹100 crore per borrower**. Airlines — up to **100%**, ceiling **₹1,500 crore per borrower** (amounts above ₹1,000 crore require proportionate promoter equity).
- **Interest ceiling:** MSMEs priced off **EBLR**, non-MSMEs off **MCLR**, with up to 0.75% spread and an overall cap of **9% p.a.**; NBFC loans capped at **13% p.a.**
- **Tenure:** 5 years including a 1-year moratorium (MSME/non-MSME); **7 years including a 2-year moratorium** for airlines.
- **Excluded non-MSME sectors:** NBFCs, power (generation/transmission/distribution), telecom service providers, sugar and ethanol, IT companies, paper and paper products, educational institutions, beverages (excluding tea and coffee) and tobacco.
- **Uptake (as on 20 August 2026):** **6,73,979 guarantees** worth **₹2,50,024 crore**; MSMEs were **97.3%** of guarantees by number and **80.79%** by value. Access is through the **Jan Samarth Portal**.
- **Historical anchor:** ECLGS 1.0–4.0 (2020–2023, under *Aatmanirbhar Bharat*) issued **1.19 crore guarantees worth ₹3.68 lakh crore** before concluding on 31 March 2023. ECLGS 2.0 used the **Kamath Committee's 26 stressed sectors**; 3.0 covered hospitality/travel/aviation; 4.0 covered healthcare and oxygen manufacturing.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (banking, MSME credit, government guarantees).

## 4. Ministry of Coal: **₹37,500 crore Surface Coal/Lignite Gasification Scheme — Ministry rebuts "no takers" reports**

**What it is.** Responding to press reports that the scheme had attracted **zero applications**, the Ministry of Coal clarified on 5 September that the claim is **premature**, since the first application window was still open (deadline **7 September 2026**), and that **Talcher Fertilisers Limited (TFL)** and **NTPC Limited** have already applied.

**Why it matters.** Two things for the exam: the scheme itself (energy security via **coal-to-chemicals**, cutting imported feedstock for urea and methanol), and the governance angle — a ministry using PIB to correct a factual claim in the media, which is exactly the PIB "clarification" genre.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Scheme for Promotion of Surface Coal/Lignite Gasification Projects**

- **Ministry:** Ministry of Coal. **Approved by the Union Cabinet on 13 May 2026**, outlay **₹37,500 crore**.
- **Objective:** accelerate coal and lignite gasification and convert domestic coal/lignite into higher-value products — **syngas, methanol, ammonia and urea** — to cut dependence on imported feedstock and strengthen energy and economic security.
- **Process so far:** roadshows in New Delhi (28 May), Hyderabad (11 June) and Mumbai (18 June 2026); **RFP issued 7 July 2026**; online application portal opened; pre-application conference on 20 July 2026.
- **Rolling rounds:** when one application window closes, the next opens the very next day and stays open **two months** — designed for projects that need long preparatory work (pre-feasibility reports, technology selection, feedstock tie-ups).
- **Expected impact:** investment of roughly **₹2.5–3 lakh crore across about 25 projects**, around **50,000 direct and indirect jobs**, and support for the national target of **100 million tonnes of coal gasification capacity by 2030**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy / Infrastructure & Energy (also usable for Environment: gasification as a "cleaner coal" pathway, with the counter-argument on lifecycle emissions).

## 5. MoEFCC (CAQM): **Special Industrial Inspection Drive in Nuh, Haryana — 17 of 35 units found non-compliant**

**What it is.** The **Commission for Air Quality Management in NCR and Adjoining Areas (CAQM)** ran a special industrial inspection drive in **Nuh district, Haryana** on 3 September 2026, deploying **eight Flying Squads** with the **Haryana State Pollution Control Board** and district administration.

**Why it matters.** A concrete illustration of a **statutory air-quality regulator with enforcement teeth** acting ahead of the winter pollution season — useful as an example in any GS3 answer on urban air pollution governance, and a reminder that CAQM's mandate covers *industrial* sources, not just stubble burning and vehicles.

**Key facts.**

- **35 units inspected:** **17 non-compliant**, 11 complying (10 fully compliant, 1 compliant with recommendations), 7 closed/non-operational at the time of inspection.
- **Violations found:** defective or leaking **wet scrubbers**; non-functional **Electrostatic Precipitators (ESPs)**; inadequate stack height for boilers/thermic fluid heaters; uncovered conveyor belts, poor water sprinkling, missing windbreakers and unpaved internal roads causing **fugitive dust**; non-compliant DG sets; **burning of wood** and use of **unapproved liquid fuel**; **OCEMS** (Online Continuous Emission Monitoring System) not installed; stack-emission monitoring reports outdated or unsubmitted; machinery run outside the **Consent to Operate (CTO)**.
- **Static link:** CAQM was created by an Act of Parliament (2021) and can issue directions binding on the NCR States; it subsumed EPCA.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (air pollution, regulatory institutions).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the **GSLV-F17/EOS-05 mission**, consider the following statements:

1. EOS-05 is India's first imaging satellite designed to operate from a geosynchronous orbit.
2. GSLV-F17 was the 19th flight of India's Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle.
3. GSLV-F17 injected EOS-05 directly into a circular geostationary orbit.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 3 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 2 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** With reference to **Zonal Councils** in India, consider the following statements:

1. They were established under the States Reorganisation Act, 1956.
2. The Union Home Minister is the Chairman of each of the five Zonal Councils, while a Chief Minister of a member State serves as Vice-Chairman on a rotational basis.
3. The recommendations made by a Zonal Council are binding on its member States.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 1 and 2 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** With reference to the **Emergency Credit Line Guarantee Scheme (ECLGS) 5.0**, consider the following statements:

1. It is implemented by the National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company (NCGTC).
2. It provides 100% credit guarantee coverage on loans extended to eligible MSMEs and 90% coverage for eligible non-MSME borrowers.
3. Scheduled passenger airline companies are among the categories eligible for support under the scheme.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (c).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: ISRO describes EOS-05 as India's first-ever imaging satellite from geosynchronous orbit, and GSLV-F17 was the 19th flight of the GSLV. Statement 3 is incorrect — the vehicle placed EOS-05 into a **Sub-Geosynchronous Transfer Orbit (Sub-GTO)**; the satellite must raise itself to its final geosynchronous slot using onboard propulsion.

**Q2 — (b).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the five Zonal Councils are statutory bodies set up under **Sections 15–22 of the States Reorganisation Act, 1956**, chaired by the Union Home Minister, with a member-State Chief Minister as Vice-Chairman by annual rotation. Statement 3 is incorrect — the role of the Zonal Councils is **purely advisory**; they are deliberative and recommendatory forums, not decision-making bodies binding on States.

**Q3 — (d).** All three are correct. ECLGS 5.0, approved on 5 May 2026, is implemented by **NCGTC**; guarantee cover is **100% for eligible MSMEs** and **90% for eligible non-MSMEs** (and 90% for airlines); **scheduled passenger airline companies** form a distinct eligible category with a ₹1,500 crore per-borrower ceiling and a 7-year tenure including a 2-year moratorium.
