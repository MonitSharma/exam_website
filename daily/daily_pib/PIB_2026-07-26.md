## 1. Kargil Vijay Diwas 2026 — nation marks 27 years of Operation Vijay at Drass

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Defence / Indian Army (Fire & Fury Corps) | **Date:** 26 July 2026 (two-day commemorative events at Drass) | **Notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security (border management, sub-conventional warfare) · GS1 — Modern India / post-Independence conflicts

**What it is.** 26 July marks the 27th anniversary of the successful conclusion of **Operation Vijay** (1999), in which the Indian Armed Forces evicted Pakistani regulars and infiltrators from the heights of the Kargil sector in Ladakh. The national homage ceremony was held at the **Kargil War Memorial, Drass**, with Raksha Mantri **Rajnath Singh** as chief guest, laying floral tributes at the Cenotaph.

**Why it matters.** Kargil is the standard UPSC anchor for questions on (i) limited high-altitude conventional conflict under a nuclear overhang, (ii) the intelligence and surveillance failures that led to the **Kargil Review Committee (1999)** and the subsequent Group of Ministers report — the origin of the Defence Intelligence Agency, the CDS proposal and integrated theatre thinking, and (iii) border infrastructure in Ladakh.

**Key facts/figures**

- Commemoration programme at Drass: wreath-laying at the Cenotaph, a documentary on Operation Vijay, **'Gaurav Gatha'** (recounting of soldiers' bravery), interaction with **Veer Naris and Veer Matas**, and a visit to the **Hut of Remembrance** and **Veer Bhoomi**.
- The Raksha Mantri **inaugurated a Virtual Walkthrough Portal** of the Kargil War Memorial — a digital-heritage access initiative for citizens who cannot travel to Drass.
- **Shaurya Vijay Yatra**: a motorcycle rally flagged off on **14 July 2026 from the National War Memorial, New Delhi**, covering roughly **1,900 km** and flagged in at the Kargil War Memorial, Drass, on 26 July. Theme: *"One Ride, One Nation, One Salute."*
- Parallel observances were held by formations across the country, including at the War Memorial in Srinagar (Chinar Corps).

## 2. PM interacts with Viksit Vibrant Village Programme 2026 participants — border villages as "first villages"

**Ministry/Body:** PMO; implemented through **MY Bharat** (Mera Yuva Bharat, Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports), linked to the **Vibrant Villages Programme** (Ministry of Home Affairs) | **Date:** interaction on 26 July 2026 (PMO release 25 July) | **Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (border area development, youth participation) · GS3 — Internal Security (border management)

**What it is.** The Prime Minister interacted by video conferencing with participants of the **Viksit Vibrant Village Programme 2026**, a youth-immersion programme in India's frontier villages. The organising idea is the government's framing of border villages not as "last villages" but as the **"first villages of the nation"** — active partners in the Viksit Bharat journey rather than passive periphery.

**Why it matters.** It is the civilian-participation limb of border management. UPSC-relevant angles: reverse migration from border villages, dual-use infrastructure, the "eyes and ears" role of border populations in a Rajputana/LAC context, and the use of youth volunteering platforms (MY Bharat) as a governance delivery channel.

**Key facts/figures**

- Conducted in **two phases, 4 June – 30 June 2026**, covering **74 vibrant villages** across **Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand**.
- **Over 400 youth participants**, representing every State and Union Territory, selected through a nationwide **online quiz** that drew **more than three lakh** entries.
- Participants lived with local communities and undertook Swachhta drives, government-scheme awareness campaigns, **Yuva Sammelans**, cultural exchange, tree plantation, **Mini Model Panchayat simulations**, interaction with local artisans, and visits to institutions of strategic importance.

## 3. Council of Ministers change: Dharmendra Pradhan resigns; Pralhad Joshi given additional charge of Education

**Ministry/Body:** President's Secretariat / PMO / Ministry of Education | **Date:** 25 July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Union executive, Articles 74–75, Allocation of Business Rules)

**What it is.** **President Droupadi Murmu accepted the resignation of Shri Dharmendra Pradhan** from the Union Council of Ministers with immediate effect. **Shri Pralhad Joshi** — currently Minister of **Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution** and of **New & Renewable Energy** — has been assigned the charge of the **Ministry of Education in addition to his existing portfolios**.

**Why it matters.** A textbook illustration of the constitutional mechanics UPSC likes to test: ministers hold office **during the pleasure of the President** (Art. 75(2)); a minister resigns by tendering resignation to the President, who acts on the **advice of the Prime Minister** (Art. 75(1)); and portfolios are allocated/redistributed by the President on the PM's advice under **Article 77(3)** read with the **Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961**. "Additional charge" is an administrative device, not a separate constitutional office.

**Key facts/figures**

- Joshi now holds three portfolios: Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution; New & Renewable Energy; and (additional charge) Education.
- Context: the change follows the NEET-UG 2026 paper-leak agitation, which has dominated the Education Ministry's agenda this month.
- Recall for prelims: the total size of the Council of Ministers is capped at **15% of the Lok Sabha's strength** by the **91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003** (Art. 75(1A)).

## 4. Mann Ki Baat, 26 July 2026 — Catch the Rain, DRDO tests, and a hornbill's return to Gir

**Ministry/Body:** PMO / Prasar Bharati (Akashvani); items span Ministry of Jal Shakti, DRDO (MoD), MoEFCC, MSME/KVIC | **Date:** 26 July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Water Resources, Science & Technology · GS1 — Indian Society & Culture

**What it is.** The PM's monthly national radio address, delivered on Kargil Vijay Diwas, doubled as a government-priorities round-up. It is a reliable source of "one-liner" prelims facts, and several items are fresh.

**Key facts/figures — water and environment**

- **Jal Shakti Abhiyan: Catch the Rain 2026** — a nationwide drive under way **since 4 July 2026** (running to early August), theme **"Catch the Rain, Where it Falls, When it Falls"**, focused on rainwater harvesting, groundwater recharge, revival of traditional water bodies and plantation. Cited examples: ~**1,500 ponds** built in **Sidhi (MP)**; Amrit Sarovars restored in **Narsinghpur**; storage created in **Nashik**; all gram panchayats of **Nandyal (AP)** joined; results also in **Korba (Chhattisgarh)**, **Bhubaneswar (Odisha)** and **Vizianagaram (AP)**. Disused borewells are being repurposed as recharge shafts.
- **Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam** — over **3.5 lakh saplings planted in a single hour** in Bhadaj, Ahmedabad by 25,000+ people (a Guinness record); **over 35 crore saplings** planted in Uttar Pradesh in a single day on **12 July 2026**.
- The **Indian Grey Hornbill** (*Ocyceros birostris*) has returned to the **Gir forests of Gujarat after nearly 60 years**, attributed to sustained habitat restoration.
- **Waste-to-wealth**, Sitamarhi (Bihar): public benches made from recycled single-use plastic — about **40 kg** of single-use plastic per bench.

**Key facts/figures — science, defence and culture**

- **DRDO** successfully tested the **Pinaka Long-Range Guided Rocket** and, days earlier, the **Kusha** missile (long-range surface-to-air programme) this month.
- **INS Mahendragiri** — inducted into the Eastern Fleet at Visakhapatnam, designed and built in India with **over 75% indigenous** content.
- **Vikram-1**, India's first privately developed orbital rocket, launched successfully from **Sriharikota**; a **BrahMos and Astra** missile agreement was concluded during the PM's Indonesia visit earlier this month.
- **Science Olympiads 2026**: India won **five golds and the top rank in Physics**; **all golds in Chemistry** (best-ever); all Indian students medalled in **Biology** including a gold; **two golds and four silvers** in Mathematics.
- **Loktak Proto-cluster** — a massive cluster of young galaxies discovered by a team including Manipur-origin astrophysicist **Dr. Ronaldo Laishram**, named after **Loktak Lake**, India's largest freshwater lake in the North-East.
- **Crafts / Vocal for Local**: Kashmir's eco-friendly **'Vagu'** mat (reeds and paddy straw; ~4 days for two artisans per mat), being revived in Srinagar; Tripura's three-stringed bamboo instrument **'Chongpreng'**. **Khadi** sales have risen **six-fold in 12 years to nearly ₹8,000 crore**. **National Handloom Day** falls on **7 August**.

## 5. Delhi Police sets up a Special Task Force for paper-leak offences under the 2024 Public Examinations Act

**Ministry/Body:** Delhi Police (Ministry of Home Affairs) | **Date:** 25 July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (examination integrity, institutional accountability) · GS3 — Internal Security (organised crime)

*What's new:* yesterday's briefing covered the **Cabinet's approval of an amendment Bill** to the Public Examinations Act. This is the **enforcement-side** follow-through — a dedicated investigating unit, not a legislative step.

**What it is.** Delhi Police has constituted a **Special Task Force (STF)** to investigate paper leaks and other examination-related offences under the **Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024**. Its remit covers examinations conducted by the **UPSC, Staff Selection Commission, Railway Recruitment Boards, Institute of Banking Personnel Selection (IBPS), the National Testing Agency**, and Central Government ministries/departments and their attached and subordinate offices for staff recruitment.

**Why it matters.** The 2024 Act criminalised organised paper-leak conspiracies but left investigation with ordinary police machinery; a specialised unit addresses the capacity gap. The STF is also mandated to **coordinate with prosecuting agencies for day-to-day trials** — a rare explicit link between investigation and speedy prosecution.

**Key facts/figures**

- STF headed by the **Deputy Commissioner of Police, Crime Branch**; overall supervision and administrative control with the **Special Commissioner of Police, Crime Branch**.
- Parent statute: **Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024** (enacted February 2024).

## Answers

**Q1 — (a).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the 2026 edition ran 4–30 June across **74 vibrant villages in Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand**, implemented through **MY Bharat**, with over 400 participants chosen by a nationwide online quiz that drew more than three lakh entries. Statement 3 is wrong — the 2026 edition was confined to the northern border States/UTs, not the Myanmar frontier.

**Q2 — (d).** All three are correct. 26 July marks the 27th anniversary of the conclusion of **Operation Vijay (1999)**; the national ceremony is at the **Kargil War Memorial, Drass (Ladakh)**, led this year by Raksha Mantri Rajnath Singh; and the **Shaurya Vijay Yatra** was flagged off on 14 July 2026 from the **National War Memorial, New Delhi**, covering roughly 1,900 km to Drass.

**Q3 — (b).** "Catch the Rain, Where it Falls, When it Falls" is the theme of the **Jal Shakti Abhiyan: Catch the Rain** campaign of the Ministry of Jal Shakti, whose 2026 drive (from 4 July) focuses on rainwater harvesting, groundwater recharge, rejuvenation of traditional water bodies and plantation.
