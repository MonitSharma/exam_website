## 1. Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas: **Biometric Aadhaar Authentication becomes mandatory for subsidised domestic LPG refills from 1 October 2026**

**What it is.** From **1 October 2026**, a domestic LPG consumer must have completed **Biometric Aadhaar Authentication (BAA)** in order to book a refill at the **regulated Retail Selling Price (RSP)** with the applicable subsidy. Consumers who have already authenticated need do nothing further. Consumers who have not will have subsidised booking enabled only once authentication is completed. Critically — and this is where most summaries get it wrong — a consumer who is **unwilling or unable** to complete BAA is **not cut off**: after registering that choice on the OMC's digital channels (web portal, app, WhatsApp chatbot or IVRS), they will be supplied LPG at **market price, without subsidy, in 5 kg or 10 kg cylinders**, subject to local availability.

**Why it matters.** This is the cleanest live example on the syllabus of the **targeting problem in subsidy delivery**, and it carries three distinct examinable threads.

First, the **leakage/diversion** thread. The stated purpose is to stop subsidised domestic cylinders being diverted to **commercial and industrial use** and to remove **duplicate and ineligible connections**. That is exactly the DBT-era logic that began with PAHAL (DBTL) in 2014-15 and the "Give It Up" campaign, extended now from bank-account seeding to **biometric identity at the point of delivery**.

Second, the **exclusion-error** thread — the standard counter-argument in any Mains answer on Aadhaar-linked delivery. Biometric authentication is known to fail for the elderly, manual labourers with worn fingerprints, and in areas of poor connectivity. The Ministry's design response is the **opt-out route** (market-price 5 kg/10 kg cylinders) plus multiple authentication channels, which blunts but does not eliminate the criticism, since the fallback is *unsubsidised* supply. Note the repeated deadline extensions — seven of them — as evidence of how hard the last mile is.

Third, the **fiscal** thread, and it is the sharpest set of numbers PIB has released on LPG this year. Domestic LPG is **sold below cost**. The implicit subsidy in **September 2026 is about ₹210 per 14.2 kg cylinder**, but it touched **₹721 per cylinder in June 2026** — the swing shows how exposed the exchequer is to international LPG prices. Accumulated **under-recoveries of public sector OMCs on domestic LPG exceeded ₹62,000 crore as on 31 August 2026** even after compensation. That is the fiscal case the Ministry makes for tightening targeting.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Effective date:** 1 October 2026 for subsidised refill booking at RSP.
- **Coverage achieved:** **27.43 crore** active domestic LPG consumers had completed BAA as on **19 September 2026** — **89.9%**.
- **Authentication channels:** (i) on the spot during refill delivery via the OMC app; (ii) at the distributor's showroom; (iii) self e-KYC on **IndianOil ONE** (Indane), **HelloBPCL** (Bharatgas), **HP PAY** (HP Gas). Video tutorials are on the **PMUY portal**.
- **Opt-out consequence:** LPG at **market price, no subsidy, in 5 kg or 10 kg cylinders**, subject to local availability.
- **Deadline history:** original deadline **30 June 2026**, extended to 31 July → 7 Aug → 15 Aug → 23 Aug → 31 Aug → 7 Sept → finally **14 September 2026**.
- **Outreach since October 2023:** over **12 crore SMS and WhatsApp messages**, distributor camps, IVRS prompts during booking.
- **Fiscal numbers:** implicit subsidy **~₹210/cylinder (Sept 2026)**, peak **₹721/cylinder (June 2026)**; Government compensation to OMCs **₹22,000 crore in FY 2022-23** and **₹30,000 crore across FY 2025-26 and FY 2026-27**; accumulated under-recoveries **> ₹62,000 crore as on 31 August 2026**.
- **Helpline:** 1800 2333 555.

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (welfare delivery, Aadhaar-based targeting, exclusion errors, citizen charters) + GS3 Economy (subsidies, under-recoveries, energy pricing).

## 2. Ministry of Law and Justice (Department of Justice): **DISHA 2.0 Regional Workshop at Goa on 21 September — Tele-Law, Nyaya Bandhu and the "Nyaya Prabodh" legal-literacy campaign**

**What it is.** The **Department of Justice** is holding a **Regional Workshop under the DISHA 2.0 Scheme** on **21 September 2026** at the **India International University of Legal Education and Research (IIULER), Goa**. **DISHA** stands for **Designing Innovative Solutions for Holistic Access to Justice**. The workshop showcases the best reels from the **"Nyaya Prabodh: Awakening to Justice" campaign — Know Your Rights in 90 Seconds**, launches a **Slogan Competition** under Nyaya Prabodh, screens a documentary on DISHA 2.0's record in Goa, and holds live interactions with **Tele-Law beneficiaries, Panel Lawyers, Village Level Entrepreneurs (VLEs) and Nyaya Sahayaks**, plus a session with law students under the **Nyaya Bandhu** pro bono programme.

**Why it matters.** Access to justice sits at the intersection of **Article 39A** (equal justice and free legal aid, a Directive Principle) and **Article 21** as interpreted in *Hussainara Khatoon* — free legal aid is part of the right to life and personal liberty. DISHA is the executive scheme that operationalises that constitutional promise **outside** the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 architecture (NALSA/SLSA/DLSA), and the two are frequently confused in prelims. The distinction worth holding: **NALSA** delivers statutory legal aid and Lok Adalats; **DISHA** is a **Department of Justice scheme** delivering *pre-litigation* legal advice, legal literacy and pro bono linkage, largely **through Common Service Centres** — which is why the Ministry of Rural Development and CSC e-Governance Services are stakeholders at this workshop rather than a bar council.

The delivery mechanism is the genuinely interesting part for Mains: **Tele-Law** uses the **CSC network's Village Level Entrepreneurs** as the physical interface and **Nyaya Sahayaks** (para-legal facilitators) as the human bridge, connecting a rural citizen by video/telephone to a **Panel Lawyer** — free for all for pre-litigation advice. This is a **digital-public-infrastructure answer to a justice problem**, and it pairs naturally in an answer with BharatNet, CSCs and the DPI argument generally.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Date and venue:** 21 September 2026, University Auditorium, **IIULER, Goa**.
- **Dignitaries:** **Governor of Goa** as Chief Guest; **Minister of State (Independent Charge) for Law and Justice**; Goa's Minister for PWD, Captain of Ports and Legal Metrology.
- **Full form:** DISHA = **Designing Innovative Solutions for Holistic Access to Justice**.
- **Campaign launched/showcased:** **Nyaya Prabodh: Awakening to Justice — Know Your Rights in 90 Seconds**, plus a new Slogan Competition.
- **Stakeholders present:** Central and State Government, **Common Service Centres (CSCs)**, **Ministry of Rural Development**, State law institutions.
- **Named DoJ initiatives referenced:** **Tele-Law**, **Nyaya Bandhu** (pro bono), **Legal Literacy and Legal Awareness Programmes**, **VIDHI-Sanjeevani**.
- **Field cadres named:** **Panel Lawyers**, **Village Level Entrepreneurs (VLEs)**, **Nyaya Sahayaks**.

### Scheme-in-focus: **DISHA 2.0 (Designing Innovative Solutions for Holistic Access to Justice)**

- **Ministry:** Law and Justice, **Department of Justice**. **Type:** Central Sector Scheme (umbrella scheme for access to justice).
- **Objective:** to make justice **accessible, understandable, affordable and available** to every citizen — to move legal aid from a courtroom entitlement to a **pre-litigation, doorstep service**, in furtherance of **Article 39A**.
- **Key features:** (i) **Tele-Law** — free pre-litigation legal advice delivered through **Common Service Centres**, connecting citizens to Panel Lawyers via video conferencing, with **VLEs** and **Nyaya Sahayaks** as the last-mile interface; (ii) **Nyaya Bandhu** — a pro bono legal services programme mobilising advocates and law students, with a mobile application; (iii) **Legal Literacy and Legal Awareness Programmes**, now fronted by the **Nyaya Prabodh** short-video campaign; (iv) **VIDHI-Sanjeevani** and allied technology-enabled legal-services components.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** all citizens for pre-litigation advice, with the design bias towards **rural, remote and underserved areas**; delivered nationally through the CSC network.
- **Significance:** DISHA is the standard illustration of **technology-enabled justice delivery** and the answer to the "pendency is not the only access problem" argument — most legal problems never reach a court at all, and DISHA targets that pre-litigation gap. Contrast it in an answer with **NALSA** under the **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987**, which is the *statutory* legal-aid channel.

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance and Polity (access to justice, Article 39A, Directive Principles, e-governance, citizen-centric service delivery; judiciary-adjacent).

## 3. Ministry of Textiles (Central Silk Board): **77th Foundation Day of the Central Silk Board — India targets No. 1 rank in global silk production by 2028-29**

**What it is.** At the **77th Foundation Day** of the **Central Silk Board (CSB)** in **Bengaluru** on 20 September, the Union Textiles Minister announced a resolve to take India to the **No. 1 position globally in silk production by 2028-29**, with an interim output target of **60,000 metric tonnes by 2030-31**. Indian silk production has risen from **26,000 MT in 2014 to 42,000 MT today**. The Minister called for **"Best Silk Farmer" awards** at district, State and national levels, and set an aspiration of raising sericulture farmers' annual income to **₹10–12 lakh** through better mulberry-leaf yields and more cocoon-rearing cycles. The Minister of State noted **Karnataka holds the leading position** in India's total silk production.

**Why it matters.** Sericulture is a textbook **allied agricultural activity** with an unusually good fit for several syllabus themes at once: it is **labour-intensive**, **land-light** (viable on smallholdings), **women-intensive**, gives **multiple income cycles a year** rather than one harvest, and links a farm activity directly to an export-facing manufacturing value chain — reeling, twisting, weaving. That makes it the natural example in an answer on **doubling farm incomes through allied activities** or on **rural non-farm employment**.

The strategic claim in the release is also worth holding: **demand for Indian silk is rising internationally at a time when China's production is declining**. China has historically produced the overwhelming majority of world raw silk, and India is the **second-largest producer**; a Chinese contraction is the only realistic route by which India's "No. 1 by 2028-29" ambition becomes arithmetic rather than aspiration. The honest counterpoint for a Mains answer: India is simultaneously a large **importer of high-grade (3A and above) bivoltine raw silk** from China, so a genuine No. 1 claim requires solving a **quality** problem, not only a volume one — which is precisely why the Joint Secretary (Silk) framed the strategy around **high-yielding silkworm hybrids, modern machinery, and AI/GIS/Machine Learning for quality inspection**.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Occasion:** 77th Foundation Day of the **Central Silk Board**, UAS-GKVK auditorium, **Bengaluru**, 20 September 2026; exhibition of silk products and technologies.
- **Production trajectory:** **26,000 MT (2014) → 42,000 MT (current)**; target **60,000 MT by 2030-31**.
- **Stated ambition:** **No. 1 in global silk production by 2028-29**.
- **Farmer income aspiration:** **₹10–12 lakh a year**, via higher mulberry-leaf yield and more cocoon-rearing cycles.
- **Leading State:** **Karnataka**, in total silk production.
- **Technology push:** high-yielding silkworm hybrids, modern machinery, **AI, GIS and Machine Learning** for quality inspection and production capacity.
- **Wider textile target referenced:** taking India's textile market to **US$300 billion**.
- **Also at the event:** exchange of **MoUs** between CSB and various organisations; **appointment letters to 28 scientists**; participation by silk farmers and weavers from **Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana and Tamil Nadu**.

**Static anchors worth carrying with this item:** the **Central Silk Board** is a **statutory body** constituted under the **Central Silk Board Act, 1948**, functioning under the **Ministry of Textiles**, headquartered at **Bengaluru**. India produces **all four commercially known varieties of silk** — **Mulberry, Tasar, Eri and Muga** — a distinction India uniquely holds. **Muga**, the golden silk, is produced almost exclusively in **Assam** and carries a **GI tag**. The central sericulture scheme is **Silk Samagra**.

**Notebook:** GS3 Agriculture (allied sectors, farm incomes, rural employment) + GS3 Economy (textiles value chain, import substitution, exports).

## 4. Ministry of Finance (Department of Public Enterprises / NLMC): **NLMC's 21st Board Meeting recommends monetisation proposals worth over ₹5,000 crore**

**What it is.** The **21st Meeting of the Board of Directors of the National Land Monetization Corporation Limited (NLMC)** was held on **18 September 2026** and reviewed the asset monetisation programme. Its headline outcome: **monetisation proposals involving assets valued at over ₹5,000 crore** were considered and recommended, covering **surplus land and building assets**. The Board also approved NLMC's **Annual Financial Statements and Directors' Report for FY 2025-26**.

**Why it matters.** NLMC is the institutional answer to a specific, long-standing problem: **CPSEs and government entities sit on very large stocks of surplus and underutilised land and buildings**, which individual entities lacked the expertise, mandate or incentive to monetise. Announced in the **Union Budget 2021-22** as an SPV for monetising land and other non-core assets, approved by the **Union Cabinet on 9 March 2022** and **incorporated on 3 June 2022** as a **wholly owned Government of India company** (authorised share capital **₹5,000 crore**, paid-up **₹150 crore**), NLMC's job is to do the **identification, due diligence, valuation and transaction-structuring** that the asset-owning entity cannot. Its mandate also extends to speeding up the **closure of loss-making CPSEs** and smoothing the **strategic disinvestment** process.

Two exam angles. First, the **taxonomy** angle, which is where prelims usually attacks: **asset monetisation is not disinvestment**. Monetisation transfers the **right to use** a **brownfield, revenue-generating** asset to a private operator for a defined period while **ownership stays with the Government**, and the asset reverts at the end of the term; disinvestment transfers **ownership of equity**. NLMC handles **land and buildings** specifically; the broader **National Monetisation Pipeline (NMP)** covers roads, railways, power transmission, telecom, gas pipelines and the rest, and **NITI Aayog** is the body that framed the NMP. Note the administrative placement that is frequently misremembered: NLMC is under the **Department of Public Enterprises, Ministry of Finance** — DPE was moved from the Ministry of Heavy Industries to the Ministry of Finance in 2021.

Second, the **critique** angle for Mains: the standing objections to land monetisation are the risk of **undervaluation** of public land in thin markets, **loss of a strategic land bank** in high-value urban locations, and **weak competitive bidding** where few buyers exist. The Board's own emphasis on "transparency, efficiency and value realisation" and on **expediting** the process is the Government's response to the parallel criticism that the programme has been **slow** relative to announcement.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Meeting:** 21st NLMC Board of Directors meeting, held **18 September 2026** (release dated 19 September).
- **Headline:** monetisation proposals for assets valued at **over ₹5,000 crore** recommended — surplus **land and building** assets.
- **Governance action:** Board approved **Annual Financial Statements and Directors' Report for FY 2025-26**.
- **What NLMC is:** a **wholly owned Government of India company** under the administrative control of the **Department of Public Enterprises (DPE), Ministry of Finance**.
- **Mandate:** undertake and facilitate monetisation of **surplus land and building assets of CPSEs and other Government entities** — identification, assessment, due diligence, valuation, transaction structuring.
- **Stated principles:** transparency, efficiency and value realisation; coordination with Ministries, Departments, CPSEs and asset-owning entities.

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (public finance, asset monetisation, National Monetisation Pipeline, PSU reform, infrastructure financing) + GS2 Governance (public sector enterprise governance).

## 5. Ministry of Defence (Indian Navy): **SLINEX-26 — 13th edition of the India–Sri Lanka bilateral maritime exercise at Visakhapatnam**

**What it is.** The **13th edition** of **SLINEX**, the **Sri Lanka–India bilateral maritime exercise**, is being conducted at **Visakhapatnam from 17 to 21 September 2026**, under the aegis of the **Eastern Naval Command**. It opened with a **Harbour Phase** — professional interactions, cross-deck visits, sharing of best practices, yoga, sports and cultural activities — to be followed by a **Sea Phase** focused on operational synergy and interoperability. **INS Kavaratti**, an **indigenous Anti-Submarine Warfare corvette**, and **INS Jyoti**, the **Fleet Tanker**, represent the Indian Navy; **SLNS Sindurala** represents the Sri Lanka Navy. PIB explicitly frames the exercise as **aligned with India's vision of MAHASAGAR**.

**Why it matters.** SLINEX is the maritime pillar of an India–Sri Lanka defence relationship that also runs a land component (**Exercise Mitra Shakti**, the army exercise) — keeping the two straight is standard prelims discipline. **Conceptualised in 2005**, SLINEX predates most of India's current bilateral naval exercise architecture and is a good illustration of **defence diplomacy as a continuity instrument**: it has run across sharp swings in the bilateral political relationship, including the period of Sri Lanka's deepest economic distress and the recurring controversies over Chinese research and survey vessels calling at Sri Lankan ports.

The **MAHASAGAR** framing in the release is the detail to underline. **MAHASAGAR — Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions** — was announced by the Prime Minister in **Mauritius in March 2025** as the successor and expansion of **SAGAR (Security and Growth for All in the Region, 2015)**. The shift is from an **Indian Ocean Region** frame to a **Global South** frame, adding trade, connectivity, capacity building and non-traditional security to what was a largely security-centric vision. Any answer on India's maritime neighbourhood policy now needs the **SAGAR → MAHASAGAR** progression, and SLINEX is the operational example.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Edition and dates:** **13th edition**, SLINEX-26, **17–21 September 2026**, at **Visakhapatnam**.
- **Structure:** **Harbour Phase** (under Eastern Naval Command) followed by **Sea Phase**.
- **Indian Navy participation:** **INS Kavaratti** (indigenous **Anti-Submarine Warfare corvette**) and **INS Jyoti** (**Fleet Tanker**).
- **Sri Lanka Navy participation:** **SLNS Sindurala**.
- **Origin:** **conceptualised in 2005**.
- **Doctrinal frame:** **MAHASAGAR** — Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions.

**Static anchors:** **INS Kavaratti** is the fourth of the **Kamorta-class (Project 28)** ASW corvettes built by **GRSE, Kolkata**, notable for its **carbon-fibre composite superstructure**. The India–Sri Lanka **army** exercise is **Mitra Shakti**; do not confuse it with SLINEX. Other India–Sri Lanka maritime engagements to keep nearby: the **Colombo Security Conclave**, which began in **2011** as an **NSA-level trilateral** on maritime security between **India, Sri Lanka and the Maldives** and now has **six members** — **Mauritius joined in 2022, Bangladesh in 2024 and Seychelles in 2025** — with a permanent **Secretariat at Colombo** and **five pillars** of cooperation; and **IMBL (International Maritime Boundary Line)** issues around the **Palk Strait** and **Katchatheevu**.

**Notebook:** GS2 International Relations (India's neighbourhood, bilateral and defence diplomacy, SAGAR/MAHASAGAR, Indian Ocean Region) + GS3 Internal Security (maritime security).

## 6. Ministry of Jal Shakti: **9th India International Water Week 2026 — 22–26 September at Bharat Mandapam, theme "Climate Resilient Water Management"**

**What it is.** The **Vice-President of India, Shri C. P. Radhakrishnan**, will inaugurate the **9th India International Water Week (IIWW) 2026** on **22 September 2026** at **Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi**. The five-day event, **22–26 September**, is organised by the **Ministry of Jal Shakti** on the theme **"Climate Resilient Water Management."** **The Netherlands and Germany** are **Partner Countries**; **fourteen States** participate as **Partner States**, and **six Central Ministries/Departments** have been invited as **Partner Ministries**. The programme runs **ten major forums** and **seven sub-themes**, with a four-day international exhibition (22–25 September) and the **Second International WASH Conference 2026** (22–24 September) organised by the **Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation**.

**Why it matters.** The theme choice is the point. India's water problem has historically been framed as one of **supply augmentation** — dams, canals, interlinking, groundwater extraction. "Climate Resilient Water Management" reframes it around **variability**: shortening monsoon windows, more intense rainfall in fewer days, Himalayan glacier retreat and glacial lake outburst risk, and simultaneous flood-and-drought exposure in the same basins within the same year. That reframing is what makes the seven sub-themes examinable as a set, because they name the policy levers: **governance, optimal use, technology and community, climate risk, innovative financing, sustainable resource management, and water for people and planet**.

Two angles to carry. First, **innovative financing for water infrastructure** appears as a distinct sub-theme — worth noting because water has been chronically **under-priced and under-financed** in India relative to power and roads, and municipal water utilities rarely recover operating costs. Second, the **federalism** angle: water is a **State List** subject (**Entry 17, List II**), with the Union's authority confined to **inter-State rivers and river valleys** under **Entry 56, List I** and **Article 262**. Fourteen Partner States and six Partner Ministries at a Union-organised water week is therefore a **cooperative-federalism convening exercise**, not a regulatory one — a useful concrete example for any answer on Centre–State coordination in water.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Event:** 9th **India International Water Week (IIWW) 2026**, **22–26 September 2026**, **Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi**.
- **Theme:** **"Climate Resilient Water Management."**
- **Inauguration:** **Vice-President Shri C. P. Radhakrishnan**, 22 September 2026.
- **Partner Countries:** **The Netherlands** and **Germany**.
- **Partner States:** **fourteen**; **Partner Ministries/Departments:** **six** invited.
- **Ten forums:** Ministerial Plenary, Global Water Leaders' Plenaries, Country Forums, Water Leaders' Forums, Practitioners' Forums, Thematic Forums, **Startup Forum**, **Youth Forum**, Industrial Forum, Side Events.
- **Seven sub-themes:** inclusive and equitable water governance; collaboration for optimal use of water resources; technology and community for water security; water in climate-related risk; **innovative financing models** for water infrastructure and modernisation; sustainable water resources management; water for people and planet.
- **Exhibition:** international technology showcase, **22–25 September**.
- **Co-located:** **Second International WASH Conference 2026**, **22–24 September**, by the **Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation** — drinking water access, sanitation infrastructure, hygiene behaviour, public health.
- **Closing:** Valedictory Session **25 September**; study tours **26 September**.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment (water security, climate adaptation and resilience, WASH) + GS2 Governance (cooperative federalism in water, international cooperation).

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct. SLINEX was **conceptualised in 2005** and is the **India–Sri Lanka bilateral maritime exercise**; the **13th edition** ran at **Visakhapatnam from 17 to 21 September 2026**, opening with a **Harbour Phase** under the **Eastern Naval Command** before the **Sea Phase**. **INS Kavaratti** (an indigenous **Kamorta-class / Project 28 ASW corvette** built by GRSE, with a carbon-fibre composite superstructure) and the fleet tanker **INS Jyoti** represented India; **SLNS Sindurala** represented Sri Lanka. **MAHASAGAR — Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions** — was announced by the Prime Minister in **Mauritius in March 2025** as the expansion of **SAGAR (Security and Growth for All in the Region, 2015)** from an **Indian Ocean Region** focus to the wider **Global South**, adding trade, connectivity and capacity building to a previously security-centric frame. The classic confusion to avoid: the India–Sri Lanka **army** exercise is **Mitra Shakti**, not SLINEX.

**Q3 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** Statement 1 is **wrong** and is the intended trap: the **Central Silk Board** is a statutory body constituted under the **Central Silk Board Act, 1948**, but it functions under the **Ministry of Textiles**, not Agriculture — headquartered at **Bengaluru**, which is why its 77th Foundation Day was held there. Statement 2 is correct: India is the world's **second largest** producer of raw silk, after **China** — hence the significance of the Minister's stated resolve to reach **No. 1 by 2028-29**. Statement 3 is correct: **Muga**, the golden silk, is produced almost exclusively in **Assam** and holds a **GI tag**. Additional facts from the release: production has risen from **26,000 MT in 2014 to 42,000 MT**, with a target of **60,000 MT by 2030-31**; **Karnataka** leads in total silk production; India uniquely produces **all four commercial varieties — Mulberry, Tasar, Eri and Muga**; the central scheme is **Silk Samagra**.
