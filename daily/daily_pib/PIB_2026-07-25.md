## 1. Cabinet approves **BHAVYA-Rasayan** — ₹3,030 crore scheme for three dedicated Chemical Parks

**Issuing body:** Cabinet Committee / Department of Chemicals & Petrochemicals, Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers (24 July 2026)

The Union Cabinet cleared *Bharat Audyogik Vikas Yojana – Rasayan* (BHAVYA-Rasayan), a scheme to create three greenfield chemical parks with plug-and-play common infrastructure. It matters because India runs a large and persistent trade deficit in chemicals and petrochemicals despite being among the world's largest chemical producers — the bottleneck is not demand but the absence of large, effluent-compliant, utility-ready industrial land, which small and mid-sized chemical units cannot build for themselves.

**Key facts**

- Total outlay **₹3,030 crore** — ₹3,000 crore for common infrastructure/basic utilities inside the parks + ₹30 crore administrative expenditure.
- Period: **5 years, FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31**.
- Central grant of **up to ₹1,000 crore per park**, conditional on a **minimum ₹500 crore contribution by the State Government**.
- Each park: minimum **8 sq km (≈2,000 acres)** of *contiguous, encumbrance-free* land.
- Parks to be developed **by State Governments, selected through a challenge method**.
- Common facilities include **CETP** (Common Effluent Treatment Plant), **TSDF** (Treatment, Storage and Disposal Facility), water supply, solvent recovery & distillation units, steam generation and distribution.

**Notebook:** GS3 – Economy (Industrial policy, infrastructure, *Atmanirbharta* in chemicals)

## 2. Cabinet clears **Ballari–Guntakal 3rd and 4th line** multitracking

**Issuing body:** Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA) / Ministry of Railways (24 July 2026)

CCEA approved third and fourth lines on the Ballari–Guntakal section spanning Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh. This is a saturated iron-ore and cement freight corridor connecting the Ballari mining belt to the Guntakal junction; multitracking separates freight from passenger traffic and removes a chronic capacity choke on the Chennai–Mumbai/Hubballi flows.

**Key facts**

- Cost **₹1,264 crore**; adds about **46 km** to the Indian Railways network.
- Covers **3 districts** across **Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh**.
- Connectivity to about **99 villages** with a population of roughly **7 lakh**.
- Planned on the **PM Gati Shakti National Master Plan** (multimodal connectivity, integrated planning).
- Claimed environmental gain: oil import saving of **1.32 crore litres** and CO₂ reduction of **6.67 crore kg**, equivalent to planting **0.27 crore trees**.

**Notebook:** GS3 – Economy (Infrastructure: railways, logistics, PM Gati Shakti)

## 3. DoT bars telecom infrastructure providers from storing or routing data outside India

**Issuing body:** Department of Telecommunications, Ministry of Communications (notification dated 20 July 2026; reported 24 July 2026)

Under the new **authorisation framework** replacing the old licensing regime, DoT has mandated that every newly authorised entity keep all telecom network systems, data, logs and associated information **within India**, with no copies routed, shared or made available abroad. The rule covers cloud-based telecom networks, mobile tower companies and satellite gateway operators — categories that were previously in a grey zone between "infrastructure provider" and "service provider".

**Key facts**

- Legal basis: **Telecommunications Act, 2023** (which replaced the Indian Telegraph Act, 1885) — part of the shift from a **licensing regime to an authorisation regime**.
- Applies to **communication infrastructure providers**: cloud-based telecom networks, mobile towers, satellite gateways.
- Requirement: storage **within India**; **no routing, sharing or availability of copies outside India**.
- Motivation: telecom-network security and data localisation.

**Why it matters for UPSC:** this is a live, concrete instance of India's data-localisation policy — useful as an example in answers on data sovereignty, the DPDP Act ecosystem, and critical information infrastructure. Note the tension examiners like: localisation strengthens sovereign control and law-enforcement access, but raises compliance costs and can invite trade friction over cross-border data flows.

**Notebook:** GS2 – Governance (regulation, data sovereignty) *and* GS3 – Sci-Tech / Internal Security

## 4. DRDO's GTRE develops India's first indigenous **350 kg-thrust class expendable turbojet engine**

**Issuing body:** DRDO – Gas Turbine Research Establishment (GTRE), Ministry of Defence (engine delivered 22 July 2026; PIB release 23–24 July 2026)

GTRE, Bengaluru designed India's first indigenous **expendable** turbojet engine in the 350 kg-thrust class; the engine was manufactured, assembled and delivered to GTRE by industry partner **Azad Engineering, Hyderabad**. "Expendable" means single-use — the class of engine that powers **subsonic cruise missiles, target drones and loitering munitions**, where the engine is consumed with the mission and so must be cheap enough to throw away but reliable enough to work first time.

**Key facts**

- Designed by **GTRE**, DRDO's gas-turbine lab (also runs the **Kaveri** fighter-engine programme).
- Industry partner: **Azad Engineering**, Hyderabad — Development-cum-Production role for manufacture and assembly.
- Thrust class: **350 kg**; category: expendable turbojet.
- Handed over by Azad Engineering CEO Rakesh Chopdar to **Dr K. Rajalakshmi Menon** (DG, Aeronautical Systems) and **Dr S.V. Ramanamurty** (Director, GTRE).
- Significance: jet-engine design is mastered by only a handful of countries; it demands advanced metallurgy (single-crystal blades, superalloys) and ultra-precision manufacturing.

**Notebook:** GS3 – Science & Technology (defence indigenisation, dual-use technology)

## 5. **NCVET recognises NIRD&PR as an Awarding Body (Dual)**

**Issuing body:** National Council for Vocational Education and Training (NCVET), Ministry of Skill Development & Entrepreneurship — recognising NIRD&PR of the Ministry of Rural Development (21 July 2026)

NCVET granted **Awarding Body (Dual)** recognition to the **National Institute of Rural Development and Panchayati Raj (NIRD&PR), Hyderabad**. "Dual" status lets an institution both *award/assess/certify* learners **and** *directly train* them in campuses or centres it owns or fully manages — collapsing the usual separation between training provider and certifying body for its own approved qualifications.

**Key facts**

- Recognising regulator: **NCVET** — the single regulator for vocational education and training, which regulates awarding bodies and assessment agencies and anchors the **National Skills Qualifications Framework (NSQF)**.
- Recipient: **NIRD&PR**, an autonomous organisation under the **Ministry of Rural Development**.
- First qualifications approved: **"Industrial General Pest Management Technician"** qualification, and a **micro-credential on Generation and Mobilisation of Own Source Revenue (OSR) by Panchayats**.
- Planned rollout in three categories: industry-relevant courses for **rural youth**; entrepreneurship courses for **Self Help Groups (SHGs)**; professional courses for **Rural Development and Panchayati Raj functionaries**.
- NCVET issued similar Awarding Body (Dual) recognitions in the same window to **NIIT Foundation** and **NITTTR Kolkata**.

**Why it matters:** the OSR micro-credential is the sleeper item here — Own Source Revenue is the central weakness of the 73rd Amendment's fiscal architecture, and certifying panchayat functionaries in OSR mobilisation is a governance-capacity intervention, not merely a skilling one.

**Notebook:** GS2 – Governance (local government capacity, Panchayati Raj, skill regulation)

## 6. Parliament reply: India's three-layer telecom architecture against cyber fraud — **ASTR, Sanchar Saathi, DIP**

**Issuing body:** Department of Telecommunications, Ministry of Communications (Parliament reply, PIB 24 July 2026); partner agency **I4C**, Ministry of Home Affairs

DoT laid out the current status of its network-level approach to cyber fraud — pre-empting misuse at the telecom layer rather than waiting for police investigation. The constitutional logic is worth internalising: **"Police" and "Public Order" are State List subjects** (Entries 1 and 2, List II, Seventh Schedule), so a Central body like DoT cannot prosecute cybercrime; it can only disconnect misused telecom resources and share intelligence with I4C and State police.

**Key facts / figures (as on 15 July 2026)**

- **ASTR** — DoT's AI and big-data analytics tool for detecting suspicious/fake subscriber identities; flagged connections go for re-verification by telecom service providers. **Over 88 lakh** mobile connections disconnected after failing re-verification.
- **Sanchar Saathi – Chakshu** — citizen portal for reporting suspected fraud communications; DoT uses reports **in aggregate**, not case-by-case. **11.18 lakh citizen reports** → **50.90 lakh** connections disconnected.
- **Digital Intelligence Platform (DIP)** — bi-directional intelligence-sharing hub linking DoT, TSPs, **I4C (MHA)** and financial-sector entities. **More than 50 SEBI-regulated entities** onboarded following SEBI advisories.
- Sanchar Saathi also hosts **CEIR** (blocking stolen handsets) and **TAFCOP** (checking how many SIMs are issued on one's ID).
- Statutory backing: **Telecommunications Act, 2023**; **I4C** established 2018, operationalised 2020.

**Notebook:** GS3 – Internal Security (cybercrime, critical infrastructure) with a GS2 federalism angle

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the *Bharat Audyogik Vikas Yojana – Rasayan* (BHAVYA-Rasayan) scheme approved in July 2026, consider the following statements:

1. It provides for the establishment of three dedicated chemical parks with a total outlay of ₹3,030 crore.
2. The Centre bears the entire cost of common infrastructure, with no financial contribution required from State Governments.
3. Each park is required to have a minimum contiguous area of about 2,000 acres of encumbrance-free land.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Consider the following statements regarding the *National Council for Vocational Education and Training* (NCVET):

1. It is the single regulator for vocational education and training in India and functions under the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship.
2. Recognition as an "Awarding Body (Dual)" allows an institution both to impart training directly and to assess and certify learners for its approved qualifications.
3. The National Institute of Rural Development and Panchayati Raj, which received such recognition in July 2026, functions under the Ministry of Panchayati Raj.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** In the context of measures against cyber fraud, "ASTR" and "Chakshu", recently in the news, are best described as:

(a) Two mobile applications of the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre for lodging first information reports online
(b) An AI-based tool for detecting fraudulent telecom subscribers, and a citizen facility for reporting suspected fraud communications, respectively
(c) Two categories of critical information infrastructure notified under the Information Technology Act, 2000
(d) A satellite-based surveillance system and a financial-transaction monitoring platform of the Reserve Bank of India

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** BHAVYA-Rasayan carries a ₹3,030 crore outlay (₹3,000 cr infrastructure + ₹30 cr administrative) for three chemical parks, each requiring a minimum 8 sq km / ~2,000 acres of contiguous encumbrance-free land. Statement 2 is wrong: the Central grant of up to ₹1,000 crore per park is conditional on a **minimum ₹500 crore contribution by the State Government**.

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** NCVET is the single regulator for VET under the Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship, and "Dual" recognition permits an awarding body to also train directly in centres it owns or fully manages. Statement 3 is wrong: **NIRD&PR is an autonomous body under the Ministry of Rural Development**, not the Ministry of Panchayati Raj.

**Q3 — (b).** **ASTR** is DoT's AI and big-data analytics tool that flags suspicious/fake mobile subscriber identities for re-verification (over 88 lakh connections disconnected as on 15 July 2026). **Chakshu** is the citizen-facing facility on the **Sanchar Saathi** portal for reporting suspected fraud calls/SMS, whose aggregate reports had led to 50.90 lakh disconnections.
