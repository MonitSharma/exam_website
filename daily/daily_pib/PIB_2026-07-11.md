## 1. MoPNG issues detailed FAQ defending the E20 rollout under the Ethanol Blended Petrol (EBP) Programme

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas (MoPNG) | **Date:** FAQ released 10 July 2026 (following clarifications of 23 June and after auto-makers' 4 July presser) | **Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (energy security, biofuels) · GS3 — Environment (emissions) · GS3 — Agriculture (farmer incomes)

The Ministry put out a point-by-point FAQ addressing public concerns on vehicle safety, mileage, pricing and the policy rationale for **E20** — petrol blended with 20% ethanol — after online misinformation and an automobile-industry press conference. India reached **20% average ethanol blending in 2025-26, roughly five years ahead of the 2030 target**, and since April 2026 all petrol sold nationwide must be E20 and meet a minimum Research Octane Number (RON) of 95. This is a clean GS3 case study linking energy security, rural incomes and climate goals.

**Key facts/figures (as cited by the Ministry):** ethanol blending has saved over **₹1.97 lakh crore** in foreign exchange, substituted about **316 lakh metric tonnes of crude oil**, avoided roughly **952 lakh tonnes of CO₂**, and transferred over **₹1.66 lakh crore to farmers**. On safety, the Ministry cited field data (e.g., Maruti Suzuki servicing ~2.84 crore vehicles in 2025-26, including ~1.5 crore older non-E20-certified vehicles, without E20-linked corrosion or abnormal wear). It stressed the rollout was a phased, two-decade "calibrated transition," not a rushed decision.

### Scheme-in-focus: Ethanol Blended Petrol (EBP) Programme
- **Ministry:** Petroleum & Natural Gas (with DFPD, MoRTH, Heavy Industries and Indian Railways in support).
- **Objective:** reduce crude-import dependence, cut vehicular emissions and raise farmer/distiller incomes by blending domestically produced ethanol into petrol.
- **Key features:** nationwide E20 mandate from April 2026 (RON ≥ 95); **1G ethanol** from sugarcane/surplus grains and **2G ethanol** from lignocellulosic biomass (paddy stubble, bagasse, bamboo) supported by **Viability Gap Funding under the PM JI-VAN Yojana**; the modified PM JI-VAN Yojana (2024) also covers Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF).
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** sugarcane and grain farmers, ethanol distillers, and the wider economy via reduced oil-import bills.
- **Significance:** flagship of India's energy-security + decarbonisation strategy; recurring prelims/mains theme on biofuels and Atmanirbharta in energy.

## 2. CSIR-IICT develops India's first indigenous process technology for the green refrigerant HFO-1234yf

**Issuing body:** CSIR–Indian Institute of Chemical Technology (CSIR-IICT), Hyderabad — under the Ministry of Science & Technology | **Date:** announced July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (indigenous R&D, Atmanirbhar Bharat) · GS3 — Environment (Montreal Protocol / Kigali Amendment)

CSIR-IICT has developed India's **first home-grown process to manufacture Hydrofluoroolefin-1234yf (HFO-1234yf)**, a fourth-generation refrigerant with an extremely low Global Warming Potential that is set to **replace high-GWP hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs)** in car air-conditioners, refrigeration and cooling. The process uses readily available raw materials and is "ready for industry transfer," reducing reliance on imported refrigerants. It matters because India, under the **Kigali Amendment (2016) to the Montreal Protocol**, has committed to freezing HFCs by 2028 and phasing them down ~80% by 2047 — so an indigenous low-GWP refrigerant is both a climate and a supply-chain win.

**Key facts:** HFOs are the successors to HFCs (which themselves replaced ozone-depleting CFCs/HCFCs); the Montreal Protocol (1987) controls ozone-depleting substances, and its **Kigali Amendment** brought HFCs — potent greenhouse gases, though not ozone-depleters — under control. *Related same-week CSIR development:* CSIR-CIMFR's **SALT (Smart Algal Liquid Tree)** — see Item 5.

## 3. IIP for May 2026 at 5.1%; MoSPI's new 2022-23 base-year series switches deflator to Output PPI

**Issuing body:** National Statistics Office, Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation (MoSPI) | **Date:** Quick Estimates released end-June/1 July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (industrial growth, statistical systems) · Reports & Indices

The **Index of Industrial Production (IIP)** rose **5.1%** year-on-year in May 2026 (index at 122.7 vs 116.7 in May 2025), led by manufacturing (+5.5%), electricity & gas (+9.9%) and capital goods (+12.9%), while mining contracted 1.6%. The exam-relevant development is methodological: MoSPI is moving the IIP to a **new base year of 2022-23** and, for the first time, adopting the **Output Producer Price Index (Output PPI) as the deflator in place of the Wholesale Price Index (WPI)** — a significant modernisation of India's industrial-statistics architecture.

**Key facts:** the IIP is compiled by the **NSO under MoSPI**; use-based classification splits output into Primary, Capital, Intermediate, Infrastructure/Construction, Consumer Durables and Consumer Non-durables goods; the three broad sectors are Mining, Manufacturing and Electricity, with Manufacturing carrying the largest weight.

## 4. Telangana to host the 8th Khelo India Youth Games in November 2026

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports (MYAS) — announced by Union Minister Mansukh Mandaviya | **Date:** 8 July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (sports policy, grassroots talent) · GS1 — Society (youth, sport)

MYAS announced that **Telangana will host the 8th Khelo India Youth Games (KIYG) 2026** in November 2026, with roughly **8,000 athletes, officials and support staff** expected. KIYG is the marquee school/youth-level competition under the **Khelo India Programme (launched 2018)**, which funds sports infrastructure and identifies grassroots talent. It is a compact governance-and-society item: know the scheme, its year of launch and the host-state rotation.

**Key facts:** the **7th KIYG (2025) was hosted by Bihar**, where Maharashtra topped the medal tally (followed by Haryana and Rajasthan); Khelo India runs both a Youth Games and a University Games stream under MYAS.

## 5. CSIR-CIMFR unveils SALT — a mobile "Smart Algal Liquid Tree" for urban air purification

**Issuing body:** CSIR–Central Institute of Mining & Fuel Research (CSIR-CIMFR), Dhanbad — under the Ministry of Science & Technology | **Date:** July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (air pollution, green tech) · GS3 — Science & Technology

CSIR-CIMFR has developed **SALT (Smart Algal Liquid Tree)**, a patented **mobile photobioreactor that uses microalgae** to absorb CO₂, release oxygen and trap dust — a compact "liquid tree" designed for dense urban and industrial areas where planting real trees is difficult. Units have been installed at **CSIR-CIMFR (Dhanbad, Jharkhand)** and at **Northern Coalfields Limited (Singrauli, Madhya Pradesh)**. It is a neat example of biotechnology applied to India's urban air-quality problem.

**Key facts:** a photobioreactor cultivates microalgae in a liquid medium; microalgae fix carbon dioxide via photosynthesis far faster per unit area than terrestrial plants, making "liquid trees" a supplement (not a replacement) for conventional afforestation.

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the Ethanol Blended Petrol (EBP) Programme, consider the following statements:
1. Under the E20 mandate, all petrol sold in India from April 2026 must contain 20% ethanol.
2. Ethanol produced from lignocellulosic biomass such as paddy stubble and bagasse is termed second-generation (2G) ethanol.
3. Viability Gap Funding for 2G ethanol and Sustainable Aviation Fuel projects is provided under the PM JI-VAN Yojana.

How many of the statements given above are correct?
(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) All three
(d) None

**Q2.** Consider the following statements regarding HFO-1234yf, in the news recently:
1. It is a low-Global-Warming-Potential refrigerant intended to replace hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs).
2. HFCs were brought under the control of the Montreal Protocol through its Kigali Amendment.
3. HFCs are controlled primarily because they deplete the stratospheric ozone layer.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q3.** With reference to the Index of Industrial Production (IIP), consider the following statements:
1. It is compiled and released by the National Statistics Office under the Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation.
2. In its revised series, the IIP is adopting the Output Producer Price Index as the deflator in place of the Wholesale Price Index.
3. Under its use-based classification, capital goods form one of the categories of the index.

How many of the statements given above are correct?
(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) All three
(d) None

## Answers

**Q1 — (c) All three.** From April 2026 all petrol is E20 (RON ≥ 95); 2G ethanol is made from lignocellulosic biomass; and the modified PM JI-VAN Yojana provides VGF for 2G ethanol and now Sustainable Aviation Fuel.

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** HFO-1234yf is a low-GWP refrigerant that replaces HFCs, and the Kigali Amendment (2016) brought HFCs under the Montreal Protocol. Statement 3 is wrong — HFCs do **not** deplete ozone; they are controlled because they are potent greenhouse gases.

**Q3 — (c) All three.** The IIP is compiled by the NSO/MoSPI; the new 2022-23 base-year series switches the deflator from WPI to the Output PPI; and capital goods is one of the six use-based categories (with primary, intermediate, infrastructure/construction, consumer durables and consumer non-durables).
