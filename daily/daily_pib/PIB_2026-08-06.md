# Daily PIB Briefing — 6 August 2026

## 1. Cabinet approves **GOBARdhan — the National Circular Bioenergy Scheme**, ₹23,731 crore

**Ministry/Body:** Union Cabinet (chaired by PM Narendra Modi); administered by the **Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas** | announced by I&B Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw, 6 August 2026

The Union Cabinet approved **GOBARdhan** as a unified national scheme to scale up **Compressed Biogas (CBG)** production, with a total outlay of **₹23,731 crore** for **FY 2026-27 to FY 2035-36**. The scheme converts agricultural residue, cattle dung, press mud, municipal organic waste and other biomass into clean fuel and organic manure. Government describes the target as a roughly **ten-fold increase in domestic CBG output**, with CBG established as "a major pillar of India's future energy mix".

**Why it matters:** This is the consolidation of a scattered CBG policy landscape — SATAT (2018), the older GOBARdhan initiative under the Department of Drinking Water & Sanitation, and CBG blending obligations — into one framework with **assured offtake + administered price + capital assistance**. It is a textbook *circular economy* intervention: waste-to-energy plus waste-to-fertiliser, linking farm income, municipal solid waste management and import substitution in natural gas.

### Scheme-in-focus: GOBARdhan (National Circular Bioenergy Scheme)

- **Name/expansion:** GOBARdhan — Galvanizing Organic Bio-Agro Resources Dhan; now re-cast as the **National Circular Bioenergy Scheme**.
- **Ministry:** Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas (nodal); Central Sector Scheme.
- **Objective:** Accelerate domestic CBG production, strengthen energy security, create rural livelihoods and build a circular bioeconomy by monetising organic waste.
- **Key features:**
  1. **Administered CBG price of ₹2,110 per MMBTU** with a minimum **10-year pricing framework**, giving producers long-term revenue visibility;
  2. **Capital assistance up to ₹2 crore per TPD** (tonne per day) of installed capacity for eligible greenfield CBG plants, plus a **credit guarantee mechanism** for MSME-based projects;
  3. A **CBG Offtake Assurance framework** — City Gas Distribution (CGD) entities must meet a notified **CBG blending obligation of 3% in FY 2026-27, 4% in FY 2027-28 and 5% from FY 2028-29** in CNG (transport) and PNG (domestic) segments;
  4. A dedicated **CBG Ecosystem Challenge Fund** for district-level implementation, plus pipeline connectivity and technology development support.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** CBG producers and MSME project developers; farmers (residue and dung monetisation, fermented organic manure); urban local bodies (organic MSW); CGD network consumers. Nationwide, FY 2026-27 to FY 2035-36.
- **Significance:** Moves CBG from a voluntary "offtake at will" model to a **mandated, priced market** — the single biggest structural fix the sector had asked for. Feeds directly into net-zero-2070, crop-residue-burning abatement and the Swachh Bharat organic-waste agenda.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Environment (waste-to-energy, circular economy) | GS3 — Economy (energy security, renewables) | GS3 — Agriculture (residue management, organic manure)

## 2. CCEA clears the 4-lane access-controlled **Guwahati–Tezpur Corridor of NH-15** in Assam — ₹8,970 crore

**Ministry/Body:** Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA); Ministry of Road Transport & Highways, 6 August 2026

The CCEA approved development of a **135.871 km, 4-lane access-controlled Guwahati–Tezpur Corridor of NH-15** in Assam at a capital cost of **₹8,970.20 crore** on **BOT (Build-Operate-Transfer) Toll mode** under NH(O). The project includes **five major bypasses totalling 58.7 km** at Baihata Chariali, Sipajhar, Kharupetia, Dekiajuli and Tezpur, and a **4.9 km Emergency Landing Facility (ELF) on the Tezpur bypass**, finalised in coordination with the **Indian Air Force**.

**Why it matters:** Two exam-relevant angles. First, the **ELF** — highway stretches doubling as emergency airstrips for the IAF are a recurring strategic-infrastructure theme in border states. Second, the corridor runs on the **north bank of the Brahmaputra** and will decongest **NH-27 (East–West Corridor)** on the south bank, improving connectivity to Arunachal Pradesh — i.e., dual-use infrastructure in the Act East / border-connectivity frame.

**Key facts:** Length 135.871 km; cost ₹8,970.20 crore; mode BOT (Toll); five bypasses (58.7 km); ELF 4.9 km at Tezpur; travel time between Guwahati and Tezpur expected to roughly halve.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Infrastructure (roads, PPP/BOT models) | GS3 — Internal Security (strategic border connectivity)

## 3. ICAR develops **India's first fully annotated Telomere-to-Telomere (T2T) genome of pigeonpea variety 'Asha'** — approved as the global reference genome

**Ministry/Body:** Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR), Department of Agricultural Research & Education, **Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare**; work led by **ICAR–National Institute for Plant Biotechnology (NIPB)**

ICAR has assembled and annotated a complete **telomere-to-telomere reference genome** of pigeonpea (*Cajanus cajan* L. Millsp.) variety **'Asha'** — the first such genome for the crop from India, and now accepted as the **global reference genome** for pigeonpea (arhar/tur). A T2T assembly means the sequence is gapless from one chromosome end to the other, including the hard-to-sequence centromeric and telomeric repeat regions.

**Why it matters:** Pulses are India's chronic import-dependence gap. A complete reference genome makes **marker-assisted and genomic selection** far more precise for traits like wilt/sterility-mosaic resistance, drought tolerance and protein content — shortening the breeding cycle for climate-resilient, high-yield varieties. It plugs straight into the **Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses (2025-26 to 2030-31)**, which targets about **35 million tonnes** of pulses by 2030-31.

**Key facts:** Genome size **752.65 million base pairs**, assembled into **92 contigs**, covering all **11 chromosomes** with **11 centromeres and 22 telomeres**; annotation identified **36,557 genes** producing **48,008 mRNAs**; transcriptome mapping showed **>99.9% read alignment**. Assembly designated **NIPB_CcT2T_4**, deposited with **NCBI** and released **20 April 2026**. Lineage: ICAR-NIPB produced the **world's first draft genome of a pulse crop in 2011**, with an improved version in 2017.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (crop improvement, pulses self-sufficiency) | GS3 — Science & Technology (genomics, biotechnology)

## 4. **C-DAC and Geological Survey of India sign an Umbrella MoU** on AI-driven mineral exploration and disaster management

**Ministry/Body:** **Centre for Development of Advanced Computing (C-DAC)** under the **Ministry of Electronics & IT** + **Geological Survey of India (GSI)** under the **Ministry of Mines**; signed at Electronics Niketan, New Delhi (early August 2026)

The MoU was signed by **E. Magesh, Director General, C-DAC** and **Pradeep Singh, Additional Director General, GSI**, in the presence of **MeitY Secretary S. Krishnan**. It pairs C-DAC's **High-Performance Computing (HPC)** stack with GSI's geoscientific datasets for **AI-based geophysical data processing, 3D subsurface mapping, mineral targeting and a domain-specific Large Language Model** for geoscience.

On the disaster side, the collaboration covers **slope-scale landslide monitoring using Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)**, landslide **early warning systems**, and **Glacial Lake Outburst Flood (GLOF)** studies.

**Why it matters:** A clean example of *compute as public infrastructure* — the same HPC/AI capability serving both **critical mineral and Rare Earth Element (REE) exploration** (economic security, supply-chain resilience under the National Critical Mineral Mission) and **Himalayan hazard early warning** (post-Chamoli, post-Sikkim GLOF policy priority).

**Key facts:** GSI was established in **1851**, functions under the **Ministry of Mines**, and is headquartered in **Kolkata**. C-DAC is MeitY's R&D organisation and India's principal HPC developer (the PARAM series, National Supercomputing Mission).

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (AI/HPC applications) | GS3 — Disaster Management (landslides, GLOF) | GS3 — Economy (critical minerals)

## 5. Bhopal hosts the **3rd BRICS Culture Working Group Meeting and BRICS Culture Ministers' Meeting** under India's 2026 Presidency

**Ministry/Body:** **Ministry of Culture**, with the Government of Madhya Pradesh | Kushabhau Thakre Convention Centre (Minto Hall), Bhopal, **5–8 August 2026**

Under **India's BRICS Presidency 2026**, the **3rd Culture Working Group (CWG) meeting** is being held on **5–6 August**, followed by the **BRICS Culture Ministers' Meeting** through **8 August**. Deliberations are organised around three priority areas:

1. **Creative economy, cultural & creative industries, copyright and Artificial Intelligence**;
2. **Protection of cultural heritage and return of cultural property**;
3. **Culture, climate and sustainable development**.

Delegates are to visit the **UNESCO World Heritage Site at Sanchi**, and the segment concludes with the adoption of a **BRICS Culture Ministers' Declaration** setting a future roadmap.

**Why it matters:** "Return of cultural property" is a live Indian diplomatic priority — repatriation of antiquities is pursued under the **UNESCO 1970 Convention** and the **Antiquities and Art Treasures Act, 1972**. The AI-and-copyright track is the newer thread: who owns training data drawn from cultural heritage, and how traditional cultural expressions are protected. Useful as a *soft power / plurilateral grouping* case study.

**Key facts:** BRICS Presidency 2026 — India. Venue Bhopal, MP; Sanchi Stupa (UNESCO WHS, inscribed 1989) on the delegate itinerary. India's BRICS calendar this month also included the **BRICS Anti-Corruption Working Group** (Hyderabad) and the **Heads of National Statistical Offices** meeting.

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the GOBARdhan scheme approved by the Union Cabinet in August 2026, consider the following statements:

1. It is a Central Sector Scheme administered by the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas.
2. It provides for an administered price for Compressed Biogas along with a minimum ten-year pricing framework.

1. It was developed for the pigeonpea variety 'Asha' by an institute under the Indian Council of Agricultural Research.
2. A telomere-to-telomere assembly implies a gap-free sequence covering the centromeric and telomeric regions of each chromosome.

(b) 2 and 3 only

**Q3.** With reference to the Geological Survey of India (GSI), consider the following statements:

1. It functions under the Ministry of Earth Sciences.
2. It was established in the nineteenth century and is headquartered in Kolkata.
3. It recently signed an umbrella agreement with C-DAC covering landslide monitoring using Synthetic Aperture Radar and Glacial Lake Outburst Flood studies.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only

## Answers

**Q1 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** GOBARdhan, the National Circular Bioenergy Scheme, has a ₹23,731 crore outlay for FY 2026-27 to FY 2035-36 and is administered by the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas. It fixes an administered CBG price of ₹2,110/MMBTU with a minimum 10-year pricing framework, offers capital assistance of up to ₹2 crore per TPD of capacity, and obliges CGD entities to blend CBG at 3% (FY27), 4% (FY28) and 5% (from FY29) in CNG (transport) and PNG (domestic).

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The genome of pigeonpea variety 'Asha' was assembled by ICAR–National Institute for Plant Biotechnology and accepted as the global reference genome. A T2T assembly is gapless end-to-end, resolving repeat-rich centromeres and telomeres — here 752.65 million base pairs in 92 contigs, covering all 11 chromosomes with 11 centromeres and 22 telomeres (two per chromosome), and 36,557 annotated genes. Pigeonpea is a diploid with 11 chromosome pairs (2n = 22).

**Q3 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** Statement 1 is incorrect: GSI functions under the **Ministry of Mines**, not the Ministry of Earth Sciences. GSI was set up in **1851** with headquarters at Kolkata. Its umbrella MoU with C-DAC (MeitY) covers AI/HPC-based geophysical data processing, 3D subsurface mapping and critical mineral/REE targeting, along with SAR-based landslide monitoring, early warning systems and GLOF studies.
