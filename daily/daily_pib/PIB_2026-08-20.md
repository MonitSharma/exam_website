## 1. Nine-judge Constitution Bench upholds the **broad definition of "industry"** laid down in *Bangalore Water Supply* (1978)

**Ministry/Body:** Supreme Court of India (implications for the Ministry of Labour & Employment)

A **nine-judge Constitution Bench headed by Chief Justice Surya Kant** has held, **by majority**, that the expansive meaning given to the term **"industry"** in the 1978 *Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Board* judgment is the **correct interpretation under the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947**. The broad test applies wherever an **employer–employee relationship** exists. Crucially, the Bench also **ring-fenced the future**: disputes arising hereafter will be governed by the **Industrial Relations Code, 2020**, which must be interpreted **on its own text and context** — the *Bangalore Water Supply* ratio will **not** govern the new Code.

**Key facts**

- The **original ruling** was delivered on **21 February 1978** by a **seven-judge Bench** in *Bangalore Water Supply & Sewerage Board v. A. Rajappa*.
- That 1978 ruling brought employees of **hospitals, educational institutions, clubs and government welfare departments** within the protective umbrella of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
- The 2026 verdict is by a **larger (nine-judge) Bench**, which is why it settles the question definitively rather than merely following precedent.
- **Forward-looking carve-out:** the **Industrial Relations Code, 2020** — one of the four labour codes that subsumes the ID Act, 1947, the Trade Unions Act, 1926 and the Industrial Employment (Standing Orders) Act, 1946 — will be read afresh.

**Why it matters:** This is a textbook illustration of two things UPSC likes: (i) **how the doctrine of precedent works** — a nine-judge Bench revisiting a seven-judge Bench; and (ii) the **transition from the old labour law architecture to the four labour codes**. Note the deliberate judicial restraint: the Court declined to import a 1978 interpretation into a 2020 statute, leaving Parliament's new text to be construed on its own terms. Notebook: **GS2 — Polity & Governance (Judiciary, statutory interpretation)**; overlaps **GS3 — Economy (labour reforms, labour codes)**.

## 2. MNRE launches **'Get Solar in 60 Secs'** — an instant-estimate tool for rooftop solar under PM Surya Ghar

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE)

MNRE has launched an interactive digital platform, **'Get Solar in 60 Secs'**, to compress the first and most confusing step of going solar — *"what size plant do I need and what will it cost me?"* — into a one-minute exercise. A household enters **name, phone number, State, district, previous month's electricity bill and sanctioned load**; the tool returns an **estimate of the ideal rooftop plant capacity, indicative cost and financial benefit**.

**Key facts**

- Operates under **PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana**, launched on **29 February 2024**.
- The scheme has enabled **more than 50 lakh households** to generate their own electricity in a little over two years.
- Target: **1 crore households**, with up to **300 units of free electricity per month**.
- Capacity goal: **30 GW of rooftop solar by 2026–27**, expected to generate around **1,000 billion units** of clean electricity and avoid an estimated **720 million tonnes of CO₂** over its lifetime.

**Why it matters:** India crossed **300 GW of installed non-fossil-fuel capacity** earlier this month, but rooftop solar remains the segment where the binding constraint is **behavioural and informational**, not technical — households do not know what they are buying. This is a "last-mile of information" intervention, a useful example for GS2 answers on **digital governance reducing transaction costs** and for GS3 answers on **decentralised renewable energy and prosumers**. Notebook: **GS3 — Environment / Economy (Renewable energy, energy transition)**.

## 3. India and Japan sign a **Memorandum of Arrangement on Maritime Security Cooperation**

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Defence (India) and Ministry of Defense (Japan)

*(Previewed on 19 August as an upcoming visit; the concrete outcome — a signed MoA — is today's development.)*

At the **India–Japan Defence Ministers' Meeting** in New Delhi, **Rajnath Singh** and Japanese Defence Minister **Shinjiro Koizumi** co-chaired talks and signed a **Memorandum of Arrangement (MoA) on Maritime Security Cooperation**. The two sides agreed to deepen the **defence technology partnership under 'Make in India'** and to strengthen coordination for the **protection of Sea Lines of Communication (SLOCs)**.

**Key facts**

- **Modes of cooperation agreed:** mutual visits by naval ships and units, joint exercises, personnel and **Subject Matter Expert exchanges**, and **logistical support** including use of ports, maintenance and repair facilities.
- **Exercise architecture** reaffirmed: **Dharma Guardian** (Army), **JAIMEX / Japan–India Maritime Exercise** (Navy), and **Veer Guardian 26** (Air Force), scheduled for later this year.
- **Doctrinal alignment:** India's **MAHASAGAR** vision (*Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions*) and Japan's **Free and Open Indo-Pacific (FOIP)** initiative were described as more closely aligned than ever.
- Builds on the **Japan–India Joint Statement** issued by the two leaders in **July 2026**; the two countries mark **75 years of diplomatic relations in 2027**.
- Mr Singh conveyed India's solidarity following the **earthquake in Japan's Kumamoto region**; Mr Koizumi acknowledged Indian Navy assistance during a medical emergency aboard a **Japan Maritime Self-Defense Force** vessel and had visited the **Western Naval Command and INS Chennai** in Mumbai the previous day.

**Why it matters:** Japan is one of only a handful of countries with which India holds a **2+2 ministerial dialogue** and a **Special Strategic and Global Partnership**. The MoA moves the relationship from declaratory Indo-Pacific language to **operational maritime domain cooperation** — logistics access, SLOC protection, interoperability. Remember the MAHASAGAR–FOIP pairing: it is the cleanest example of two national maritime doctrines being explicitly mapped onto each other. Notebook: **GS2 — International Relations (India–Japan, Indo-Pacific)**; overlaps **GS3 — Internal Security (maritime security)**.

## 4. **14th India–Nepal Joint Working Group on Border Management** begins in Dehradun

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Home Affairs (India) and Ministry of Home Affairs (Nepal)

The **14th meeting of the India–Nepal Joint Working Group (JWG) on Border Management** opened in **Dehradun, Uttarakhand**, for a two-day session focused on **strengthening border security and border infrastructure**, alongside other issues of mutual interest.

**Key facts**

- **Indian delegation:** led by **Pausumi Basu**, Joint Secretary, Ministry of Home Affairs.
- **Nepali delegation:** led by **Anand Kafle**, Joint Secretary, Nepal's Ministry of Home Affairs.
- The India–Nepal border is an **open border** of roughly **1,751 km**, touching **five Indian States** — Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal and Sikkim.
- The border is guarded on the Indian side by the **Sashastra Seema Bal (SSB)**, which is also the **lead intelligence agency** for the Indo-Nepal and Indo-Bhutan borders.

**Why it matters:** The open border is simultaneously India–Nepal's greatest asset (free movement of people, the *roti-beti* relationship) and its most persistent security vulnerability — used for **smuggling, fake Indian currency notes, human trafficking and third-country infiltration**. The JWG is the standing institutional mechanism where these are handled at the official level, distinct from the political-level **India–Nepal Joint Commission** (Foreign Ministers) and the **Boundary Working Group** (survey and demarcation). Notebook: **GS3 — Internal Security (border management)**; overlaps **GS2 — International Relations (India and its neighbourhood)**.

## Answers

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** The August 2026 verdict was delivered by a **nine-judge Constitution Bench headed by Chief Justice Surya Kant**, upholding by majority the broad meaning of "industry" laid down in *Bangalore Water Supply and Sewerage Board v. A. Rajappa*. Statement 2 is wrong on the number: the 1978 judgment (21 February 1978) was delivered by a **seven-judge**, not five-judge, Bench. The Court expressly held that future disputes fall under the **Industrial Relations Code, 2020**, to be interpreted on **its own text and context**, and that the *Bangalore Water Supply* ratio will not govern that interpretation — so statement 3 is correct.

**Q2 — (b) 2, 3 and 4 only.** PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana is implemented by the **Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE)**, not the Ministry of Power — statement 1 is wrong. Launched on 29 February 2024, it targets **1 crore households** with up to **300 units of free electricity per month** and **30 GW of rooftop solar by 2026–27** (about 1,000 billion units of clean electricity and ~720 million tonnes of CO₂ avoided over system life). The **'Get Solar in 60 Secs'** tool launched by MNRE takes name, phone number, State, district, **previous month's electricity bill and sanctioned load** and returns an estimate of ideal plant capacity, cost and benefit. More than **50 lakh households** have been covered so far.

**Q3 — (d) All four.** **Dharma Guardian** is the India–Japan **Army** exercise and **Veer Guardian** the **Air Force** exercise (the maritime one is JIMEX/JAIMEX). **MAHASAGAR** — *Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions* — is India's maritime vision, the successor framing to SAGAR, and **Free and Open Indo-Pacific (FOIP)** is **Japan's** initiative. At the 20 August Defence Ministers' Meeting both sides described the alignment between MAHASAGAR and FOIP as more important than ever, and signed a **Memorandum of Arrangement on Maritime Security Cooperation**.
