## 1. Ministry of Civil Aviation: **MoUs signed with 22 States/UTs for the next phase of Modified UDAN — Challenge Mode and a fresh Routes Bidding Round launched**

**What it is.** The Ministry of Civil Aviation signed Memoranda of Understanding with 22 States and Union Territories on 22 September 2026, formally opening the next phase of the **Modified UDAN (Regional Connectivity Scheme)**. The MoUs mark the launch of a **"Challenge Mode"** for airport/helipad development and a new **Routes Bidding Round** for airline operators.

**Why it matters.** Modified UDAN was approved as a ten-year successor to the original UDAN and shifts the scheme's centre of gravity from route subsidies alone to **hard infrastructure in unserved and remote regions**. Challenge Mode makes central support competitive — States that commit land, viability support and clearances faster get projects first — which is a live example of **cooperative-competitive federalism** in infrastructure delivery.

**Key facts.**
- Signatories include Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Gujarat, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal.
- Modified UDAN was launched by the Prime Minister on **4 July 2026**; period **FY 2026-27 to 2035-36 (10 years)**; outlay **₹28,840 crore** of budgetary support.
- Physical targets: **100 airports** to be developed from existing unserved airstrips; **200 modern helipads** in hilly, remote, island and aspirational regions; support to **441 aerodromes**.
- Allocation break-up: airport development **₹12,159 crore**; operation & maintenance **₹2,577 crore**; helipads **₹3,661 crore**; airline **viability gap funding ₹10,043 crore**.
- Original UDAN's record as of July 2026: **679 routes operationalised** across **95 airports**, **1.68 crore passengers** on **3.58 lakh flights**.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Modified UDAN (Regional Connectivity Scheme)**

- **Ministry:** Civil Aviation.
- **Objective:** make flying affordable and accessible in Tier-2/Tier-3, hilly, island and aspirational districts by combining airport/helipad creation with viability gap funding on thin routes.
- **Key features:** (i) ten-year horizon with a dedicated ₹28,840 crore budgetary outlay; (ii) **Challenge Mode** — competitive selection of States/airport projects rather than first-come allotment; (iii) VGF-backed **Routes Bidding Rounds** for airlines; (iv) explicit helipad component for hilly, remote and island areas.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** 22 States/UTs on board at this stage; 441 aerodromes in the support universe.
- **Significance:** links directly to the Viksit Bharat infrastructure pipeline and to regional equity in connectivity — a recurring GS3 theme on infrastructure and a GS2 theme on Centre–State delivery models.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure); cross-tag GS2 Governance (Centre–State).

## 2. Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment: **NAMASTE crosses 3.4 lakh workers profiled; waste pickers now formally inside the scheme's coverage**

**What it is.** The Ministry released consolidated progress figures for **NAMASTE (National Action for Mechanised Sanitation Ecosystem)**, the scheme that seeks to end hazardous manual cleaning of sewers and septic tanks by mechanising sanitation work and building a formal, socially-secured workforce.

**Why it matters.** NAMASTE is the operational arm of the **Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Act, 2013**, and now of the **Supreme Court's directions** on eliminating hazardous cleaning. The striking new element is the **extension of profiling to waste pickers** — a category historically outside any welfare net — which brings the informal urban sanitation economy into a state-recognised database for the first time at scale.

**Key facts.**
- **90,186 sewer and septic tank workers (SSWs) validated** across all **36 States/UTs**; of these **86,468 men, 3,713 women, 5 transgender**.
- **2,51,756 waste pickers profiled** across **34 States/UTs** — **1,35,233 men, 1,16,498 women, 25 transgender**. Combined: over **3.4 lakh workers** documented.
- **87,008 PPE kits** distributed; **753 safety-equipment sets** dispatched to **Emergency Response Sanitation Units (ERSUs)**.
- Health insurance registrations: **27,034** sewer workers and **7,526** waste pickers.
- Under the **Swachhta Udyami Yojana** component, **983 beneficiaries** received capital subsidy totalling **₹34.16 crore** for mechanised sanitation enterprises.
- Delivery network: **17 training providers** and **33 resource organisations**.

### Scheme-in-focus: **NAMASTE (National Action for Mechanised Sanitation Ecosystem)**

- **Ministry:** Social Justice and Empowerment (with MoHUA; implemented through the **National Safai Karamcharis Finance and Development Corporation, NSKFDC**).
- **Objective:** zero fatalities in sanitation work, zero hazardous manual cleaning, and all sanitation work performed by skilled, equipped and socially-secured workers.
- **Key features:** (i) profiling and validation of SSWs and, now, **waste pickers**; (ii) **PPE kits** and **ERSU** safety equipment for urban local bodies; (iii) **health insurance** linkage (Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY); (iv) **capital subsidy for sanitation entrepreneurship under Swachhta Udyami Yojana**.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** 3.4 lakh+ workers across 34–36 States/UTs.
- **Significance:** the clearest current test of whether mechanisation plus formalisation can displace a caste-linked hazardous occupation — high-value for GS1 (social exclusion) and GS2 (vulnerable sections, welfare schemes) alike.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance / Welfare schemes for vulnerable sections; cross-tag GS1 Society.

## 3. Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas: **7th High-Level Meeting of the OPEC–India Energy Dialogue**

**What it is.** Union Minister **Hardeep Singh Puri** and **OPEC Secretary General Haitham Al Ghais** held the **7th High-Level Meeting of the OPEC–India Energy Dialogue** on 22 September 2026, the institutionalised producer–consumer platform between India and the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries.

**Why it matters.** The meeting was held against **oil-market disruption linked to the Strait of Hormuz**, with Brent reported above **US$100 a barrel** — a direct stress test of India's energy security, current account and inflation arithmetic. The Indian position articulated was that **sustained producer–consumer dialogue is essential to support upstream investment** and to keep supply reliable and affordable; OPEC's side acknowledged India as a principal driver of future global demand growth.

**Key facts.**
- Format: institutionalised bilateral dialogue between **OPEC** and India's **Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas**; this was the **7th** high-level meeting.
- Agenda: short-, medium- and long-term oil market outlook; **energy security**; **adequacy of investment** across the petroleum value chain.
- Context: disruption to flows through the **Strait of Hormuz** affecting major importers including India and China; Brent crude reported beyond **US$100/bbl**.
- Structural point: India is among the world's largest crude importers, with import dependence in the high-80s percentage range — every sustained $10/bbl move materially widens the oil import bill.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (groupings & agreements involving India); cross-tag GS3 Economy (energy security).

## 4. Ministry of Ayush: **11th Ayurveda Day (23 September 2026) — theme "Ayurveda for a Healthier Tomorrow"; national ecosystem data released**

**What it is.** The Ministry of Ayush set out participation and system-level data ahead of the **11th Ayurveda Day**, observed on **23 September 2026** under the theme **"Ayurveda for a Healthier Tomorrow"**. Ayurveda Day is being positioned as a **year-round national health communication campaign** rather than a single observance.

**Why it matters.** The data is the cleanest recent statement of how far **Ayush has been mainstreamed into the public health system** — co-location in NHM facilities, salaried Ayush manpower funded by the Centre, and the Ayushman Arogya Mandir network. It is the standard UPSC hook for questions on integrating traditional medicine with the formal health system and on India's soft-power projection through traditional knowledge.

**Key facts.**
- Participation: over **9,700 events** across **30 States/UTs**; more than **3.6 lakh registered participants**; nearly **89.3 lakh citizens** pledged support via the microsite; **10 countries and territories** participating with **1,900+ overseas participants** (UK, Afghanistan, Bulgaria, Spain, Algeria, Australia, Denmark, Iceland, USA, British Indian Ocean Territory).
- **National Ayush Mission (NAM)** releases to States/UTs: **₹6,406.99 crore** between **FY 2014-15 and FY 2025-26**.
- **12,260 Ayush Ayushman Arogya Mandirs** operational as of **19 September 2026**, against a NAM target of **12,500**.
- Ayush services co-located in **13,249 public health facilities** under NHM; salaries funded for **25,322 Ayush doctors** and **5,666 Ayush paramedics**.
- Education: **17 new Ayush colleges** approved under NAM (12 of them Ayurveda); India now has **593 Ayurveda colleges** with **45,245 UG** and **5,673 PG** seats.
- **AIIA** achieved empanelment with **all 32 general insurance companies** (February 2026); **AYURGYAN** scheme released **₹17.79 crore** for **34 research projects**, of which Ayurveda received **₹9.38 crore** for 17.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance (Health; issues relating to development and management of Social Sector/Services).

## 5. Ministry of Information and Broadcasting: **72nd National Film Awards conferred at Ekta Nagar, Gujarat — first ceremony held outside Delhi**

**What it is.** President **Droupadi Murmu** presented the **72nd National Film Awards** (for films of 2024) at **Ekta Nagar (Kevadia), Gujarat**, on 22 September 2026. Winners had been announced on **18 July 2026**. This is the **first time the ceremony has been held outside Delhi**, under a policy of taking national awards to India's major tourist destinations.

**Why it matters.** Two exam angles. First, **cultural federalism and decentralised cultural policy** — moving a national honour out of the capital and tying it to a heritage/tourism site. Second, the awards remain the state's principal instrument for recognising **linguistic diversity in Indian cinema**, including a first-time award to a **Santhali** film in the non-feature category.

**Key facts.**
- **Best Feature Film:** *Article 370*.
- **Best Actor (Leading Role):** shared by **Mammootty** (*Bramayugam*) and **Kartik Aaryan** (*Chandu Champion*).
- **Best Actress (Leading Role):** **Yami Gautam** (*Article 370*).
- **Best Director:** **Rajkumar Periasamy** (*Amaran*).
- **Dadasaheb Phalke Award (2024):** **Anant Nag**, veteran Kannada actor.
- **Best Popular Film Providing Wholesome Entertainment:** *Kalki 2898 AD*.
- Awards spanned Tamil, Malayalam, Telugu, Kannada, Marathi and other languages; a **Santhali** film was recognised in the non-feature category for the first time.
- Venue note: Ekta Nagar is the site of the **Statue of Unity**, Kevadia, Narmada district, Gujarat.

**Destination notebook:** GS1 — Indian Society / Art & Culture; useful also for Prelims "awards & institutions".

## 6. Ministry of Home Affairs: **3rd National Conference of ANTF Heads inaugurated — Vision Document for Narcotics Control (2026–29) operationalised** *(UPDATE)*

*(Flagged as upcoming in the 21 September briefing; what is new is that the conference has been inaugurated, its theme and pillar structure are on record, and specific launches were made.)*

**What it is.** Union Home Minister **Amit Shah** inaugurated the **3rd National Conference of Heads of Anti-Narcotics Task Forces (ANTF) of States/UTs**, held **22–23 September 2026** in New Delhi, with participation from all **36 States/UTs**, under the theme **"United Resolve, Shared Responsibility."**

**Why it matters.** It converts the **Vision Document for Narcotics Control (2026–2029)** from a policy statement into an implementation review mechanism with State-level owners, and is the standard institutional answer to GS3 questions on drug trafficking, synthetic drugs and darknet-enabled crime.

**Key facts.**
- Four strategic pillars of the Vision Document (2026–29): **enforcement, intelligence and operations**; **precursors and synthetic drugs control**; **demand and harm reduction**; **capacity building and coordination**.
- **Eight technical sessions**, covering network dismantling, demand reduction, enforcement effectiveness, synthetic drug control, destruction of illicit cultivation and darknet investigations.
- Launches: **four television commercials** on drug awareness; **foundation stones for new NCB Zonal complexes at Lucknow and Dehradun**; a **revamped NCB website**.
- **Telangana ANTF** adjudged the best-performing ANTF among States/UTs.
- Stated goal: a **Drug-Free India by 2047**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security (challenges to internal security through communication networks; money laundering and organised crime).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the **Modified UDAN (Regional Connectivity Scheme)**, consider the following statements:

1. It has been approved for a period of ten years from FY 2026-27 to FY 2035-36.
2. Its budgetary outlay is over ₹28,000 crore, of which the largest single head is viability gap funding for airlines.
3. It includes a dedicated component for developing modern helipads in hilly, remote, island and aspirational regions.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Consider the following statements about the **NAMASTE** scheme:

1. It is implemented by the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs alone.
2. Its coverage has been extended from sewer and septic tank workers to waste pickers.
3. It provides capital subsidy for sanitation-related enterprises under the Swachhta Udyami Yojana component.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** The **OPEC–India Energy Dialogue**, in the news recently, is best described as:

(a) A multilateral treaty obliging OPEC members to supply crude oil to India at concessional rates
(b) A dispute-settlement mechanism under the International Energy Agency for oil-pricing disagreements
(c) A joint venture between OPEC and Indian public sector oil companies for overseas exploration
(d) An institutionalised producer–consumer dialogue between OPEC and India's Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.**
Statement 1 is correct: Modified UDAN runs for ten years, FY 2026-27 to FY 2035-36. Statement 2 is **incorrect** on the second half — the outlay is ₹28,840 crore, but the largest single head is **airport development at ₹12,159 crore**, ahead of airline viability gap funding at ₹10,043 crore. Statement 3 is correct: ₹3,661 crore is earmarked for 200 modern helipads in hilly, remote, island and aspirational regions.

**Q2 — (c) 2 and 3 only.**
Statement 1 is **incorrect**: NAMASTE is a scheme of the **Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment**, implemented in coordination with MoHUA through NSKFDC — not by MoHUA alone. Statement 2 is correct: 2,51,756 waste pickers have now been profiled across 34 States/UTs, alongside 90,186 validated sewer and septic tank workers. Statement 3 is correct: 983 beneficiaries have received ₹34.16 crore in capital subsidy under the Swachhta Udyami Yojana component.

**Q3 — (d).**
The OPEC–India Energy Dialogue is an institutionalised producer–consumer platform between OPEC and the Ministry of Petroleum and Natural Gas; its 7th High-Level Meeting was held on 22 September 2026 between Minister Hardeep Singh Puri and OPEC Secretary General Haitham Al Ghais. It carries no concessional-pricing obligation, is not an IEA mechanism, and is not an exploration joint venture.
