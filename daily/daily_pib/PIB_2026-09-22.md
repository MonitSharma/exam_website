## 1. Ministry of Commerce & Industry: **India–New Zealand FTA to enter into force on 20 October 2026**

**What it is.** The India–New Zealand Free Trade Agreement, signed on **27 April 2026** at Bharat Mandapam by Commerce & Industry Minister Piyush Goyal and New Zealand's Trade Minister Todd McClay, will **enter into force on 20 October 2026 (Vijaya Dashami)**. Both sides completed internal processes; the New Zealand Parliament passed the enabling legislation on **16 September 2026**. The date was announced jointly by the two ministers on 21 September.

**Why it matters.** This is India's newest FTA with a developed economy, and the template is the one UPSC keeps returning to: full market access on the export side, hard carve-outs on agriculture and dairy, and services/mobility as the real prize. It also operationalises the **India–New Zealand Strategic Partnership: Roadmap to 2030**, announced during PM Modi's **July 2026 visit to New Zealand — the first by an Indian PM in four decades**.

**Key facts and figures**
- **Goods (India's gains):** every tariff line covering **100% of India's exports** to New Zealand becomes duty-free from day one; New Zealand's peak tariffs of **up to 10%** are eliminated. Immediate gainers — textiles and apparel, leather and footwear, gems and jewellery, engineering goods, processed foods. India also gets duty-free inputs: wooden logs, coking coal, metal scrap.
- **Agricultural protection (India's carve-outs):** **dairy, animal meat (except sheep), key agricultural commodities, sugar and edible oils excluded** from tariff concessions.
- **Calibrated access for NZ:** apples, kiwifruit and **Manuka honey** get access only through **Tariff Rate Quotas (TRQs) with a Minimum Import Price and seasonal import windows**, plus safeguards.
- **Agriculture Productivity Partnership:** dedicated Action Plans for kiwifruit, apples and honey; **Centres of Excellence** for orchard management, post-harvest practices, supply chains, food safety and sustainable beekeeping; a **Joint Agriculture Productivity Council** to monitor that market access is matched by cooperation.
- **Investment:** New Zealand commits to facilitate **USD 20 billion** of investment into India (agriculture, manufacturing, infrastructure, start-ups).
- **Services & mobility:** access across roughly **118 sectors** (IT, professional services, audio-visual, construction, tourism); **MFN treatment locked in across about 139 sub-sectors**; **5,000 Temporary Employment Entry visas** a year for skilled Indians and **1,000 Working Holiday visas** a year for young Indians; **uncapped student mobility** with post-study work rights of **up to 3 years for STEM graduates and 4 years for doctoral scholars**.
- **Pharma:** New Zealand will accept inspection approvals from **US FDA, EMA, UK MHRA and Health Canada**, cutting market-entry time for Indian pharma and medical devices.
- **Trade base:** bilateral merchandise trade was about **USD 1.1 billion in 2025-26**; aspirational goal of doubling two-way goods and services trade to **NZ$7 billion (~₹35,000 crore) by 2030**.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 (International Relations — India's bilateral trade diplomacy) with a cross-entry in GS3 (Economy — external sector, FTAs and agricultural sensitivity).

## 2. Ministry of Health and Family Welfare: **7th National Formulary of India launched; Government approves the Biovigilance Programme of India**

**What it is.** At the **6th National Pharmacovigilance Week (17–23 September 2026)** programme organised by the **Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission (IPC)** in New Delhi, MoS for Health & Family Welfare **Anupriya Patel** launched the **7th Edition of the National Formulary of India (NFI)**, a *Guidance Document for Hospitals on Safety Monitoring and Reporting of Medical Device-Related Adverse Events*, and the **ADR-PvPI 2.0 mobile app for iOS**. She also announced that the Government has **approved the Biovigilance Programme of India**.

**Why it matters.** India now has a three-legged post-market safety architecture — medicines, devices and now transplant biologicals. For UPSC this is the cleanest available example of regulatory state-capacity building in health, and the IPC/pharmacopoeia angle links directly to India's "pharmacy of the world" positioning.

**Key facts and figures**
- **Biovigilance Programme of India:** IPC will lead reporting, assessment, monitoring and prevention of **adverse events associated with medicines and biological products used in organ and tissue transplantation**, including products administered to **both donors and recipients**.
- The three vigilance programmes now cover: **PvPI** (Pharmacovigilance Programme of India — adverse drug reactions, coordinated by the National Coordination Centre at IPC); **MvPI** (Materiovigilance Programme of India — medical devices); **Biovigilance** (transplantation).
- India has moved from **123rd position (2009–2014) to 8th globally** in contributions to the **WHO patient safety database (VigiBase)**.
- Around **1,150 ADR reporting centres** operational across public and private hospitals and medical colleges; network to be extended to the **primary healthcare level**.
- **~25 countries** recognise and accept the **Indian Pharmacopoeia**; IPC became a member of the **Pharmacopoeial Discussion Group in 2023**.
- Pharmacovigilance Week theme: *"Promoting Rational Use of Medicines through Pharmacovigilance: A Step Towards Safer Healthcare."*
- Digital stack: **NFI Online, IP Online, ADR-PvPI 2.0 app, Adverse Drug Reaction Monitoring System (ADRMS)**; AI and advanced data analytics flagged for expansion.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Biovigilance Programme of India**
- **Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Health and Family Welfare; implemented through the **Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission (IPC)**, Ghaziabad.
- **Objective:** systematic reporting, assessment, monitoring and prevention of adverse events linked to medicines and biological products used in **organ and tissue transplantation**.
- **Key features:** (i) IPC as the lead national coordinating body; (ii) coverage of products given to donors *and* recipients; (iii) designed to sit alongside PvPI (drugs) and MvPI (devices) as the third vigilance pillar; (iv) feeds the same digital reporting backbone (ADRMS / ADR-PvPI app).
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** transplant donors and recipients nationwide; transplant centres, hospitals and tissue banks as reporting nodes.
- **Significance:** closes the last major gap in India's post-market safety surveillance, and strengthens India's claim to global leadership in pharmacovigilance sciences rather than only in drug manufacturing.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 (Governance — health-sector regulation, institutions like IPC) with a cross-entry in GS3 (Science & Technology — pharmaceuticals, biotechnology regulation).

## 3. Ministry of Mines: **National Seminar on India's Critical Minerals Recycling Ecosystem and Circular Economy**

**What it is.** Union Minister for Coal and Mines **G. Kishan Reddy** inaugurated the National Seminar on *"India's Critical Minerals Recycling Ecosystem and Circular Economy"*, organised by the Ministry of Mines with the **Materials Recycling Association of India (MRAI)** in New Delhi on 21 September. The theme: recovering critical minerals from **End-of-Life (EOL) products** and e-waste as **secondary sources**.

**Why it matters.** India's critical-minerals strategy so far has been read mainly through exploration and overseas acquisition (KABIL, the National Critical Mineral Mission, offshore and auctioned blocks). This release shifts the emphasis to the **recycling/secondary-source leg** — which is where India's actual near-term leverage lies, given that it imports most of its critical minerals. Note the explicit framing of **Jan Bhagidari** (public participation) as a mineral-security instrument: citizens are asked to treat e-waste and EOL products as resources rather than refuse.

**Key facts and figures**
- Ministers present: **G. Kishan Reddy** (Coal and Mines); **Satish Chandra Dubey** (MoS, Coal and Mines); **Keshav Chandra** (Secretary, Ministry of Mines).
- Strategic end-uses of critical minerals identified: **clean-energy transition, defence preparedness, digital economy, advanced manufacturing, electronics**.
- Secondary sources flagged: **e-waste, industrial waste and other End-of-Life materials**.
- Emphasis on the full chain — **exploration, processing, recovery and recycling** — plus start-ups, research institutions and responsible e-waste management.
- MoS Dubey pressed for **village-to-village grassroots outreach** to channel recyclable resources into the **formal** recycling ecosystem (the informal-sector problem in e-waste).

**Destination notebook:** GS3 (Economy — mineral resources and resource security) with cross-entries in GS3 Environment (circular economy, e-waste management) and GS3 Science & Technology (recycling technology).

## 4. PIB Backgrounder (Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs): **"Reclaiming the Land Beneath the Waste" — transforming India's legacy dumpsites**

**What it is.** A PIB Backgrounder consolidating the legacy-waste remediation record under **Swachh Bharat Mission–Urban (SBM-U)** and **SBM-U 2.0**, built around four case studies: **Gorakhpur (UP), Bhalswa (Delhi), Bhandewadi (Nagpur) and Yakalaspura (Raichur, Karnataka)**.

**Why it matters.** Legacy dumpsites are the most physically visible failure of urban solid-waste management and a standing GS3 Environment / GS2 Urban Governance question. This backgrounder is useful because it gives named programmes (**Lakshya Zero Dumpsite**, **DRAP**), hard tonnage figures, and a clean process description of **biomining** — all of which are examinable.

**Key facts and figures**
- **SBM-U** launched **October 2014**; **SBM-U 2.0** launched **October 2021**.
- **3.66 crore metric tonnes** of legacy waste remediated between **January and December 2025**; **459 Urban Local Bodies across 29 States** achieved **complete** dumpsite remediation in that period.
- **Lakshya Zero Dumpsite** — structured, time-bound scientific remediation of dumpsites across States/ULBs under SBM-U 2.0.
- **Dumpsite Remediation Accelerator Programme (DRAP)** — launched **November 2025**, a nationwide mission-mode initiative to fast-track remediation of remaining dumpsites.
- **Gorakhpur:** 40-acre site, ~**400 tonnes/day** processed by biomining; now the **"Kachre Se Kanchan" Rapti Eco Park** (~500 visitors/day), with an SHG-run restaurant (*Shakti Ki Rasoi*).
- **Bhalswa, Delhi:** ~70 acres; biomining in mission mode since **July 2022**; **99.07 lakh metric tonnes** of legacy waste remediated by **31 July 2026**; more than half the site reclaimed by May 2026. *Swachhata Hi Seva 2025* was launched nationally from Bhalswa.
- **Nagpur (Bhandewadi):** **over 10 lakh metric tonnes** treated; site converted into an **Integrated Municipal Solid Waste Processing Facility** (material recovery, composting, waste-derived fuel, CCTV and real-time monitoring).
- **Raichur (Yakalaspura):** 35-acre site, **~62,252 metric tonnes** processed, **~22 acres** reclaimed; **5–7 acres Miyawaki forest** with ~**60,000 bamboo and native saplings**; plantation began **5 June 2026** (World Environment Day). Concept: *"Toxic Waste to Ecological Wealth."*
- **Biomining** separates legacy waste into **refuse-derived fuel (RDF), black soil, recyclables and inert material**; RDF goes to cement plants for **co-processing**.
- Accountability instruments: **City Solid Waste Action Plans**, the **SBM-U portal**, and annual **Swachh Survekshan** rankings.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Dumpsite Remediation Accelerator Programme (DRAP)**
- **Ministry:** Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, under **Swachh Bharat Mission–Urban 2.0**.
- **Objective:** fast-track, in mission mode, the scientific remediation of the remaining legacy dumpsites in Indian cities.
- **Key features:** (i) launched November 2025 as a nationwide accelerator; (ii) works through biomining/bioremediation of accumulated waste; (iii) paired with fresh-waste processing contracts so that new legacy waste does not re-accumulate; (iv) environmental safeguards — fire prevention, leachate management.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** urban local bodies with unremediated dumpsites; residents of surrounding habitations.
- **Significance:** converts SBM-U from a sanitation programme into an **urban land-reclamation and ecological-restoration** programme, and creates measurable targets (tonnes remediated, acres reclaimed) that Swachh Survekshan can score.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 (Environment — solid waste management, circular economy) with a cross-entry in GS2 (Governance — urban local bodies, centrally sponsored schemes).

## 5. Ministry of Ayush: **Sowa-Rigpa Working Group holds its first meeting under PCIM&H**

**What it is.** The first meeting of the **Sowa-Rigpa Working Group**, constituted under the **Pharmacopoeia Commission for Indian Medicine & Homoeopathy (PCIM&H)**, was held on **18 September 2026** at PCIM&H, **Ghaziabad (Uttar Pradesh)**. It was chaired by **Dr. Kousthubha Upadhyaya**, Director, PCIM&H, with four Sowa-Rigpa experts including **Dr. Padma Gurmet, Director, National Institute of Sowa-Rigpa, Leh**.

**Why it matters.** Sowa-Rigpa — the trans-Himalayan (Amchi) system of medicine practised in Ladakh, Himachal, Sikkim, Arunachal and Darjeeling — is the newest of the recognised Indian systems of medicine, but it has had **no statutory pharmacopoeia or formulary of its own**. Without those, quality standards and regulatory enforcement under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act cannot be applied to Sowa-Rigpa drugs. This meeting formally begins that standardisation work.

**Key facts and figures**
- The Working Group will prepare **Formularies, Pharmacopoeias and other statutory and non-statutory documents** for Sowa-Rigpa drugs, on a step-wise action plan.
- Work to be done **within the ambit of PCIM&H** and in accordance with the **Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940** and Rules thereunder.
- **PCIM&H** is a **subordinate office under the Ministry of Ayush**; it develops Pharmacopoeias and Formularies and functions as the **Central Drug Testing-cum-Appellate Laboratory** for Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy.
- **National Institute of Sowa-Rigpa** is at **Leh, Ladakh**.
- *Static hook:* Sowa-Rigpa is often called the "Science of Healing"; it is the only AYUSH system with a distinct trans-Himalayan Buddhist medical lineage, and shares diagnostic concepts with Tibetan medicine.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 (Governance — AYUSH institutions, drug regulation) with cross-entries in GS1 (Society/Culture — indigenous knowledge systems, Himalayan traditions) and GS3 (Science & Technology — standardisation of traditional medicine).

**Q1.** Consider the following statements regarding the India–New Zealand Free Trade Agreement, which is to enter into force in October 2026:

1. From the date of entry into force, every tariff line covering 100% of India's exports to New Zealand becomes duty-free.
2. Dairy products, sugar and edible oils have been kept out of India's tariff concessions.

(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

1. The Narcotics Control Bureau functions under the Ministry of Home Affairs.
2. The Central Bureau of Narcotics functions under the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance.
3. Anti-Narcotics Task Forces (ANTFs) are constituted at the level of States and Union Territories.

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1, 2 and 3

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** All of India's export tariff lines to New Zealand go duty-free from day one, and dairy, animal meat (except sheep), key agricultural commodities, sugar and edible oils are excluded from India's concessions. Statement 3 is wrong: apples, kiwifruit and Manuka honey get only **calibrated** access through **Tariff Rate Quotas with a Minimum Import Price and seasonal import windows**, with safeguards for domestic farmers.

**Q2 — (b) Ministry of Ayush.** PCIM&H is a subordinate office under the **Ministry of Ayush**, responsible for developing Pharmacopoeias and Formularies and acting as the Central Drug Testing-cum-Appellate Laboratory for Indian Systems of Medicine and Homoeopathy. (The **National Institute of Sowa-Rigpa** is at **Leh**.)

**Q3 — (c) 1, 2 and 3.** The **NCB** is under the **Ministry of Home Affairs**; the **CBN** (and the **DRI**) are under the **Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance**; **ANTFs** are State/UT-level bodies — heads of ANTFs from all **36 States/UTs** attend the national conference convened by NCB.
