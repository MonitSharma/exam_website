## 1. Ministry of Environment, Forest & Climate Change: **Process begins for release of captive-bred Great Indian Bustards into the wild at Desert National Park, Jaisalmer** (3 Oct)
- **What:** PIB reports that, "in culmination of 10 years of dedicated efforts", Union Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav and the Rajasthan Chief Minister initiated the process for release of captive-bred Great Indian Bustards (GIBs) into the wild at Desert National Park, Jaisalmer. Press reports put the release schedule at 3 Oct (Ramdevra) and 4 Oct (Sudasari area), with ten birds having completed "rewilding" training in a specially designed tunnel; the exact number for the first release is to be fixed after assessment by Wildlife Institute of India (WII) scientists.
- **Why it matters:** This is the first move from *ex-situ* conservation breeding to *in-situ* rewilding for one of India's most threatened birds. It tests whether captive-bred birds can survive predation, power lines and habitat loss — the three main killers of the species. It also marks a shift from species-specific work to an **ecosystem (grassland) approach**: the National Board for Wildlife standing committee recently decided to fold Lesser Florican and Bengal Florican into the bustard recovery programme.
- **Key facts:** Project GIB launched **2016**; captive breeding began **2019**; first captive-bred chick **March 2023**; rewilding training since **2025**. Release site originally planned in Kachchh (Gujarat) but Thar chosen as native habitat. Post-release tracking will study behaviour and survival. "Jump-start" technique: fertile captive eggs swapped with infertile wild eggs so wild females incubate and rear the chicks. Other candidate grasslands named: MP, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Andhra Pradesh.
- **Static link (standard knowledge):** GIB is Rajasthan's State bird, IUCN *Critically Endangered*, Schedule I of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, and listed in CMS Appendix I; Desert National Park (Jaisalmer–Barmer) is its main stronghold.
- **Notebook:** GS3 Environment (species conservation, ex-situ vs in-situ, grasslands, renewable-energy vs biodiversity conflict).

## 2. Ministry of Finance (NABARD group): **NABKISAN lists India's first WASH-focused Social Bond, raises ₹180 crore** (3 Oct)
- **What:** NABKISAN Finance Limited, a NABARD subsidiary, listed on the **NSE** a **5-year** social bond (matures September 2031) that raised **₹180 crore**, subscribed about **1.8x**. Coupon **8.10%**; rated **CRISIL AAA (Stable)** and **CARE AAA (Stable)**. Proceeds will expand access to safe water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) in rural and underserved communities. **Water.org** acted as technical advisor and knowledge partner.
- **Why it matters:** It shows sustainable-finance instruments moving beyond "green" into labelled **social** bonds for basic services, using private capital to supplement budget-funded missions such as Jal Jeevan Mission and Swachh Bharat Mission. It is a useful example for answers on innovative financing for SDG 6 and on the role of NABARD in rural infrastructure.
- **Key facts:** Issuer — NABKISAN Finance Ltd (NABARD subsidiary); NABARD Chairman cited: Dr Shaji Krishnan V; MD & CEO of NABKISAN: Immanuvel Ganesan. Earlier precedent: NABARD's first social bonds (₹1,040.50 crore, Sept 2023).
- **Notebook:** GS3 Economy (sustainable finance, rural credit, bond market) + GS2 Governance (water & sanitation delivery; SDG 6).

## 3 Prelims MCQs

**Q1.** With reference to the captive-bred Great Indian Bustard release initiated in October 2026, consider the following statements:
1. The release is being carried out at Desert National Park, Jaisalmer, rather than the originally planned Kachchh site.
2. The captive-breeding programme began in 2019 and the first captive-bred chick was born in March 2023.
3. The Great Indian Bustard is classified as "Endangered" on the IUCN Red List.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q2.** Consider the following about the social bond listed by NABKISAN Finance Limited in October 2026:
1. It is India's first social bond dedicated exclusively to the WASH sector.
2. NABKISAN is a subsidiary of NABARD.
3. The bond has a tenor of 10 years.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 only (b) 1 and 2 only (c) 2 and 3 only (d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q3.** The "jump-start" technique used in India's Great Indian Bustard conservation breeding programme involves:
(a) Artificial insemination of wild females with captive-bred semen
(b) Exchanging fertile captive eggs with infertile eggs of wild females so wild birds incubate and rear the chicks
(c) Releasing adult birds fitted with satellite collars directly into protected areas
(d) Translocating wild chicks from Rajasthan to Gujarat

## Answers

1. **(b)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. The GIB is *Critically Endangered* (not "Endangered") on the IUCN Red List.
2. **(b)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct; the bond is a 5-year instrument (maturing September 2031), not 10-year.
3. **(b)** — Fertile eggs from captive breeding are swapped with infertile eggs of wild females, which then incubate and rear the chicks.
