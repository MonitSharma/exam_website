## 1. Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation (NSO): **PLFS Monthly Bulletin, August 2026 — overall LFPR rises to 55.6%; rural unemployment falls to 4.1%, lowest since January 2026**

**What it is.** The National Statistical Office released the **seventeenth** Periodic Labour Force Survey Monthly Bulletin, for **August 2026**. Headline reading: labour force participation strengthened, worker-population ratio hit its highest level since March 2026, and the rural unemployment rate declined.

**Why it matters.** The monthly PLFS series (methodology revised from January 2025 to give monthly and quarterly all-India estimates) is now the standard high-frequency labour indicator for GS3 Economy. August 2026's story is a **rural-led improvement with an urban stall** — rural LFPR, WPR and UR all improved year-on-year while every urban indicator either flatlined or slipped. Female LFPR rising for a second consecutive month, driven entirely by rural women, is the other point worth carrying into an answer on female labour force participation.

**Key facts and figures (persons aged 15+, Current Weekly Status)**

| Indicator (Aug 2026) | All-India | Rural | Urban |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LFPR** | **55.6%** (July: 55.4%) | 58.2% (July: 58.0%) | 50.4% (unchanged) |
| **WPR** | **52.8%** (July: 52.5%) | 55.8% (July: 55.4%) | 47.0% (unchanged) |
| **UR** | **5.0%** (July: 5.1%) | **4.1%** (July: 4.5%) | 6.8% (July: 6.7%) |

- **Year-on-year:** overall LFPR up 0.6 pp (55.0% → 55.6%); rural LFPR up 1.2 pp (57.0% → 58.2%); urban LFPR **down** 0.5 pp (50.9% → 50.4%).
- **Female LFPR:** 34.8% overall (July: 34.4%), up **1.1 pp** year-on-year. Rural female LFPR 39.4% (up 2.0 pp y-o-y); urban female LFPR 25.4% (**down** 0.7 pp y-o-y).
- **Female WPR** rose year-on-year to **33.0%** from 32.0%.
- **Rural UR at 4.1%** is the lowest since January 2026; rural female UR fell to **3.9%**, rural male UR to 4.3%.
- Overall male UR declined for **three consecutive months** to 4.9% (from 5.4% in May 2026).
- **Sample:** 3,70,160 persons surveyed — 2,11,353 rural and 1,58,807 urban.

**Prelims hooks.** PLFS is conducted by **NSO under MoSPI**. Monthly estimates follow the **Current Weekly Status (CWS)** approach at the **all-India level only**. LFPR = labour force ÷ population; WPR = workers ÷ population; UR = unemployed ÷ labour force. Rural UR (4.1%) is **lower** than urban UR (6.8%) — a counter-intuitive fact that examiners like.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Employment, Growth & Development); Reports & Indices.

## 2. Ministry of Defence (DRDO): **'VIMARSH' DRDO–Industry Synergy Meet — framework to share DRDO software source codes with licensee industries; new MSME/start-up entry package**

**What it is.** At **VIMARSH**, a DRDO–Industry Synergy Meet in New Delhi on 15 September 2026, the Raksha Mantri launched (i) a **standardised, secure framework for sharing DRDO-developed software source codes with licensee industries**, and (ii) a package to lower technical and financial entry barriers for **MSMEs and deep-tech start-ups** — direct funding, incubation support and dedicated access to DRDO testing facilities.

**Why it matters.** Source-code sharing is the substantive news. Modern platforms are **software-defined**; without access to source code, a licensee producing a DRDO-designed system cannot maintain, upgrade or manage **obsolescence** of its software over the platform's life, and the state remains locked into the original developer. A standardised sharing framework is therefore a structural step in the ToT (transfer of technology) regime, not just a goodwill gesture — and it is the kind of specific, citable reform an Aatmanirbharta answer needs.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Nine (09) Licensing Agreements for Transfer of Technology (LAToTs)** handed over to **13 manufacturing partners** for commercial production.
- **Strategic MoUs** exchanged with the **Society of Indian Defence Manufacturers (SIDM)** and **Laghu Udyog Bharati (LUB)**.
- DRDO signed a contract with the **Quality Council of India** for **SAMAR (System for Advance Manufacturing Assessment and Rating) version 2.0**, to benchmark the manufacturing maturity of domestic defence enterprises.
- Reforms recalled in the address: **Positive Indigenisation Lists**, **iDEX**, **ADITI**, the **Technology Development Fund**, and the allocation of **25% of the defence R&D budget to the private sector**.
- Priority future technologies named: **AI, quantum computing, hypersonics and directed energy**.
- Stated 2047 goals: Aatmanirbharta in critical technologies, higher indigenous content, multi-fold growth in defence exports.

**Prelims hooks.** **iDEX** = Innovations for Defence Excellence (Defence Innovation Organisation). **ADITI** = Acing Development of Innovative Technologies with iDEX. **SAMAR** is run with the **Quality Council of India**. **LAToT** is the licensing instrument through which DRDO transfers technology to industry.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Indigenisation, Defence R&D); GS3 — Internal Security (Defence industrial base).

## 3. Ministry of Rural Development: **DoRD–PFRDA MoU creates 'Pension Sakhis' — SHG cadres to carry retirement security to rural households**

**What it is.** The **Department of Rural Development** and the **Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority (PFRDA)** signed an MoU (signed 14 September, announced 15 September 2026) to build a community-based outreach cadre of **'Pension Sakhis'** across rural India. The cadre will be drawn from the existing **DAY-NRLM** community architecture, including **Banking Correspondent (BC) Sakhis** and trained community resource persons.

**Why it matters.** This is the clean example of a recurring GS2/GS3 theme: **financial inclusion has moved from access to adequacy**. Rural households now largely have bank accounts, but account ownership does not translate into retirement planning — especially for households on irregular agricultural and informal incomes. The design choice is also worth noting: rather than building a new delivery channel, the government is **re-tasking an existing women-led SHG cadre** from banking facilitation to long-term financial security, at near-zero marginal institutional cost.

**Key facts and figures**

- Signed by **Shri Amit Shukla** (Joint Secretary, DoRD) and **Ms. Sumeet Kaur Kapoor** (Executive Director, PFRDA), in the presence of **Shri Rohit Kansal**, Secretary, DoRD.
- Platform: **Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana – National Rural Livelihoods Mission (DAY-NRLM)**.
- Target groups: SHG members, rural women, **Lakhpati Didis** and other rural participants.
- Functions of Pension Sakhis: pension awareness and financial literacy, **facilitating enrolment**, and post-enrolment support.
- Pension product in focus: the **National Pension System (NPS)**.
- Monitoring through digital dashboards and mobile/web solutions, including the Department's own **LoKoS** system.
- Rationale cited: rising life expectancy and changing demographic patterns.

**Prelims hooks.** **PFRDA** is a statutory body under the **PFRDA Act, 2013**, regulating NPS and APY. **DAY-NRLM** is under the **Ministry of Rural Development**; **BC Sakhi** and **Lakhpati Didi** are both NRLM-linked. LoKoS is DoRD's own SHG bookkeeping/monitoring system.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance (Welfare schemes, social security); GS3 — Economy (Financial inclusion, Inclusive growth).

## 4. Ministry of Science & Technology (DBT / BRIC-NIPGR): **SPROUT facility inaugurated — season-independent phenotyping for climate-resilient crops; SpeedSeed cuts the chickpea generation cycle to ~40 days**

**What it is.** The Union MoS (I/C) for Science & Technology inaugurated **SPROUT — "SpeedSeed Phenotyping and Resource Optimization for Unified Trait Analysis"** — at **BRIC-National Institute of Plant Genome Research (BRIC-NIPGR)**, New Delhi. It is a **season-independent, controlled-environment facility** for high-throughput phenotyping, precision stress screening and evaluation of germplasm, breeding populations, mutants, transgenic and genome-edited lines, with a **major focus on chickpea**.

**Why it matters.** The bottleneck in modern crop improvement is no longer gene discovery — genomics and genome editing have made that fast — but **phenotyping**: actually measuring how a candidate line performs under drought, heat or salinity, which normally waits on the growing season. A controlled-environment facility decouples that step from the calendar. Paired with **SpeedSeed**, which compresses generation advancement, it shortens the whole path from **gene discovery → trait validation → released variety**.

**Key facts and figures**

- **SpeedSeed**: optimised protocols can reduce the **chickpea generation cycle to approximately 40 days**; usable inside SPROUT or adapted to other controlled-environment facilities.
- Positioned under the **BioE3 policy** (Biotechnology for Economy, Environment and Employment), which lists **climate-resilient agriculture** among its strategic thematic sectors.
- **DBT track record, 2014–2026:** more than **300 projects** supported in agriculture biotechnology and allied areas; more than **50 improved varieties** across rice, wheat, chickpea, mustard, vegetables and forestry species with tolerance to drought, floods, salinity, pests and diseases.
- **17 climate-resilient rice varieties** developed under a flagship genomics-assisted breeding programme, now cultivated across nearly **15 lakh hectares**.
- A sapling was planted under the **"Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam"** campaign.

**Prelims hooks.** **BRIC** = Biotechnology Research and Innovation Council, the DBT umbrella body under which NIPGR now sits. **Phenotyping** = measuring observable traits; **genotyping** = reading the genetic makeup. **BioE3** expands to Biotechnology for **E**conomy, **E**nvironment and **E**mployment. NIPGR is located in New Delhi.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Biotechnology); GS3 — Agriculture (Crop improvement, climate-resilient agriculture).

## 5. Ministry of Commerce & Industry: **India and MERCOSUR sign the First Additional Protocol to their PTA — electronic Certificates of Origin get legal parity with paper**

**What it is.** On **14 September 2026**, India and the MERCOSUR bloc signed the **First Additional Protocol** to the **India–MERCOSUR Preferential Trade Agreement**. It amends **Article 16 of Annex III** (Rules of Origin) to provide that **Certificates of Origin issued in electronic format shall have the same legal validity and identical value as those issued on paper** — provided they are issued and electronically signed under each Party's domestic law by duly authorised entities and officials.

**Why it matters.** Certificates of Origin are the documentary gatekeepers of preferential tariffs: without a valid CoO, a consignment pays MFN duty regardless of the trade agreement. Moving them to electronic form cuts issuance and verification time and transaction costs, and reduces the scope for document fraud. It is also a concrete instance of **trade facilitation / paperless trade** — a theme running through the WTO TFA and India's own trade-digitalisation push — and, incidentally, the first time this 2004 agreement has been amended at all.

**Key facts and figures**

- Signed by **Commerce Secretary Shri Rajesh Agarwal** with the **Ambassador of Uruguay to India** and the **Ambassador of Paraguay to India**, in the presence of Uruguay's Foreign Minister.
- **India–MERCOSUR PTA:** signed **25 January 2004**; entered into force **1 June 2009**.
- Coverage: preferential tariff concessions on **450 tariff lines by India** and **452 tariff lines by MERCOSUR**.
- **MERCOSUR** (Southern Common Market): founded by the **Treaty of Asunción, 1991**; founding members **Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay**. Venezuela has been suspended since 2016; Bolivia has acceded as a full member.
- **Entry into force:** after India and the respective MERCOSUR member states complete domestic ratification and exchange legal notifications.

**Prelims hooks.** MERCOSUR is a **customs union** (common external tariff), not merely a free trade area. A **PTA** is the shallowest form of trade agreement — tariff concessions on a limited list of lines, not substantially all trade. Distinguish MERCOSUR from the **Andean Community** and the **Pacific Alliance**.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (Regional groupings, India–Latin America); GS3 — Economy (External sector, trade agreements).

## 6. Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment: **SHREYAS — ₹3,509.39 crore spent and 1.18 lakh SC/OBC beneficiaries across four components since 2014-15**

**What it is.** The Ministry released a consolidated performance statement for **SHREYAS (Scholarships for Higher Education for Young Achievers Scheme)**, the umbrella scheme for SC and OBC higher-education support. Cumulative expenditure from **2014-15 to 2025-26** across its four major components is about **₹3,509.39 crore**, with a cumulative beneficiary count of **1,18,248**.

**Why it matters.** SHREYAS is the standard example of **targeted educational affirmative action beyond reservation** — it works on the financing side rather than the seat-allocation side. The component-wise numbers also let you make a sharper point in a GS1/GS2 answer: the bulk of the money (₹2,199 crore of ₹3,509 crore) goes to **research fellowships**, while the **overseas scholarship** component is deep but very narrow (764 beneficiaries in twelve years), and Free Coaching has actually **shrunk** in the last two years.

### Scheme-in-focus: **SHREYAS (Scholarships for Higher Education for Young Achievers Scheme)**

- **Ministry:** Social Justice & Empowerment (Department of Social Justice & Empowerment).
- **Objective:** reduce financial barriers to higher education and professional opportunity for **Scheduled Caste and Other Backward Class** students.
- **Four components:**

| Component | Coverage | Expenditure 2014-15 to 2025-26 | Beneficiaries |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Free Coaching** | SC & OBC, coaching for competitive/professional exams | ₹145.10 crore | 23,208 |
| **Top Class Education** | SC students in premier institutions | ₹703.44 crore | 35,237 |
| **National Overseas Scholarship (NOS)** | SC students studying abroad | ₹461.57 crore | 764 |
| **National Fellowship for SC Students (NFSC)** | SC scholars in M.Phil/PhD research | ₹2,199.28 crore | 59,039 |
| **Total** | — | **₹3,509.39 crore** | **1,18,248** |

- **Trend points:** Top Class Education expenditure rose from ₹19.37 crore / 1,568 beneficiaries (2014-15) to **₹117.19 crore / 4,742 beneficiaries (2025-26)** — the highest in the period. NOS beneficiaries peaked at **114 in 2023-24**, then 80 (2024-25) and 67 (2025-26). Free Coaching fell from ₹17.68 crore / 2,136 students (2024-25) to **₹9.87 crore / 854 students (2025-26)**. NFSC spent **₹213.45 crore for 4,218 scholars** in 2025-26.
- **Significance:** the only one of the four components open to **both SC and OBC** students is Free Coaching; Top Class Education, NOS and NFSC are SC-specific.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance (Welfare schemes for vulnerable sections; mechanisms for protection of SCs/OBCs); GS1 — Society (Social empowerment).

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statement 3 is incorrect and is the trap: in August 2026 the **rural UR was 4.1%**, *lower* than the **urban UR of 6.8%**. Statements 1 and 2 are correct — PLFS is conducted by the NSO under MoSPI, and the monthly bulletins give all-India estimates of LFPR, WPR and UR on the Current Weekly Status basis.

**Q2 — (c) All three.** MERCOSUR was created by the **Treaty of Asunción (1991)** with Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay and Uruguay as founding members. The India–MERCOSUR PTA was signed on 25 January 2004 and **entered into force on 1 June 2009**. The First Additional Protocol, signed on 14 September 2026, amends **Article 16 of Annex III (Rules of Origin)** to give electronic Certificates of Origin legal parity with paper ones.

**Q3 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** Statement 1 is incorrect: SHREYAS is implemented by the **Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment**, not the Ministry of Education. Statement 2 is correct — the National Overseas Scholarship supports SC students studying abroad (764 beneficiaries, 2014-15 to 2025-26). Statement 3 is correct — the **NFSC accounted for ₹2,199.28 crore** of the scheme's ₹3,509.39 crore total, the largest of the four components.
