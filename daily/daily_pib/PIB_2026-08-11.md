## 1. Lok Sabha passes the **Kerala (Alteration of Name) Bill, 2026** — Kerala to become "Keralam"

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Home Affairs / Lok Sabha Secretariat

**What it is:** The Bill, introduced in the Lok Sabha on 10 August 2026 by Union Home Minister Amit Shah, was passed by **voice vote on 11 August 2026** amid Opposition sloganeering. It changes the official name of the State from **Kerala** to **Keralam** in the **First Schedule** (and consequential entries in the Fourth Schedule) of the Constitution.

**Why it matters:** This is the cleanest available live illustration of **Article 3** — the "name change" limb that Prelims repeatedly tests. Renaming a State is *not* a constitutional amendment under Article 368; it is done by ordinary law passed by simple majority under Article 3 read with Article 4.

**Key facts / figures**

- **Constitutional route:** Article 3 empowers Parliament to form new States and to alter the areas, boundaries or **names** of existing States.
- **Two mandatory conditions (proviso to Article 3):** (i) the Bill can be introduced **only on the recommendation of the President**, and (ii) the President must first **refer the Bill to the concerned State Legislature** for its views within a specified period.
- **The State Legislature's views are recommendatory, not binding** on Parliament.
- **Article 4:** consequential changes to the First and Fourth Schedules are **not** deemed amendments under Article 368.
- **Timeline:** Kerala Legislative Assembly passed a unanimous resolution on **24 June 2024** seeking the change → Union Cabinet approved on **24 February 2026** → introduced in Lok Sabha **10 August 2026** → passed by Lok Sabha **11 August 2026**.
- "Keralam" is the name of the State in Malayalam; the demand is framed as aligning the official English name with the linguistic/native usage.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Union & its Territory, Articles 1–4; Parliamentary procedure)

## 2. **NABL launches India's first Accreditation Scheme for Mobile Food Testing Laboratories**

**Issuing body:** National Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration Laboratories (**NABL**) — a constituent board of the **Quality Council of India (QCI)**

**What it is:** On **11 August 2026**, at the **"Gunvatta Samanvaya — A Multilateral MoU Exchange Ceremony"** in New Delhi, NABL launched India's **first-ever accreditation scheme for mobile food testing laboratories**, under its **Integrated Assessment Programme**. Until now, accreditation frameworks assumed a fixed-premises laboratory; mobile vans had no benchmarked quality assurance route.

**Why it matters:** Food-safety surveillance in India increasingly relies on mobile units ("Food Safety on Wheels" type vans) that reach rural and remote areas where no NABL-accredited fixed lab exists. Without accreditation, results from such vans have weak evidentiary and regulatory standing. This scheme converts them into **quality-assured, internationally benchmarked** testing points, enabling faster regulatory action.

**Key facts / figures**

- **NABL** is the national accreditation body for testing and calibration laboratories, functioning as a **constituent board of QCI** (QCI itself is a non-profit set up in 1997 jointly by the Government of India and Indian industry associations — FICCI, CII, ASSOCHAM).
- Accreditation of testing laboratories is benchmarked to the international standard **ISO/IEC 17025**.
- Stated objectives: reliable and quality-assured testing **closer to communities**, stronger food-safety surveillance, higher consumer confidence, **faster regulatory intervention**, and reduced duplication among regulators.
- Launched under NABL's **Integrated Assessment Programme**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (regulatory bodies, food safety); GS3 — Science & Technology (standards, quality infrastructure)

## 3. Agriculture Ministry launches the **National Scholarship Portal for agricultural students** — ₹21.35 crore released via DBT

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare / **ICAR**

**What it is:** Union Agriculture and Rural Development Minister **Shivraj Singh Chouhan** launched the **National Scholarship Portal** for students of agricultural education on **11 August 2026** at **ICAR-IARI, Pusa, New Delhi**, and released scholarship and fellowship funds through it in the same event.

**Why it matters:** ICAR scholarships and fellowships (National Talent Scholarship, Junior/Senior Research Fellowships, Post-Matric assistance) have historically been routed through institutions, causing delays. Shifting to a portal + **monthly DBT** model is a governance-delivery change of the kind UPSC likes to pair with DBT/JAM questions.

**Key facts / figures**

- **₹21.35 crore** released directly through **Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT)** to **more than 6,000 students** at launch.
- Agriculture students will now receive scholarship amounts **every month through DBT**, removing institution-level delays.
- Launch venue: **ICAR–Indian Agricultural Research Institute (IARI), Pusa** — India's premier agricultural research institute (the "Pusa Institute").
- Framing: making agricultural education "stronger, more practical and better connected with opportunities" — farms, agri-startups and cooperatives.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (agricultural education & research); GS2 — Governance (DBT, e-governance in welfare delivery)

## 4. **MoSPI signs two MoUs — with IDEAS and ISI Kolkata — including a tool for GDP "Nowcasting"**

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation (**MoSPI**)

**What it is:** MoSPI signed two MoUs for research studies with the **Institute of Data Engineering, Analytics and Science (IDEAS) Foundation** and the **Indian Statistical Institute (ISI), Kolkata**. MoSPI provides financial assistance and data support; the institutes deliver research outputs.

**Why it matters:** India's GDP estimates are released with a lag (advance/provisional/revised estimates). **Nowcasting** uses high-frequency indicators to estimate the *current* quarter's GDP before official numbers are out — a live example of the statistical-system reforms MoSPI has been pushing (alongside PLFS monthly bulletins and the revised base-year exercise).

**Key facts / figures**

- **Deliverable 1:** IDEAS to develop a **desktop-based software tool for GDP Nowcasting**.
- **Deliverable 2:** A **harmonised indicator framework** linking administrative data across government **health, nutrition and well-being** schemes.
- **IDEAS** — Institute of Data Engineering, Analytics and Science Foundation — is the **Technology Innovation Hub (TIH) hosted at ISI Kolkata** under the DST's National Mission on Interdisciplinary Cyber-Physical Systems (NM-ICPS).
- MoSPI is the nodal ministry for the National Statistical Office (NSO), NSS surveys, CPI/IIP and National Accounts.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (national income accounting, statistical system); GS2 — Governance (evidence-based policy, administrative data)

## 5. **Independence Day 2026 to be themed on "150 Years of Vande Mataram" and "Yuva Shakti for Viksit Bharat@2047"**

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Defence (nodal ministry for the Red Fort ceremony) / PIB

**What it is:** The Government announced the format of the **80th Independence Day** celebrations at the Red Fort on 15 August 2026. **For the first time, "Vande Mataram" will be sung from the ramparts of the Red Fort**, at the moment the Prime Minister arrives, before the flag-unfurling and the address to the nation.

**Why it matters:** 2026 marks **150 years since Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay composed "Vande Mataram" (1875)**, and the anniversary has been the organising theme of Republic Day 2026 as well. Expect static questions on the national song's history and its constitutional status relative to the national anthem.

**Key facts / figures**

- Twin themes: **"150 Years of Vande Mataram"** and **"Yuva Shakti for Viksit Bharat@2047"**.
- Around **2,500 NCC cadets and MY Bharat volunteers** to form a **floral depiction of "Vande Mataram"** on Gyanpath, opposite the Red Fort ramparts.
- An **IAF Mi-17 helicopter** to carry a commemorative banner for 150 years of Vande Mataram and shower flower petals.
- Approximately **5,000 Special Guests** invited.
- **Static anchors:** Vande Mataram was composed by **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay in 1875** and later included in his 1882 novel **Anandamath**; it was **first sung at the 1896 Calcutta session of the Indian National Congress** (by Rabindranath Tagore). On **24 January 1950**, the Constituent Assembly (President Dr. Rajendra Prasad) declared that Vande Mataram **"shall be honoured equally with Jana Gana Mana and shall have equal status with it"** — but only *Jana Gana Mana* is the National Anthem; Vande Mataram is the **National Song**, and unlike the anthem it has **no statutory protection** comparable to the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Modern Indian History & Culture (national symbols, freedom movement); GS2 — Polity (national symbols and their status)

## 6. **DHR-ICMR's S.H.I.N.E. 2026 connects school students to health research across 83 locations**

**Issuing body:** Department of Health Research (DHR) — **Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR)**, Ministry of Health & Family Welfare

The 2026 edition was held on **11 August 2026 across 83 locations**, comprising ICMR institutes and DHR's **Model Rural Health Research Units (MRHRUs)**.

**Why it matters:** It operationalises the Prime Minister's "Spend One Day as a Scientist" call and is a rare, exam-friendly example of a **research-institution-led scientific temper initiative** (Article 51A(h)) rather than a school-curriculum one.

**Key facts / figures**

- Eligibility: **Classes 9–12**, from Government, Government-aided and private schools; **no entry fee**.
- Activities: guided laboratory tours, research exhibitions, poster walks, "Storytelling Through Science" sessions on ICMR's work (COVID-19 testing, Covaxin, drone-based health delivery, TB research), and live demonstrations.
- Hosted at **ICMR institutes** and **DHR's Model Rural Health Research Units (MRHRUs)** — MRHRUs are DHR-supported research units attached to district/sub-district hospitals in rural areas.
- 2026 cycle: nominations/submissions close **11 September 2026**; results **17 November 2026**.
- ICMR, headquartered in New Delhi, is India's apex body for formulation, coordination and promotion of biomedical research, funded through the **Department of Health Research**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (scientific temper, research ecosystem); GS2 — Governance (health research institutions)

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the constitutional procedure for altering the name of a State in India, which one of the following statements is correct?

(a) A Bill for the purpose can be introduced in Parliament only on the recommendation of the President, after the President has referred it to the concerned State Legislature for its views
(b) The views expressed by the concerned State Legislature are binding on Parliament
(c) Such a Bill must be ratified by the Legislatures of not less than one-half of the States before it is presented to the President for assent
(d) Alteration of the name of a State requires a constitutional amendment passed by a special majority under Article 368

---

**Q2.** Consider the following statements with reference to the accreditation scheme for mobile food testing laboratories launched in August 2026:

1. It was launched by the National Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration Laboratories, which is a constituent board of the Quality Council of India.
2. It is India's first accreditation scheme specifically for mobile food testing laboratories.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 2 only
(c) Both 1 and 2
(d) Neither 1 nor 2

---

**Q3.** With reference to "Vande Mataram", consider the following statements:

1. It was composed by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay and later included in his novel *Anandamath*.
2. It was first sung at the 1896 session of the Indian National Congress.
3. The Constituent Assembly resolved that it shall be honoured equally with, and have equal status as, "Jana Gana Mana".

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) All three
(d) None

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a).** The proviso to **Article 3** requires two things before a Bill altering a State's name is introduced: the **President's recommendation**, and a **prior reference to the State Legislature** for its views within a specified period. Those views are **not binding** (so (b) is wrong). Ratification by half the States (Article 368 proviso) is not required (so (c) is wrong), and consequential changes to the First and Fourth Schedules are expressly **not** deemed amendments under Article 368 by virtue of **Article 4** (so (d) is wrong).

**Q2 — (c).** Both are correct. **NABL** is a **constituent board of the Quality Council of India (QCI)** and is the national accreditation body for testing and calibration laboratories (benchmarked to ISO/IEC 17025); the scheme launched on 11 August 2026 under NABL's Integrated Assessment Programme is India's **first** accreditation scheme for **mobile** food testing laboratories.

**Q3 — (c).** All three are correct. Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay composed it in **1875** and included it in **Anandamath (1882)**; it was **first sung at the 1896 Calcutta session** of the INC by Rabindranath Tagore; and on **24 January 1950** the Constituent Assembly declared that it **"shall be honoured equally with Jana Gana Mana and shall have equal status with it"**. Note the distinction: *Jana Gana Mana* is the **National Anthem**, Vande Mataram is the **National Song**.
