## 1. PIB Backgrounder (Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas): **Incentive Scheme for Promotion of Domestic PNG Connections**

The PIB Research Unit today issued a backgrounder on the Incentive Scheme for Promotion of Domestic Piped Natural Gas (PNG) Connections, approved in August 2026 and operational from **1 September 2026**. The problem it addresses is specific: City Gas Distribution (CGD) entities lay networks but a large share of connections stay un-activated because the capital cost per household takes roughly a decade to recover. The scheme rewires that economics by linking cheap domestic gas allocation to *billed, active* connections rather than pipes laid.

**Key facts**

- Around **1.74 crore** domestic PNG connections existed nationally as of August 2026.
- Eligible CGD entities get an **additional 200 SCM of domestically produced APM (Administered Price Mechanism) gas** for **every incremental billed domestic PNG connection** above a threshold prescribed for their Geographical Area (GA).
- Expected effect: payback period on the per-household capex falls from about **10 years to nearly 3 years**.
- To be implemented in **two tranches over six months**.
- Administered by MoPNG; CGD networks are authorised and regulated by the **PNGRB** (Petroleum and Natural Gas Regulatory Board, under the PNGRB Act, 2006).

**Why it matters:** PNG is central to raising natural gas to **15% of India's primary energy mix by 2030** (from roughly 6–7%). It is also a clean-cooking and urban air-quality lever, and an incentive design worth noting — output-linked (active connections) rather than input-linked (km of pipeline).

### Scheme-in-focus: **Incentive Scheme for Promotion of Domestic PNG Connections**

- **Ministry:** Petroleum & Natural Gas (implemented through CGD entities authorised by PNGRB)
- **Objective:** Raise the number of *active* domestic PNG connections by making household connections commercially viable for CGD companies.
- **Key features:** (i) 200 SCM of extra APM gas per incremental billed connection above a GA-specific threshold; (ii) benefit tied to billing, not to network laid; (iii) rollout in two tranches over six months; (iv) effective 1 September 2026.
- **Beneficiaries:** Urban and peri-urban households in authorised CGD geographical areas; indirectly, CGD operators.
- **Significance:** Shortens capex payback from ~10 years to ~3 years; supports the 15%-gas-in-energy-mix target, clean cooking and reduced LPG subsidy dependence.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure/Energy) and Environment (clean cooking, air quality).

## 2. Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change: **India to host the World Circular Economy Forum 2026 at Gandhinagar, 15–18 September**

India will host the **World Circular Economy Forum (WCEF) 2026** for the first time — and the first time the forum comes to **South Asia** — at Gandhinagar, Gujarat, on the theme **"Circular Economy: Transition for People and Prosperity."** WCEF is the flagship global circular-economy convening initiated by **Sitra, the Finnish Innovation Fund**; the 2025 edition was held in São Paulo, Brazil. India had declared its candidacy at the 12th Regional 3R and Circular Economy Forum in Jaipur.

**Key facts**

- **Dates/venue:** 15–18 September 2026, Gandhinagar, Gujarat.
- **Co-organisers:** MoEFCC, **Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB)**, Sitra (Finland), Government of Gujarat and the **Gujarat Pollution Control Board**.
- **Scale:** over **2,000 stakeholders from 65+ countries**; an exhibition with **125+ national and international exhibitors** showcasing start-up and investment-ready circular solutions.
- To be inaugurated on 15 September by Union Environment Minister **Bhupender Yadav** with the Gujarat Chief Minister.
- India's circular-economy potential has been officially pegged at a market value of **over $2 trillion and close to 10 million jobs by 2050**.

**Why it matters:** Links directly to Extended Producer Responsibility rules (plastic, e-waste, battery, tyre, used oil), **Mission LiFE**, the Resource Efficiency and Circular Economy agenda, and India's positioning as a Global South convener on sustainability.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (waste management, resource efficiency); GS2 — IR (India as Global South convener).

## 3. Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare (ICAR / ICRISAT): **Dryland Congress 2026 concludes with the "Delhi Declaration on Drylands" (3D)**

The three-day Dryland Congress 2026 in New Delhi closed with the adoption of the **Delhi Declaration on Drylands (3D)**, endorsed at the closing plenary by participating governments, institutions and partners. The Declaration frames a shared Global South agenda for transforming dryland agriculture through **South–South and triangular cooperation**, knowledge exchange and innovation.

**Key facts**

- **Dr M.L. Jat**, Secretary DARE and Director General ICAR, called for **ICAR-NIAP (National Institute of Agricultural Economics and Policy Research)** to be mandated as the institutional lead for **foresight work on dryland agriculture**.
- New agreements/MoUs announced with the **Global Crop Diversity Trust**; **ICRISAT** exchanged MoUs with Avior Life Sciences, **NIFTEM**, Wipro Ltd, and the **Angul and Rayagada district administrations of Odisha**.
- Same day, **ICAR-CAZRI (Central Arid Zone Research Institute, Jodhpur)** reported that its improved **moth bean** (*Vigna aconitifolia*) varieties showed strong resilience through an **El Niño year** — a practical demonstration of drought-hardy arid-zone crops.

**Why it matters:** Drylands cover a large share of India's cultivated area and are the front line of desertification and land degradation (link: **UNCCD**, India's land-degradation-neutrality commitment, and the Bonn Challenge pledge). "Foresight" institutionalisation and district-level MoUs are the operational novelty here.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (dryland/rainfed farming, crop diversification) and Environment (desertification); GS2 — IR (South–South cooperation).

## 4. Ministry of Ayush: **BRICS New Delhi Declaration backs an Expert Working Group on Traditional Medicine**

The **New Delhi Declaration** adopted at the **18th BRICS Summit** welcomes the proposed establishment of a **BRICS Expert Working Group on Traditional, Complementary and Integrative Medicine (TCIM)** under the BRICS Health Track — a first for the grouping, and an Indian Chairship deliverable. *(The Summit itself was covered on 11 September; what is new is this specific health-track outcome.)*

**Key facts**

- The Working Group is envisaged as a standing platform for dialogue, knowledge-sharing, research collaboration and **evidence-based approaches** to traditional medicine among BRICS members.
- The Declaration also calls for deeper cooperation on **research into medicinal plants and herbal preparations** for therapeutic and preventive use, and recognises the **shared traditional pharmacopoeias** of BRICS countries as a base for safe, effective and affordable therapeutic products.
- Groundwork: the **BRICS Meeting on TCIM held at Chandigarh on 21 July 2026** under India's Chairship agreed the **Terms of Reference** for the proposed group.

**Why it matters:** Slots alongside the **WHO Global Traditional Medicine Centre at Jamnagar**, the **WHO Traditional Medicine Global Summit** process, and India's push on Ayush exports and standardisation. Useful as a concrete example of India converting a plurilateral chairship into an institutional outcome.

**Notebook:** GS2 — IR (BRICS, plurilateral groupings) and Health/Governance; GS1 — Culture (traditional knowledge systems).

## 5. Ministry of Home Affairs (Department of Official Language): **Hindi Diwas 2026 and the 6th All India Official Language Conference, Mumbai, 14 September**

The Union Home Minister will address the inaugural session of **Hindi Diwas 2026** and the **6th All India Official Language Conference** in Mumbai on Monday, 14 September 2026, with the Maharashtra Chief Minister and Deputy Chief Ministers present.

**Key facts**

- **Hindi Diwas marks 14 September 1949**, when the Constituent Assembly adopted **Hindi in Devanagari script as the official language of the Union** — now **Article 343**.
- A special session is devoted to **Town Official Language Implementation Committees (TOLICs / NARAKAS)**, the grassroots machinery for official-language implementation.
- A special discussion session is centred on the **National Song 'Vande Mataram'**, with **Joydeep Chattopadhyay**, a fifth-generation descendant of **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay**, present.
- An exhibition with stalls by ministries, banks and institutions accompanies the conference.

**Static hooks worth revising:** Article 343 (official language of the Union); Article 344 (Official Language Commission and Parliamentary Committee); Articles 345–347 (State official languages); **Article 351** (directive to promote the spread of Hindi); the **Official Languages Act, 1963**; and the **Eighth Schedule**, which now lists **22 languages**. Vande Mataram was written by Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay, published in the novel *Anandamath* (1882) and first sung at the **1896 Calcutta session of the INC**; it has **equal status with Jana Gana Mana**, but no specific constitutional article prescribes it.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (constitutional provisions on official language, federal language politics); GS1 — Society/Culture.

## 6. Ministry of Railways: **Bairabi–Sairang line completes one year; August 2026 traffic and freight numbers released**

Two Railways releases today. The first marks **one year of the Bairabi–Sairang broad-gauge line in Mizoram**, inaugurated on 13 September 2025; the second reports August 2026 operating performance.

**Bairabi–Sairang, one year on**

- **51.38 km** broad-gauge line from **Bairabi (Kolasib district)** to **Sairang**, about 15 km from **Aizawl** — the only rail link connecting Mizoram's capital to the national network. Built at a cost of roughly **₹8,000 crore** across extremely difficult hill terrain.
- In one year: **1,100+ trips**, **over 15 lakh passengers**, and **2,500+ freight wagons** received at Sairang, of which about **70%** was cement, stone and sand.
- Three long-distance services connect Sairang with **Guwahati, Kolkata and Delhi**, including the **Sairang–Anand Vihar Rajdhani Express**.

**August 2026 performance**

- Freight loading **137.9 million tonnes**, up **5.4%** over 130.9 MT in August 2025; **freight revenue up 6.27%**.
- Commodity growth: **iron ore +12.1%**, clinker +10.4%, domestic container +9.2%, coal +6%, finished steel +4.9%, mineral oil +3.1%, fertilisers +1.6%, other goods +8.2%.
- Passengers carried: **67.27 crore** (vs 64.43 crore in August 2025); **suburban traffic +9.02%**.
- **14 Amrit Bharat services** regularised; **5 new Gati Shakti Cargo Terminals (GCTs)** commissioned.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (infrastructure, logistics, freight modal share); GS1 — Geography (North-East connectivity); GS2 — Governance (regional integration of the North-East).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the *Incentive Scheme for Promotion of Domestic PNG Connections*, consider the following statements:

1. Eligible City Gas Distribution entities receive an additional allocation of domestically produced APM gas for every incremental *billed* domestic PNG connection above a prescribed threshold.
2. The scheme is administered by the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy.
3. It is expected to reduce the payback period on per-household capital expenditure from about ten years to nearly three years.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Consider the following pairs of events held or to be held in India in 2026 and their associated outcomes/themes:

1. World Circular Economy Forum 2026 — "Circular Economy: Transition for People and Prosperity"
2. Dryland Congress 2026 — Delhi Declaration on Drylands
3. BRICS Summit 2026 — proposal for an Expert Working Group on Traditional, Complementary and Integrative Medicine

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) All three
(d) None

---

**Q3.** With reference to the constitutional and statutory position of the official language of the Union, consider the following statements:

1. Hindi Diwas commemorates the day in 1949 on which the Constituent Assembly adopted Hindi in the Devanagari script as the official language of the Union.
2. Article 351 of the Constitution directs the Union to promote the spread of the Hindi language.
3. The Eighth Schedule to the Constitution currently lists 22 languages.
4. 'Vande Mataram' has been accorded a status specifically superior to that of 'Jana Gana Mana' by a provision of the Constitution.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1, 2 and 3 only
(b) 1 and 4 only
(c) 2, 3 and 4 only
(d) 1, 2, 3 and 4

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** The incentive is 200 SCM of additional APM gas per incremental *billed* domestic connection above a Geographical Area threshold, and the payback period is expected to fall from about 10 years to nearly 3 years. Statement 2 is wrong: the scheme belongs to the **Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas**, with CGD entities regulated by the PNGRB.

**Q2 — (c) All three.** WCEF 2026 (Gandhinagar, 15–18 September) carries the theme "Circular Economy: Transition for People and Prosperity"; the Dryland Congress 2026 in New Delhi concluded with the Delhi Declaration on Drylands (3D); and the 18th BRICS Summit's New Delhi Declaration welcomed the proposed BRICS Expert Working Group on TCIM under the Health Track.

**Q3 — (a) 1, 2 and 3 only.** Hindi Diwas marks 14 September 1949 (Article 343); Article 351 is the directive on development and spread of Hindi; the Eighth Schedule lists 22 languages. Statement 4 is wrong — 'Vande Mataram' is the National Song and is held to have **equal status** with the National Anthem, and no constitutional provision grants it superior status.
