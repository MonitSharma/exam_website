## 1. India commissions its first-ever geothermal wells at Puga Valley, Ladakh

**Ministry/Body:** ONGC Energy Centre (under Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas), with UT Ladakh administration; announced by Union Minister Pralhad Joshi (New & Renewable Energy) | **Date:** commissioned 18–19 July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Energy (renewables, baseload clean power) · GS1 — Geography (Himalayan tectonics, geothermal provinces)

India's first and deepest twin geothermal wells were commissioned at **Puga Valley in eastern Ladakh**, at an altitude of over **14,000 feet**. The wells form the technical backbone for India's **first 1 MW demonstration-scale geothermal power plant** — the country's first serious step from geothermal *potential* to geothermal *generation*.

**Key facts**
- **Two wells, each ~1,000 m deep.** First reached target depth on 22 May 2026; the second was spudded on 3 June and completed on 8 July 2026.
- **Executing agency:** ONGC Energy Centre; drilling by SEROS Drilling Pvt Ltd, with Iceland's **ÍSOR** as geothermal consultants.
- **Temperature:** 135 °C recorded at just 400 m depth; deeper reservoir sections are expected to exceed 200 °C.
- **Why the heat is there:** Puga lies along the **Indus Suture Zone** — the Indian–Eurasian collision boundary. Deep faults let meteoric water (glacial melt) circulate down, get superheated, and rise as hot springs and sulphur fumaroles.

**Why it matters:** Geothermal is one of the very few renewables that gives **24×7 baseload power**, unlike intermittent solar and wind — a decisive advantage for a high-altitude border region where grid supply and diesel logistics are both fragile. GSI pegs India's geothermal potential at roughly **10 GW**; other known provinces include **Tattapani (Chhattisgarh), Manikaran (Himachal), Cambay (Gujarat) and Surajkund (Jharkhand)**.

## 2. Two big defence-diplomacy engagements begin on 20 July: Exercise Pitch Black 2026 and Operation Southern Readiness 26-2

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Defence (Indian Air Force; Indian Navy — Southern Naval Command) | **Date:** both commence 20 July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (Indo-Pacific, defence diplomacy) · GS3 — Internal Security (maritime security, capacity building)

**(a) Exercise Pitch Black 2026 — Australia.** An IAF contingent of **four Rafale fighters, two C-17 Globemaster III transports and over 120 air warriors** has arrived in northern Australia for the Royal Australian Air Force's premier multinational air combat exercise.
- **Biennial**, hosted by the RAAF; **first held at Darwin in 1983**. The name comes from its emphasis on **night-time flying** over large unpopulated areas.
- **Dates:** 20 July – 7 August 2026. **Venues:** RAAF Bases Darwin, Tindal (and Amberley), Northern Territory.
- Around **19–20 nations** participating — Australia, USA, Japan, India, Indonesia, Singapore, France, Germany, South Korea, Thailand, Philippines, New Zealand, Canada, Malaysia and others.
- **First time Indian Rafales** take part in Pitch Black.

**(b) Operation Southern Readiness 26-2 — Kochi.** The Indian Navy hosts a four-day multinational **maritime security training engagement** at Southern Naval Command, Kochi, **20–23 July 2026**.
- Conducted under **Combined Task Force 154 (CTF 154)** — the dedicated **Training Task Force** of the **Combined Maritime Forces (CMF)**, a multinational partnership of 40+ nations headquartered in Bahrain. India joined the CMF as a full member in 2023 and has been leading CTF 154 activity.
- Not a combat manoeuvre: it blends classroom instruction, simulator-based scenarios and at-sea deployment to build tactical skills across partner navies.

**Why it matters:** Together these mark India operating at both ends of the Indo-Pacific — as a *participant* in Western Pacific air-power interoperability and as a *host and trainer* in Indian Ocean maritime security. That "net security provider / preferred security partner" framing is standard GS2 material.

## 3. MoSPI releases the quarterly update of the PAIMANA infrastructure dashboard — framework expands to 165 indicators

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) | **Date:** July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (infrastructure, data-driven governance) · Reports & Indices

**PAIMANA** — *Project Assessment, Infrastructure Monitoring & Analytics for Nation-building* — is MoSPI's performance dashboard tracking key infrastructure sectors through standardised indicators. The latest quarterly release **adds 44 new indicators, taking the framework to 165 indicators**.

**Sector highlights from the update**
- **Civil aviation:** 98,920.10 tonnes of cargo carried in April 2026 (+115% YoY); number of aerodromes nearly doubled in a decade — from **81 (2014) to 165** at present.
- **Telecom:** wireless/mobile data consumption of **2,85,376 petabytes** in FY26 (+24.7% YoY); telephone subscribers at **133.06 crore** (+10.7% YoY); tele-density **93.26%**.
- **Roads:** **9,360 km** of National Highways constructed in FY26.
- **Railways:** average broad-gauge wagon carrying capacity at a record **64.0 tonnes per wagon** (2024–25).
- **Power:** India's energy-storage requirement projected to rise **more than ten-fold** — from **87 GWh in 2027–28 to 888 GWh by 2035–36**.

**Why it matters:** The storage projection is the exam-relevant number — it quantifies why round-the-clock renewables, pumped hydro and battery storage policy have moved to the centre of India's energy transition. PAIMANA also illustrates the shift from ministry-wise reporting to a single standardised statistical dashboard.

## 4. India's best-ever Chemistry Olympiad result — all four students win gold at the 58th IChO, Tashkent

**Ministry/Body:** Department of Atomic Energy (via HBCSE, Mumbai); supported by DAE, DST and Ministry of Education through the National Steering Committee on Science Olympiads | **Date:** PIB release 19 July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (education, talent pipelines) · GS3 — Science & Technology

All four Indian students won **gold** at the 58th International Chemistry Olympiad (IChO) 2026, held in **Tashkent, Uzbekistan, from 10–19 July 2026** — India's **first-ever all-gold haul** at the IChO.

**Key facts**
- Medallists: Debadatta Priyadarshi (Bhubaneswar), Harshit Singla (Mandi Gobindgarh), Kabeer Chillar (Delhi), Sandeep Kuchi (Hyderabad).
- **India placed first in the country-wise tally**, jointly with China, Vietnam and Individual Participants Group B.
- Largest IChO ever: **363 students from 93 countries**.
- This was India's **27th appearance**; across all appearances 32% of Indian students have won gold, 51% silver, 17% bronze — rising to 43% gold in the last ten appearances.
- Team trained by the **Homi Bhabha Centre for Science Education (HBCSE)**, Mumbai — a national centre of TIFR under the Department of Atomic Energy.

**Why it matters:** A clean illustration of a publicly-funded talent-identification pipeline (NSEC → INChO → OCSC → international team) delivering results. Prelims commonly tests *which body runs the Science Olympiad programme* — the answer is **HBCSE**, not NCERT or the Ministry of Education directly.

## 5. Health Ministry strengthens disease surveillance; launches a monthly NHM best-practices webinar series

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (MoHFW) | **Date:** national review meeting 16–17 July 2026; webinar series launched on World Population Day (11 July 2026) | **Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (health systems, cooperative federalism) · GS3 — Science & Technology (surveillance, data systems)

MoHFW concluded a **two-day National Review Meeting on Disease Surveillance and Public Health Programmes** in New Delhi, bringing together senior health leadership from **all 36 States and UTs** to review implementation of key public health programmes, assess surveillance progress, and strengthen data-driven governance.

Separately, the Ministry launched a **Monthly Webinar Series on Best Practices under the National Health Mission (NHM)** — a year-round platform where States and UTs present successful innovations and exchange implementation experience so that scalable, evidence-based practices spread faster. ## 1. Coal Ministry pushes the Rs 37,500 crore surface coal/lignite gasification scheme

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Coal | **Date:** 20 July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS3 — Economy/Energy Security

The Ministry of Coal held a pre-application conference for the **Rs 37,500 crore Scheme for Promotion of Surface Coal/Lignite Gasification Projects**, drawing public-sector enterprises, private firms, technology providers and prospective applicants. The linked parliamentary reply also restated the National Coal Gasification Mission's target of **100 million tonnes (MT) coal gasification capacity by 2030**.

**Key facts**
- Current operational/under-implementation capacity is about **22.6 MTPA**: Jindal Steel Ltd. about 8 MTPA, Talcher Fertilisers Ltd. about 2.6 MTPA, and eight projects under the earlier Rs 8,500 crore incentive scheme about 12 MTPA.
- The new scheme, approved on **13 May 2026**, is expected to utilise nearly **75 MTPA** of coal/lignite and catalyse investments of about **Rs 2.5-3 lakh crore**.
- Incentives are up to **20% of eligible plant and machinery cost**, against 15% capex support under the earlier Rs 8,500 crore scheme.
- Application deadline mentioned in the PIB release: **7 September 2026**.

**Scheme-in-focus: Scheme for Promotion of Surface Coal/Lignite Gasification Projects**
- **Ministry:** Ministry of Coal.
- **Objective:** Promote cleaner and value-added use of domestic coal/lignite and reduce dependence on imported energy and chemical products.
- **Key features:** financial incentive for eligible projects; competitive application/RFP process; downstream products include synthetic natural gas, ammonia, methanol, DRI, synthetic fuels and chemicals; links to the 100 MT by 2030 National Coal Gasification Mission target.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** public and private project developers; coal-bearing regions; downstream sectors using gasification products.
- **Significance:** Energy security, import substitution, coal-to-chemicals manufacturing and estimated employment generation of about **50,000**.

## 4. Power Ministry updates status of pumped storage projects

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Power / Central Electricity Authority | **Date:** 20 July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS3 — Energy / Environment

The Power Ministry told Rajya Sabha that **11 Pumped Storage Projects (PSPs)** with aggregate installed capacity of **15,870 MW** are under construction, while **six PSPs** with **8,140 MW** have been concurred/appraised by the Central Electricity Authority and are yet to be taken up for construction.

**Key facts**
- PSPs store surplus energy during off-peak periods and supply power during peak demand; they also provide frequency regulation, voltage support and black-start capability.
- CEA has revised DPR concurrence guidelines, reducing the timeline from **90 days to 50 days** for PSPs.
- The Government has exempted **off-stream closed-loop PSPs** from CEA concurrence, irrespective of capital expenditure quantum, while developers may still seek CEA technical guidance.
- CERC has provided **100% waiver of ISTS charges for 25 years** from commercial operation date for PSPs where construction work is awarded on or before **30 June 2028**.

**Why it matters:** PSPs are long-duration storage assets. They are central to India's renewable-energy integration problem because solar and wind expansion needs grid-scale storage and balancing services.

## 6. DoLR clarifies NAKSHA pilot in Goa; original land records remain protected

**Ministry/Body:** Department of Land Resources, Ministry of Rural Development | **Date:** 20 July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS2 — Governance / GS3 — Land Records & Digital Governance

The Department of Land Resources, with Goa's Directorate of Settlement and Land Records, issued a clarification on the **National Geospatial Knowledge-based Land Survey of Urban Habitations (NAKSHA)** pilot project in Goa. It said existing property titles, boundaries and official land records remain secure and unaffected by the mapping exercise.

**Key facts**
- NAKSHA is executed under the **Digital India Land Records Modernization Programme (DILRMP)**.
- It is a **GIS-based urban land parcel mapping** initiative for town planning, land administration and public utility layouts.
- The department clarified that NAKSHA is not a land acquisition exercise and is not designed to increase local property taxes.
- Non-resident landowners are protected; physical absence during a survey visit does not affect legal ownership rights.
- The formal inquiry process in Goa is scheduled from **16 July to 14 August 2026**.

**Why it matters:** Land records modernisation is a high-yield governance theme. This release is useful for explaining how digital mapping can improve urban administration while requiring safeguards for title security, due process and public trust.
