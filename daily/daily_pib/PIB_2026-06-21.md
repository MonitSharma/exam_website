## 1. 12th International Day of Yoga (IDY) 2026 — PM leads from Kolkata's Red Road; theme "Yoga for Healthy Ageing"

**Issuing body:** Ministry of AYUSH / Morarji Desai National Institute of Yoga (MDNIY); PM-led national event at Red Road, Kolkata — 21 June 2026
**Notebook:** GS1 (Society / Art & Culture — yoga as living heritage) · GS2 (IR — health diplomacy & soft power)

The 2026 International Day of Yoga was observed on **21 June** with the theme **"Yoga for Healthy Ageing"**, with the Prime Minister leading the **Common Yoga Protocol (CYP)** at the iconic **Red Road, Kolkata**. (June's earlier briefings had only flagged the *countdown*; today is the actual observance, with the year's theme and venue now confirmed.) Why it matters: IDY is a recurring prelims favourite (date, theme, nodal ministry, UN origin) and a flagship example of India's cultural soft power.

**Key facts/figures:**
- **Theme 2026:** *Yoga for Healthy Ageing* — positioning yoga as a lifelong practice for active, dignified ageing.
- IDY is observed on **21 June**, the **summer solstice** (longest day in the Northern Hemisphere); the UN proclaimed it in **2014** on India's initiative, first observed **2015**.
- **Global reach:** through the Indian Council for Cultural Relations (ICCR), **210+ Indian Missions** organised celebrations at nearly **2,500 locations worldwide**; the national CYP session began at **5:00 AM**.
- Yoga is inscribed on **UNESCO's Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity** (2016).

## 2. Indian Coast Guard inducts its first indigenous Air Cushion Vehicle (hovercraft H-561)

**Issuing body:** Indian Coast Guard / Ministry of Defence (induction at Goa — 18 June 2026)
**Notebook:** GS3 (Internal Security — coastal security; Defence indigenisation / Aatmanirbhar Bharat)

The Indian Coast Guard inducted **H-561**, the **first of six indigenous Air Cushion Vehicles (ACVs)** built by **Chowgule & Company Pvt. Ltd., Goa**. An ACV (hovercraft) rides on a cushion of air and can move over **water, mudflats, marshes, sandbanks and shallow coastal stretches** that conventional boats cannot reach — ideal for patrolling India's creeks and littoral zones. Why it matters: a clean Aatmanirbhar Bharat + coastal-security example (post-26/11 maritime domain awareness), and a likely prelims hit on indigenous platforms.

**Key facts/figures:**
- Order: **six ACVs** under a **₹387.44 crore** contract signed **24 October 2024**.
- Performance: cruising speed ~**52 knots**, can carry **42 personnel** plus an **8-tonne payload**, endurance up to **9 hours**.
- Roles: high-speed coastal patrol, surveillance/reconnaissance, interception of suspicious vessels, and search-and-rescue (SAR).
- H-561 is to be **based at Haldia, West Bengal**, strengthening surveillance in shallow eastern-coast waters.

## 3. ISRO–Dept. of Atomic Energy to build heating tech for lunar landers to survive 100–200 days

**Issuing body:** ISRO (Dept. of Space) in collaboration with the Department of Atomic Energy (DAE) — announced by ISRO Chairman at the CSIR-RISE Conclave, Bengaluru (mid-June 2026)
**Notebook:** GS3 (Science & Tech — space technology, lunar exploration)

ISRO and the DAE are jointly developing an **artificial heating system** to keep lunar landers alive through the bitterly cold **lunar night** — targeting an operational life of **100–200 days** versus the **14 days** of Chandrayaan-3's *Vikram* lander. The lunar day/night each last ~14 Earth days; temperatures swing from ~**+121 °C** by day to below **−130 °C** at night, when solar power vanishes and electronics/batteries fail. Why it matters: directly relevant to **Chandrayaan-4 (sample return)** and the **Bharatiya Antariksha Station / Gaganyaan** roadmap.

**Key facts/figures:**
- **DAE** contributes nuclear/thermal expertise (radioisotope-based heating concepts); **ISRO** the space-engineering integration.
- Aim: enable spacecraft to survive **multiple lunar day–night cycles**, allowing far longer experiments and more data.
- Builds on Chandrayaan-3 (Aug 2023, **Shiv Shakti Point**, lunar south pole) — Vikram + *Pragyan* operated only one lunar day.

## 4. Small Hydro Power (SHP) Development Scheme — implementation guidelines (FY 2026-27 to 2030-31) launched

**Issuing body:** Ministry of New & Renewable Energy (MNRE) — guidelines launched by Secretary, MNRE; SECI designated implementing agency (mid-June 2026)
**Notebook:** GS3 (Environment / Economy — clean energy, energy security, North-East & remote-area development)

MNRE has **operationalised** the **Small Hydro Power (SHP) Development Scheme** by issuing its detailed guidelines and designating the **Solar Energy Corporation of India (SECI)** as the **National Programme Implementing Agency (NPIA)**. (The scheme itself was approved by the Union Cabinet in March 2026; the *new development* is the guidelines + agency that make it operational.) Why it matters: small hydro is a clean, decentralised source well-suited to hilly, remote and border areas, and supports India's non-fossil capacity goals.

> **Scheme-in-focus — Small Hydro Power (SHP) Development Scheme**
> - **Ministry:** New & Renewable Energy (MNRE); **Centrally Sponsored Scheme** for **FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31**; outlay **₹2,584.60 crore**; implementing agency **SECI**.
> - **Objective:** add ~**1,500 MW** of new small-hydro capacity and revive the sector, especially in the Himalayan/North-East and difficult terrains.
> - **What counts as SHP:** projects up to **25 MW** capacity (small hydro = 1–25 MW in India).
> - **Key features:** (i) Central Financial Assistance of **₹3.6 crore/MW or 30%** of project cost (cap ₹30 cr/project) for **North-Eastern States & international-border districts**; (ii) **₹2.4 crore/MW or 20%** (cap ₹20 cr/project) for **other States**; (iii) expected to leverage ~**₹15,000 crore** of investment and create rural employment.
> - **Significance:** supports the **non-fossil-fuel capacity target** (India crossed 50% non-fossil installed capacity ahead of its 2030 NDC goal) and brings clean, firm renewable power to off-grid and remote regions.

## 5. India's Bimal N. Patel elected Judge of the International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea (ITLOS)

**Issuing body:** 36th Meeting of States Parties to UNCLOS, UN HQ New York (15–19 June 2026); welcomed by Ministry of External Affairs / EAM
**Notebook:** GS2 (International Relations — international law, maritime governance, UN bodies)

Indian jurist **Bimal N. Patel** was **elected a Judge of ITLOS** for the **2026–2035** term, securing **115 of 168 valid votes**. He will **assume office on 1 October 2026**, succeeding India's outgoing member **Neeru Chadha**. Why it matters: it keeps India represented on the principal global tribunal for the law of the sea at a time of rising maritime/UNCLOS disputes (South China Sea, deep-sea mining, maritime delimitation), and is a high-probability prelims item (ITLOS basics + the personality).

**Key facts/figures:**
- **ITLOS:** independent judicial body established under the **UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), 1982**; began functioning **1996**; **headquartered in Hamburg, Germany**; has **21 elected judges** (9-year terms).
- Patel is a member of the **UN International Law Commission (ILC)** and Vice-Chancellor, **Rashtriya Raksha University**; also on the **National Security Advisory Board**.
- ITLOS adjudicates disputes on interpretation/application of UNCLOS, including the **prompt release of vessels** and the **Area** (deep-seabed) matters.
