## 1. PM launches the Modified UDAN Scheme; inaugurates new Jodhpur Airport terminal

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Civil Aviation | **Date:** 4 July 2026, Jodhpur (Rajasthan) | **Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (infrastructure, regional connectivity)

The Prime Minister inaugurated Jodhpur Airport's new terminal building (₹480 crore, ~23,000 sq. m, 2-million-passenger annual capacity, built for a 5-Star GRIHA green-building rating) and launched a **Modified UDAN (Ude Desh ka Aam Naagrik) Scheme** with an outlay of **₹28,840 crore over the next 10 years**. It matters because UDAN (originally launched 2016) is India's flagship regional connectivity scheme, and this revamp signals a decadal, capital-intensive push into underserved/remote air connectivity ahead of Viksit Bharat@2047.

**Key facts:** target — development of **100 aerodromes and 200 helipads**; includes procurement of indigenously-made aircraft/helicopters (HAL Dhruv, Dornier platforms) for underserved regions; part of a wider ₹1.06 lakh-crore package of projects the PM dedicated/launched at Balotra the same day, plus the foundation stone for **Jaipur Metro Rail Phase 2** (over ₹13,000 crore).

## 2. CG Semi's OSAT facility at Sanand, Gujarat — commercial semiconductor chip packaging begins

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Electronics & Information Technology (India Semiconductor Mission) | **Date:** 4 July 2026, Sanand, Ahmedabad | **Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology / Economy (manufacturing, Make in India)

The PM inaugurated CG Semi's **OSAT (Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test)** facility — a **₹7,600 crore** joint venture between CG Power & Industrial Solutions, Japan's Renesas Electronics, and Thailand's Stars Microelectronics — marking commercial production at one of India's first major chip-packaging plants under the **India Semiconductor Mission (ISM)**. OSAT covers assembly, testing and packaging (not chip fabrication itself) for automotive, industrial, telecom/5G, IoT and power-sector chips. Distinct from earlier MeitY items in this notebook (BHASHINI, Digital India anniversary, Kisan Sarathi), this is about hardware manufacturing capacity, not software/digital services.

**Key facts:** pilot (G1) plant already running at ~0.5 million units/day; targeted peak annual capacity of **4.7 billion units within five years**; a second facility (G2) expected online later in 2026.

## 3. Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas — LPG supply disruption from the Strait of Hormuz crisis easing

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas | **Date:** update reported 2 July 2026 (Minister Hardeep Singh Puri) | **Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (energy security) · GS2 — International Relations (West Asia spillover)

India's LPG consumption fell **8% year-on-year in H1 2026** (14.74 MT vs 15.95 MT in H1 2025) as the West Asia conflict disrupted Strait of Hormuz shipments — India sources ~60% of its LPG needs from imports, of which ~90% normally transits the Strait. The Ministry says domestic LPG/PNG/CNG supply is now maintained at **100%**, commercial LPG supply has been restored to pre-crisis levels (19-kg commercial cylinder price cut by ₹173 from 1 July), and additional US cargoes have made the US India's largest LPG supplier between March–June. This is a clean GS3 energy-security case study: import dependency, chokepoint vulnerability (Hormuz), and diversification response.

**Key facts:** May 2026 LPG consumption hit a 5-year low (~2.13 MT); oil marketing companies' under-recovery losses reported at **₹74,781 crore** up to 30 June (₹2.1 lakh crore cumulative with prior year); current crude/product strategic reserves cover ~76–80 days; government flagged plans to expand strategic reserve capacity.

## 4. India prepares for the BRICS Women's Ministerial Meeting, Kochi (6–9 July)

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Women & Child Development, with Ministry of External Affairs (India's BRICS Chairship) | **Date:** Third Preparatory Meeting held 29 June 2026 (virtual); reported ahead of the Kochi meet | **Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (BRICS) · GS1 — Society (gender)

Ahead of hosting the **BRICS Women's Working Group Meeting (6–7 July)** and the **BRICS Women Ministerial Meeting (8–9 July)** in Kochi, Kerala — under the theme *"Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability"* — the WCD Secretary chaired the third preparatory meeting with BRICS member-country representatives, UN Women and FICCI. It matters as a live example of India's 2026 BRICS chairship agenda extending beyond economics into gender/social policy multilateralism.

**Key facts:** priority areas — women in governance & leadership; financial/digital inclusion; women's entrepreneurship and skill development; women's role in climate action, food security and nutrition; outputs include a Digital Repository of Best Practices and BRICS Women's Capacity-Building Guidelines.

## 5. National Workshop on "Tiger Re-introduction: Opportunities & Challenges" — Alwar, Rajasthan

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Environment, Forest & Climate Change / National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA) | **Date:** 28 June 2026, Alwar (Sariska) | **Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (wildlife conservation)

Union Minister Bhupender Yadav inaugurated a national workshop bringing together 12 Chief Wildlife Wardens and 18 Field Directors to deliberate on scientific strategies for **tiger reintroduction, supplementation and recovery** in tiger-deficient landscapes — relevant as India's tiger population and reserve network expand under Project Tiger. Case studies were presented from Sariska, Mukundara Hills, Panna, Veerangana Durgavati, Satkosia, Simlipal, Rajaji, Sahyadri and Navegaon-Nagzira Tiger Reserves, with a special session revisiting **Project Cheetah** — billed as the world's first intercontinental large-carnivore reintroduction.

**Key facts:** NTCA identified specific Tiger Reserves for focused recovery interventions; technical sessions covered prey augmentation, habitat restoration and landscape connectivity; field visit held to Sariska Tiger Reserve.

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** Consider the following statements regarding the "Modified UDAN Scheme" launched on 4 July 2026:
1. It has a total outlay of approximately ₹28,840 crore spread over the next 10 years.
2. It targets the development of 100 aerodromes and 200 helipads.
3. It is being implemented for the first time, replacing an earlier scheme that had no regional connectivity component.

How many of the above statements are correct?
(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) All three
(d) None

**Q2.** "OSAT," seen in the news in the context of CG Semi's facility at Sanand, Gujarat, refers to:
(a) Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test
(b) Open Standard Automated Trading
(c) Overseas Strategic Assets Treaty
(d) Optical Sensor Array Technology

**Q3.** With reference to the National Workshop on "Tiger Re-introduction: Opportunities & Challenges" held in June 2026, consider the following statements:
1. It was inaugurated at Alwar, Rajasthan, the district in which Sariska Tiger Reserve is located.
2. It was organised under the aegis of the National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA).
3. A special session at the workshop revisited Project Cheetah.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1. (b) Only two** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct (₹28,840 crore over 10 years; 100 aerodromes/200 helipads target). Statement 3 is incorrect: UDAN is not new — it was originally launched in 2016 as India's Regional Connectivity Scheme; 4 July 2026 saw a *modified/revamped* version, not a first-time launch.

**Q2. (a) Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test** — OSAT covers the assembly, testing and packaging stage of semiconductor manufacturing (distinct from wafer fabrication), which is what CG Semi's Sanand plant undertakes under the India Semiconductor Mission.

**Q3. (d) 1, 2 and 3** — All three statements are correct: the workshop was held at Alwar (Sariska Tiger Reserve's district), organised under NTCA, and included a special session on Project Cheetah.
