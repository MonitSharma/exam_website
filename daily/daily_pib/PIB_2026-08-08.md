## 1. MHA standardises 27 place and feature names in Arunachal Pradesh on Survey of India maps

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Home Affairs, in consultation with the Government of Arunachal Pradesh (release of 7 Aug 2026)

The Centre has formally identified **27 places and features in Arunachal Pradesh by standard place and feature names** on the official maps of the **Survey of India**. The stated purpose is "accurate recognition and better awareness among the public at large" — but the timing and content make this an unmistakable assertion of sovereign cartographic authority over a state China claims as "Zangnan/South Tibet" and for which Beijing has periodically issued its own lists of "standardised" names.

**Key facts:**

- **27 features** in all — categorised as **land areas, passes, a lake and a monument**.
- The four **passes**: **Dzo La, Riza La, Pukur La and Thag La**. Thag La ridge was a flashpoint of the 1962 war.
- Named land areas include **Longju** (site of the 1959 clash), **Maja**, **Bisa**, **Jairampur**, **Kamlang Nagar**, **Bara Kundun** and **Chhota Kundun**.
- One lake — **Sambho Sarovar**; one monument — **Sher-e-Thapa Memorial**. **Jaswant Garh** (after Rifleman Jaswant Singh Rawat, MVC, of 1962) is listed as a land area.
- Maps are viewable on the Survey of India state-maps portal.

**Why it matters:** Survey of India is India's **national mapping agency** (under the Department of Science & Technology) and the sole authority for official maps of India. Naming and cartography are instruments of effective administration under international practice, so this is a state-practice response to Chinese renaming exercises rather than a mere administrative housekeeping act.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India–China border) + Polity/Governance (Survey of India, cartographic sovereignty); GS1 map-based prelims prep.

## 2. India delivers the first Beam Catcher for FAIR — the international mega-science accelerator at Darmstadt

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Science & Technology (DST) with the Department of Atomic Energy; executed by CSIR-CMERI Durgapur and Bose Institute Kolkata (7 Aug 2026)

The **first of three Beam Catcher units** designed in India has reached the **Facility for Antiproton and Ion Research (FAIR)** site in **Darmstadt, Germany**, and awaits installation. The beam catcher is a core element of the **Super Fragment Separator (Super-FRS)**, the sub-system that produces FAIR's radioactive ion beams — it absorbs the remnants of the primary heavy-ion beam after it strikes the production target.

**Key facts:**

- India became a **founding member of FAIR** by signing the convention at **Wiesbaden, Germany, on 4 October 2010**.
- India's contribution is funded **50:50 by DST and DAE**. **Bose Institute, Kolkata** is the designated Indian shareholder in FAIR GmbH and the nodal implementing institution.
- The beam catcher was **designed by CSIR-CMERI, Durgapur** and manufactured by a Kanpur firm. It absorbs beams of **0.4–1.5 GeV/nucleon**; peak energy density in the graphite absorber can reach **~300 J/g per pulse**, causing thermal shock waves — the central design challenge.
- Absorbers are **fine-grained graphite and hyper-pure copper**, held at **~10⁻⁷ mbar** vacuum, water-cooled and iron-shielded. One unit weighs about **40 tonnes**.
- Other Indian in-kind supplies: **58 ultra-high vacuum chambers** (Bengaluru), **932 km of multi-core IT/diagnostic cables** (Chennai), **524 ultra-stable power converters** (ECIL, Hyderabad), plus cryogenic components.
- Indian scientists also build detectors for the **NuSTAR** (Nuclear Structure and Astrophysics) and **CBM** (Compressed Baryonic Matter) experiments.

**Why it matters:** A textbook case of **"mega-science diplomacy"** — India's participation in FAIR sits alongside **ITER, CERN, LIGO-India, TMT and SKA**, and the in-kind model deliberately upgrades Indian industry to international precision-engineering benchmarks.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (particle physics, international S&T collaboration, indigenisation).

## 3. GeM completes a decade — cumulative GMV crosses ₹20 lakh crore

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Commerce & Industry (7 Aug 2026); GeM completes ten years on **9 August 2026**

**Government e Marketplace (GeM)** has crossed **₹20 lakh crore in cumulative Gross Merchandise Value** across **more than 3.78 crore orders** in ten years of operation.

**Key facts:**

- Annual GMV rose from **₹422 crore in FY 2016-17** to **over ₹5 lakh crore in each of FY 2024-25 and FY 2025-26**. In the first four months of FY 2026-27 alone: **₹1,47,888 crore**.
- The **first ₹10 lakh crore took over eight years**; the **next ₹10 lakh crore took under two years**.
- Sellers with completed profiles: **3,339 → 25.45 lakh**. Primary buyer organisations: **1,707 → 1.37 lakh**. Registered **MSEs: 2,424 → 12.25 lakh**.
- **MSEs** have fulfilled orders worth **₹9.07 lakh crore — 45.6% of cumulative GMV**. **2.24 lakh women-led MSEs** delivered **50 lakh+ orders worth ₹99,147 crore**; **42,242 startups** won orders worth **₹65,633 crore**.
- An **IIT Delhi study** (FY 2023-24 to FY 2025-26) estimated monetised benefit of **₹86,571.69 crore** and **net social saving of ₹1,76,411.46 crore**. On 101 comparable items, GeM was cheaper for **74.3%**, with a **spend-weighted saving of 13.6%**.
- Ease-of-doing-business measures: transaction charge cut from **0.50% to 0.30%**; orders **up to ₹10 lakh exempt** (so **97% of orders pay no fee**); fee capped at **₹3 lakh**; vendor assessment fees cut by up to **92%**.
- GeM now uses **AI/ML to flag suspected cartelisation, collusion and order splitting**.

**Why it matters:** GeM is the standard example of **digital public infrastructure applied to public procurement** — transparency, MSE inclusion and fiscal savings in one instrument. The Economic Survey 2021-22 had earlier recorded procurement savings through the platform.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (public procurement, MSMEs); GS2 — Governance (e-governance, transparency).

## 4. CCRAS and BIS sign an MoU to build global quality standards for Ayurveda

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of AYUSH — Central Council for Research in Ayurvedic Sciences (CCRAS) with the Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS), 7 Aug 2026

**CCRAS**, the apex Ayurveda research body under the Ministry of AYUSH, signed an **MoU with BIS**, India's National Standards Body, to jointly develop standards, strengthen laboratory and conformity assessment mechanisms, scientifically validate traditional knowledge and contribute to international standardisation.

**Key facts:**

- This is **BIS's 100th MoU** and its **first with an AYUSH organisation**.
- **128 Indian Standards** are currently under the **Ayurveda Sectional Committee** of BIS; **243 standards across AYUSH domains** have been developed in the last three years.
- CCRAS institutes and laboratories are already accredited under **NABL and ISO** standards; **30 peripheral CCRAS institutes and centres** participated virtually.
- Signed by **Prof. (Vaidya) Rabinarayan Acharya** (DG, CCRAS) and **Shri Sanjay Pant** (DDG, Standardisation-II, BIS), in the presence of **Vaidya Rajesh Kotecha**, Secretary, Ministry of AYUSH.

**Why it matters:** The binding constraint on Ayurveda's export and global acceptance has been **absence of internationally benchmarked quality and conformity standards**, not absence of demand. This links traditional-knowledge validation to the formal standards architecture — relevant to WHO's traditional medicine work and to India's IPR/traditional knowledge (TKDL) narrative.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance/Health (regulation, standard-setting bodies); GS3 — Science & Technology (traditional knowledge, indigenisation).

## 5. India–Bhutan hold a high-level bilateral meeting on trans-border rivers

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Jal Shakti (7 Aug 2026, New Delhi)

Union Jal Shakti Minister **Shri C R Paatil** led the Indian side; Bhutan was led by **Lyonpo Gem Tshering, Minister for Energy & Natural Resources**.

**Key facts:**

- Focus areas: **trans-border river management, flood forecasting and flood management, and hydrological data sharing**.
- The two sides reviewed progress on the **Punatsangchhu-I Hydroelectric Project**, implemented in Bhutan in partnership with India, and discussed measures for its expeditious implementation.
- Discussions covered **upgrading and strengthening Bhutan's hydro-meteorological network** and **expanding the scope of the existing hydrological data-sharing mechanism**.

**Why it matters:** Rivers rising in Bhutan (the Manas, Sankosh, Wangchhu/Raidak and Torsa systems) feed the Brahmaputra basin, and Bhutan's real-time hydro-met data is central to **flood forecasting in Assam and West Bengal**. Note the contrast with India's northern neighbour: with Bhutan, hydrological data sharing is institutionalised and cooperative; with China it is treaty-dependent and episodic. Hydropower is also the anchor of India–Bhutan economic ties.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India–Bhutan, neighbourhood first); GS1/GS3 — Geography & Disaster Management (Brahmaputra basin, flood forecasting).

## 6. ABDM's 'Scan and Register' crosses 25 crore OPD registrations

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Health and Family Welfare — National Health Authority (7 Aug 2026)

The **Scan and Register Service** (earlier called Scan and Share) under the **Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission** has crossed **25 crore OPD registrations**. A patient scans a QR code at the facility using any ABDM-enabled Personal Health Record (PHR) app, gives consent, and shares their **ABHA** profile — replacing manual, paper-based registration.

**Key facts:**

- Launched **October 2022**; now averaging **nearly 4 lakh registrations per day**.
- Operational at **30,800 healthcare facilities** — **24,323 public and 6,481 private** — across **36 States/UTs and 756 districts**.
- State leaders: **Bihar (7.30 crore+)**, followed by **Uttar Pradesh (4.09 crore)**, **Andhra Pradesh (3.38 crore)** and **Jammu & Kashmir (1.39 crore)**.
- Facility leaders: **AIIMS New Delhi (54.29 lakh)**, AIIMS Bhopal (29.10 lakh), AIIMS Bhubaneswar (23.93 lakh), AIIMS Patna (19.62 lakh), AIIMS Raipur (18.63 lakh).
- NHA CEO: **Dr. Sunil Kumar Barnwal**.

### Scheme-in-focus: Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM)

- **Ministry:** Health & Family Welfare; implemented by the **National Health Authority (NHA)**. Launched nationally on **27 September 2021** (pilot as National Digital Health Mission, 15 August 2020).
- **Objective:** build the digital backbone for an **integrated, interoperable and consent-based digital health ecosystem**.
- **Key building blocks:** (i) **ABHA number/address** — unique health ID; (ii) **Healthcare Professionals Registry (HPR)**; (iii) **Health Facility Registry (HFR)**; (iv) **Unified Health Interface (UHI)** and **PHR apps** for record exchange.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** all citizens; Scan and Register alone now spans 36 States/UTs, 756 districts and 30,800 facilities.
- **Significance:** consent is the architectural core — records move only when the patient authorises it. Note that **Bihar and UP lead**, which cuts against the assumption that digital health adoption tracks state income.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance & Health (digital public infrastructure, health service delivery).

**Q1.** With reference to the 27 places and features in Arunachal Pradesh recently identified by standard names on official maps, consider the following statements:

(b) 2 and 3 only

(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q2.** The "Facility for Antiproton and Ion Research (FAIR)", seen in the news recently, is best described as:

(a) A deep-underground neutrino observatory being built in Tamil Nadu with international collaboration
(b) A multipurpose particle accelerator facility under construction at Darmstadt, Germany, of which India is a founding member
(c) A gravitational-wave detector being set up in Maharashtra as part of the global LIGO network
(d) A fusion energy reactor being built in France with India as a participating partner

**Q3.** With reference to the Government e Marketplace (GeM), consider the following statements:

1. It functions under the administrative control of the Ministry of Commerce and Industry.
2. Micro and Small Enterprises have accounted for more than 40 per cent of its cumulative Gross Merchandise Value.
3. All orders placed on the platform, irrespective of value, are exempt from transaction charges.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the MHA acted in consultation with the Arunachal Pradesh government, and the 27 features include four passes (Dzo La, Riza La, Pukur La, Thag La), a lake (Sambho Sarovar) and a monument (Sher-e-Thapa Memorial). Statement 3 is wrong — the **Survey of India functions under the Department of Science and Technology**, not the MHA.

**Q2 — (b).**
FAIR is a large multipurpose particle accelerator facility being built at **Darmstadt, Germany**; India became a founding member by signing the convention at Wiesbaden on 4 October 2010, with contributions funded 50:50 by DST and DAE. Option (a) describes the India-based Neutrino Observatory, (c) LIGO-India, and (d) ITER.

**Q3 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
GeM is under the **Ministry of Commerce and Industry**; MSEs have fulfilled orders worth ₹9.07 lakh crore, or **45.6%** of cumulative GMV — above 40%. Statement 3 is wrong: only orders **up to ₹10 lakh** are exempt from transaction charges (which covers about 97% of orders, but not all); above that a charge of 0.30% applies, capped at ₹3 lakh.
