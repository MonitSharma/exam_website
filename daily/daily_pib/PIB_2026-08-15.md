## 1. PM's 80th Independence Day address: the **'Sapta Dhara'** seven-pillar framework for Viksit Bharat @2047

**Issuing body:** Prime Minister's Office / Cabinet Secretariat (Red Fort address, 15 Aug 2026)

Delivering his 13th consecutive Independence Day address from the ramparts of the Red Fort, the Prime Minister unveiled **'Sapta Dhara'** — a seven-pillar (literally "seven streams") blueprint to accelerate India towards developed-nation status by 2047. It is the organising frame for the rest of the speech's announcements, so expect it as a Mains value-addition template across GS3.

**The seven Dharas:**

| Pillar | Core thrust |
|---|---|
| **Manufacturing power** | End-to-end domestic value chains — design to components; zero-defect quality, cost, global scale |
| **Agriculture & food processing** | Integrated farm-to-export chains for millets, spices, fruits; transition to chemical-free farming |
| **Technology & innovation** | From tech *market* to global innovation *hub* — AI, quantum, space, robotics, DPI, Made-in-India 6G |
| **Gati Shakti** | High-speed multimodal logistics — cities, highways, railways, airports, modern industrial seaports |
| **Defence Shakti** | Strategic self-reliance; export next-gen tech — drones, counter-drone systems, hypersonics |
| **Green & blue economy** | Green hydrogen, renewables, storage + full use of ocean, coastal and marine potential |
| **India's soft power** | Culture, yoga, Ayurveda, tourism; leadership in gaming, VFX, animation, creative economy |

**Key facts:** 80th Independence Day; Vande Mataram rendered chorally at the Red Fort for the first time, marking **150 years of the national song**; PM also framed India's arc from the "Fragile Five" tag of 2014 to fastest-growing major economy. On semiconductors, he said **three fabs are already operational** with exports expected, and **five to eight more** likely over the next 7–8 years. He closed with solidarity for regions hit by monsoon floods and landslides.

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (growth model, manufacturing, logistics) — also GS1 Society/Culture for the soft-power pillar.

## 2. Youth package from the Red Fort: **1 crore youth to be AI-trained**, free online coaching, and a 5–15 sports talent hunt

**Issuing body:** PMO (implementation to run through Education / Skill Development & Entrepreneurship / Youth Affairs & Sports)

Three distinct youth-facing announcements were made in the same address, all in mission mode:

- **AI skilling mission:** **one crore young people to be trained in artificial intelligence over the next year** through a nationwide mission-mode initiative.
- **Free online coaching:** the PM said "coaching classes burden the poor and the middle class" and announced a government-provided **free online coaching network for competitive examinations**, to be built by combining Digital Public Infrastructure, teachers and talent. Directly relevant to the equity-of-access debate in exam preparation.
- **Sports talent identification:** a nationwide campaign targeting **children aged 5–15** across villages and cities.

**Why it matters:** This is the clearest attempt yet to convert the "demographic dividend" argument into a delivery mechanism, and it lands weeks after large youth protests at Jantar Mantar in Delhi. For Mains, it pairs with the coaching-industry regulation debate and with DPI-as-welfare-delivery (the same logic as DigiLocker, ABDM and BHASHINI).

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (education, welfare delivery via DPI); GS3 Economy (skilling, employability).

## 3. Energy security push: **National Deepwater Exploration Mission**, 100 GW nuclear by 2047, fast-breeder mastery

**Issuing body:** PMO, with Department of Atomic Energy / Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas implications

Energy independence was positioned as an *urgent national priority*, with the PM noting that a large share of the budget still goes to importing petrol, diesel and gas, and that the world is "weaponising" whatever it possesses — petrol, diesel, fertilisers, medicines, technology chips.

**Key facts and figures:**

- Announcement of a **National Deepwater Exploration Mission** to tap ocean hydrocarbon resources.
- **99% of India's coastline** had earlier been designated "no-go" for exploration; the PM said this has been opened up — "no-go areas turned into go-ahead areas" — calling the earlier position a "poverty of thought."
- Last year's **'Samudra Manthan'** offshore exploration programme cited as the first step, with **₹85,000 crore** allocated.
- **Nuclear:** target of **100 GW by 2047** (from roughly 7.5 GW in 2025); **five new reactors operational in 6–7 years**; India **mastered fast-breeder reactor technology this year** — a major step towards closing the fuel cycle and self-reliance in nuclear fuel.
- Legislative anchor: the **SHANTI Act** (Sustainable Harnessing and Advancement of Nuclear Energy for Transforming India), which repealed the Atomic Energy Act, 1962 and the Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act, 2010, gave statutory recognition to the AERB, and opened nuclear generation to private and limited foreign participation. *(New development: this is the first Red Fort articulation of the 100 GW target as the Act's operative goal.)*
- **PNG networks** have expanded from **70 cities 12 years ago to over 700 cities today**, connecting **1.75 crore households**.
- **Critical minerals** flagged as central to self-reliance, linked to the National Critical Minerals Mission.

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (energy security, infrastructure) and GS3 Science & Technology (nuclear fuel cycle, fast breeder reactors).

## 4. A national **Civil Defence volunteer network** announced; internal security framed around "Dimagi Naxals"

**Issuing body:** PMO / Ministry of Home Affairs (Directorate General Fire Service, Civil Defence & Home Guards)

The PM announced that India will build a **"vibrant Civil Defence network"** — a large, modern civil defence volunteer force trained to handle contemporary crises, with citizens familiarised with modern protective systems. Civil Defence in India operates under the **Civil Defence Act, 1968**, administered by MHA; the announcement signals a significant scaling-up beyond the existing town-based structure, and follows the renewed salience of civil preparedness after Operation Sindoor.

On Left Wing Extremism, the PM said armed Naxalism in the forests has been eliminated, but **"Dimagi Naxals"** (ideological or "mindset" Naxals) remain and must be identified and isolated — a formulation carried forward from the previous year's address.

**Why it matters:** Civil defence is an under-prepared area in most UPSC answers on disaster management and internal security; note the distinction between **NDMA/NDRF (disaster response)** and **Civil Defence (citizen volunteer preparedness under MHA)**.

**Notebook:** GS3 Internal Security & Disaster Management; GS2 Governance (centre–state machinery).

## 5. Independence Day honours: **78 gallantry awards** by the President; **1,057 police and service medals** by MHA

**Issuing bodies:** Ministry of Defence (gallantry awards) and Ministry of Home Affairs (police/fire/civil defence medals), announced 14 August 2026

**Gallantry awards (armed forces & CAPFs) — 78 in total, 13 posthumous:**

- **9 Kirti Chakras** (7 posthumous)
- **1 Bar to Shaurya Chakra** and **19 Shaurya Chakras** (1 posthumous)
- **5 Bars to Sena Medal (Gallantry)** and **36 Sena Medals (Gallantry)**
- **3 Nao Sena Medals (Gallantry)**, **5 Vayu Sena Medals (Gallantry)**
- Additionally **89 Mention-in-Despatches** (75 Army, 10 Air Force, 4 Navy)

**MHA police medals — 1,057 personnel** of Police, Fire, Home Guard & Civil Defence and Correctional Services:

- **301 Medals for Gallantry** (272 police, 29 fire service)
- **92 President's Medals for Distinguished Service** (83 police, 4 fire, 3 civil defence & home guard, 2 correctional)
- **664 Medals for Meritorious Service**
- Regional split of the 301 gallantry medals: **197 from LWE-affected areas, 51 from J&K, 12 from the North-East, 41 from other regions** — a useful data point on where India's internal security burden actually sits.

**Prelims trap to remember:** Kirti Chakra and Shaurya Chakra are **peacetime** gallantry awards (order: Ashoka Chakra > Kirti Chakra > Shaurya Chakra); the wartime series is Param Vir Chakra > Maha Vir Chakra > Vir Chakra.

**Notebook:** GS3 Internal Security; GS2 Polity (President's powers under Article 18 / national honours).

## 6. President's **Address to the Nation on the eve of the 80th Independence Day**

**Issuing body:** Rashtrapati Bhavan, 14 August 2026

The President's annual address covered India's journey since Independence, the contribution of citizens to nation-building, economic progress, and the role of **youth and women** in building Viksit Bharat @2047. She paid tribute to freedom fighters and soldiers, recalled the suffering of Partition, and underlined India's commitment to heritage, **environmental protection, national security and a rules-based international order**.

**Notable first:** the **full version of Vande Mataram was played before the address**, followed by the national anthem — a first for this ceremony, tied to the 150th year of the national song.

**Why it matters:** The President's address is a standard source for constitutional-morality and nation-building quotes in GS2 and Essay. Note the constitutional distinction — the **national song (Vande Mataram)** has no statutory protection comparable to the **national anthem (Jana Gana Mana)**, which is protected under the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971.

**Notebook:** GS2 Polity & Governance; Essay.

## 7. NBA releases **₹15.52 crore** in Access and Benefit Sharing proceeds from mustard genetic resources

**Issuing body:** National Biodiversity Authority, Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (13–14 August 2026)

The **National Biodiversity Authority (NBA)** released **₹15.52 crore** in Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) proceeds, collected from **Pioneer Overseas Corporation**, which used Indian mustard (*Brassica juncea*) genetic resources to develop **10 commercial hybrid varieties**. The money goes to **26 State Biodiversity Boards and 3 Union Territory Biodiversity Councils**, allocated primarily in proportion to **area under mustard cultivation**. **Rajasthan received the largest share, ₹6.43 crore.**

*New development note:* an NBA ABS disbursal for **rice** (₹8.22 crore) was covered on 2 August. What is new here is the crop, the far larger corpus, and the explicit use of the **cultivated-area formula** where the original resource providers cannot be identified — the standard Indian answer to the "benefit-sharing with whom?" problem under the Nagoya Protocol.

**Statutory framework to memorise:** ABS flows from the **Biological Diversity Act, 2002** (as amended in 2023), implementing India's obligations under the **CBD** and the **Nagoya Protocol on Access and Benefit-sharing (2010, in force 2014)**. Three-tier structure: **NBA (national) → State Biodiversity Boards → Biodiversity Management Committees** at local body level, with **People's Biodiversity Registers** maintained by BMCs.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment & Biodiversity; GS3 Agriculture (plant genetic resources, hybrid seed industry).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the **'Sapta Dhara'** framework announced from the Red Fort on 15 August 2026, which of the following is **NOT** one of its seven pillars?

(a) Gati Shakti
(b) Green and blue economy
(c) Cooperative federalism
(d) India's soft power

---

**Q2.** Consider the following statements regarding India's **Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS)** mechanism:

1. It derives from the Biological Diversity Act, 2002, and implements India's obligations under the Nagoya Protocol.
2. People's Biodiversity Registers are maintained by the State Biodiversity Boards.
3. Where the original providers of a biological resource cannot be identified, benefit-sharing proceeds may be distributed among biodiversity authorities of areas where the resource is cultivated.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** With reference to the **SHANTI Act** and India's nuclear energy sector, consider the following statements:

1. The Act repeals both the Atomic Energy Act, 1962 and the Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act, 2010.
2. It grants statutory recognition to the Atomic Energy Regulatory Board.
3. India has set a target of 100 GW of installed nuclear capacity by 2047.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (c) Cooperative federalism.** The seven Dharas are manufacturing, agriculture and food processing, technology and innovation, Gati Shakti, Defence Shakti, green and blue economy, and India's soft power.

**Q2 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** Statement 2 is wrong: People's Biodiversity Registers are prepared and maintained by **Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)** at the local body level, not by State Biodiversity Boards. Statements 1 and 3 correctly describe the framework used for the ₹15.52 crore mustard ABS release.

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The SHANTI Act replaces both earlier statutes with a single law, gives the AERB statutory status, opens the sector to private and limited foreign participation, and underpins the 100 GW-by-2047 target reiterated from the Red Fort (against roughly 7.5 GW installed in 2025).
