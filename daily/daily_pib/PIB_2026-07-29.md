## 1. MoSPI confirms the revised Consumer Price Index series — base year shifted from 2012=100 to 2024=100

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation (MoSPI) — statement by MoS (I/C) Rao Inderjit Singh in Lok Sabha, 29 July 2026.

India's retail inflation is now measured on a completely rebased CPI. The new series, released on 12 February 2026, derives its item basket and weights from the **Household Consumption Expenditure Survey (HCES) 2023-24** — the first rebasing in over a decade. This matters because CPI is the RBI's nominal anchor under the flexible inflation-targeting framework, so a change in basket and weights changes what the 4% ± 2% target is actually measuring.

**Key facts:**

- Base year revised from **2012=100 to 2024=100**; first index of the new series released 12 February 2026.
- Price collection extended to **1,465 rural markets and 1,395 urban markets across 434 towns**.
- **12 online markets** added across 12 towns each with population above 25 lakh, to capture e-commerce price variation — a first for Indian CPI.
- Weighted item basket expanded from **299 to 358 items** (goods 259 → 308; services 40 → 50).
- CPI 2024 series data available from **January 2026**; CPI 2012 series data ends December 2025. Direct comparison across the two series is **not feasible** due to changes in basket, weights, market coverage and methodology.
- Inflation under the new series (combined): Jan-26 **2.74%**, Feb **3.21%**, Mar **3.40%**, Apr **3.48%**, May **3.93%**, Jun-26 (provisional) **4.38%**. Rural inflation has run consistently above urban since February.
- The **Expert Group Report on Comprehensive Updation of CPI** was released on 29 January 2026, ahead of the first index. Linking factors were published, and back-series data (Jan 2013 – Dec 2024) is on the **e-Sankhyiki Portal**.

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy — Inflation & Statistical Systems. *(Also cross-tag: Reports & Indices.)*

## 2. Home Ministry: India now has **zero** Left Wing Extremism-affected districts

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Home Affairs — written reply by MoS Nityanand Rai in Rajya Sabha, 29 July 2026.

MHA formally recorded that after nearly six decades, no district in the country is currently categorised as LWE-affected. The reply is a consolidated data dump on the "Whole of Government" approach under the **National Policy and Action Plan to address LWE (2015)** — the security-plus-development-plus-rights triad that is a standing GS3 Internal Security theme.

**Key facts — the decline curve:**

| Point in time | LWE-affected districts |
|---|---|
| Baseline | 126 |
| April 2018 | 90 |
| July 2021 | 70 |
| April 2024 | 38 |
| December 2025 | 08 |
| March/April 2026 | **0** |

- New post-LWE categories: **37 'Legacy & Thrust' districts** (no longer affected but needing continued support) and **1 'District of Concern'** (violence contained, organisational structure dismantled).
- Violence data: incidents perpetrated by Maoists fell from **486 (2023) → 375 (2024) → 234 (2025) → 33 (till 21 July 2026)**; civilian deaths 107 → 132 → 64 → 6; security force deaths 32 → 19 → 36 → 5.
- **Security side:** ₹3,805.04 crore released since 2014-15 under the **Security Related Expenditure (SRE)** scheme; ₹1,761 crore of works sanctioned under the **Special Infrastructure Scheme**; **663 fortified police stations** built; ₹1,303.68 crore to central agencies under **ACALWEMS**.
- **Surrender-cum-rehabilitation:** ₹5 lakh immediate grant for higher-ranked cadres, ₹2.5 lakh for others, plus vocational training with a **₹10,000/month stipend for three years**.
- **Development side:** 15,189 km of roads under **RRP** and **RCPLWEA**; **9,497 telecom towers**; 47 ITIs and 49 Skill Development Centres; **179 Eklavya Model Residential Schools**; 6,025 post offices with banking services, 1,804 bank branches, 1,321 ATMs.
- **Rights:** **21,26,268 forest title deeds** distributed (20,20,268 individual + 1,06,000 community).
- **New institutional move:** one-third of security camps in LWE-free areas are being converted into **Jan Suvidha Kendras**. The first is at **Netanar, Jagdalpur, Chhattisgarh** — the *Shaheed Veer Gundadhur Seva Dera Kendra*.
- Total central assistance across all LWE schemes, 2021-22 to 2025-26: **₹4,430.23 crore**.

**Notebook:** GS3 Internal Security — Left Wing Extremism. *(Cross-link to GS2: Seventh Schedule — Police and Public Order are State subjects; the Centre only supplements.)*

## 3. Global Tiger Day 2026 — NTCA launches an Eco-tourism webpage; India at 3,682 tigers and 58 tiger reserves

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change / National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA). Event addressed by Union Minister Bhupender Yadav, New Delhi, 29 July 2026.

Global Tiger Day is observed every 29 July. The Ministry used the occasion to launch an integrated eco-tourism platform and release five conservation publications — the substantive takeaway for Prelims is the institutional architecture (NTCA, IBCA, GTRP, TX2) plus current numbers.

**Key facts:**

- India's tiger population stands at **3,682**, across **58 tiger reserves**.
- **NTCA Eco-tourism webpage** launched — a single integrated platform linking authentic websites of all tiger reserves for safari and accommodation bookings (ntca.gov.in/eco-tourism).
- **Five publications released:** July 2026 edition of *STRIPES* (NTCA outreach journal); **Security Audit Report of 24 Tiger Reserves**; a habitat suitability assessment for **Sloth Bears** across tiger landscapes; *Tigers of the World* (published by **IBCA and Global Tiger Forum**); and *The Rehman Khera Tiger*.
- **NTCA Awards** presented for tiger conservation and protected-area management.
- **Background worth memorising:** A century ago roughly **1,00,000** wild tigers roamed Asia; today they occupy **less than 8%** of their historic range. Three subspecies — **Bali, Javan and Caspian tigers** — are extinct. The **St. Petersburg Tiger Summit (2010)** of the **13 Tiger Range Countries** launched the **Global Tiger Recovery Programme (GTRP)**, established Global Tiger Day on 29 July, and set the **TX2 goal** of doubling wild tiger numbers by 2022.
- The **International Big Cat Alliance (IBCA)** was flagged as India's vehicle for global knowledge-sharing on big cats.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment — Wildlife Conservation & Institutions.

## 4. India has signed Digital Public Infrastructure MoUs with **24 countries**

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Electronics & IT — submitted by MoS Jitin Prasada in Lok Sabha, 29 July 2026.

India Stack has become an instrument of foreign policy. MeitY tabled the full country list of India Stack MoUs, the status of DigiLocker exports, and the UPI internationalisation table — an unusually complete factual base for both GS2 (IR) and GS3 (Sci-Tech) answers on DPI diplomacy and Global South leadership.

**Key facts:**

- **MoUs with 24 countries** for sharing population-scale digital solutions. Earliest signings June 2023 (Armenia, Sierra Leone, Suriname, Antigua and Barbuda); most recent **Seychelles, 8 February 2026**. The list spans the Caribbean (Trinidad and Tobago, Jamaica, St. Kitts and Nevis, Guyana), Africa (Tanzania, Kenya, Ethiopia, Gambia, Lesotho), Latin America (Cuba, Colombia, Venezuela, Brazil), the Indo-Pacific (Papua New Guinea, Lao PDR, Fiji, Sri Lanka, Maldives, Mongolia) and Armenia.
- **DigiLocker is the lead export:** an **Implementation Framework Agreement and NDA concluded with Kenya**; development of a foundational DigiLocker solution has **commenced in Cuba**.
- Other solutions under bilateral discussion: **e-Sanjeevani, e-Office, e-Hospital** with Lao PDR, Seychelles, Venezuela, Armenia, Fiji, Guyana, Colombia.
- **Social Impact Fund (SIF)** approved — a government-led multistakeholder fund for financial and technical support to DPI pilots in the Global South, particularly Low- and Middle-Income Countries.
- **UPI internationalisation:** live in **Bhutan, France, Mauritius, Nepal, Qatar, Singapore, Sri Lanka, UAE, Cambodia and Greece**. Note the newest entries — **Greece (P2P, May 2026)**, **Cambodia (P2M, June 2026)** and **Nepal P2P via NCHL (June 2026)**. Singapore and UAE are the oldest links (2021–22).
- Institutional anchors for foreign study visits: **UIDAI, NeGD, NPCI, NIC, C-DAC**. Recent delegations from Kenya, Rwanda, Nepal, Indonesia, South Africa.
- Policy lineage: DPI was a priority of the **Digital Economy Working Group** under India's **G20 Presidency (2023)**; the **G20 New Delhi Leaders' Declaration** recognised DPI as a development accelerator.

## 5. WCD rolls out a unified Mission Vatsalya Portal integrating TrackChild, Khoya-Paya and CCTNS

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Women and Child Development — reply by MoS Savitri Thakur in Rajya Sabha, 29 July 2026.

The Ministry has folded India's scattered child-protection IT systems into one platform. The interoperability with the Home Ministry's CCTNS is the genuinely significant bit: FIRs for missing children can now be machine-matched against the Mission Vatsalya database, which is the kind of DPI-for-welfare convergence UPSC likes as an example of e-governance in social sector delivery.

**Key facts:**

- **Statutory base:** Juvenile Justice (Care and Protection of Children) Act, 2015. Structures created: State Child Protection Society, **Child Welfare Committees (Sections 27-30)**, Juvenile Justice Boards, District Child Protection Units, Child Care Institutions. **Section 106** places primary implementation responsibility on States/UTs; **Section 109** gives NCPCR and SCPCRs the monitoring role.
- **Portals integrated:** **TrackChild** (missing/found children) and the **Khoya-Paya** application (missing/sighted children — any citizen can report). Also integrated: NCPCR's **GHAR (Go Home and Re-Unite)** portal.
- **Interoperability with MHA's CCTNS** allows matching of FIRs of missing children with the Mission Vatsalya database.
- **Child Helpline 1098** — 24×7×365, integrated with **ERSS-112** and **Women Helpline 181**; dedicated WCD control rooms in all **36 States and UTs**.
- **Related NCPCR tools:** **MASI** (Monitoring App for Seamless Inspection) for real-time CCI monitoring; **CiSS application** under the **Baal Swaraj portal** for children in street situations; upgraded **E-Baal Nidan** complaint portal.

### Scheme-in-focus: Mission Vatsalya

- **Name & Ministry:** Mission Vatsalya — Ministry of Women and Child Development. Centrally Sponsored Scheme on a predefined Centre-State cost-sharing basis, in all States and UTs.
- **Objective:** Deliver care, protection and rehabilitation services to children in difficult circumstances, including orphans, under the JJ Act 2015 framework.
- **Key features:**
  1. **Institutional care** through Child Care Institutions providing age-appropriate education, vocational training, recreation, healthcare and counselling.
  2. **Non-institutional care** through **Sponsorship, Foster Care, Adoption and After Care** — with a cash entitlement of **₹4,000 per month** per eligible child.
  3. **Guiding principle:** institutionalisation of children as a **measure of last resort**; family-based care is the default preference.
  4. **Emergency outreach** via Child Helpline 1098, plus a unified digital monitoring portal.
- **Beneficiaries:** Children in need of care and protection and children in conflict with law, across all 36 States and UTs.
- **Significance:** Operationalises the JJ Act's deinstitutionalisation philosophy and the "best interest of the child" standard, while using DPI-style integration (CCTNS, ERSS-112) to convert a welfare scheme into a traceability system for missing children.

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance — Welfare Schemes for Vulnerable Sections / Child Rights. *(Cross-tag GS1 Society.)*

## 6. Corporate Mitra Scheme — Budget 2026-27 initiative rolls out in the North East

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Corporate Affairs / Indian Institute of Corporate Affairs (IICA), Shillong. Awareness webinar for Mizoram held 27 July, reported 29 July 2026.

A scheme announced in the **Union Budget 2026-27** that creates an accredited cadre of MSME support para-professionals. Worth logging because it is a new named scheme with a clear structure, and because it targets the compliance-capacity gap in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities — a standard GS3 question area on MSME formalisation.

**Key facts:**

- **Announced in Union Budget 2026-27**; aim is to empower MSMEs, primarily in **Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities**, in service of Viksit Bharat @ 2047.
- Creates a cadre of accredited para-professionals — **"Corporate Mitras"** — drawn from young graduates, trained to handle MSMEs' **non-core functions**: regulatory, compliance, financial, taxation, accounting and governance needs.
- **IICA Shillong** is the **nodal agency** for rollout across the North East Region.
- **NER incentives:** **200 seats reserved exclusively** for the region with a **50% fee concession**.
- Course registration opened **15 July 2026**, open till **31 July 2026**, hosted on the **SWAYAM Plus** platform.
- Professional bodies involved: **ICMAI** (lead co-host), with **ICSI** and **ICAI** as support co-hosts; Mizoram University facilitated the Mizoram webinar (650+ participants).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the revised Consumer Price Index (CPI) series announced by the Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation, consider the following statements:

1. The base year has been revised from 2012=100 to 2024=100, with the item basket and weights derived from the Household Consumption Expenditure Survey 2023-24.
2. The revised series has, for the first time, incorporated online markets to capture prices on e-commerce platforms.
3. The number of weighted items at the All-India level has been reduced from 358 to 299 to simplify the basket.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Consider the following statements regarding the Government of India's response to Left Wing Extremism (LWE):

1. Under the Seventh Schedule of the Constitution, Police and Public Order are State subjects, and the Centre only supplements State efforts.
2. The National Policy and Action Plan to address Left Wing Extremism was approved in 2015.
3. Security camps in areas freed from LWE are being converted into Jan Suvidha Kendras, the first of which was established in Jharkhand.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** With reference to Global Tiger Day and international tiger conservation, consider the following statements:

1. Global Tiger Day, observed annually on 29 July, was established at the St. Petersburg Tiger Summit of 2010.
2. The TX2 goal adopted at that Summit aimed at doubling the global wild tiger population by 2022.
3. The Bali, Javan and Caspian tiger subspecies are now extinct.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the base year moved to 2024=100 using HCES 2023-24 weights, and 12 online markets were added across towns with population above 25 lakh. Statement 3 inverts the change — the basket was **expanded from 299 to 358** items (goods 259→308, services 40→50), not reduced.

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Police and Public Order are indeed State subjects under the Seventh Schedule, and the National Policy and Action Plan to address LWE dates to 2015. Statement 3 is wrong on location: the first Jan Suvidha Kendra was set up at **Netanar, Jagdalpur, Chhattisgarh** (the Shaheed Veer Gundadhur Seva Dera Kendra), not in Jharkhand.

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct. The 2010 St. Petersburg Tiger Summit of the 13 Tiger Range Countries launched the Global Tiger Recovery Programme, established Global Tiger Day on 29 July, and set the TX2 doubling target for 2022; the Bali, Javan and Caspian subspecies are extinct, and tigers now occupy under 8% of their historic range.
