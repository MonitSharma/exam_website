## 1. Ministry of Earth Sciences: **'Swachh Sagar, Surakshit Sagar 5.0'** launched — week-long coastal clean-up from 12–19 September

Dr Jitendra Singh, Union Minister of State (Independent Charge) for Earth Sciences, launched the **5th edition** of *Swachh Sagar, Surakshit Sagar (SSSS v.5.0)* on 3 September 2026. Now in its fifth year, the campaign has been positioned as a **Jan Bhagidari** movement for ocean and beach conservation, drawing on the vision of the **Deep Ocean Mission**.

**Why it matters:** This is India's flagship citizen-facing marine-litter campaign — the operational, participatory arm of a marine pollution agenda that otherwise sits in treaty language (UNCLOS, the ongoing Global Plastics Treaty negotiations, SDG 14 *Life Below Water*). For Mains, it is a ready example of behavioural-change instruments complementing regulation in environmental governance.

**Key facts**

- Nodal ministry: **Ministry of Earth Sciences**; implementation support from **NIOT (National Institute of Ocean Technology), Chennai**, which hosts the pledge portal.
- Campaign window: **12–19 September 2026** — a week-long "celebration of our oceans".
- India's coastline cited by the Minister: **over 11,000 km**, east to west.
- Activities: beach clean-up drives at **100+ locations**, human chain, cycling rallies, marathons, Ozone Day activities, and an Ocean Conservation Pledge.
- **First-ever national "Ocean Olympiad"** for students and youth is being introduced this edition.
- Digital launches: SSSS v.5.0 website, official ocean-conservation pledge, introductory video and activity-schedule video.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (marine pollution, conservation campaigns); GS2 — Governance (Jan Bhagidari / participatory models).

## 2. Ministry of Electronics & IT: **Digital India BHASHINI takes Language AI to Nepal** — 'BHASHINI SANGAM' workshop, Kathmandu *(EXPANDED)*

**What's new since yesterday's one-liner:** the 3 September PIB release puts detail on record — the specific meeting with Kathmandu University's **DPI-AI centre** and its Vice-Chancellor, the four target languages, the two under-represented scripts, and the June 2026 MoU that underpins the engagement. Promoted to a full item on that basis.

The **Digital India BHASHINI Division (DIBD)**, MeitY, in collaboration with the **Embassy of India in Kathmandu** and **Kathmandu University**, held the *BHASHINI SANGAM: International Engagement Workshop (Nepal)*. DIBD teams met Kathmandu University's **Centre for Digital Public Infrastructure and Artificial Intelligence (DPI-AI)** and its Vice-Chancellor to scope co-development of a voice-first language platform for Nepal.

**Why it matters:** This is **DPI diplomacy** — after UPI and Aadhaar-style stacks, India is now exporting its *language* AI layer. It converts BHASHINI from a domestic inclusion tool into an instrument of neighbourhood soft power, and lands squarely in the GS2 "India and its neighbourhood" space at a moment when India–Nepal ties are otherwise dominated by flood-relief and border-management headlines.

**Key facts**

- **BHASHINI** = India's National Language Translation Mission under **MeitY**; builds open AI models and datasets for Indian languages to democratise digital access.
- Languages discussed for model development: **Nepali, Newari, Tamang and Gurung**.
- Traditional scripts flagged as digitally under-represented: **Prachalit Lipi** and **Ranjana Lipi**.
- Builds on an **MoU signed in June 2026** between DIBD and Kathmandu University's DPI-AI centre.
- Framing concept: **"low-resource languages"** — languages with too little digital text/speech data to train conventional AI models.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India–Nepal, DPI diplomacy); GS3 — Science & Technology (AI, digital public infrastructure).

## 3. Ministry of Science & Technology (CSIR): **CSIR-CRRI demonstrates 60% Reclaimed Asphalt Pavement on NH-716** — a first for Indian highways

**CSIR-Central Road Research Institute (CSIR-CRRI)**, New Delhi, with Oriental Structural Engineers Pvt Ltd (OSEPL) and Ooms India, demonstrated the use of **60% Reclaimed Asphalt Pavement (RAP)** in the **Dense Bituminous Macadam (DBM)** layer on **NH-716 (Chennai–Tirupati, Package-I)**. Senior NHAI and Government of Tamil Nadu officials witnessed the trial.

**Why it matters:** Indian highway practice has generally capped RAP at **20–30%**. Doubling that cuts virgin bitumen — almost all of which India **imports** — reduces cost and quarrying of fresh aggregate, and gives the circular economy a concrete infrastructure application. Useful as a specific, non-generic example for questions on circular economy, sustainable infrastructure and import substitution.

**Key facts**

- **RAP** = milled/removed old bituminous road material reprocessed into new pavement mixes.
- Conventional Indian usage: **20–30% RAP**; this trial: **60% RAP** in the DBM layer.
- Location: **NH-716**, Chennai–Tirupati corridor, Package-I.
- Lead institution: **CSIR-CRRI** (a CSIR national laboratory under the Ministry of Science & Technology); demonstration observed by **NHAI**.
- Benefits cited: lower virgin bitumen consumption, lower construction cost, alignment with **circular economy** principles.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology / Infrastructure / Environment (circular economy, sustainable construction).

## 4. Ministry of Ports, Shipping & Waterways: **₹24,700 crore Kendrapara shipbuilding cluster gets in-principle approval** under the Shipbuilding Development Scheme

The **National Shipbuilding and Heavy Industries Park Odisha Limited (NSHIPOL)** received **in-principle approval** under the **Shipbuilding Development Scheme (SbDS)** of MoPSW for a greenfield shipbuilding and heavy-industries cluster in **Kendrapara district, Odisha**. NSHIPOL had applied on 30 July 2026.

**Why it matters:** India builds under **1%** of global tonnage. SbDS is the Centre's attempt to fix this through **state-level clusters with shared infrastructure** rather than isolated yards — the same cluster logic used in textiles (PM MITRA) and electronics. Pair this with the *Sagar Samvad* 100-vessel target (29 Aug briefing) and the Maritime Amrit Kaal Vision 2047 for a complete maritime-sector answer.

**Key facts**

- Investment: **₹24,700 crore**; scheme: **Shipbuilding Development Scheme (SbDS)**, MoPSW.
- Location: **northern bank of the Mahanadi**, opposite the **IFFCO plant at Paradip**, Kendrapara, Odisha.
- Scale: shipyards of **~1.2 million Gross Tonnage (GT)** annual capacity; **2,000–2,500 acres** with a **2–2.5 km waterfront**.
- Delivery vehicle: an **SPV** involving the **Odisha Maritime Board (OMB)** and **Paradip Port Authority (PPA)**.
- Recommended by the **Inter-Ministerial Group for Shipbuilding (IMGB)**; final approval requires a **Detailed Project Report**, **environmental clearance** and **Coastal Regulation Zone (CRZ)** clearance.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (infrastructure, manufacturing, ports & shipping); GS1 — Geography (Mahanadi delta, Paradip).

## 5. Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying: **'Mission Rangeen Machhli 2031' formally released at Agatti, Lakshadweep** *(UPDATE)*

**What's new since the 30 August briefing:** the plan was then only *scheduled* for release. Vice-President **C. P. Radhakrishnan** has now **actually released** it at the Fisheries Outreach Programme in **Agatti, Lakshadweep**, with the site, statutory backing and Lakshadweep-specific numbers now on record.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Mission Rangeen Machhli 2031**

- **Name / type:** Mission Rangeen Machhli 2031 — a **Strategic Action Plan for Ornamental Fisheries Development** (not a new funded scheme; a roadmap layered on existing programmes).
- **Ministry:** Department of Fisheries, **Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying (MoFAHD)**.
- **Objective:** a **uniform national framework** for the organised growth of ornamental fisheries — decorative fish for aquariums and ornamental ponds — replacing today's fragmented, largely informal trade.
- **Key features (4):**
  1. **Scientific breeding and management** standards for ornamental species.
  2. **Biosecurity** protocols to prevent disease spread and invasive-species escape.
  3. **Quality assurance** and certification for domestic sale and export.
  4. **Regulatory compliance**, anchored in the *Sustainable Harnessing of Fisheries in the EEZ Rules, 2025* and the *Guidelines for Sustainable Harnessing of Fisheries in the High Seas by Indian-Flagged Fishing Vessels, 2025*.
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** ornamental fish breeders, island and coastal communities, exporters. **Lakshadweep** hosts **300+ ornamental fish species**; an ornamental fish **hatchery at Agatti** is operational and **three rearing units** stand approved under **PMMSY**.
- **Significance:** ornamental fisheries is a high-value, low-volume, women- and household-friendly livelihood — a textbook Blue Economy diversification away from capture fisheries pressure on reef ecosystems.
- Present at the event: Union Minister **Rajiv Ranjan Singh** and MoS **Prof. S. P. Singh Baghel** (MoFAHD).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture & Allied Sectors (fisheries, Blue Economy); GS1 — Geography (Lakshadweep, coral atolls).

**1.** With reference to the *Swachh Sagar, Surakshit Sagar (SSSS)* campaign, consider the following statements:

1. It is implemented by the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change.
2. The 2026 edition introduces a national "Ocean Olympiad" for students and youth for the first time.
3. The campaign is linked to the Deep Ocean Mission.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**2.** Consider the following statements regarding **BHASHINI**:

1. It is India's National Language Translation Mission implemented under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology.
2. In 2026 it engaged Kathmandu University to develop Language AI models for Nepali, Newari, Tamang and Gurung.
3. It is designed only for the 22 languages listed in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**3.** The **Shipbuilding Development Scheme (SbDS)**, seen in the news recently, is administered by which one of the following?

(a) Ministry of Heavy Industries
(b) Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways
(c) Ministry of Commerce and Industry
(d) Ministry of Defence (Department of Defence Production)

## Answers

**1. (b)** — Statement 1 is **incorrect**: SSSS is spearheaded by the **Ministry of Earth Sciences**, with NIOT Chennai hosting the pledge portal, not MoEFCC. Statement 2 is correct — the 2026 edition (v.5.0) launches the **first-ever national Ocean Olympiad**. Statement 3 is correct — the Minister explicitly framed the campaign as inspired by the **Deep Ocean Mission**. The 5th edition runs 12–19 September 2026.

**2. (a)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is **incorrect**: BHASHINI's stated purpose is inclusion across Indian languages generally, and its 2026 Nepal engagement covers **non-Eighth-Schedule, low-resource languages** such as Newari, Tamang and Gurung — so a restriction to the 22 scheduled languages is wrong.

**3. (b)** — The **Shipbuilding Development Scheme** is a scheme of the **Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways**, under which NSHIPOL received in-principle approval for the ₹24,700 crore Kendrapara cluster in Odisha. It promotes **state-level shipbuilding clusters** with shared infrastructure; final approval still needs a DPR plus environmental and CRZ clearances.
