# Daily PIB Briefing — 1 August 2026

*Source focus: Press Information Bureau & Government of India releases, 31 July – 1 August 2026. Cabinet Day dominates this edition — four major approvals across Petroleum, Renewable Energy, Agriculture and Sports — plus a Tribal Affairs R&D pact and the start of World Breastfeeding Week.*

---

## 1. Cabinet approves **'Samudra Manthan'** — National Offshore Exploration Scheme, ₹84,084 crore

**Ministry/Body:** Union Cabinet | Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas

**What it is:** A new central sector scheme with an outlay of **₹84,084 crore up to 2030-31** to aggressively expand exploration in India's offshore sedimentary basins — deepwater, ultra-deepwater and frontier areas. It is the largest single hydrocarbon-exploration push India has announced.

**Why it matters:** India imports over 85% of its crude requirement. Only a fraction of India's ~3.36 million sq km of sedimentary basin area is under active exploration. Samudra Manthan is the supply-side complement to the demand-side reforms (OALP, HELP, the Oilfields (Regulation and Development) Amendment Act, 2025) — a classic GS3 energy-security question.

**Key facts:**

- Outlay **₹84,084 crore**, period **up to FY 2030-31**.
- Targeted addition of **over 600 MMTOE** (million metric tonnes of oil equivalent) to India's hydrocarbon reserves.
- Covers the full value chain: large-scale **seismic data acquisition, processing and interpretation**; accelerated **exploratory drilling** in deepwater/ultra-deepwater; **scientific drilling in frontier basins**; and **common offshore production and evacuation infrastructure**.
- Provides for an **integrated Oil & Gas Manufacturing and Services Zone**, plus digital programme management, capacity building and international outreach.
- Framed around **Make in India / Atmanirbhar Bharat** — building a globally competitive domestic offshore-technology ecosystem.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure: Energy) / Energy Security

## 2. Cabinet approves **Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana (PM-SSY)** — 5,000 MW floating solar with storage

**Ministry/Body:** Union Cabinet | Ministry of New & Renewable Energy

**What it is:** A **₹5,070 crore** scheme (FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31) to develop **5,000 MW of Floating Solar Photovoltaic (FSPV)** capacity on reservoirs and industrial ponds, each project mandatorily paired with **co-located Energy Storage Systems (ESS)**.

**Why it matters:** Floating solar solves two constraints at once — land scarcity for utility-scale solar, and evaporation losses from reservoirs. The mandatory storage component is the policy signal: India is moving from "add MW" to "add *firm, dispatchable* MW", addressing the duck-curve and grid-balancing problem that pure solar creates.

**Key facts:**

- Outlay **₹5,070 crore**; capacity target **5,000 MW**.
- **Minimum 2 hours of storage per project**, aggregating to at least **10,000 MWh** across the programme.
- **Central Financial Assistance of ₹1 crore per MW**, released **after successful commissioning** (outcome-linked, not upfront).
- Up to **₹50 lakh per project** for feasibility studies — **bathymetry and hydrography** assessments, environmental studies and preparatory work.
- Projected to avoid roughly **10 million tonnes of CO₂ annually** and create an estimated **16,000–17,000 jobs**.

**Prelims trap:** Do not confuse **PM Surya Sarovar Yojana** (floating solar on water bodies, utility scale, with storage) with **PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana** (rooftop solar for households, launched February 2024).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Renewable Energy) / Economy (Infrastructure)

## 3. Cabinet approves continuation of **PM-KISAN** for 2026-27 to 2030-31 — ₹3.15 lakh crore

**Ministry/Body:** Union Cabinet | Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare

**What it is:** Extension of the Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi — India's flagship **income-support (DBT) scheme for landholding farmer families** — for a further five years with an outlay of **₹3.15 lakh crore**.

**Why it matters:** PM-KISAN is the template for India's shift from **price-based subsidy to direct income transfer** in agriculture. Its continuation with an unchanged ₹6,000/year quantum (despite inflation) is itself an exam-worthy debate: coverage vs adequacy, tenant-farmer exclusion, and land-record digitisation as the eligibility bottleneck.

**Key facts / scheme profile:**

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Ministry | Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare |
| Type | Central Sector Scheme (100% Union funding) |
| Benefit | **₹6,000 per year**, in **three equal instalments of ₹2,000**, via **DBT** |
| Beneficiaries | Over **9.49 crore** farmers |
| Cumulative transfer so far | Over **₹4.47 lakh crore** across **23 instalments** |
| Women farmers | Over **₹1.06 lakh crore** received; nearly **1 in 4** beneficiaries is a woman |
| New outlay | **₹3.15 lakh crore** (2026-27 to 2030-31) |

- Government-cited evaluations: **over 92% of beneficiaries** used funds for agricultural purposes; foodgrain production grew **21.18%** from 2020-21 to 2025-26.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (Farm income, subsidies, DBT) | GS2 — Governance (welfare delivery)

## 4. Cabinet approves **revamped Khelo India Scheme** + enhanced **ANSF** — ₹36,441 crore

**Ministry/Body:** Union Cabinet | Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports

**What it is:** A combined **₹36,441 crore** package for 2026-27 to 2030-31 covering (a) an expanded **Khelo India Scheme** and (b) a strengthened **Assistance to National Sports Federations (ANSF)** scheme.

**Why it matters:** This is roughly **eight times** the previous Khelo India outlay, and it comes as India pursues hosting ambitions for major multi-sport events. The design logic — a continuous "**school to Olympic podium**" pathway integrating sport with education and high-performance training — is the answerable core for a GS2 governance/GS1 society question on sports policy.

**Key facts:**

- Total **₹36,441 crore**, split as **₹29,054 crore** (Khelo India) + **₹7,387 crore** (ANSF), over five years (2026-31).
- Nearly **8×** the outlay of the earlier Khelo India Scheme.
- ANSF funds: **coaching, sports science, modern equipment, international exposure, coaching camps, engagement of foreign experts**, and participation in major international competitions.
- Objective: an unbroken athlete-development pathway from grassroots/school sport to elite podium performance.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (welfare schemes, human resource development) | GS1 — Society

## 5. Ministry of Tribal Affairs signs **MoU with CSIR-NBRI** on tribal bioresources and RET medicinal plants

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Tribal Affairs (MoTA) + CSIR–National Botanical Research Institute, Lucknow

**What it is:** A **three-year MoU** (signed 30 July 2026) to promote R&D on the **conservation and ethnobotanical assessment of tribal bioresources**, with a specific focus on **Rare, Endangered and Threatened (RET) medicinal plant species**.

**Why it matters:** This sits precisely at the intersection of three UPSC themes — **biodiversity conservation**, **protection of traditional knowledge from biopiracy**, and **tribal community rights**. The consent and benefit-sharing architecture is the examinable part.

**Key facts:**

- Signed by **Manish Thakur** (Additional Secretary, MoTA) and **Ajit Kumar Shasany** (Director, CSIR-NBRI), in the presence of Secretary MoTA **Ranjana Chopra**.
- Scientific interventions: **tissue culture facilities** and **specialised seed banks** for RET medicinal plants.
- **Capacity building of tribal youth** in plant propagation, conservation techniques and biodiversity management.
- **Prior Informed Consent (PIC)** of the concerned **Gram Sabhas** is mandatory for all field surveys and ethnobotanical assessments — in line with the **Biological Diversity Act, 2002**.
- **Intellectual property** generated will be **jointly owned** by MoTA and CSIR-NBRI, held in trust for or equitably shared with contributing tribal communities; financial benefits routed through **National/State Biodiversity Funds** and MoTA welfare schemes.

**Link it to:** Biological Diversity Act, 2002 (and 2023 amendment); National Biodiversity Authority; Nagoya Protocol on Access and Benefit Sharing; PESA, 1996 and the Forest Rights Act, 2006 (Gram Sabha as the consent-giving unit).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Biodiversity, ABS) | GS2 — Governance (Tribal welfare) | GS1 — Society

## 6. **World Breastfeeding Week 2026** observed nationwide, 1–7 August

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Women & Child Development (with Ministry of Health & Family Welfare)

**What it is:** India's annual observance of World Breastfeeding Week, 1–7 August, driving a national campaign on early initiation and exclusive breastfeeding.

**Why it matters:** Infant and young child feeding practices are a direct determinant of the **IMR, under-5 mortality and stunting** indicators tracked by NFHS and the SDGs. Expect this as a nutrition/health-governance link with **Poshan 2.0** and **Mission Saksham Anganwadi**.

**Key facts:**

- Observed **1–7 August** every year. WHO's 2026 theme: *"Breastfeeding for a sustainable start in life: strengthen what works."*
- Campaign messages: breastfeeding within the **"Golden Hour"** (first hour after birth); **exclusive breastfeeding for the first 6 months**; **continued breastfeeding up to 2 years and beyond** with appropriate complementary feeding.
- Target audience: families, communities, and frontline healthcare workers.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (Health, Women & Child Development) | GS3 — Human Development indicators

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the **'Samudra Manthan'** scheme recently approved by the Union Cabinet, consider the following statements:

1. It is a scheme of the Ministry of Earth Sciences for deep-ocean mineral exploration.
2. It provides for seismic data acquisition, exploratory drilling in deepwater and ultra-deepwater areas, and scientific drilling in frontier basins.
3. It envisages the setting up of an integrated Oil and Gas Manufacturing and Services Zone.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Consider the following statements regarding the **Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana (PM-SSY)**:

1. It supports the development of floating solar photovoltaic projects on reservoirs and industrial ponds.
2. Every project under the scheme must have a minimum of two hours of co-located energy storage.
3. Central Financial Assistance is disbursed upfront at the time of project sanction.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 1 and 2 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** The Ministry of Tribal Affairs recently signed a three-year MoU with **CSIR-NBRI**. In this context, which of the following is/are stipulated under the agreement?

1. Prior Informed Consent of the concerned Gram Sabhas is required for field surveys and ethnobotanical assessments.
2. Intellectual property generated will be jointly owned and benefits shared equitably with contributing tribal communities.
3. The agreement focuses on conservation of Rare, Endangered and Threatened medicinal plant species through tissue culture and seed banks.

Select the correct answer using the code given below:

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (b) 2 and 3 only.**
Statement 1 is incorrect: Samudra Manthan is the **National Offshore Exploration Scheme** under the **Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas** (₹84,084 crore till 2030-31) for **oil and gas**, not deep-ocean minerals — that is the Deep Ocean Mission of the Ministry of Earth Sciences. Statements 2 and 3 correctly describe its coverage.

**Q2 — (b) 1 and 2 only.**
Statement 3 is incorrect: the CFA of **₹1 crore per MW is released *after* successful commissioning**, not upfront; only feasibility-study support (up to ₹50 lakh per project) is available at the preparatory stage. Statements 1 and 2 are correct — 5,000 MW of FSPV with a minimum 2-hour ESS per project, aggregating to at least 10,000 MWh.

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.**
All three are correct. The MoU mandates **Prior Informed Consent from Gram Sabhas** (consistent with the Biological Diversity Act, 2002), provides for **joint IP ownership with equitable benefit-sharing** routed through National/State Biodiversity Funds and MoTA schemes, and targets **RET medicinal plant conservation** via tissue culture facilities and specialised seed banks.
