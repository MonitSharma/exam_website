## 1. Union Cabinet (infrastructure & logistics planning): **Integrated Transport and Logistics Authority (ITLA) approved as a Special Purpose Vehicle** (6 Oct)

**What / why it matters:** Cabinet approved a new body to end fragmented planning across transport agencies. It is meant to bring roads, railways, ports, shipping, civil aviation, inland waterways, coastal shipping, urban mobility and logistics under one planning, appraisal and monitoring lens — directly relevant to PM Gati Shakti-style multimodal integration and the National Logistics Policy, 2022.

**Key facts:**
- Prepares a **National Transport Master Plan** with a 10+ year horizon; checks sectoral and annual plans for consistency and multimodal integration.
- **Technical appraisal and monitoring of projects costing ₹500 crore or above.**
- Builds a **National Transport Data Repository** integrating GSTN, FASTag, Vahan and GPS datasets.
- Advises on updating the **National Logistics Policy, 2022**; supports capacity building and research.
- Administrative ministry and head of ITLA were not stated in the reports retrieved.

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (Infrastructure & Logistics); GS2 Governance (institutional design).

## 2. Union Cabinet (MSME / finance): **₹10,000 crore government commitment to the SME Growth Fund (SGF)** (6 Oct)

**What / why it matters:** Addresses the gap in *growth-stage equity* for small and medium enterprises that already have established operations but cannot get long-term equity from banks. Announced in Union Budget 2026-27; Cabinet has now cleared the commitment.

**Key facts:**
- Structured as an **Alternative Investment Fund (AIF)**; government's aggregate commitment ₹10,000 crore.
- **Direct equity investments** for capacity expansion, technology adoption, overseas market entry and acquisitions.
- **Majority of allocation to small and medium manufacturing enterprises**; priority to industrial clusters in **Tier-II and Tier-III cities**.
- Managing entity / ministry not named in the retrieved reports (not verified; do not assume SIDBI).

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (MSMEs, capital formation, manufacturing).

### Scheme-in-focus: **SME Growth Fund (SGF)**
- **Ministry:** Union Cabinet decision, Budget 2026-27 announcement (implementing agency not verified).
- **Objective:** Provide patient growth equity to scale SMEs into "future global champions".
- **Key features:** AIF route; ₹10,000 crore government commitment; direct equity; manufacturing-heavy allocation; Tier-II/III cluster priority.
- **Beneficiaries:** Viable, scalable small and medium enterprises, especially manufacturers.
- **Significance:** Fills the missing-middle financing gap, supports Make in India and regional job creation.

## 3. Ministry of Environment, Forest & Climate Change: **India–Japan Joint Crediting Mechanism (JCM) — Operational Manual launched** (30 Sep, reported 6 Oct)

**What / why it matters:** The JCM is a bilateral carbon-crediting arrangement under **Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement**, letting Japan finance low-carbon projects in India and take part of the resulting credits. The Operational Manual covers the full project cycle — from Project Idea Note to credit issuance — and makes India's first Article 6.2 framework practically usable.

**Key facts:**
- Memorandum of Cooperation: **7 August 2025**; Rules of Implementation adopted **8 June 2026**; Operational Manual: **30 September 2026**.
- Targets advanced low-carbon technologies and climate-finance mobilisation.
- Source note: dates are from a secondary PIB-summary site; Carbon Pulse independently reported the rules' adoption in June 2026 (PIB text not retrieved).

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment (carbon markets, climate finance); GS2 International Relations (Japan).

## 4. Chemicals sector: **India Chem 2026 curtain-raiser; BHAVYA Rasayan chemical parks** (event 22–24 Oct, Mumbai)

Outlay and implementing department differ across sources — not verified.

## 3 Prelims MCQs

**Q1.** With reference to the Integrated Transport and Logistics Authority (ITLA) approved by the Union Cabinet, consider the statements:
1. It is a Special Purpose Vehicle meant to integrate planning across modes of transport.
2. It will technically appraise and monitor infrastructure projects costing ₹500 crore or above.
3. It will build a National Transport Data Repository using datasets such as FASTag and Vahan.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q2.** Consider the following statements about the SME Growth Fund (SGF) cleared by the Cabinet in October 2026:
1. It is structured as an Alternative Investment Fund with a ₹10,000 crore government commitment.
2. It provides subsidised bank loans rather than equity.
3. A majority of its allocation is meant for small and medium manufacturing enterprises.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q3.** The India–Japan Joint Crediting Mechanism (JCM) is operated under which provision of the Paris Agreement?
(a) Article 2.1
(b) Article 4.2
(c) Article 6.2
(d) Article 9.1

## Answers
1. **(d)** All three are correct per the Cabinet decision: SPV, ₹500 crore appraisal threshold, and a National Transport Data Repository integrating GSTN, FASTag, Vahan and GPS data.
2. **(b)** SGF is an AIF with ₹10,000 crore commitment and a manufacturing-heavy allocation; statement 2 is wrong because it makes direct equity investments, not loans.
3. **(c)** Article 6.2 allows bilateral cooperative approaches and transfer of internationally transferred mitigation outcomes; the JCM is such an arrangement.
