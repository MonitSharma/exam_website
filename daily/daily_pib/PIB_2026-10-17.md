## 1. Ministry of Commerce & Industry (CGPDTM, DPIIT) with Ministry of Ayush: **Status Report on Ayush-related inventions — filings up ~8x in a decade** (16 Oct 2026)

- **What it is:** The Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs & Trademarks (CGPDTM, under DPIIT) compiled FY2014-15 to FY2025-26 data on patent applications in Ayush systems. The Ministry of Ayush publicised it; PIB headline: "Ayush Innovation Sees Eightfold Rise in Patent Filings".
- **Why it matters:** Shows growing IP-awareness in traditional medicine, and links to the debate on protecting traditional knowledge (TKDL, Section 3(p) of the Patents Act, WIPO GRATK treaty).
- **Key facts:**
  - Total **4,077 applications; 711 grants** (grant rate ~17%; many recent filings still pending).
  - Annual filings rose from **63 (FY15) to 590 (FY26)** — about 836% growth. Peak: 606 in FY2022-23; dip to 299 in FY2023-24.
  - Annual grants rose from 3 (FY16) to 204 (FY24). Post-COVID years (FY21–FY26) account for ~70% of filings and ~92% of grants.
  - Applicants: natural persons 2,113 (51.9%); educational institutions 995; corporates/others 700; small entities 144; startups 125. **Maharashtra** leads (744 filings, 142 grants).
  - Related, static background: **TKDL** (CSIR) is a database of traditional-knowledge formulations used by patent offices as prior-art evidence; **Section 3(p), Patents Act 1970** bars patents on traditional knowledge/mere aggregation of known properties; **WIPO GRATK Treaty** (adopted 24 May 2024) requires patent applicants to disclose country of origin/source of genetic resources and associated traditional knowledge.
- **Notebook:** GS3 Science & Tech / IPR; GS2 Health & Governance (Ayush).

## 2. NITI Aayog (Frontier Tech Hub): **Data Centre Evaluation Framework for States** (released 14 Oct 2026, New Delhi)

- **What it is:** A "decision-support tool" for States to evaluate data-centre investment proposals, prepared with inputs from the Ministry of Power and MeitY. It is explicitly **not an additional regulatory layer**.
- **Why it matters:** India's data-centre capacity is growing fast with AI demand; the framework ties approvals and incentives to power, water and sustainability readiness, addressing concerns on grid stress and water use.
- **Key facts:**
  - **Five pillars:** Power & Energy; Water & Cooling; Siting & Environmental Sustainability; Economic & Strategic Value; Policy, Governance & Implementation.
  - Recommends planning before approval (node/corridor-level assessment of power, water, fibre, land), outcome-linked (KPI-based) incentives with suspension triggers, a State-wise single-window Data Centre Approval Matrix, and standardised **PUE, WUE and CUE** reporting (BEE, BIS, MeitY).
  - Facilities above 20 MW expected to have automated demand-response readiness; water-balance studies over 20–30 years.
  - Capacity figures (≈375 MW in 2020 to ≈1.5 GW now) are from a secondary summary — treat as approximate.
- **Notebook:** GS3 Economy / Infrastructure / Sci-Tech (digital infrastructure), Environment; GS2 Governance (Centre–State, NITI Aayog's role).

## Prelims MCQs

**Q1.** With reference to the recent Status Report on Ayush-related inventions, consider the following statements:
1. The data were compiled by the Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs & Trademarks, which functions under DPIIT.
2. Annual patent filings in Ayush systems rose from 63 in FY2014-15 to 590 in FY2025-26.
3. Corporates account for the largest share of total filings among all applicant types.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q2.** Consider the following statements regarding the Data Centre Evaluation Framework recently released in India:
1. It was released by the NITI Aayog's Frontier Tech Hub.
2. It is a binding statutory regulation that States must enforce on all data-centre projects.
3. It is structured around five pillars including Power & Energy and Water & Cooling.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 1 and 2 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 2 and 3 only

**Q3.** With reference to protection of traditional knowledge in India, consider the following statements:
1. The Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL) is maintained by CSIR and is used by patent offices as prior-art evidence.
2. Section 3(p) of the Patents Act, 1970 bars patents on traditional knowledge or mere aggregation of known properties.
3. The WIPO treaty on Intellectual Property, Genetic Resources and Associated Traditional Knowledge (2024) requires disclosure of origin of genetic resources in patent applications.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

1. **(a)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Natural persons (2,113 filings, 51.9%) — not corporates (700) — are the largest applicant category.
2. **(c)** — Statements 1 and 3 are correct. The framework is a decision-support tool, explicitly not an additional regulatory layer.
3. **(d)** — All three are correct: TKDL (CSIR), Section 3(p) of the Patents Act, and the WIPO GRATK Treaty adopted 24 May 2024.
