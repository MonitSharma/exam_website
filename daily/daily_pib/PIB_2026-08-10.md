## 1. MoSPI releases the PLFS Quarterly Bulletin for April–June 2026

**Issuing body:** National Statistical Office (NSO), Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) | **Date:** 10 August 2026

**What it is.** The National Statistical Office has released the Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS) Quarterly Bulletin for the quarter April–June 2026. Since the survey redesign that took effect in **January 2025**, PLFS produces **both monthly and quarterly estimates for rural *and* urban India** — earlier, quarterly bulletins covered urban areas only and rural data came once a year. This is the labour-market series UPSC now expects candidates to be fluent in.

**Why it matters.** PLFS is the official source for India's employment/unemployment numbers. The quarterly bulletin uses the **Current Weekly Status (CWS)** approach — a person is counted as unemployed if she did not work even one hour on any day in the seven days preceding the survey but was seeking/available for work.

**Key facts to carry.**

- Three headline indicators: **LFPR** (labour force ÷ population), **WPR** (workers ÷ population), **UR** (unemployed ÷ labour force).
- Two measurement approaches in PLFS: **Usual Status (ps+ss)** — 365-day reference; **Current Weekly Status (CWS)** — 7-day reference. Monthly and quarterly bulletins use CWS.
- Latest verified monthly benchmark (June 2026 bulletin, released July 2026): overall **UR 5.5%**, **LFPR 54.4%**, **WPR 51.4%** for persons aged 15+. Rural UR **5.0%**, urban UR **6.6%** (down from 7.1% in June 2025). Female LFPR (15+) **32.7%**, up 0.7 percentage point year-on-year.
- *Caveat:* the detailed April–June 2026 quarterly tables were not independently retrievable at the time of writing; treat the June-2026 monthly figures above as the confirmed reference point and check the MoSPI press note for the quarterly numbers.

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (employment, official statistics) → also Reports & Indices.

## 2. Asiatic lion population rises from 523 (2015) to 891 (2025)

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (Union Minister Shri Bhupender Yadav) | **Date:** 10 August 2026

**What it is.** MoEFCC has placed on record that India's Asiatic lion (*Panthera leo persica*) population has climbed from **523 in 2015** to **891 in 2025**, crediting forest staff, conservationists and local communities. The figure comes from the **16th Asiatic Lion Population Estimation**, conducted in **May 2025**.

**Why it matters.** The Asiatic lion is a single-population species — the entire wild global population lives in and around the **Gir landscape in Saurashtra, Gujarat**. That makes it acutely vulnerable to a single epidemic or catastrophe, which is exactly why range expansion beyond Gir is the conservation story here, and why the long-pending Supreme Court direction on translocating some lions to **Kuno (Madhya Pradesh)** keeps resurfacing.

**Key facts to carry.**

- 2020 estimate: **674**. 2025 estimate: **891** — a rise of about **32%** in five years.
- The 2025 census ran **10–13 May 2025** across roughly **35,000 sq km**, covering **58 talukas in 11 districts** of Gujarat.
- Composition: **196 adult males, 330 adult females, 140 sub-adults, 225 cubs**. Adult females up ~27% from 260 (2020).
- **384 lions** inside protected forest/sanctuary areas; lions in non-forested areas rose from **340 (2020) to 507 (2025)** — i.e., a majority now live outside notified protected areas, in a human-dominated landscape.
- Gir National Park with the adjoining Gir and Pania sanctuaries hold roughly **394 lions** as the core population.
- **Project Lion** was announced by the Prime Minister on **15 August 2020**; the species' range has expanded from about 30,000 sq km (2020) to nearly 35,000 sq km.
- IUCN status: **Endangered**; Schedule I of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972; CITES Appendix I.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment (species conservation, protected areas).

## 3. TRAI begins phase-wise rollout of the 1601-series for utilities, courier and logistics

**Issuing body:** Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI), Ministry of Communications | **Date:** 10 August 2026

**What it is.** TRAI has issued a Direction taking the **1601 numbering series** — until now reserved for RBI/SEBI/IRDAI/PFRDA-regulated financial entities — beyond BFSI, onboarding entities from **utilities, courier and logistics** sectors in a phased manner for their service and transactional voice calls.

**Why it matters.** This is a consumer-protection and financial-fraud measure dressed as a numbering decision. A caller-ID prefix that only verified entities can use lets a subscriber tell a genuine bank/electricity-board/courier call from an impersonation attempt. It sits alongside 140-series (promotional calls) and the Sanchar Saathi ecosystem as India's answer to spoofed-caller fraud.

**Key facts to carry.**

- **140-series** → promotional/telemarketing calls. **1600/1601-series** → service and transactional calls only (OTPs, transaction alerts, policy renewals, delivery updates).
- 1600-series numbers are **outgoing-only**; customers cannot call back on them.
- Numbers are allocated **directly to the eligible entity**, not to aggregators or intermediaries, after verification by the access provider — this is what makes the series a trust signal.
- TRAI is a statutory regulator under the **TRAI Act, 1997**; appeals from its decisions lie to **TDSAT**.

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (regulatory bodies, consumer protection); GS3 Internal Security (cyber/financial fraud).

## 4. APEDA facilitates India's first sea-route export of GI-tagged Mithila Makhana

**Issuing body:** APEDA, Department of Commerce, Ministry of Commerce and Industry | **Date:** 9–10 August 2026

**What it is.** APEDA has enabled the **first-ever commercial shipment of GI-tagged Mithila Makhana (foxnut) by sea** — **18 metric tonnes in seven containers** from Bihar to **Australia**. Makhana had so far travelled almost entirely by air; moving to sea freight cuts logistics cost sharply and opens up bulk-volume markets.

**Why it matters.** A textbook case of GI + logistics + farmer-price linkage. The consignment was procured **directly from farmers in Darbhanga**, who received roughly **18% above the prevailing market price** — the classic argument for shortening the export value chain.

**Key facts to carry.**

- Flagged off from the **Integrated Irradiation-cum-Packhouse at Bihta, Patna**, by Bihar's Agriculture Minister Shri Vijay Kumar Sinha.
- Exporter: **NIAD Green**; logistics by **Jitban Supply Chain Pvt Ltd**, a startup supported under APEDA's **BHARATI** initiative (Bharat's Hub for Agritech, Resilience, Advancement and Incubation for Export Innovation).
- **Bihar accounts for nearly 85% of India's makhana production.** *Mithila Makhana* received its GI tag in 2022.
- In **FY 2025-26**, India exported over **7,000 MT** of makhana and value-added makhana products to **more than 20 destinations**, including the USA, West Asia and Africa.
- APEDA is a statutory body under the **APEDA Act, 1985**, and handles scheduled agri and processed-food exports; **MPEDA** handles marine products.

**Notebook:** GS3 Agriculture (GI, agri-exports, value chains); GS3 Economy (trade).

## 5. Chief of the Naval Staff on a four-day official visit to Mauritius

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Defence / Indian Navy | **Date:** 10 August 2026

**What it is.** Chief of the Naval Staff **Admiral Krishna Swaminathan** is on a four-day official visit to **Mauritius (10–13 August 2026)** to consolidate the bilateral maritime partnership — talks with the Mauritian leadership on maritime security, defence cooperation, capacity building and military collaboration.

**Why it matters.** Mauritius is a pivot of India's Western Indian Ocean posture and one of the earliest beneficiaries of the **SAGAR** vision, now framed under **MAHASAGAR** (Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions). India provides EEZ surveillance, hydrographic support and platforms to a country whose EEZ vastly exceeds its land area.

**Key facts to carry.**

- Existing cooperation: joint **EEZ surveillance** with the **National Coast Guard of Mauritius**, training exchanges, port visits and **hydrographic surveys**.
- Mauritius participates in India-led maritime engagements — **IONS** (Indian Ocean Naval Symposium), **MILAN**, the International Fleet Review, the **Goa Maritime Conclave**, **IOS SAGAR** and **Exercise AIKEYME**.
- Related anchors for the region: the **Agalega** facilities, India's role in the **Colombo Security Conclave**, and the Indian Ocean Commission (India is an observer).

**Notebook:** GS2 International Relations (India and its neighbourhood, Indian Ocean); GS3 Internal Security (maritime security).

## 6. Scheme-in-focus: Jiyo Parsi

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Minority Affairs | **Date:** 10 August 2026 (release on medical assistance, child care and elderly care components)

**Name & ministry.** **Jiyo Parsi** — a **central sector scheme** of the Ministry of Minority Affairs, launched in **2013-14**.

**Objective.** To arrest and reverse the decline in the population of the **Parsi (Zoroastrian)** community in India, a notified minority under the **National Commission for Minorities Act, 1992**.

**Key features (three components).**

1. **Medical Assistance** — financial assistance to Parsi couples for infertility treatment, pregnancy complications and new-born complications; available where **annual family income is up to ₹30 lakh**.
2. **Health of Community** — assistance towards **care of children and of dependent elderly** family members; available where **annual family income is up to ₹15 lakh**.
3. **Advocacy** — counselling, outreach and awareness programmes.

**Beneficiaries / coverage.** Since inception the scheme has facilitated the birth of **more than 490 Parsi children** and disbursed **₹35.05 crore** in financial assistance to Parsi couples. An **IIPS (International Institute for Population Sciences)** evaluation found near-universal acknowledgement of its usefulness among respondents; continuation is under consideration for the next Finance Commission cycle.

**Significance.** The Parsi population fell from about **1,14,000 in 1941 to 57,264 in the 2011 Census** — one of the sharpest demographic declines of any Indian community, driven by late marriage, low fertility, high out-migration and community endogamy rules. Jiyo Parsi is a rare instance of an Indian welfare scheme explicitly designed as **pro-natalist policy for a micro-minority**, which makes it a good example for questions on minority rights, demographic policy and Article 29/30 debates.

**Notebook:** GS1 Society (demography, minorities); GS2 Governance (welfare schemes for minorities).

## Answers

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.**
Statement 1 is correct: the 2025 estimation put the population at 891, against 674 in 2020. Statement 2 is incorrect — only about 384 lions were recorded inside protected forest/sanctuary areas, while 507 were in non-forested, largely human-dominated areas; the majority now live *outside* notified protected areas, which is the central conservation challenge. Statement 3 is correct: Project Lion was announced on 15 August 2020.

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.**
APEDA was set up under the APEDA Act, 1985, and functions under the Department of Commerce, Ministry of Commerce and Industry (statement 1 correct). Mithila Makhana is a GI-tagged product (statement 2 correct). Bihar contributes roughly 85% of national makhana output (statement 3 correct). The August 2026 consignment was 18 MT in seven containers, shipped to Australia.

**Q3 — (b).**
Jiyo Parsi is a central sector scheme of the **Ministry of Minority Affairs**, launched in 2013-14 to arrest the decline of the Parsi (Zoroastrian) population, which fell from about 1,14,000 in 1941 to 57,264 in the 2011 Census. It has three components — Medical Assistance, Health of Community, and Advocacy — and has supported the birth of over 490 Parsi children with ₹35.05 crore in assistance.
