## 1. India's first direct commercial container freight train reaches Nepal

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Railways — Northeast Frontier Railway (NFR) with **Container Corporation of India (CONCOR)**; PIB release of 28 July 2026 on the movement completed on **20 July 2026**.

The first-ever **direct commercial container freight train** from an Indian port to Nepal ran from **Kolkata (Syama Prasad Mookerjee Port)** to the **Nepal Customs Yard at Biratnagar**, using the **Jogbani–Biratnagar broad-gauge cross-border rail link**. Crucially, the cargo moved **without transshipment at the border** — a first for India–Nepal rail trade.

**Key facts**

- Consignment: **40 forty-foot containers of canola** (bulk edible-oil feedstock), hauled by CONCOR.
- Operates under the **revised India–Nepal Rail Services Agreement / Rail Transit framework**, given effect by a **Letter of Exchange signed on 13 November 2025**, which permits direct movement of containerised and bulk cargo to Biratnagar.
- Expected to cut **logistics costs by 20–30%** for Nepali industry and materially reduce transit time.
- Biratnagar is served by an **Integrated Check Post (ICP)** — one of the India-funded ICPs on the Nepal border (Birgunj, Biratnagar, Bhairahawa, Nepalgunj).

**Why it matters:** A rare item that sits in *both* GS2 and GS3. It is concrete evidence of India's **"Neighbourhood First"** delivering on connectivity rather than declarations, and of the shift from *transit rights on paper* to *seamless multimodal logistics*. Pair it with the India–Nepal Treaty of Trade and Treaty of Transit, and with the landlocked-country access question under **Article V of GATT (freedom of transit)**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India–Nepal, Neighbourhood First, connectivity diplomacy) · GS3 — Infrastructure/Economy (multimodal logistics, PM Gati Shakti, port-hinterland linkage).

## 2. Centre imposes stock holding limits on sugar dealers (1 August – 30 November 2026)

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution — **Department of Food and Public Distribution (DFPD)**; order announced 28 July 2026.

To curb hoarding and speculative "paper trade" that was creating an **artificial perception of scarcity**, the Centre has imposed temporary stock limits on sugar **dealers** (not mills) across the country.

**Key facts**

- **Limit:** no dealer may hold more than **4,000 quintals** of sugar at any time at any location; **no stock may be retained beyond 30 days** from the date of receipt.
- **Validity:** **1 August 2026 to 30 November 2026** — i.e. covering the lean pre-crushing months before the new sugar season.
- **Legal basis:** issued under **Section 3 of the Essential Commodities Act, 1955**, read with **Clauses 7 and 16 of the Sugar (Control) Order, 2025**.
- **Compliance:** all dealers must declare stocks and update stock positions **weekly** on the DFPD online portal.
- Stated trigger: hoarding by traders/intermediaries and speculative transactions **without actual physical movement of sugar from mills**.

**Why it matters:** A live, dated illustration of how the **Essential Commodities Act, 1955** is actually used — a standing prelims favourite. Note the policy arc: the 2020 amendment sought to deregulate stock limits except in "extraordinary circumstances" (war, famine, extraordinary price rise, natural calamity), the farm-law repeal of 2021 restored the earlier position, and orders like this show the ECA remains the Centre's primary price-stabilisation instrument alongside buffer stocks and export/import calibration.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (price stabilisation, food management, agricultural marketing) · GS2 — Governance (regulatory instruments, consumer protection).

## 3. Amritsar becomes India's second "spoke" airport under the Hub & Spoke international framework

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Civil Aviation; inaugurated 28 July 2026.

The Ministry inaugurated **Hub & Spoke international flight operations from Amritsar**, making it the **second airport after Varanasi** to join India's Hub & Spoke framework for international passenger movement. Passengers can now complete **check-in, baggage drop, customs and immigration locally at Amritsar** and connect onward overseas via **Delhi** on a single ticket.

**Key facts**

- Amritsar's effective global reach expands from **7 direct international routes to over 20 international destinations** through single check-in ticketing.
- Design goal: retain Indian outbound traffic within Indian hubs instead of ceding it to **Dubai, Doha, Singapore and Bangkok**.
- Ministry plans roughly **4 primary international hubs and about 40 regional spoke airports** (reported target of ~44 airports overall).
- Ministry studies cited: aviation hub development could add about **0.4 million direct and indirect jobs and USD 30 billion** to the economy **by 2030**; and by **2047**, about **16 million jobs and nearly USD 1.4 trillion** cumulatively.

**Why it matters:** This is the operational face of the **National Civil Aviation Policy** ambition to make India a global aviation hub, and it complements **UDAN/RCS-Modified UDAN** (which builds domestic spokes). The bottleneck it addresses is not runway capacity but **customs/immigration processing at origin** — a good example of "soft infrastructure" reform delivering hard economic gains.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Infrastructure & Economy (civil aviation, transport connectivity, services exports).

## 4. Health Ministry observes World Hepatitis Day 2026; NVHCP completes eight years

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Health and Family Welfare; 28 July 2026. Keynote by **Anupriya Patel**, MoS (Health & Family Welfare and Chemicals & Fertilizers).

Marking **World Hepatitis Day (28 July)** under the global theme **"Hepatitis: Let's Break It Down"**, the Ministry took stock of eight years of the **National Viral Hepatitis Control Programme (NVHCP)**, launched on World Hepatitis Day **2018** under the National Health Mission.

**Key facts**

- **Over 21 crore beneficiaries screened**; **more than 6 lakh patients** given **free treatment**.
- Target of **one treatment centre in every district surpassed** — **1,153 treatment centres**, including **70+ specialised treatment sites** for advanced care.
- Goal: eliminate **viral hepatitis as a public health threat by 2030**, aligned with **SDG Target 3.3**.
- Framing used: **"Hepatitis-Mukt Bharat."**
- Context: India is among the **top 10 countries** contributing to the global hepatitis B burden.

**Scheme-in-focus — National Viral Hepatitis Control Programme (NVHCP)**

- **Ministry:** Health & Family Welfare, implemented under the **National Health Mission**; launched 28 July 2018.
- **Objective:** eliminate hepatitis C by 2030 and achieve significant reduction in hepatitis B infection/mortality, in line with SDG 3.3.
- **Key features:** (i) free screening, diagnosis and treatment across the continuum of care; (ii) universal **hepatitis B birth-dose vaccination** and immunisation of health workers; (iii) district-level treatment centres plus model treatment centres for complicated cases; (iv) emphasis on **safe injection practices, blood safety and infection control**.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** nationwide and free of cost at public facilities; 21+ crore screened to date.
- **Significance:** one of the largest publicly funded hepatitis elimination programmes globally, and a strong Mains example of **preventive + curative integration** within NHM.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance/Social Justice (health, SDG delivery, NHM) · GS3 — Science & Technology (public health, vaccination).

## 5. Raksha Mantri unveils two policy reforms for Indian Coast Guard personnel and their families

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Defence; announced by **Raksha Mantri Rajnath Singh** at **Kartavya Bhavan-2, New Delhi**, 28 July 2026.

Two distinct reforms were approved for the **Indian Coast Guard (ICG)** — one on **gender parity in induction and service conditions**, the other on **ex-gratia for families of personnel missing at sea**.

**Key facts**

- **Reform 1 — gender-neutral induction:** women candidates to be inducted into both **Short Service Appointments (SSA)** and **Permanent Appointments (PA)** **at par with male counterparts**, with equal opportunities for career progression **from the ab-initio training stage** onwards.
- **Reform 2 — ex-gratia streamlining:** enables a **"presumed dead" declaration** for personnel reported missing at sea while on duty, so that families are not made to wait through prolonged administrative processes for financial assistance.
- Static hooks: the ICG was formally established under the **Coast Guard Act, 1978** (raised 1 February 1977), functions under the **Ministry of Defence**, and is headed by the **Director General, Indian Coast Guard**. Its mandate covers maritime search and rescue, marine environment protection, anti-smuggling and coastal security.

**Why it matters:** Feeds two different Mains threads — **women in the armed forces** (following the Supreme Court's permanent-commission jurisprudence and the opening of the NDA to women) and **welfare of uniformed personnel**. The "presumed dead" reform is a neat administrative-reform example: the fix is procedural, not financial.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security/Defence (maritime security, coastal security architecture) · GS2 — Governance & Society (gender parity in state institutions, administrative reform).

## 6. Ministry of Tourism signs MoU with Air India to promote India as a global tourism destination

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Tourism and **Air India Limited**; signed in New Delhi on 28 July 2026 in the presence of **Gajendra Singh Shekhawat**, Union Minister for Tourism and Culture.

A strategic framework to market India abroad under the **Incredible India** brand, pairing the Ministry's destination-promotion machinery with Air India's domestic and international network.

**Key facts**

- Stated aims: raise India's tourism visibility, strengthen **inbound tourism**, and position India as a **global transit and travel hub**.
- Activities: **joint global promotional campaigns** under Incredible India; **co-branded marketing and destination storytelling** across digital and offline platforms; tourism content distributed through Air India customer touchpoints including **in-flight entertainment**.
- Nature of the MoU: **non-commercial, non-binding and non-exclusive**, with **no financial commitment** by either party.
- **Duration:** two years initially, extendable by one year by mutual agreement.
- Officials present: Bhuvnesh Kumar (Secretary, Tourism), Suman Billa (Additional Secretary & DG-Tourism), Harikishore S (Joint Secretary), and Air India's Nipun Aggarwal (CCO) and P. Balaji.

**Why it matters:** Read alongside item 3 — both are pieces of the same strategy of converting India from a *destination* into a *transit hub*. Tourism is a high-employment-elasticity services sector, and inbound tourism is a **services export** that earns foreign exchange. Useful GS3 economy material on services-led growth.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (services sector, tourism, foreign exchange earnings) · GS2 — Governance (public–private partnership with a PSU-turned-private carrier).

**Q3.** The National Viral Hepatitis Control Programme (NVHCP), which completed eight years in July 2026, is best described as:

(a) A World Health Organization–funded vertical programme implemented directly by district hospitals, independent of the National Health Mission
(b) A programme under the National Health Mission providing free screening, diagnosis and treatment, aiming to eliminate viral hepatitis as a public health threat by 2030 in line with SDG Target 3.3
(c) A component of Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY that reimburses hepatitis treatment only for hospitalised beneficiaries
(d) A research programme of the Indian Council of Medical Research for developing indigenous hepatitis vaccines

## Answers

**Q1 — (a).** The order was issued under **Section 3 of the Essential Commodities Act, 1955**, read with Clauses 7 and 16 of the **Sugar (Control) Order, 2025**, capping dealer holdings at **4,000 quintals** per location with a **30-day** retention limit, valid **1 August – 30 November 2026**. It applies to **dealers**, not mills — statement 3 is incorrect.

**Q2 — (a).** Amritsar was inaugurated on **28 July 2026** as the **second spoke airport after Varanasi**, expanding its reach from 7 direct routes to **20+ international destinations** via Delhi with single check-in ticketing and local customs/immigration clearance. The framework is run by the **Ministry of Civil Aviation**, not the MEA — statement 3 is incorrect.

**Q3 — (b).** NVHCP was launched on **World Hepatitis Day, 28 July 2018** under the **National Health Mission**, Ministry of Health and Family Welfare. It offers **free** screening, diagnosis and treatment; **21+ crore** people have been screened and **6 lakh+** treated, with **1,153 treatment centres** (including 70+ specialised sites). Its goal is elimination of viral hepatitis as a public health threat by **2030**, aligned with **SDG Target 3.3**.
