## 1. PM inaugurates 'Param Pragya' — AI-enabled supercomputing facility at IIT Delhi's Sonipat campus

**Issuing body:** Prime Minister's Office / Ministry of Education / IIT Delhi (under the National Supercomputing Mission ecosystem)

**What it is:** The Prime Minister, as Chief Guest at the **57th Convocation of IIT Delhi**, inaugurated **'Param Pragya'**, an **Artificial Intelligence–enabled high-performance computing (HPC) facility** at the institute's **Sonipat campus in Haryana**. The facility is meant to anchor IIT Delhi's work in AI, data science, advanced computing and interdisciplinary research.

**Why it matters:** The "Param" naming line places the facility in the lineage of India's indigenous supercomputing programme, which began with **PARAM 8000 (1991, C-DAC)** and now runs through the **National Supercomputing Mission (NSM)**. Academic-campus HPC is the bottleneck for large-model AI research in India — putting a Param-class machine on a university campus, rather than only in a national lab, is the policy shift worth noting.

**Key facts:**

- Inaugurated at the **57th IIT Delhi Convocation**; **over 3,000 students** graduated at the ceremony.
- Located at IIT Delhi's **Sonipat (Haryana) campus**, not the main Hauz Khas campus.
- The PM conferred the **President's Gold Medal**, **Shankar Dayal Sharma Gold Medal**, **Director's Gold Medal** and **Perfect Ten Gold Medals**.
- **National Supercomputing Mission** context: steered jointly by **MeitY** and the **Department of Science and Technology (DST)**, implemented by **C-DAC** and **IISc Bengaluru**; PARAM-series systems have been deployed across IITs, IISERs, NITs and national labs.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (indigenous computing, AI infrastructure); GS2 — Governance (higher-education research capacity).

## 2. 'Prakriti Gyan Dham' — India's first eco-educational hub, dedicated by CSIR-NBRI at Lucknow

**Issuing ministry:** Ministry of Science and Technology (MoS&T) — Union MoS (Independent Charge) Dr Jitendra Singh; developed by **CSIR–National Botanical Research Institute (CSIR-NBRI)**

**What it is:** A **first-of-its-kind eco-educational hub** dedicated to the nation at the **Maharishi Parashar Bioresource Centre (MPBC), Banthra, Lucknow (Uttar Pradesh)**. It combines botanical heritage, biodiversity conservation, environmental education and experiential learning in one public-facing campus — an attempt to close the "science–society" gap that CSIR labs are often criticised for.

**Why it matters:** This is a rare example of a national research laboratory converting its germplasm and conservation assets into a **public environmental-education asset**, and simultaneously routing its plant-based technologies to a **women's self-help-group network** for livelihood generation. Both threads — *ex situ* conservation of RET (Rare, Endangered and Threatened) plants, and CSIR-to-SHG technology transfer — are directly usable in GS3 Environment and GS2 Governance answers.

**Key facts / figures:**

- **Bamboosteum** with **43 indigenous and exotic bamboo species**.
- **Vibhav Enclave — Threatened Plants of India Garden**, a dedicated space for rare, endangered and threatened species.
- Interpretive trails and gardens including the **PAaVan Path** and an **Indian Heritage Garden**, with **QR-enabled audio** guidance.
- **Four plant-based technologies transferred to the Kudumbashree State Poverty Eradication Mission, Kerala** — Herbal Anti-Dandruff Hair Care Oil, *Frotus* (lotus-inspired perfume), *Frotus: Rudra* perfume, and a Herbal Nano Serum — aimed at women-led enterprise.
- A **low-cost photobioreactor** technology was transferred to a Lucknow-based private firm; a **documentary on India's Heritage Gardens** was released; an herbal formulation developed with Himalaya was launched.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Environment (biodiversity, RET species, *ex situ* conservation) and Science & Technology (CSIR technology transfer); GS2 — Governance (SHG-linked livelihoods).

## 3. NeGD and AAERI launch 'AAERI Verify' — DigiLocker-based credential verification for students going to Australia

**Issuing ministry:** Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) — **National e-Governance Division (NeGD)**, with the **Association of Australian Education Representatives in India (AAERI)**

**What it is:** A **digital, consent-based document-verification platform** launched at the AAERI Annual Convention in New Delhi. It is **integrated with DigiLocker**, so Australian universities can — with the student's explicit consent — pull **source-verified, digitally signed** academic, identity and financial records directly from the issuing authority instead of relying on scanned paper.

**Why it matters:** This is **Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) being exported as a cross-border trust layer**. India's DPI story so far has been domestic (Aadhaar, UPI, DigiLocker) or bilateral in payments; using DigiLocker to authenticate credentials for a *foreign* regulator/university system is a new use-case, and it directly addresses the reputational problem of forged marksheets and inflated financial statements in the international student pipeline.

**Key facts / figures:**

- **Two-stage verification**: an initial check before the university application, then a **post-admission verification of the sponsor's financial details**.
- Cuts manual verification from roughly **5–9 days to about 120–240 seconds**.
- **DigiLocker**: a flagship DPI initiative of **MeitY's NeGD** under the **Digital India** programme, with **over 72 crore registered users**; enables issuance, storage, sharing and real-time verification of digitally signed documents.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance (e-governance, DPI, service delivery) and International Relations (education mobility, India–Australia); GS3 — Science & Technology (digital infrastructure).

## 4. DPIIT signs five MoUs with industry to deepen the startup ecosystem

**Issuing ministry:** Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), Ministry of Commerce and Industry

**What it is:** DPIIT signed **five strategic MoUs** — with **Cashfree Payments, Vultr India, Cars24 Services, Darwin Dynamics LLP** and the **Council for Startup India (CSI)** — to improve recognised startups' access to digital infrastructure, technology, mentorship, capital and global markets.

**Why it matters:** The Startup India policy has shifted from *recognition and tax benefits* to *brokered access* — the State acting as a convenor that negotiates infrastructure and mentorship terms on behalf of startups rather than funding them directly. Note the explicit **Tier-II, Tier-III and rural** emphasis: startup activity in India remains heavily concentrated in a handful of metros, and this is the stated corrective.

**Key facts:**

- **Cashfree Payments** — digital payments stack for startups; **Vultr India** — cloud/compute infrastructure; **Cars24** — AI, skilling, mentorship and investment in mobility innovation.
- **Darwin Dynamics LLP** and the **Council for Startup India** — entrepreneurship expansion into **Tier-II, Tier-III and rural regions**, plus mentorship, capacity building, investment readiness, leadership development and global market access.
- Sits alongside DPIIT's existing Startup India instruments: the **Fund of Funds for Startups (FFS)**, the **Startup India Seed Fund Scheme (SISFS)** and the **Credit Guarantee Scheme for Startups (CGSS)**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (entrepreneurship, industrial policy, regional dispersal of growth).

## 5. V.O. Chidambaranar Port becomes India's first port with ISO 50001:2018 energy-management certification

**Issuing body:** V.O. Chidambaranar (VOC) Port Authority, Thoothukudi (Tamil Nadu) — Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways

**What it is:** VOC Port Authority received **ISO 50001:2018 certification for its Energy Management System (EnMS)** from the **Indian Register Quality Systems (IRQS)** — the **first Indian port** to do so. Separately, **Bureau Veritas** recognised it as India's first major port to be **Scope-2 emission-free**.

**Why it matters:** Ports are among the hardest infrastructure assets to decarbonise, and the **Maritime Amrit Kaal Vision 2047** / **Harit Sagar** green-port guidelines set targets that have so far lacked verified, third-party-audited demonstrations. VOC Port supplies a measurable template. Note the precise scope of the claim — **Scope-2**, i.e. purchased electricity — not Scope-1 (direct combustion, e.g. tugs and cargo handling equipment) or Scope-3.

**Key facts / figures:**

- **Renewables met 93.89% of the port's total electricity consumption in FY 2025-26**.
- **Net carbon emissions cut by about 45%**.
- **Scope-2 emissions** = *indirect* greenhouse-gas emissions from **purchased** electricity, steam, heating or cooling. A port is Scope-2 emission-free when renewable generation attributable to it exceeds its total electricity consumption in the period.
- **ISO 50001:2018** is the international standard for establishing, implementing, maintaining and continually improving an EnMS. **ISO** was established in **1947**, headquartered in **Geneva**.
- VOC Port is one of India's **major ports** (governed by the **Major Port Authorities Act, 2021**), located at Thoothukudi on the Gulf of Mannar coast.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Environment (decarbonisation, emission scopes) and Infrastructure (ports, Maritime Vision 2047).

## 6. Scheme-in-focus (UPDATED): PM E-DRIVE — Ministry of Heavy Industries amends the electric two-wheeler incentive

**Name:** **PM E-DRIVE** — Prime Minister's **E**lectric **D**rive **R**evolution in **I**nnovative **V**ehicle **E**nhancement Scheme

**Ministry:** Ministry of Heavy Industries (MHI)

**Objective:** Accelerate electric vehicle adoption, build out public charging infrastructure, and grow the domestic EV manufacturing ecosystem — the successor framework to FAME-II.

**What's new (August 2026):** MHI amended the scheme's incentive structure for **electric two-wheelers (e-2W)**, fixing the demand incentive at **₹2,500 per kWh of battery capacity, capped at ₹5,000 per vehicle**, for the period **April 2025 – March 2028**. This is the **halved, second-phase rate**: the first-year rate was ₹5,000/kWh with a ₹10,000 cap. Reporting also indicates the **e-2W demand-incentive window closed on 31 July 2026** after an extension from the original 31 March 2026 date — aspirants should treat the *taper design* as the examinable point, and confirm the current live status on the MHI PM E-DRIVE portal.

**Key features (3–4):**

1. **Tapering demand incentive** tied to battery capacity (₹/kWh) with an absolute per-vehicle cap — deliberately front-loaded, so early adopters get more and the subsidy withdraws as the market matures.
2. **Vehicle-segment coverage** across e-2W, **e-3W**, **e-ambulances**, **e-trucks** and **e-buses**, plus **upgradation of testing agencies**.
3. **Charging infrastructure** support for public EV charging stations — the component that has lagged most sharply in implementation.
4. **Aadhaar-authenticated e-voucher** mechanism for claiming the demand incentive at the point of purchase.

**Beneficiaries / coverage:** Reported support covers roughly **24.79 lakh electric two-wheelers**, **3.16 lakh e-three-wheelers** and **14,028 e-buses**. The scheme was **notified on 29 September 2024**, initially for two years, with implementation subsequently extended to **31 March 2028**.

**Significance:** PM E-DRIVE is the clearest live example of a **sunset-designed subsidy** — a demand incentive built to shrink on a pre-announced schedule rather than continue indefinitely. Useful as a GS3 case study on subsidy rationalisation, and as a counterpoint on why charging-infrastructure rollout has not kept pace with vehicle uptake.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (subsidy design, industrial policy) and Environment (transport decarbonisation).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to **'Param Pragya'**, recently in the news, consider the following statements:

1. It is an Artificial Intelligence–enabled high-performance computing facility inaugurated at the Sonipat campus of IIT Delhi.
2. The National Supercomputing Mission, under which India's PARAM-series systems are deployed, is steered jointly by the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology and the Department of Science and Technology.
3. It is India's first supercomputer to be installed outside a national laboratory.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q2.** Consider the following statements about **'AAERI Verify'**, recently launched:

1. It is a document-verification platform integrated with DigiLocker for students applying to Australian universities.
2. It has been launched by the National e-Governance Division under the Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology.
3. Verification under the platform takes place without requiring the student's consent, to prevent tampering.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 1 and 2 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** The **V.O. Chidambaranar Port Authority** was recently in the news for becoming India's first port to be recognised as **"Scope-2 emission-free"**. In greenhouse-gas accounting, **Scope-2 emissions** refer to:

(a) Direct emissions from sources owned or controlled by the organisation, such as fuel burnt in its own vehicles and boilers
(b) Indirect emissions arising from the generation of purchased electricity, steam, heating or cooling consumed by the organisation
(c) All indirect emissions occurring in the organisation's value chain, including those of suppliers and customers
(d) Emissions of short-lived climate pollutants such as black carbon and methane only

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
Statement 1 is correct: Param Pragya is an AI-enabled HPC facility at IIT Delhi's Sonipat campus in Haryana, inaugurated at the institute's 57th Convocation. Statement 2 is correct: the National Supercomputing Mission is jointly steered by MeitY and DST, and implemented by C-DAC and IISc Bengaluru. Statement 3 is incorrect: PARAM-series systems have been deployed on academic campuses (IITs, IISERs, NITs) for years under NSM, so this is not the first such installation.

**Q2 — (b) 1 and 2 only.**
Statements 1 and 2 are correct — AAERI Verify is a DigiLocker-integrated platform launched by NeGD (MeitY) with the Association of Australian Education Representatives in India. Statement 3 is incorrect: the platform is explicitly **consent-based** — Australian universities access records only with the student's explicit consent, which is the core privacy safeguard of the design.

**Q3 — (b).**
Scope-1 covers direct emissions from owned or controlled sources (option a); Scope-2 covers indirect emissions from **purchased** energy — electricity, steam, heating, cooling (option b, correct); Scope-3 covers all other value-chain emissions (option c). Option (d) misdescribes the classification entirely. VOC Port met **93.89%** of its FY 2025-26 electricity needs from renewables and cut net carbon emissions by about **45%**.
