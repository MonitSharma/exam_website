# Daily PIB Briefing — 5 August 2026

## 1. Parliament passes the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026 — apex court strength goes from 34 to 38

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Law and Justice (Department of Justice) | Moved by Union Minister Arjun Ram Meghwal

The Lok Sabha passed the **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026**, which raises the sanctioned strength of the Supreme Court. The Bill amends the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act, 1956 to increase the number of judges *other than the Chief Justice of India* from **33 to 37** — taking the total, including the CJI, from **34 to 38**. It gives legislative backing to a four-judge increase already effected through an **Ordinance promulgated on 16 May 2026**, and the amended provision takes effect **retrospectively from 16 May 2026**.

**Why it matters:** Article 124(1) fixes the Supreme Court at the CJI "and such number of other Judges as Parliament may by law prescribe" — so the strength is a matter of ordinary legislation, not constitutional amendment. The strength has risen in steps: 8 (1950) → 11 (1956) → 14 (1960) → 18 (1978) → 26 (1986) → 31 (2009) → 34 (2019) → **38 (2026)**. The stated driver is the persistent gap between institution and disposal of cases (pendency).

**Key facts:** Parent Act — Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act, 1956; constitutional hook — Article 124(1); ordinance route used under Article 123; retrospective effect from 16 May 2026.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Judiciary; Parliament & Legislation; Ordinance-making power)

## 2. PM E-DRIVE: 26.54 lakh EVs supported, but zero public chargers installed so far

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Heavy Industries (Lok Sabha written replies, 4 August 2026)

In replies tabled in Parliament, the Ministry of Heavy Industries disclosed the running score on India's flagship EV-adoption scheme, **PM E-DRIVE (PM Electric Drive Revolution in Innovative Vehicle Enhancement)**. Cumulative demand incentives have supported the sale of about **26.54 lakh electric vehicles** as of July 2026, against a total scheme outlay of **₹10,900 crore**, of which roughly **₹2,322 crore** has been utilised. Of the **₹2,000 crore** earmarked for **Electric Vehicle Public Charging Stations (EVPCS)**, **6,562 chargers have been sanctioned — none commissioned yet**.

On the bus side, only **523 e-buses** had been deployed under the **PM-eBus Sewa–Payment Security Mechanism (PSM)** scheme as on 10 July 2026; ₹4,391 crore has been provided under the PSM to back procurement of **14,028 e-buses** by State Transport Undertakings.

**Why it matters:** A textbook "outlay vs outcome" case for GS3 — demand-side subsidy uptake is running well ahead of supply-side charging infrastructure, which is the classic chicken-and-egg constraint on EV penetration. Also useful for the answer-writing point that PSM schemes de-risk STU payments rather than directly subsidising buses.

**Key facts:** PM E-DRIVE outlay ₹10,900 crore; EVPCS component ₹2,000 crore targeting ~22,100 fast chargers for e-4Ws, ~48,400 for e-2W/3Ws and ~1,800 for e-buses across 9 high-EV-penetration cities and key highways. Separately, the Ministry clarified it has **no separate phased national policy** to incentivise flex-fuel vehicles running on blends above E20.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure) / Environment (Clean mobility, decarbonisation)

## 3. GI & Beyond 2.0 Summit in New Delhi — Digital Handloom Atlas to be launched

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Textiles — Office of the Development Commissioner (Handlooms), with the Handloom Export Promotion Council (HEPC)

The **GI & Beyond 2.0 Summit** is being held on **5 August 2026** at the Dr. Ambedkar International Centre, New Delhi, to push India's **Geographical Indication (GI)-tagged handloom and handicraft products** into domestic and global markets. Over **100 GI-registered handloom and handicraft products** are on display, with overseas buyers from around **10 countries**, exporters, MNCs, registered proprietors of GI products, e-commerce platforms and academia participating. **Minister of State for External Affairs and Textiles Pabitra Margherita** is chief guest; Textiles Secretary Neelam Shami Rao is guest of honour.

Headline deliverable: the launch of the **Digital Handloom Atlas — "One Platform. Endless Markets"**, a digital platform to showcase Indian handloom products and connect artisans directly to global markets. Experts from **IIT Madras** are presenting applications of **AI for strengthening the GI ecosystem** (traceability, authentication, anti-counterfeiting).

**Why it matters:** GI is a community-held IPR — the registered proprietor is typically a producer association or state body, not an individual — so GI-led export promotion is simultaneously an IPR, artisan-livelihood and export-diversification story. Ties directly to the run-up to **National Handloom Day (7 August)**, whose **12th edition** falls this week.

**Key facts:** GIs in India are governed by the **Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999**, administered by the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks (GI Registry, Chennai) under **DPIIT**; India's first GI was **Darjeeling Tea (2004-05)**. National Handloom Day commemorates the launch of the **Swadeshi Movement on 7 August 1905**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (IPR, MSME/handloom exports) | GS1 — Art & Culture (traditional crafts)

## 4. PM-AJAY Adarsh Gram: 47,328 villages selected; 46,642 works completed

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment (Rajya Sabha written reply by MoS Ramdas Athawale)

The government told Parliament that **47,328 villages** have been selected across the country under the **Adarsh Gram component** of PM-AJAY — including 435 in Telangana. Against **3,28,131 development works** and **1,10,51,213 beneficiaries** identified, **46,642 works have been completed**, benefitting about **47.59 lakh** people so far.

### Scheme-in-focus: Pradhan Mantri Anusuchit Jaati Abhyuday Yojana (PM-AJAY)

- **Ministry:** Social Justice and Empowerment (Department of Social Justice and Empowerment); Centrally Sponsored Scheme launched in **2021-22** by merging three earlier schemes.
- **Objective:** Reduce poverty among Scheduled Caste communities by generating additional employment, raising incomes and improving socio-economic development indicators in SC-dominated areas.
- **Three components:**
  1. **Adarsh Gram** — integrated development of villages with **≥50% SC population**, aiming at saturation of **50 monitorable socio-economic indicators across 10 sectors** (education, health, sanitation, drinking water, nutrition, livelihoods, etc.);
  2. **Grants-in-Aid to States/UTs** for socio-economic betterment projects and skill development;
  3. **Hostels** — construction/repair of hostels in higher educational institutions.
- **Merged predecessors:** Pradhan Mantri Adarsh Gram Yojana (PMAGY), Special Central Assistance to Scheduled Caste Sub-Plan (SCA to SCSP), and Babu Jagjivan Ram Chhatrawas Yojana.
- **Significance:** A "saturation-model" scheme — the unit of measurement is the village and the indicator, not the individual beneficiary, which makes it a good example of outcome-based targeting in welfare delivery.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance (welfare schemes for vulnerable sections) | GS1 — Society (social empowerment of SCs)

## 5. Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 introduced in Lok Sabha

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Finance — Union Finance Minister Nirmala Sitharaman (4 August 2026)

The Finance Minister introduced the **Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026** in the Lok Sabha, which seeks to further amend the **Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007**, the **Income-tax Act, 2025** and the **Finance Act, 2026**. She also placed before the House an explanatory statement (in Hindi and English) setting out the circumstances that necessitated immediate legislation, and explained the **Income-tax (Amendment) Ordinance, 2026**, which the Bill is intended to replace.

**Why it matters:** This is the first significant amendment cycle to the **Income-tax Act, 2025** — the new code that replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 — so it is a live example of post-enactment fine-tuning of a major legislative overhaul. Amending the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 (the statute under which the **RBI** regulates payment systems and which underpins the **Board for Regulation and Supervision of Payment and Settlement Systems**) puts it at the intersection of tax law and financial-market regulation.

**Key facts:** Introduced during the **Monsoon Session 2026** (20 July – 13 August 2026). Since it replaces an Ordinance, Article 123 requires the Ordinance to cease to operate six weeks from the reassembly of Parliament unless replaced by an Act.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (taxation, financial sector) | GS2 — Polity (ordinance-to-statute route)

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the strength of the Supreme Court of India, consider the following statements:

1. The number of judges of the Supreme Court, other than the Chief Justice of India, is fixed by the Constitution and can be altered only by a constitutional amendment.
2. The Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026 raises the total sanctioned strength of the Court, including the Chief Justice of India, to 38.
3. The increase in strength given effect by the 2026 Bill had earlier been brought into force through an Ordinance.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

1. It is implemented by the Ministry of Heavy Industries.
2. It includes a dedicated financial component for setting up Electric Vehicle Public Charging Stations.

**Q3.** With reference to Geographical Indications (GIs) in India, consider the following statements:

1. GIs are registered and protected under a law enacted in 1999 and administered under the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade.
2. A Geographical Indication is typically held by an individual proprietor rather than by an association of producers.
3. The GI & Beyond 2.0 Summit, 2026 was organised by the Ministry of Textiles to promote GI-tagged handloom and handicraft products.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 3 only
(b) 2 only
(c) 1 and 2 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

## Answers

**Q1 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** Statement 1 is incorrect: Article 124(1) provides for the CJI "and such number of other Judges as Parliament may by law prescribe" — the strength is fixed by *ordinary law* (the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act, 1956), not by the Constitution itself. The 2026 Bill raises judges other than the CJI from 33 to 37 (total 38) and gives statutory backing to the Ordinance of 16 May 2026, with retrospective effect from that date.

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** PM E-DRIVE is a Ministry of Heavy Industries scheme with a ₹10,900 crore outlay, including ₹2,000 crore earmarked for EV Public Charging Stations (6,562 chargers sanctioned, none commissioned as of the reply). The PM-eBus Sewa–PSM provides ₹4,391 crore as a payment security backstop for STUs procuring 14,028 e-buses; 523 were deployed as on 10 July 2026.

**Q3 — (a) 1 and 3 only.** Statement 2 is incorrect — a GI is a **collective/community right**: the registered proprietor is usually an association of producers, a cooperative or a state agency, not an individual. GIs are governed by the Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999, administered through the GI Registry at Chennai under DPIIT. The GI & Beyond 2.0 Summit was held on 5 August 2026 in New Delhi by the Office of the Development Commissioner (Handlooms), Ministry of Textiles, with HEPC.
