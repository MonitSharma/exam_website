## 1. MoEFCC: International Workshop on **Tiger Landscapes — Conservation, Bioeconomy and Ecotourism**; **IBCA–ADB MoU** signed

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change — jointly with the **National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA)**, the **International Big Cat Alliance (IBCA)** and the **Asian Development Bank (ADB)**. New Delhi, 2–3 September 2026.

**What it is:** Union Environment Minister Shri Bhupender Yadav inaugurated a two-day international workshop on *"Tiger Landscapes: Conservation, Bioeconomy and Ecotourism as Pillars for Livelihoods and Income Generation."* Delegates from tiger-range countries, Centre and State forest departments, researchers and development partners attended.

**Why it matters:** This is the framing shift UPSC likes to test — tiger landscapes treated as **natural capital / natural assets**, not just protected areas. The workshop's premise is that biodiversity conservation, local livelihoods, ecotourism and a forest **bioeconomy** must be designed together, and that **nature-positive finance** is the missing link. It is also IBCA's first substantive engagement with a multilateral development bank.

**Key facts:**

- **IBCA–ADB MoU** signed at the workshop — cooperation on landscape conservation, sustainable development, capacity building, knowledge exchange and **nature-positive financing**.
- **IBCA**: launched by India in **April 2023** (at the 50-years-of-Project-Tiger commemoration); **headquartered in India**; conservation focus on **seven big cats** — tiger, lion, leopard, snow leopard, cheetah, **jaguar and puma** (the last two not found in India).
- **NTCA**: **statutory** body under the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 (inserted by the 2006 amendment), chaired by the Union Environment Minister; administers **Project Tiger**.
- The Minister flagged **Sundarbans** — climate stress on a tiger habitat that is also a mangrove and delta system.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 Environment (conservation architecture, ecotourism, bioeconomy, conservation finance) → also GS2 IR for IBCA as an India-headquartered treaty-based body.

## 2. Ministry of Commerce & Industry: **India–Afghanistan Joint Working Group on Trade, Commerce and Investment** holds virtual meeting

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Commerce & Industry (Department of Commerce). Meeting held **1 September 2026**, virtually.

**What it is:** Officials from both sides — commerce and industry departments, **customs**, regulatory authorities and diplomatic missions — reviewed bilateral trade and agreed on follow-up action in identified areas.

**Why it matters:** India's engagement with Afghanistan is being rebuilt through **technical/economic channels** rather than formal recognition. A trade JWG is precisely that instrument — it keeps commerce, connectivity and humanitarian trade alive while the political question stays open. For GS2 IR this is a clean case study in **calibrated engagement**.

**Key facts / agenda covered:**

- **Trade facilitation and customs cooperation**, including enhanced **data-sharing**.
- **Visa facilitation for Afghan traders**; **banking and financial** channel issues.
- **Pharmaceutical and agricultural trade**; energy cooperation; **tariff concessions**.
- **Cargo connectivity and port-related issues** — the standing India–Afghanistan connectivity problem, given the absence of direct land access through Pakistan (hence **Chabahar Port** and the **air freight corridor** as the workaround routes).
- Both sides agreed to **continued institutional engagement**.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 International Relations (India–Afghanistan; India and its neighbourhood) → cross-link to GS3 Economy (trade facilitation, connectivity corridors).

## 3. Department of Consumer Affairs: **CCPA fines Dial4Trade ₹10 lakh** for online listing of **ammonium nitrate**; seven platforms under scrutiny

**Issuing body:** Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA), Department of Consumer Affairs, Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution. Order reported 1–2 September 2026.

**What it is:** CCPA passed a final order penalising the B2B marketplace **Dial4Trade ₹10 lakh** for hosting listings of **ammonium nitrate** — a regulated explosive substance — as if it were an ordinary product, without disclosing that lawful purchase requires a **licence**. The platform's **Section 79 IT Act "safe harbour"** defence was rejected for want of due diligence.

**Why it matters:** Two exam-relevant threads meet here. (i) **Consumer law**: presenting a licence-controlled hazardous substance as an ordinary listing was held to be **misleading advertising and an unfair trade practice** — an expansive reading of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019. (ii) **Internal security**: ammonium nitrate is the classic IED precursor; unregulated online listings are a supply-chain vulnerability. CCPA also held that **reactive takedown after detection is not sufficient** for regulated substances — a meaningful narrowing of intermediary safe harbour in the consumer-law space.

**Key facts:**

- Order passed under **Sections 10, 20 and 21 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019** (Section 10 constitutes the CCPA; Section 21 covers false/misleading advertisements).
- Listings carried prices, packaging details, a "Contact Supplier / Send Enquiry" button and **images depicting blast effects**.
- Directive: **immediately discontinue** listing, hosting, advertising or facilitating sale of ammonium nitrate **and any substance classified as an explosive under the Explosives Act, 1884**.
- Part of a **wider CCPA examination of seven e-commerce platforms** on the online sale of hazardous chemicals, explosives and precursors.
- Background: ammonium nitrate is regulated under the **Explosives Act, 1884** read with the **Ammonium Nitrate Rules, 2012**.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 Governance (regulatory bodies — CCPA; consumer protection) → GS3 Internal Security (IED precursors, e-commerce as a vulnerability).

## 4. Ministry of Labour & Employment: **EPFO opens a one-time Amnesty window for exempted PF trusts** under the **EPF Scheme, 2026**

**Issuing body:** Employees' Provident Fund Organisation (EPFO), Ministry of Labour & Employment.

**What it is:** EPFO has invited applications from **Provident Fund Trusts recognised under the Income-tax Act, 1961 but holding no formal exemption order** under provident-fund law, to get their exempted status **retrospectively regularised**. The amnesty provisions are a **transitional measure under the Employees' Provident Fund Scheme, 2026** (notified 29 June 2026); operational guidelines were issued on 11 July 2026.

**Why it matters:** A large number of company PF trusts have run for years on income-tax recognition alone, without a valid exemption order under **Section 17 of the EPF & MP Act, 1952** (or **Section 143 of the Code on Social Security, 2020**). That grey zone put subscribers' balances at legal risk. The amnesty regularises them rather than penalising them — an example of **compliance-by-amnesty** as a governance technique, and a live illustration of the **Code on Social Security, 2020** displacing the 1952 Act's provisions.

### Scheme-in-focus: **EPFO Amnesty Scheme, 2026 (exempted PF trusts)**

- **Name / parent:** Amnesty provisions under the **EPF Scheme, 2026**; administered by EPFO under the Ministry of Labour & Employment.
- **Objective:** One-time retrospective regularisation of the exempted status of PF trusts that lack a formal exemption order.
- **Key features:** (i) **retrospective recognition** of the trust; (ii) **waiver of minimum employee-strength and minimum-corpus requirements**; (iii) the **three-year prior-compliance condition deemed satisfied**; (iv) **withdrawal of pending assessments**.
- **Window:** applications open until **28 December 2026** (a six-month window).
- **Beneficiaries:** subscribers of company/establishment PF trusts whose exempted status was legally infirm.
- **Significance:** protects members' accumulations, cleans up EPFO's exempted-establishment register, and eases the transition to the Social Security Code architecture.

*(Distinct from the **Employees' Enrolment Campaign, 2026** — the employer-side amnesty for un-enrolled workers, window closing 31 October 2026 — covered on 22 August. Do not confuse the two.)*

**Destination notebook:** GS2 Governance / Social Justice (social security administration) → GS3 Economy (labour codes, formalisation).

## 5. Ministry of Science & Technology: Push for **wider awareness of the ₹1 lakh crore RDI Fund** at the **TDB's 30th Foundation Day**

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Science & Technology / Department of Science and Technology (DST) — **Technology Development Board (TDB)** 30th Foundation Day, 1 September 2026.

**What it is:** Union Minister Dr. Jitendra Singh called for wider outreach on the **Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) Fund** so that eligible applicants understand its **purpose, eligibility and process**. He noted the volume of applications itself signals confusion, and stressed the Fund is **not to be treated as a conventional loan facility**.

**Why it matters:** The RDI Fund is the Government's principal instrument for shifting India's R&D spend from public-sector-dominated to **private-sector-led** — the standing critique in every Economic Survey that India's **GERD** is low and that business-enterprise R&D is a small share of it. Awareness/absorption problems in a ₹1 lakh crore corpus are exactly the "last-mile of policy" point examiners look for.

### Scheme-in-focus: **RDI Scheme / RDI Fund**

- **Name / ministry:** Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) Scheme — **Department of Science and Technology (DST)**, Ministry of Science & Technology.
- **Approved:** Union Cabinet, **1 July 2025**; **corpus ₹1 lakh crore**.
- **Objective:** catalyse **private-sector-led** research, development and innovation, especially in **sunrise and strategic sectors**, by de-risking capital at the near-commercialisation stage.
- **Architecture:** apex governance by the **Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF)** Governing Board; **DST** is the nodal implementing department; funds flow through **second-level fund managers** rather than direct grants; support is largely **long-tenure, low- or nil-interest loans** (and equity in deep-tech), not subsidy.
- **Priority sectors:** artificial intelligence, biotechnology, clean/renewable energy, **deep-tech manufacturing, semiconductors** and the digital economy.
- **Significance:** complements ANRF (operationalised 2024) on the basic-research side; targets the **"valley of death"** between lab-proven technology and commercial deployment.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 Science & Technology (R&D funding architecture, indigenisation) → GS3 Economy (innovation-led growth, GERD).

## 6. Ministry of Road Transport & Highways: **17 bids received for the ₹8,300 crore Rameshwar–Paradeep Coastal Corridor**, Odisha

**Issuing body:** National Highways Authority of India (NHAI), Ministry of Road Transport & Highways.

**What it is:** NHAI reported a strong bidding response for the **160-km access-controlled greenfield coastal National Highway from Rameshwar to Paradeep** in Odisha — **Package-I (Rameshwar–Konark, 79.4 km, four-lane): 10 bids**; **Package-II (Konark–Paradeep, 80.7 km, two-lane with paved shoulder): 7 bids**.

**Why it matters:** A concrete instance of the **coastal-corridor / port-connectivity** strand of infrastructure policy — greenfield access-controlled highways designed to serve **ports, tourism circuits and a new airport** simultaneously, and to decongest existing NHs.

**Key facts:**

- Approved by the **Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA)** in **June 2026** at a capital cost of **₹8,300 crore**.
- To be awarded under the **Hybrid Annuity Mode (HAM)**.
- **Design speed 100 km/h**; expected to cut Rameshwar–Paradeep travel time by about **2 hours 30 minutes**.
- Provides an alternative to **NH-16 and NH-316**; improves access to **Puri, Konark, Paradeep Port** and the upcoming **Puri International Airport**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 Economy — Infrastructure (highways, PPP models: BOT vs HAM vs EPC; port-led development).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the **International Big Cat Alliance (IBCA)**, consider the following statements:

1. It was launched by India in 2023 and is headquartered in India.
2. Its conservation focus covers seven big cat species, of which two are not found in the wild in India.
3. It is a body constituted under the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** With reference to the **Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) Scheme**, consider the following statements:

1. It was approved by the Union Cabinet with a corpus of ₹1 lakh crore.
2. It is anchored by the Anusandhan National Research Foundation, with the Department of Science and Technology as the nodal implementing department.
3. Its primary instrument is outright capital grants to public research laboratories.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** The **Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA)** recently penalised an online marketplace for listing **ammonium nitrate**. In this context, consider the following statements:

1. The CCPA is a statutory authority established under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.
2. Ammonium nitrate is regulated as an explosive substance under the Explosives Act, 1884.
3. The CCPA can pass orders against false or misleading advertisements.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
IBCA was launched by India in April 2023 (marking 50 years of Project Tiger) and is headquartered in India, so 1 is correct. It covers seven big cats — tiger, lion, leopard, snow leopard, cheetah, jaguar and puma — of which the **jaguar and puma** are not found in India, so 2 is correct. IBCA is an **intergovernmental alliance**, not a body created under the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972; the statutory body under that Act is the **NTCA**. Hence 3 is incorrect.

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
The Cabinet approved the RDI Scheme on 1 July 2025 with a ₹1 lakh crore corpus (1 correct), anchored by ANRF with DST as the nodal department (2 correct). Its design is deliberately **not** a grant scheme for public labs — it deploys long-tenure, low- or nil-interest loans (and equity in deep-tech) through second-level fund managers to catalyse **private-sector-led** R&D, which is why the Minister cautioned against treating it as a conventional loan facility either. Hence 3 is incorrect.

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.**
The CCPA was established under **Section 10 of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019** (1 correct). Ammonium nitrate is a regulated explosive substance under the **Explosives Act, 1884**, read with the Ammonium Nitrate Rules, 2012 (2 correct). Under **Section 21** of the 2019 Act, the CCPA can issue directions and impose penalties for false or misleading advertisements — the ground used against Dial4Trade (3 correct).
