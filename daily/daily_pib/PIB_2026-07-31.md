## 1. Favara–UPI cross-border payment corridor goes live between the Maldives and India

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Finance (with NPCI International Payments Ltd) and the Maldives Monetary Authority (MMA)

**What it is:** The **Favara–UPI** cross-border payment corridor was declared operational on **30 July 2026**. Favara is the **Maldives' domestic Instant Payment System**, run by the Maldives Monetary Authority; linking it to India's **UPI** allows real-time person-to-person remittances from the Maldives to India — a sender initiates in **Maldivian Rufiyaa** and the beneficiary is credited in **Indian Rupees** through UPI.

**Why it matters:** This is the second sovereign *instant-payment-system-to-instant-payment-system* linkage India has built (after the India–Singapore UPI–PayNow corridor), as distinct from the simpler "UPI acceptance abroad" model. For UPSC, it sits at the intersection of **digital public infrastructure (DPI) as an instrument of foreign policy** and the **G20/CPMI agenda of making cross-border retail payments cheaper, faster and more transparent**. It also feeds directly into India's **Neighbourhood First** and **MAHASAGAR / SAGAR** vision in the Indian Ocean Region, where the Maldives hosts a large Indian expatriate workforce whose remittances currently move through costlier correspondent-banking channels.

**Key facts:**

- Live from **30 July 2026**; follows a bilateral agreement of **July 2025** and a compressed testing phase.
- **Phase 1:** person-to-person (P2P) remittances only, offered initially by **Bank of Maldives Plc** and **Maldives Islamic Bank Plc** through their mobile banking apps.
- **Future phases:** QR-code-based **merchant (P2M) payments**, which would matter for Indian tourists and for Maldivian merchants.
- Indian side implemented by **NPCI International Payments Limited (NIPL)**, the international arm of NPCI.
- Useful contrast to remember: **UPI–PayNow (Singapore, Feb 2023)** was the world's first cloud-based linkage of two fast payment systems; the Maldives corridor extends that template westward into the IOR.

## 2. Ministry of Panchayati Raj launches **DAANVEER** — crowd-funding digital equipment for Gram Panchayats

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Panchayati Raj (with the National Informatics Centre and DigiHaat)

**What it is:** **DAANVEER** is a citizen-participation platform, launched at Krishi Bhawan by the Secretary, Ministry of Panchayati Raj, that lets individuals, corporates and organisations **donate standardised, quality-verified computer bundles to Gram Panchayats**. Donations are routed through the **Meri Panchayat mobile app**; donors pick from pre-approved bundles mapped to **state-specific technical specifications**, and the platform allocates them to eligible Gram Panchayats through a transparent digital process.

**Why it matters:** The e-governance stack at the panchayat level — **eGramSwaraj, AuditOnline, Gram Manchitra, Meri Panchayat, the Panchayat Development Index** — presumes working hardware and connectivity at the GP office. DAANVEER is an admission that the **last-mile hardware gap**, not software, is now the binding constraint on the 73rd Amendment's promise of local self-government. It is also a template for **CSR/citizen co-financing of public goods** rather than pure budgetary provision.

**Key facts:**

- Target universe: roughly **2.5 lakh Gram Panchayats** — the basic tier of rural local self-government under Part IX of the Constitution.
- Delivery channel: **Meri Panchayat app**; technical partners **NIC** and **DigiHaat**.
- Donations are in kind (pre-specified computer bundles), not cash — designed to prevent leakage and specification mismatch.
- Stated intent is to evolve from a one-off "close the computer gap" drive into a **standing citizen-powered ecosystem** for strengthening local institutions.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (Local Self-Government, e-Governance, Devolution)

## 3. Scheme-in-focus: **DISHA 2.0** — the Centre's ₹255-crore access-to-justice scheme

**Issuing body:** Department of Justice, Ministry of Law & Justice

**Name:** *Designing Innovative Solutions for Holistic Access to Justice* (**DISHA**) **2.0** — a **Central Sector Scheme**, 100% centrally funded.

**Objective:** To leverage technology, community-level intermediaries and AI-enabled tools so that citizens can access legal advice and legal-aid services *before* disputes reach court — i.e. shifting from litigation management to **pre-litigation legal empowerment**.

**Key features (four components):**

1. **Tele-Law** — pre-litigation legal advice to citizens through Common Service Centres and Village Level Entrepreneurs, connecting them to panel lawyers.
2. **Nyaya Bandhu** — pro bono legal services by volunteer advocates, delivered through a mobile/web application.
3. **Legal Literacy and Legal Awareness Programme (LLLAP)** — mass awareness of rights and entitlements.
4. **VIDHI–Sanjeevani** (*Vision for Integrated Delivery of Harmonised Legal Initiatives*) — **new in 2.0**: a centralised integrated dashboard for real-time monitoring of implementation and service delivery, to be integrated with the **Nyaya Setu AI chatbot** for AI-enabled legal assistance.

**Beneficiaries / coverage:** Cumulative outreach target of **3 crore beneficiaries**; the predecessor DISHA scheme (2021–26) reported over **2.37 crore beneficiaries**.

**Outlay and period:** **₹255 crore**, implementation from **1 April 2026 to 31 March 2031**.

**Significance:** Read this alongside **Article 39A** (Directive Principle — equal justice and free legal aid), the **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987** (NALSA/SLSA/DLSA architecture) and the **eCourts Mission Mode Project**. DISHA operates *upstream* of NALSA's court-centric legal aid: its logic is that most poor litigants need advice at the dispute-formation stage, not a lawyer after filing.

**Linked development the same day:** NIC's **Artificial Intelligence Division** and the **Centre of Excellence (eCourts), NIC Pune** have developed **LegRAA — the Legal Research Analysis Assistant** — an AI tool to support judges with legal research, document analysis and decision support, to be rolled out to the judiciary nationwide under the eCourts Project. Related eCourts tools worth noting: **Digital Courts 2.1**, and the speech-to-text engine **SHRUTI (ASR)** and translation engine **PANINI**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Judiciary, Access to Justice, Government Schemes)

## 4. Army launches indigenous **Xtreme Weather Grade (XWG) Diesel** — fluid down to −42 °C

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Defence (Indian Army) with **Bharat Petroleum Corporation Limited (BPCL)**

**What it is:** The **Chief of the Army Staff, General Dhiraj Seth**, along with BPCL CMD **Sanjay Khanna**, launched a specially formulated **Xtreme Weather Grade diesel** that remains **fluid at temperatures as low as −42 °C**. It can be used across the Army's **existing vehicle fleet without any engine modification**, and was developed in **under a year**.

**Why it matters:** At high altitude and in extreme cold, ordinary diesel **waxes** — paraffin crystals form and clog fuel filters and lines, immobilising vehicles. Along the **Northern and Eastern borders** — Siachen, eastern Ladakh, the high passes on the LAC — winter temperatures routinely fall below −30 °C, and vehicle immobilisation is an operational, not merely logistical, problem. Domestic formulation also removes a **strategic import dependency** on specialised cold-flow fuels.

**Key facts:**

- Cold-flow performance: fluid down to **−42 °C**.
- **Drop-in fuel** — no engine or fleet modification required.
- Developed indigenously by the Indian Army in partnership with **BPCL** (a Maharatna oil marketing PSU under the Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas).
- The Army Chief described it as a "game-changing indigenous innovation" for **mobility, operational readiness and mission reliability**, and flagged more specialised fuels for diverse operational needs.
- Concept anchor for the exam: **cloud point / pour point / cold filter plugging point (CFPP)** are the parameters that define a fuel's cold-weather usability.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Indigenisation, Defence Technology) · GS3 — Internal Security (Border Infrastructure)

## 5. Drug price control: NPPA has fixed ceiling prices of **935 scheduled formulations** and retail prices of **3,845 new drugs**

**Issuing body:** Department of Pharmaceuticals, Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers (reply by MoS **Anupriya Patel** in the Rajya Sabha)

**What it is:** In a Parliament reply, the government stated that as on **23 July 2026**, the **National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority (NPPA)** had fixed **ceiling prices for 935 scheduled drug formulations** and **retail prices for 3,845 new drugs**, to keep essential medicines within affordable limits.

**Why it matters:** This is the cleanest available snapshot of how India's **drug price control architecture** actually functions — a favourite Prelims/Mains area under *Issues relating to health* and *Government policies and interventions*. The chain runs: **NLEM (Health Ministry) → Schedule-I of DPCO, 2013 → ceiling price fixed by NPPA → enforcement**.

**Key facts:**

- **NLEM (National List of Essential Medicines)** is published by the **Ministry of Health & Family Welfare**; the current list carries **388 medicines**.
- Medicines in **Schedule-I of the Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013** are "scheduled formulations" — NPPA fixes their **ceiling price**.
- For **non-scheduled formulations**, a manufacturer may not raise the MRP by **more than 10% over the MRP of the preceding 12 months**.
- **DPCO, 2013** is issued under the **Essential Commodities Act, 1955**; **NPPA** is an attached office of the **Department of Pharmaceuticals** (not a statutory regulator).
- Monitoring inputs come from **Price Monitoring and Resource Units (PMRUs)** in states, State Drugs Controllers, open-market sampling, market databases and consumer complaints.
- Distinguish clearly: **NPPA = prices**; **CDSCO / DCGI = quality, safety and approvals**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance & Social Justice (Health, Regulatory Bodies) · GS3 — Economy (Pharmaceutical Sector)

## 6. India's first dedicated **Department of Military Medicine** inaugurated at Lucknow

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Defence (Armed Forces Medical Services) — inaugurated virtually by Raksha Mantri **Rajnath Singh**

**What it is:** The **Department of Military Medicine** at the **Command Hospital (Central Command), Lucknow** is the country's **first dedicated academic and operational department devoted exclusively to military medicine** — the specialty covering combat casualty care, operational and environmental physiology (high altitude, cold injury, diving, aviation medicine), and battlefield trauma systems.

**Why it matters:** Military medicine has historically been taught as a scatter of subspecialties across AFMC Pune and command hospitals. A dedicated department creates a **doctrinal and research home** for it — indigenous protocols suited to India's own theatres (Siachen, deserts, high-altitude LAC, maritime domain) rather than imported NATO-derived doctrine. It also feeds India's growing role in **HADR (Humanitarian Assistance and Disaster Relief)** deployments abroad.

**Key facts:**

- Location: **Command Hospital (Central Command), Lucknow**; inaugurated **29 July 2026**.
- Mandate: structured education and training in military and operational medicine; **postgraduate programmes** in combat medicine, combat surgery and trauma care; indigenous doctrine and research.
- Stated to complement a proposed **Global Military Medicine Centre** envisaged under the **Raksha Strategy Vision 2026–30**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security & Defence (Capability Building) · GS2 — Health (Medical Education)

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the **Favara–UPI cross-border payment corridor** made operational in July 2026, consider the following statements:

1. Favara is the domestic instant payment system of the Maldives, operated by the Maldives Monetary Authority.
2. In its first phase, the corridor enables both person-to-person remittances and QR-code-based merchant payments.
3. The linkage is implemented on the Indian side by NPCI International Payments Limited.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Consider the following pairs of components of the **DISHA 2.0** scheme and their descriptions:

1. **Tele-Law** — pre-litigation legal advice delivered through Common Service Centres
2. **Nyaya Bandhu** — pro bono legal services rendered by volunteer advocates
3. **VIDHI–Sanjeevani** — a centralised dashboard for real-time monitoring, integrated with an AI chatbot

How many of the pairs given above are correctly matched?

(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) All three
(d) None

---

**Q3.** With reference to the regulation of drug prices in India, consider the following statements:

1. The National List of Essential Medicines is published by the National Pharmaceutical Pricing Authority.
2. Ceiling prices are fixed for formulations listed in Schedule-I of the Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013.
3. For non-scheduled formulations, manufacturers may not increase the maximum retail price by more than 10 per cent over the MRP of the preceding twelve months.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** Favara is indeed the Maldives' instant payment system run by the Maldives Monetary Authority, and NPCI International Payments Limited (NIPL) is the Indian implementing entity. Statement 2 is wrong: Phase 1 covers **only P2P remittances** through two Maldivian banks; QR-based merchant (P2M) payments are planned for a later phase.

**Q2 — (c) All three.** DISHA 2.0's four components are Tele-Law (pre-litigation advice via CSCs/VLEs), Nyaya Bandhu (pro bono legal services), the Legal Literacy & Legal Awareness Programme, and the newly added VIDHI–Sanjeevani dashboard integrated with the Nyaya Setu AI chatbot. All three pairs are correctly matched.

**Q3 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** Statement 1 is wrong: the **NLEM is published by the Ministry of Health & Family Welfare**; NPPA (an attached office of the Department of Pharmaceuticals) *fixes prices* for the formulations that NLEM feeds into Schedule-I of the DPCO, 2013. Statements 2 and 3 correctly describe the DPCO mechanism, including the 10% annual cap on MRP increases for non-scheduled formulations.
