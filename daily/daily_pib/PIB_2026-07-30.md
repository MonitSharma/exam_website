## 1. DPIIT sets up a Toy Sector Task Force — target 5% of the global toy market by 2032

**Issuing body:** Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), Ministry of Commerce & Industry

**What it is:** At the stakeholder meet *"Team Up for Toys: Driving SCALE, Innovation and Exports"* at Vanijya Bhawan, New Delhi, Union Minister of Commerce & Industry Shri Piyush Goyal announced the constitution of a **Task Force** for the toy sector and released the **Toy Sector Playbook**. The target set is a **5% share of the global toy market by 2032**.

**Why it matters:** Toys are a classic labour-intensive, MSME-dominated, cluster-based manufacturing sector — the exact segment India needs for employment-elastic growth. The 2020 quality-control order route (BIS mandatory certification + tariff hikes) already flipped India from a net importer to a net exporter; the Task Force marks the shift from *import substitution* to *global value chain integration*.

**Key facts:**

- **Six objectives of the Task Force:** (i) integration with global value chains, (ii) strengthening manufacturing capabilities, (iii) resolving value-chain bottlenecks, (iv) creating a skilled workforce and employment, (v) enabling design and innovation, (vi) enhancing ease of doing business.
- **Toy Sector Playbook** — a roadmap for manufacturers, startups, MSMEs, investors and entrepreneurs covering manufacturing ecosystems, value chains, standards and compliance, skilling, product innovation, IP, exports and emerging markets.
- Ecosystem as presented at the meet: **21,000+ MSMEs**, **770+ DPIIT-recognised toy startups**, nearly **50 toy clusters**, and **1,800+ BIS licensees**.
- India is reported to have become the world's **fourth-largest toy exporter** in 2025. *(Industry/trade estimate, not an official PIB figure — treat the ranking as indicative.)*

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Manufacturing, MSMEs, Export Promotion)

## 2. Anhydrous Ammonia notified as a fertiliser under the FCO, 1985 — a three-year window

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare (Gazette notification dated **23 July 2026**)

**What it is:** The Centre has permitted the **manufacture and direct agricultural use of Anhydrous Ammonia** as a fertiliser for a period of **three years**, notified under **Clause 20A** of the **Fertiliser (Inorganic, Organic or Mixed) (Control) Order, 1985** — the enabling clause that allows provisional/experimental introduction of a new fertiliser.

**Why it matters:** Anhydrous ammonia carries a **minimum 82% nitrogen by weight**, the **highest nitrogen concentration of any commercially available fertiliser** (urea is ~46% N). Direct field application therefore moves far less bulk per unit of nitrogen delivered, cuts the conversion step to urea, and offers a route to reduce India's heavy **urea import dependence** while improving **nitrogen-use efficiency (NUE)** — a core subsidy-reform and soil-health objective.

**Key facts:**

- Nutrient specification: **≥82% N**, water-free (gaseous/liquefied under pressure) fertiliser.
- Legal route: **Clause 20A, FCO 1985**; three-year period running from **23 July 2026**.
- FCO, 1985 is issued under the **Essential Commodities Act, 1955** and governs fertiliser quality, price and distribution.
- Handling caution is the flip side: anhydrous ammonia is toxic and corrosive, so the three-year window functions as a supervised trial for storage, transport and application protocols.
- **Wider context (June 2026, for linkage only):** the Centre's green-urea roadmap envisages procurement of **7.24 lakh MT of green ammonia** annually through an SECI e-reverse auction under **National Green Hydrogen Mission Mode 2A**, with a **150 TPD green urea pilot** by **NETRA** (NTPC's R&D arm) at **Pudimadaka, Andhra Pradesh**, integrating water electrolysis with carbon capture and utilisation.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (Fertiliser policy, Input subsidies, Soil health)

## 3. C-DOT and FSID set up a Centre of Excellence for next-generation telecom at IISc Bengaluru

**Issuing body:** Centre for Development of Telematics (C-DOT), Department of Telecommunications, Ministry of Communications — with the Foundation for Science Innovation and Development (FSID) at IISc Bengaluru

**What it is:** C-DOT and FSID signed an agreement to establish a **Centre of Excellence (CoE) for advanced telecom research** at **IISc Bengaluru**. It becomes **C-DOT's fifth CoE**, after those at **IIT Kanpur, IIT Gandhinagar, IIT Roorkee and IIT Hyderabad**.

**Why it matters:** This is the institutional plumbing of India's push from *standards-taker* to *standards-maker* in telecom. C-DOT is the government's telecom R&D arm; anchoring a CoE at IISc puts pre-standardisation research on 6G and post-quantum security inside a national lab-academia-startup pipeline, feeding the Bharat 6G Vision and indigenous-equipment security goals.

**Key facts:**

- Research focus: **5G-Advanced/6G**, **very large-scale MIMO**, **integrated sensing and communication (ISAC)**, **AI-native communication networks and devices**, **quantum and post-quantum communication**, and **cybersecurity**.
- Mandate: wireless communication research, **prototype development**, **IP creation**, and industry-academia collaboration with startups, MSMEs and researchers.
- Signed by **Omprakash Subbarao**, CEO, FSID, and **Dr Rajkumar Upadhyay**, CEO, C-DOT.
- **FSID** — established **2020**, based at IISc Bengaluru, headed by **Prof. G. Rangarajan**, Director, IISc.
- **C-DOT** — the telecom R&D centre under DoT; earlier PIB coverage this month noted its role alongside ASTR, Sanchar Saathi and DIP in the anti-cyber-fraud architecture.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Telecom R&D, 6G, Quantum communication)

## 5. Indian Navy launches 'Machilipatnam' — seventh Mahe-class ASW Shallow Water Craft

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Defence / Indian Navy; built by **Cochin Shipyard Limited (CSL)**

**What it is:** **Machilipatnam (yard no. BY 529)**, the **seventh of eight Mahe-class Anti-Submarine Warfare Shallow Water Craft (ASW SWC)**, was launched at CSL, Kochi, on **29 July 2026**. The launch was performed by **Smt. Goolrukh Anand**, in the presence of **Vice Admiral Atul Anand**, Additional Secretary, Department of Military Affairs.

**Why it matters:** ASW SWCs fill a specific capability gap — hunting **midget and conventional submarines in shallow littoral waters** where larger corvettes cannot operate effectively — alongside **coordinated ASW operations, low-intensity maritime operations and mine-laying**. With Chinese submarine activity in the Indian Ocean Region a standing concern, near-coast underwater domain awareness is a live strategic requirement.

**Key facts:**

- Class: **Mahe-class ASW SWC**, eight ships, all from **Cochin Shipyard Limited** — a public-sector yard, reinforcing indigenous warship-building depth.
- **Over 80% indigenous content**, aligned with *Aatmanirbhar Bharat* in defence manufacturing.
- Roles: underwater surveillance, anti-submarine warfare, **mine warfare** and coastal defence.
- Named after the **historic port city of Machilipatnam, Andhra Pradesh** — the class follows a naming convention of coastal/port towns.
- **What's new since earlier coverage:** the 18 July briefing noted the *commissioning* of **INS Malvan** (second of class, commissioned 22 July 2026); today's item is the *launch* of the **seventh** hull — remember the distinction between **launch** (hull enters water) and **commissioning** (ship enters service as *INS*).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security & Defence (Indigenisation, Maritime security); GS2 linkage — IR (IOR balance)

## 6. New polypore fungus *Microporellus kolhapurensis* described from the Western Ghats

**Issuing body:** Published in ***Nelumbo***, the research journal of the **Botanical Survey of India (BSI)**, Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change

**What it is:** Researchers have described a **new species of polypore (bracket) fungus**, ***Microporellus kolhapurensis***, found growing on rotting wood in the **Bediv area of Bhudargad taluka, Kolhapur district, Maharashtra** (near the Ajra border). It is named after Kolhapur district in recognition of its biodiversity.

**Why it matters:** Polypores are **wood-decay fungi** — the principal recyclers of lignin and cellulose in forest ecosystems, and therefore central to nutrient cycling and carbon turnover. Fungal diversity remains one of the most under-documented components of the **Western Ghats**, a UNESCO World Heritage site and one of the world's eight "hottest" biodiversity hotspots; each such description strengthens India's baseline for the **Global Biodiversity Framework** targets and for **access and benefit-sharing** claims.

**Key facts:**

- Discovered by **Dr Anjali Patil**, Head, Department of Botany, Rajaram College, Kolhapur, with research scholar **Yogesh Patil**.
- Morphology: **yellowish-orange to greyish-brown**, umbrella-shaped with a **lateral stalk**, ground-growing habit, large pores, **teardrop-shaped spores** and distinctive thick-walled **cystidia**; fruits mainly **July–September**.
- Only ***Microporellus obovatus*** had previously been recorded from Maharashtra — this is the **second** species of the genus in the state; the genus has about **36 recognised species** worldwide.
- **BSI:** established **1890**, headquartered in **Kolkata**, an **attached office** of MoEFCC; mandated to survey, document and research India's plant (and allied) diversity. Its journal is ***Nelumbo*** (formerly *Bulletin of the Botanical Survey of India*).
- Compare with the **Zoological Survey of India (ZSI)** — established 1916, also Kolkata, journal *Records of the Zoological Survey of India*.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Biodiversity, Western Ghats, Institutions)

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the recent notification permitting the use of Anhydrous Ammonia as a fertiliser in India, consider the following statements:

1. It has been notified under the Fertiliser (Inorganic, Organic or Mixed) (Control) Order, 1985, which itself is issued under the Essential Commodities Act, 1955.
2. Anhydrous ammonia contains a minimum of 82% nitrogen by weight, the highest nitrogen concentration among commercially available fertilisers.
3. The permission has been granted on a permanent basis with immediate effect.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q2.** Consider the following statements regarding the Centre for Development of Telematics (C-DOT):

1. It functions under the Department of Telecommunications, Ministry of Communications.
2. Its newly announced Centre of Excellence at IISc Bengaluru is its first such centre to be set up outside the IIT system.
3. The Centre of Excellence will work on areas including 6G, integrated sensing and communication, and post-quantum communication.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** Consider the following statements about the Botanical Survey of India (BSI):

1. It was established in 1890 and is headquartered in Kolkata.
2. It is an attached office under the Ministry of Science and Technology.
3. *Nelumbo* is its research journal, in which the new polypore fungus *Microporellus kolhapurensis* was described.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the FCO, 1985 is issued under the Essential Commodities Act, 1955, and anhydrous ammonia carries ≥82% nitrogen by weight (against urea's ~46%). Statement 3 is wrong — the approval, granted under **Clause 20A** of the FCO, is for a **three-year** period beginning 23 July 2026, i.e. a supervised trial, not a permanent notification.

**Q2 — (d).** All three are correct. C-DOT is the telecom R&D arm under the Department of Telecommunications, Ministry of Communications. The IISc Bengaluru centre is C-DOT's **fifth** CoE and its first outside the IIT system — the earlier four are at IIT Kanpur, IIT Gandhinagar, IIT Roorkee and IIT Hyderabad. Its research agenda spans 5G-Advanced/6G, very large-scale MIMO, integrated sensing and communication, AI-native networks, and quantum/post-quantum communication.

**Q3 — (b).** Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Statement 2 is wrong on the parent ministry: BSI is an **attached office of the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change**, not of the Ministry of Science and Technology. *Nelumbo* (formerly the *Bulletin of the Botanical Survey of India*) carried the description of *Microporellus kolhapurensis*, the second *Microporellus* species recorded from Maharashtra.
