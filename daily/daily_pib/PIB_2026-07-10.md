## 1. Ministry of Education releases UDISE+ 2025-26 and the Performance Grading Index (PGI 2.0 & PGI-D) 2025-26

**Ministry:** Ministry of Education (Department of School Education & Literacy) | **Date:** UDISE+ released 7 July 2026; PGI reports released around 9–10 July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (education data systems, evidence-based policy) · GS1 — Society (education & equity) · Reports & Indices

The Ministry of Education released the **UDISE+ (Unified District Information System for Education Plus) Report 2025-26**, the government's foundational school-education database covering **over 14.8 lakh schools, ~1.03 crore teachers and 26+ crore students**. Alongside it, the Department of School Education & Literacy released the **Performance Grading Index 2.0 for States/UTs (PGI-S)** and the **Performance Grading Index for Districts (PGI-D)** for 2025-26 — the government's annual school-education benchmarking exercise. Together they form the empirical backbone for NEP-2020 monitoring and central school-education transfers.

**Key facts / figures:**
- **UDISE+:** Secondary-stage **GER rose to 71.7%** (from 68.5% in 2024-25); national dropout rates 1.8% (Preparatory), 3.6% (Middle), 7.0% (Secondary); **teachers up to ~1.03 crore, women 54.9%** of the workforce; schools with computers up to 69.9% and internet to 67.4%.
- **PGI 2.0 (States/UTs):** 1,000 points across **70 indicators** in two categories (Outcomes; Governance & Management) spanning six domains; **no State/UT reached the top grade** again this year. PGI-D grades districts on 600 points; **473 districts improved** their score and 89 achieved a grade-level jump.

**Why it matters:** UDISE+ (enrolment/infrastructure data) and PGI (relative performance grading) are a classic prelims pair — know that both are run by the **Department of School Education & Literacy** under the Ministry of Education, and that PGI *grades in bands* (it does not rank No. 1–No. 36).

## 2. PM-SETU cleared for pan-India rollout across 200 ITI clusters — ₹1,237.58 crore Strategic Investment Plans approved

**Ministry:** Ministry of Skill Development & Entrepreneurship (MSDE) | **Date:** 4th National Steering Committee, 7 July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (skilling, PPP delivery) · GS3 — Economy (employability, human capital, demographic dividend)

The 4th **National Steering Committee (NSC)** of **PM-SETU** — the Centre's flagship **ITI (Industrial Training Institute) upgradation programme** (widely referenced as *Pradhan Mantri Skilling and Employability Transformation through Upgraded ITIs*) — approved a **nationwide rollout across 200 ITI clusters** and cleared **Strategic Investment Plans worth ₹1,237.58 crore**, with the first tranche of plans for **Odisha, Gujarat and Telangana**. The scheme, announced in Union Budget 2024-25, aims to upgrade **1,000 government ITIs through a hub-and-spoke cluster model** with co-investment from industry.

**Key facts:** Meeting chaired by Debashree Mukherjee, Secretary, MSDE; industry anchors include Jindal (Odisha) and ArcelorMittal Nippon Steel India (Gujarat); model = government ITIs upgraded in industry-led clusters to align training with local employer demand.

**Why it matters:** A high-yield GS3 case study on **converting demographic dividend into skilled employability** and on **PPP in vocational training**. Pair it with Skill India, the National Skill Development Mission and the "hub-and-spoke" delivery design.

## 3. India–Australia Third Annual Summit (Melbourne) — uranium, critical minerals and the ACITI trilateral

**Ministry:** Prime Minister's Office / Ministry of External Affairs | **Date:** 8–10 July 2026, Melbourne | **Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India–Australia, Indo-Pacific, Quad partners) · GS3 — Economy/Energy (critical minerals, civil nuclear)

On the Australia leg of PM Modi's three-nation visit, the **Third India–Australia Annual Summit** with PM Anthony Albanese produced **18 major outcomes** deepening the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership. The headline outcome was the finalisation of **administrative arrangements under the India–Australia Civil Nuclear Agreement, clearing the way for Australian uranium exports to India** for exclusively peaceful purposes. The two sides also set up an **India–Australia Critical Minerals Investment Partnership** (co-investment across five identified projects, plus long-term **lithium offtake** agreements), adopted a **Joint Declaration on Defence & Security Cooperation** and a **Maritime Security Collaboration Roadmap**, and committed to early conclusion of a **Comprehensive Economic Cooperation Agreement (CECA)** and a Bilateral Investment Treaty.

**Key facts:** The visit also carried forward the **ACITI — Australia–Canada–India Technology and Innovation partnership** — a *trilateral* (Australia, Canada, India — **not** Indonesia) focused on critical minerals, resilient supply chains, green-energy innovation and responsible AI.

**Why it matters:** Ties together UPSC's favourite Indo-Pacific threads — uranium/civil-nuclear supply, critical-minerals de-risking away from China, and minilateral (trilateral) groupings. Note that Australia is a Quad partner and a major supplier of both uranium and battery minerals (lithium).

## 4. India secures its first overseas rare-earth assets — Midwest–PERMINAS–NFTDC magnet MoU with Indonesia

**Ministry:** Ministry of Mines / Ministry of External Affairs (private-sector-led, government-facilitated) | **Date:** signed during PM's Indonesia State Visit, 6–8 July 2026 | **Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (critical minerals, manufacturing, supply-chain security) · GS2 — International Relations

Among the ~20 outcomes of the India–Indonesia State Visit, a landmark **MoU** was signed between India's **Midwest Ltd (Midwest Energy)** and the **Non-Ferrous Materials Technology Development Centre (NFTDC), Hyderabad**, and Indonesia's state-owned **PT PERMINAS (Perusahaan Mineral Nasional)** — marking the **first time an Indian private enterprise has secured overseas rare-earth assets** to build a global supply chain. The collaboration targets **rare-earth permanent-magnet manufacturing** — critical for EVs, wind turbines, electronics and defence — with a stated goal of producing **~5,000 tonnes of rare-earth oxide, enough for ~20,000 tonnes of magnets, over about four years**.

**Key facts:** China controls roughly **70% of global rare-earth mining and ~85–90% of processing**, so overseas heavy-rare-earth access directly de-risks India's clean-energy and defence supply chains. Domestically this complements the **National Critical Mineral Mission** and **KABIL (Khanij Bidesh India Ltd)**, the PSU created to acquire strategic minerals abroad.

**Why it matters:** A clean GS3 example of **critical-mineral supply-chain diversification** and India's move up the rare-earth value chain (from ore to magnets). Distinct from today's CA "Astra export to Indonesia" item — this is the minerals/manufacturing dimension of the same visit.

## 5. Sirsa Kinnow gets a GI tag — Haryana's first GI-tagged fruit

**Ministry:** Ministry of Commerce & Industry (DPIIT — Geographical Indications Registry) | **Date:** reported 8–9 July 2026 (certificate sealed at Chennai, 28 March 2026) | **Notebook:** GS1 — Art & Culture / GS3 — Economy (agri-value addition, IPR)

The **Geographical Indications Registry** under DPIIT (Ministry of Commerce & Industry) granted a **GI tag to Sirsa Kinnow**, making it **Haryana's first GI-tagged fruit** (its first exclusive agricultural GI product). The application was filed by the **Kharisureran Farmer Producer Company Ltd, Sirsa**. Interestingly, **Kinnow is not native to India** — it is a citrus **hybrid developed in early-20th-century California (a cross of 'King' mandarin × 'Willow Leaf' mandarin)** and introduced to India's north-western states later. The GI does not certify native origin; it certifies that the fruit, *as grown in Sirsa's specific agro-climatic conditions*, has acquired a distinct quality and reputation.

**Key facts:** GI tags are granted under the **Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999**; a GI protects a *place-linked* reputation and is a form of intellectual property benefiting the local producer community (here, an FPC).

**Why it matters:** GI tags are a recurring prelims theme. The exam-worthy nuance here: a **non-native, hybrid** fruit can still earn a GI because GI protects *local reputation/quality*, not botanical origin.

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the **Performance Grading Index (PGI 2.0)** and **UDISE+** released for 2025-26, consider the following statements:
1. Both are compiled by the Department of School Education & Literacy under the Ministry of Education.
2. UDISE+ is primarily an enrolment and school-infrastructure database, while PGI grades States/UTs on school-education performance.
3. Under PGI 2.0, exactly one State/UT is placed in the highest grade each year.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q2.** The **ACITI partnership**, seen in the news around the India–Australia Annual Summit, is a technology-and-innovation grouping of:
(a) Australia, Canada and India
(b) Australia, China and India
(c) Australia, Indonesia and India
(d) ASEAN, Canada and India

**Q3.** Consider the following statements regarding **rare earth elements** in the context of India's critical-minerals strategy:
1. China accounts for a dominant share of both global rare-earth mining and processing capacity.
2. Rare-earth permanent magnets are used in electric vehicles, wind turbines and defence systems.
3. KABIL (Khanij Bidesh India Ltd) is the public-sector company mandated to acquire mineral assets abroad.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Both UDISE+ and the PGI are run by the Department of School Education & Literacy (Ministry of Education); UDISE+ is the enrolment/infrastructure database while PGI benchmarks performance. Statement 3 is wrong — PGI uses *grade bands* (multiple States can share a grade), and in 2025-26 **no** State/UT reached the highest grade.

**Q2 — (a) Australia, Canada and India.** ACITI = the **A**ustralia–**C**anada–**I**ndia **T**echnology and **I**nnovation partnership, a trilateral focused on critical minerals, resilient supply chains and responsible AI. (Indonesia is not a member — a common trap given the concurrent India–Indonesia visit.)

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** China dominates both rare-earth mining (~70%) and processing (~85–90%); rare-earth magnets are vital for EVs, wind turbines and defence; and KABIL, a joint-venture PSU, is tasked with acquiring strategic mineral assets overseas — the backdrop to India's first overseas rare-earth (magnet) MoU with Indonesia.
