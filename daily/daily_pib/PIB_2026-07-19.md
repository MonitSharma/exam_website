## 1. NITI Aayog releases India's first **Investment Friendliness Index (IFI) 2026** — Gujarat tops

**Issuing body:** NITI Aayog (released 17–18 July 2026)

**What it is:** The first-ever nationwide index scoring States and UTs on how effectively they create, enable and sustain a conducive investment environment. It is explicitly framed not as a league table but as a diagnostic tool for States to locate policy gaps and undertake reforms — the same logic NITI used for the SDG India Index and the Export Preparedness Index.

**Why it matters:** Investment climate is a *State* subject in practice (land, labour enforcement, power tariffs, single-window clearances). The IFI operationalises competitive-cooperative federalism on the single most-cited constraint on India's manufacturing ambition. Expect it to become the standard citation for "ease of doing business has moved from national to sub-national."

**Key facts & figures:**

- Coverage: **28 States and 8 UTs**, assessed on **84 indicators** across **8 pillars** (policy & governance, infrastructure, business facilitation, fiscal management, among others).
- Ranking of 17 major States: **Gujarat 56.6 (1st)**, **Maharashtra 53.7 (2nd)**, **Tamil Nadu 53.3 (3rd)**.
- "Top Performers" category: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, **Goa**, **Odisha**.
- Hilly & North-Eastern States category: **Uttarakhand 1st**, followed by **Assam** and **Himachal Pradesh**.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 Governance (competitive federalism, centre–state economic relations) + GS3 Economy (investment climate) + Reports & Indices.

## 2. Union Cabinet approves **Regional Connectivity Scheme – Modified UDAN**, ₹28,840 crore over 10 years

**Issuing body:** Cabinet Committee / Ministry of Civil Aviation (approved 17 July 2026)

**What it is:** A ten-year successor framework to the original UDAN (Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik) regional connectivity scheme, extending its scope beyond fixed-wing airport connectivity to **heliports and seaplanes/water aerodromes**, with a sharpened focus on remote regions, hilly terrain and aspirational districts.

**Why it matters:** UDAN is the textbook example of a **viability gap funding (VGF)** model applied to a social-infrastructure market failure — thin routes that are commercially unviable but developmentally essential. The redesign is a case study in scheme evolution: from route subsidy alone to route subsidy *plus* airside asset creation.

**Key facts & figures:**

- Period: **FY 2026-27 to FY 2035-36** (10 years); total outlay **₹28,840 crore** from GoI budgetary support.
- **₹10,043 crore** earmarked as **viability gap funding** over 10 years.
- **₹12,159 crore** over 8 years to develop **100 airports** from existing unserved airstrips; plus **200 heliports** planned.
- Decade targets: **120 new destinations** and **4 crore passengers**.
- Original UDAN's 9-year record: **663 routes** operationalised across **95** airports/heliports/water aerodromes; **3.41 lakh+ flights**; **162.47 lakh passengers**.

### Scheme-in-focus: Regional Connectivity Scheme – Modified UDAN

- **Ministry:** Civil Aviation
- **Objective:** Make air travel affordable and accessible in underserved/unserved regions, and build the airside infrastructure to sustain it.
- **Key features:** (i) VGF on selected regional routes with capped airfares; (ii) capital support to convert unserved airstrips into functioning airports; (iii) extension to heliports and seaplane operations; (iv) priority to hilly, remote and aspirational districts.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** Tier-2/3 towns, hill and NE States, island territories; 120 new destinations targeted.
- **Significance:** Anchors regional economic geography — tourism, medical access, and time-sensitive cargo — to a subsidised aviation network.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 Economy (infrastructure, VGF/PPP models) + GS2 Governance (centrally sponsored scheme design).

## 3. Ministry of Power releases **draft CAFE-III fuel economy norms** for public consultation

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Power / Bureau of Energy Efficiency (draft released 17 July 2026)

**What it is:** A revised draft of the **Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE-III)** norms for M1-category passenger vehicles, to succeed CAFE-II when it lapses on **31 March 2027**. CAFE norms regulate the *fleet-wide average* fuel consumption of each manufacturer rather than individual models — a sales-weighted target that pushes the product mix toward cleaner powertrains.

**Why it matters:** CAFE is India's principal demand-side lever on **crude import dependence** and transport-sector CO₂. The draft's most significant innovation is a first-ever **carbon-neutrality factor** for ethanol, biofuels and compressed biogas, formally recognising the biogenic-carbon argument in Indian regulation — directly linking the E20/EBP ethanol programme to vehicle-emission accounting.

**Key facts & figures:**

- Applies to M1 passenger vehicles manufactured or imported for sale, **FY 2027-28 to FY 2031-32**.
- Targets tighten from **3.996 litres/100 km (94.76 gCO₂/km) in 2027-28** to **3.3273 litres/100 km (78.90 gCO₂/km) in 2031-32**.
- **Super credits** for clean technologies: battery-electric and range-extended EVs count as **3 vehicles**; plug-in hybrids and flex-fuel strong hybrids **2.5×**; strong hybrids **1.6×**; flex-fuel ethanol vehicles **1.1×**.
- Carbon-neutrality factors let manufacturers discount declared tailpipe CO₂ for ethanol/biofuel/CBG use before compliance assessment.
- Comments due by **6 August 2026**; draft hosted on Ministry of Power and BEE websites.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 Environment (emission standards, energy efficiency) + GS3 Economy (energy security, auto sector) — links to the Energy Conservation Act framework and the EBP programme.

## 4. Third **India–EU Trade and Technology Council (TTC)** meets in Brussels; Horizon Europe association talks launched

**Issuing bodies:** Ministry of Commerce & Industry, MeitY, Ministry of External Affairs (with the European Commission), 15–16 July 2026

**What it is:** The third ministerial meeting of the India–EU TTC — a strategic coordination platform (India has such a council only with the EU and the US) organised around working groups on **strategic technologies/digital governance**, **clean and green technologies**, and **trade, investment and resilient value chains**.

**Why it matters:** The headline outcome is agreement to open **formal negotiations on India's association to Horizon Europe**, the EU's ~**€93.5 billion** flagship research and innovation programme. Association from 2027 would let Indian researchers and institutions draw EU funding directly and *lead* consortia rather than participate as third-country partners — a structural upgrade in India's science diplomacy.

**Key facts & figures:**

- Venue/date: **Brussels, 15 July 2026**; third TTC meeting.
- Horizon Europe budget **≈ €93.5 billion**; negotiations targeted for conclusion by **end-2026**, association effective **2027**.
- First **India–EU Innovation Hub** to be set up on **EV charging technologies and testing** (standards and interoperability).
- Launch of an **EU–India Deep Tech Startup Partnership**, initially focused on clean and green technologies.
- Cooperation announced across **AI, semiconductors and clean technologies**.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 International Relations (India–EU, bilateral/regional groupings, science diplomacy) + GS3 Science & Technology.

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the Investment Friendliness Index (IFI) 2026, consider the following statements:

1. It was released by NITI Aayog and is the first index of its kind in India.
2. It assesses States and Union Territories across eight pillars using 84 indicators.
3. Among the major States, Gujarat secured the top rank.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Consider the following statements regarding the Regional Connectivity Scheme – Modified UDAN approved in July 2026:

1. It has been approved for a period of ten years from FY 2026-27 to FY 2035-36.
2. Its scope has been extended beyond airports to include heliports and seaplanes.
3. The entire outlay of the scheme is provided as viability gap funding to airline operators.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** The Corporate Average Fuel Economy (CAFE) norms, in the news recently, are notified under the administrative purview of which of the following?

(a) Ministry of Road Transport and Highways, through the Central Motor Vehicles Rules only
(b) Ministry of Power, with the Bureau of Energy Efficiency as the implementing body
(c) Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, through the Central Pollution Control Board
(d) Ministry of Heavy Industries, through the Automotive Research Association of India

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The IFI 2026 is NITI Aayog's first-ever investment friendliness index, covering 28 States and 8 UTs on 84 indicators across 8 pillars; Gujarat topped the major-States list with 56.6, ahead of Maharashtra (53.7) and Tamil Nadu (53.3).

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The scheme runs FY 2026-27 to FY 2035-36 and now covers heliports and seaplanes. Statement 3 is wrong: of the ₹28,840 crore total outlay, ₹10,043 crore is VGF while ₹12,159 crore is for developing 100 airports from unserved airstrips — so VGF is only a part of the outlay.

**Q3 — (b).** CAFE norms in India are framed by the **Ministry of Power** with the **Bureau of Energy Efficiency**, flowing from the Energy Conservation Act framework — not by MoRTH, MoEFCC or the Ministry of Heavy Industries. The draft CAFE-III norms (comments due 6 August 2026) apply to M1 passenger vehicles from FY 2027-28.
