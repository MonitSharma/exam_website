## 1. MoSPI: Real GDP grows **7.8%** in Q1 (April–June) of FY 2026-27

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI), National Statistics Office — press note released 31 August 2026.

India's economy expanded **7.8% in real terms** in the first quarter of FY 2026-27, comfortably ahead of the RBI's projection of about 7% for the quarter. The release also carried the **National Accounts Statistics (NAS) 2026** publication, which revises the annual estimates for FY 2022-23, 2023-24 and 2024-25 on the new base year.

**The numbers to memorise:**

| Aggregate | Q1 FY 2026-27 | Q1 FY 2025-26 | Growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real GDP (constant 2022-23 prices) | ₹81.36 lakh crore | ₹75.46 lakh crore | **7.8%** |
| Nominal GDP (current prices) | ₹88.27 lakh crore | ₹80.00 lakh crore | **10.3%** |
| Real GVA | ₹73.82 lakh crore | ₹68.21 lakh crore | **8.2%** |
| Nominal GVA | ₹80.53 lakh crore | ₹72.24 lakh crore | **11.5%** |

**Three methodology points that are far more examinable than the growth number itself:**

- **Base year 2022-23.** The new series of annual and quarterly GDP estimates was released on **27 February 2026**; Provisional Estimates for FY 2025-26 followed on **5 June 2026**.
- **Benchmark-Indicator methodology.** Quarterly GDP is *not* independently measured — the previous year's annual estimate is extrapolated using indicators (IIP, GST data, listed-company results, PFMS, port cargo, rail tonne-km, e-VAHAN registrations, etc.). It broadly follows the **IMF's Quarterly National Accounts Manual, 2017**.
- **Double deflation for manufacturing.** The new series deflates **output and intermediate consumption separately** using distinct Producer Price Indices, instead of applying a single deflator to both. This is the internationally recommended practice and a long-standing demand of Indian statisticians. MoSPI explicitly cautions that under double deflation the implicit manufacturing GVA deflator can turn **negative** when input prices rise faster than output prices — so a falling deflator need not mean falling prices.

Also released on the same day: a new **Banking Services Price Index (BkSPI)** with base 2022-23 feeding the series, and a **Sub-Sectoral Trial Index of Services Production (ISP) for June 2026** (base 2024–25) — India's still-experimental attempt at a high-frequency services output index, the services analogue of the IIP.

**Why it matters:** GVA growing faster than GDP (8.2% vs 7.8%) means **net indirect taxes (taxes minus subsidies) grew more slowly** than the rest of the economy — the standard reading is a subsidy/tax-collection composition effect, and it is exactly the kind of "explain the GDP–GVA wedge" question that shows up in interviews and GS3 answers.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Growth & Development; Indian Economy and Planning). Also Reports & Indices.
**Next data point:** Q2 (July–September) FY 2026-27 estimates due **30 November 2026**.

## 2. Ministry of Rural Development: **1,338 Panch Sammelans** completed to roll out the VB-G RAM G Act

**Issuing ministry:** Ministry of Rural Development, 31 August 2026.

The nationwide campaign of **district-level Panch Sammelans** — convened to orient Panchayati Raj functionaries on the **Viksit Bharat–Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) Act, 2025**, the statute that replaced MGNREGA — has crossed **1,338 Sammelans** with participation of over **5.26 lakh** elected representatives, functionaries and officials.

**Key facts:**

- **23 States and UTs** have completed their scheduled Panch Sammelans: Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Goa, Gujarat, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Mizoram, Nagaland, Punjab, Sikkim, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Tripura, Uttarakhand, West Bengal, Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, Jammu & Kashmir, and Puducherry.
- **Gujarat, Jammu & Kashmir and West Bengal** went beyond the district level and held Sammelans at additional administrative tiers.
- Content covered: **Viksit Gram Panchayat Plan (VGPP)**, the **Rural Rozgar Guarantee Card**, asset creation, convergence, **social audit** and grievance redressal.

*New-development note:* the Act itself has appeared only as a passing Parliament-reply line in this briefing series (4 August). What is new here is the **implementation architecture** — the Centre is treating PRI capacity-building, not budget allocation, as the binding constraint on the new law.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Viksit Bharat–G RAM G Act, 2025**

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | Rural Development (Department of Rural Development) |
| **Status** | Passed by Parliament 18–19 December 2025; replaces the Mahatma Gandhi NREGA, 2005 |
| **Objective** | Link the rural wage-employment guarantee to durable asset creation and income security under the Viksit Bharat 2047 frame |
| **Guarantee** | **125 days** of wage employment per rural household in a financial year (up from 100 under MGNREGA) |
| **Permissible works** | Four thematic domains — (i) water security, (ii) rural infrastructure, (iii) livelihood-related infrastructure, (iv) mitigation of extreme weather events |
| **Planning instrument** | **Viksit Gram Panchayat Plan (VGPP)**, prepared by the Gram Panchayat |
| **Entitlement document** | **Rural Rozgar Guarantee Card** (successor to the MGNREGA job card) |
| **Accountability** | Statutory social audit and grievance redressal; Gram Panchayat placed at the centre of planning, implementation and monitoring |

**Why it matters:** This is the single biggest rural-welfare statutory change of the decade and a near-certain GS2 (welfare schemes, devolution to PRIs) and GS3 (rural employment, drought-proofing) question. The examinable tension: a **125-day guarantee with a works menu tilted toward asset creation** raises the classic demand-driven-vs-supply-driven debate that dominated MGNREGA evaluation literature.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (Welfare schemes, Panchayati Raj / devolution). Cross-reference GS3 — Rural Economy & Employment.

## 3. PM at the **SCO Summit in Bishkek**; India–Uzbekistan elevated to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership

**Issuing body:** Prime Minister's Office / Ministry of External Affairs, 30–31 August 2026.

The Prime Minister's Central Asia leg (29 August – 1 September) concluded its Uzbekistan half and moved to Kyrgyzstan for the **SCO Summit at Bishkek**.

**Uzbekistan (State Visit, 29–30 August) — outcomes:**

- India and Uzbekistan **elevated bilateral ties to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership**; a Joint Statement and a List of Outcomes (MoUs across culture, digital, defence-industrial and economic cooperation) were issued.
- The Prime Minister was conferred the **"Oliy Darajali Do'stlik" Order** — Uzbekistan's **highest honour for foreign leaders**.
- He laid a wreath at the **Shastri Memorial** in Tashkent (Lal Bahadur Shastri died there in January 1966, the day after the Tashkent Declaration) and visited **Tashkent State University of Oriental Studies**.

**Bishkek (31 August) — bilaterals on the SCO sidelines:**

- **Kyrgyz Republic:** bilateral meeting with President **Sadyr Japarov**; the PM was also invited to the opening of the **6th World Nomad Games**.
- **Iran:** meeting with President **Masoud Pezeshkian**. India reiterated that all issues must be resolved **through dialogue and diplomacy**, and flagged two national interests — the **safety of Indian nationals** in the region, and **unhindered transit of energy and goods** (read: Chabahar and the INSTC).
- **Armenia:** meeting with Prime Minister **Nikol Pashinyan** on defence, economy, pharmaceuticals, science & technology, culture and education, plus West Asia.

*New-development note:* the 28 August briefing previewed the Uzbekistan visit. What is new is the **outcome set** — the partnership upgrade, the state honour, and the pivot to the SCO Summit.

**Why it matters:** The through-line of the whole trip is **connectivity**. Uzbekistan is one of only two doubly landlocked countries in the world; India's access to Central Asia runs through Chabahar and the INSTC, which is precisely why the Iran bilateral emphasised "unhindered transit". Note also the **SCO membership trap**: Iran is a full member (since 2023), Armenia is only a **dialogue partner**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India's extended neighbourhood; regional groupings — SCO).

## 4. Ministry of Finance: **NPCI International and Uzbekistan's NIPC to enable UPI payments via UZQR**

**Issuing ministry:** Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Services) / NPCI International Payments Ltd (NIPL), 31 August 2026 — an outcome of the Uzbekistan visit.

**NIPL**, the international arm of the National Payments Corporation of India, signed a commercial agreement with Uzbekistan's **National Interbank Processing Centre (NIPC)** to allow **Indian UPI apps to scan Uzbekistan's national QR code standard, UZQR**, for merchant payments. The arrangement is expected to go live **within about a year**.

**Key points:**

- This is a **QR-acceptance / interoperability** model — Indian travellers pay Uzbek merchants using their existing UPI apps. It is *not* a full person-to-person cross-border remittance linkage (that is the **UPI–PayNow** model with Singapore).
- Fits the established NIPL playbook: UPI acceptance abroad in the UAE, Singapore, Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Mauritius, France and others; **NIPL also exports the UPI stack itself** (e.g. to Peru and Trinidad & Tobago).
- Comes days after **UPI's tenth anniversary** (24–25 August 2026) and reinforces digital public infrastructure as an instrument of Indian economic diplomacy.

**Why it matters:** For prelims, learn the **three distinct UPI internationalisation models** — (i) QR acceptance abroad, (ii) bilateral P2P remittance linkage (UPI–PayNow), (iii) licensing/deploying the UPI technology stack in a partner country. Examiners love asking which model applies to which country.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Digital Public Infrastructure, payments). Cross-reference GS2 — IR (economic diplomacy).

## 5. Ministry of Defence: **INS Nipun commissioned** — India's second indigenous Diving Support Vessel

**Issuing ministry:** Ministry of Defence / Indian Navy, 31 August 2026, Naval Dockyard Mumbai.

The Indian Navy commissioned **INS Nipun**, the **second Nistar-class Diving Support Vessel (DSV)**, built by **Hindustan Shipyard Limited (HSL), Visakhapatnam** — a Ministry of Defence PSU.

**Key facts:**

- **Indigenous content of roughly 75%**, designed in-house and built domestically under the Aatmanirbhar Bharat defence push.
- Role: **deep-sea saturation diving, submarine rescue and underwater salvage/intervention**. A DSV is the surface **mother ship** for a **Deep Submergence Rescue Vehicle (DSRV)** — India operates DSRVs on both the Western and Eastern seaboards.
- Its sister ship **INS Nistar** was commissioned in July 2025; the name revives that of the Navy's original submarine rescue vessel.

**Why it matters:** Submarine rescue is a genuine strategic capability, not a ceremonial one — only a handful of navies operate DSV/DSRV combinations. Pair this with the ongoing **Project 75(I)** and P-75 Scorpène submarine programmes for a complete underwater-domain answer. Also a clean example of an **MoD shipyard PSU** delivering a specialised platform, useful for the "defence indigenisation" question.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (indigenisation, defence) and Internal Security (maritime domain).

## 6. MoEFCC: **4th Indo-German Environment Forum** in New Delhi — theme "Together For More Resilience"

**Issuing ministry:** Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change, announced 31 August 2026; Forum held from **1 September 2026** in New Delhi.

Union Minister **Bhupender Yadav** and Germany's Federal Environment Minister **Carsten Schneider** open the **fourth edition** of the Indo-German Environment Forum, the German Environment Ministry's principal bilateral format with India, organised together with business associations.

**Key facts:**

- **Theme:** *"Together For More Resilience"*.
- **Focus areas:** circular economy, biodiversity, climate action and adaptation, clean technology and sustainable finance.
- Roughly **200 participants**; the German minister travels with a high-level business delegation, with **investment mobilisation** and **resource efficiency** on the agenda.
- The **third** edition was also held in New Delhi (2019); the Forum sits alongside the older **Indo-German Energy Forum** and the Indo-German Development Cooperation programme.

**Why it matters:** Germany is India's largest trading partner in the EU and its most consistent bilateral partner on climate finance and circular-economy technology. The Forum is the venue where India's **resource-efficiency and circular-economy agenda** — Extended Producer Responsibility rules, e-waste, battery waste, LiFE — gets institutional European backing. Slot it into the "India's climate diplomacy is bilateral as well as multilateral" argument.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (climate cooperation, circular economy). Cross-reference GS2 — IR (India–Germany, India–EU).

## Answers

**Q1 — (b) Only two.**
Statement 1 is **incorrect**: Real **GVA** grew **8.2%** while Real **GDP** grew **7.8%** — GVA grew *faster*, implying net indirect taxes grew more slowly than the rest of the economy. Statement 2 is correct: the new series uses **base year 2022-23** (released 27 February 2026). Statement 3 is correct: quarterly estimates use the **Benchmark-Indicator method**, extrapolating the previous year's annual estimate using high-frequency indicators, broadly per the IMF's Quarterly National Accounts Manual, 2017.

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.**
The VB-G RAM G Act, 2025 raised the wage-employment guarantee from 100 days to **125 days** per rural household per financial year; it **replaced MGNREGA, 2005**; and the **Viksit Gram Panchayat Plan (VGPP)** is the Gram Panchayat-level planning instrument under it, alongside the **Rural Rozgar Guarantee Card**, statutory social audit and grievance redressal.

**Q3 — (b) 1 and 2 only.**
Statement 1 is correct — the summit was held at **Bishkek**, capital of the Kyrgyz Republic, where the Prime Minister held bilaterals with the Presidents of Kyrgyzstan and Iran and the Prime Minister of Armenia. Statement 2 is correct — **Iran became a full member of the SCO in 2023**. Statement 3 is **incorrect** — **Armenia is a dialogue partner**, not a full member of the SCO.
