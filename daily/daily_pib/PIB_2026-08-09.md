## 1. India crosses **300 GW of installed non-fossil-fuel power capacity**

**Ministry:** New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) | **Announced by:** Union Minister Shri Pralhad Joshi

India's installed electricity generation capacity from non-fossil sources — renewables plus nuclear — has crossed the **300 GW** mark. This is the headline metric in India's Panchamrit pledge at COP26, where the target is **500 GW of non-fossil capacity by 2030**.

**Key facts / figures**

- Non-fossil installed capacity stood at about **297.36 GW as on 30 June 2026**; it has since crossed 300 GW.
- **Renewable energy alone: 288.58 GW** (30 June 2026). Nuclear adds roughly **8.78 GW**.
- Source split within RE (approx., 30 June 2026): **Solar ~162.15 GW** (largest), **Wind ~56 GW**, **Large hydro ~51.96 GW**, **Bio-power ~11.75 GW**, **Small hydro ~5.18 GW**.
- **30.58 GW** of non-fossil capacity added in **January–June 2026** alone — about **25% higher** than the same period a year earlier.
- Roughly **90 GW** added over the last two years; RE capacity is now nearly **four times** the 76.38 GW of 2014.

**Why it matters:** India had already met the Nationally Determined Contribution goal of **50% of installed capacity from non-fossil sources ahead of the 2030 deadline**. Note the standard prelims trap — this is a target on *installed capacity*, not on *electricity actually generated*; thermal still supplies the majority of units generated because of higher plant load factors. The residual challenges are storage, transmission evacuation and grid balancing, which is why recent Cabinet decisions (PM Surya Sarovar Yojana, floating solar with storage) bundle storage with generation.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Energy / Infrastructure.

## 2. **CPGRAMS** emerges as one of the world's largest digital public grievance redress platforms

**Ministry:** Personnel, Public Grievances & Pensions — **Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG)**

The Centralised Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System (CPGRAMS) is the Government's single online platform for citizens to lodge grievances against any Central Ministry, Department or subordinate organisation, available 24×7 through the **pgportal.gov.in** portal, the UMANG app and Common Service Centres.

**Key facts / figures**

- **Over 15.2 lakh grievances** received on CPGRAMS between **1 January and 15 July 2026**.
- **Average disposal time: 13 days** for Central Ministries/Departments — down from **157 days in 2014** and about 15 days in 2025.
- The **10-Step CPGRAMS Reforms** and the **Comprehensive Guidelines of August 2024** cut the mandated redressal timeline from **30 days to 21 days**.
- A **Review Meeting Module** enables periodic review by senior officers; **CPENGRAMS** is the parallel system for pension grievances, alongside the **Bhavishya** pension portal.
- Grievance appeal and a feedback call-centre mechanism allow citizens to rate the quality of redress, not merely its speed.

**Why it matters:** A textbook GS2 example of **e-governance improving citizen–State interface** and of "minimum government, maximum governance". Useful as an answer example on administrative reform, the Sevottam framework and the Right of Citizens to Time-Bound Delivery of Goods and Services Bill idea. Also links to the **2nd Administrative Reforms Commission's 12th Report ("Citizen Centric Administration")**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance / e-Governance / Citizens' Charters.

## 3. Union Home Minister presents the **President's Colour to the Puducherry Police**

**Ministry:** Home Affairs | **Presented by:** Shri Amit Shah, Union Minister for Home Affairs and Cooperation

The President's Colour (also referred to as the *Nishaan*) is the **highest honour conferred on a uniformed force** by the President of India, awarded for exceptional service and distinguished achievement. It was presented to the Puducherry Police at the Puducherry Armed Police Ground, Gorimedu, on 9 August 2026.

**Key facts / figures**

- **President Droupadi Murmu approved** the conferment on **8 May 2026**.
- Attended by Lt Governor **K. Kailashnathan** and Chief Minister **N. Rangasamy**.
- Constitutional context: Puducherry is a **Union Territory with a legislature** under **Article 239A**; "police" and "public order" are State List entries, but in UTs the Administrator/LG retains substantial control — the Government of Union Territories Act, 1963 governs Puducherry.

**Why it matters:** Reinforces the police-modernisation and morale-building strand of internal security, and is a ready hook for the recurring question of **police reforms** (Prakash Singh v. Union of India, 2006 directions — State Security Commission, fixed tenure for DGP, separation of investigation and law-and-order wings).

## 4. Scheme-in-focus: **Post-Matric Scholarship for Scheduled Caste Students (PMS-SC)** — ₹1,178.20 crore central share to Karnataka in three years

**Ministry:** Social Justice & Empowerment (Department of Social Justice & Empowerment) | **Stated in:** Rajya Sabha reply by MoS **Shri Ramdas Athawale**

- **Name:** Post-Matric Scholarship for Scheduled Castes (PMS-SC) — a **Centrally Sponsored Scheme**, among the oldest continuing SC-welfare schemes.
- **Objective:** Financial assistance to SC students pursuing education **beyond Class X** (post-matriculation / post-secondary), so that cost is not a barrier to completing higher and professional education.
- **Key features:**
  1. Implemented **through State Governments and UT Administrations**, with the Centre releasing its committed share.
  2. Operates in **Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) mode since 2021-22** — funds go straight to the student's bank account.
  3. Release of the central share is **contingent on States/UTs uploading finally verified payment data on the National Scholarship Portal (NSP)**, an accountability trigger.
  4. Covers maintenance allowance plus reimbursement of compulsory non-refundable fees.
- **Coverage / figures (Karnataka, central share):** **₹421.08 crore (2023-24)**, **₹422.90 crore (2024-25)**, **₹334.22 crore (2025-26)** — total **₹1,178.20 crore** over three financial years.
- **Significance:** Directly addresses the **higher-education transition and dropout gap** for SC students, a persistent equity concern flagged by AISHE data. The NSP-verification condition illustrates how DBT plus digital verification is being used to plug leakages — and also explains why release figures fluctuate year to year.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance / Welfare Schemes for Vulnerable Sections; GS1 — Social empowerment.

## 5. Vice-President launches **'Har Ghar Tiranga Abhiyan'** in the Andaman & Nicobar Islands on **August Kranti Diwas**

**Body:** Vice-President's Secretariat (campaign anchored by the **Ministry of Culture**)

Vice-President **Shri C. P. Radhakrishnan** launched the Har Ghar Tiranga campaign on **9 August 2026** by hoisting the National Flag at **Flag Point, Sri Vijaya Puram** (the renamed Port Blair), during a two-day visit to the islands. Separately, the Prime Minister paid homage to the participants of the **Quit India Movement** on its anniversary.

**Key facts / figures**

- **9 August** is observed as **August Kranti Diwas**, marking the launch of the **Quit India Movement in 1942** — the "Do or Die" call given by Mahatma Gandhi at the Gowalia Tank Maidan (August Kranti Maidan), Bombay, after the failure of the Cripps Mission.
- Har Ghar Tiranga began in 2022 under **Azadi ka Amrit Mahotsav**; it was enabled by amendments to the **Flag Code of India, 2002** permitting the flag to be flown by night on an open display, and permitting **polyester and machine-made** flags.
- Islands' symbolism: **Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose hoisted the Tricolour at Port Blair on 30 December 1943**, and the Cellular Jail (Kala Pani) housed revolutionaries such as **Vinayak Damodar Savarkar and Batukeshwar Dutt**. The Vice-President also paid tribute at the Cellular Jail.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Modern Indian History (Quit India Movement) / Art & Culture; GS2 — National symbols and the Flag Code.

## 6. **Biotechnology plus AI will drive the next industrial revolution** — India targets a **$1 trillion bioeconomy by 2047**

**Ministry:** Science & Technology — **Department of Biotechnology (DBT)** | **Stated by:** Dr Jitendra Singh, MoS (I/C) Science & Technology

The Minister argued that the fourth industrial revolution will be **AI-driven and biotechnology-driven**, with AI compressing timelines in **gene sequencing, diagnostics and drug discovery**, and positioned India's **BioE3 Policy** as the framework for capturing that shift.

**Key facts / figures**

- **BioE3 Policy** = **Bio**technology for **E**conomy, **E**nvironment and **E**mployment — approved by the Union Cabinet in **August 2024**, implemented by DBT.
- **Six strategic thematic sectors** under BioE3: (i) bio-based chemicals, biopolymers and enzymes; (ii) functional foods and smart proteins; (iii) precision biotherapeutics, including cell and gene therapy; (iv) climate-resilient agriculture; (v) carbon capture and utilisation; (vi) marine and space biotechnology.
- Policy instruments: **Biofoundries and Bio-AI hubs**, biomanufacturing scale-up facilities, and support for regulatory streamlining and a skilled workforce.
- **Target:** a **$1 trillion bioeconomy by 2047**, up from roughly **$165–180 billion** in recent years — one of the fastest-growing segments of the Indian economy.
- Adjacent DBT initiatives referenced: **SUJVIKA**, an AI-driven biotech product data portal for transparency in product approvals and market data.

**Why it matters:** BioE3 marks a shift from viewing biotechnology as a health-sector concern to treating it as a **manufacturing and climate strategy** — "bio-based" substitution for petrochemicals. Pair it with the **National Green Hydrogen Mission** and **IndiaAI Mission** in answers on future-facing industrial policy.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology / Indian Economy (industrial policy).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**1.** With reference to India's recently announced milestone of crossing **300 GW of installed non-fossil-fuel power capacity**, consider the following statements:

1. Non-fossil-fuel capacity, as officially reported, includes nuclear power in addition to renewable energy sources.
2. Solar is the single largest contributor to India's installed renewable energy capacity.
3. India's target under the Panchamrit commitments is to reach 500 GW of non-fossil-fuel energy capacity by 2030.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**2.** Consider the following statements regarding the **Post-Matric Scholarship Scheme for Scheduled Caste students**:

1. It is a Central Sector Scheme fully funded and directly implemented by the Union Government without State participation.
2. It supports students pursuing education beyond the matriculation stage.
3. Since 2021-22, scholarship amounts are transferred to beneficiaries in Direct Benefit Transfer mode.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 2 only

---

**3.** The **BioE3 Policy**, seen in the news, is associated with which of the following?

(a) A framework of the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change for access and benefit sharing from biological resources
(b) A policy of the Department of Biotechnology for high-performance biomanufacturing covering the economy, environment and employment
(c) A scheme of the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy for bio-energy and compressed biogas plants
(d) An initiative of the Ministry of Agriculture for climate-resilient seed varieties developed through genome editing

---

## Answers

**1. (d) 1, 2 and 3**
Non-fossil capacity as officially reported covers renewables **and** nuclear (RE ~288.58 GW plus nuclear ~8.78 GW as on 30 June 2026). Solar, at about 162 GW, is the largest RE source. The Panchamrit pledge announced at COP26 sets 500 GW of non-fossil energy capacity by 2030.

**2. (b) 2 and 3 only**
Statement 1 is wrong: PMS-SC is a **Centrally Sponsored Scheme** implemented **through State Governments and UT Administrations**, with the Centre releasing a committed central share — not a Central Sector Scheme. Statement 2 is correct — "post-matric" means education beyond Class X. Statement 3 is correct — the scheme has run in DBT mode since 2021-22, with central release contingent on verified data uploaded to the National Scholarship Portal.

**3. (b)**
BioE3 stands for **Biotechnology for Economy, Environment and Employment** — approved by the Union Cabinet in August 2024 and implemented by the **Department of Biotechnology**, Ministry of Science and Technology. It promotes high-performance biomanufacturing across six thematic sectors and underpins the $1 trillion bioeconomy target for 2047. Option (a) describes the ABS mechanism under the Biological Diversity Act; option (c) conflates it with bio-energy schemes such as GOBARdhan/SATAT.
