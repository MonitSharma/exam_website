## 1. Ministry of Finance (Department of Economic Affairs): **Two-day Conference of State Finance Ministers and Finance Secretaries — "Financing India's Journey towards Viksit Bharat"**

**What it is.** The Ministry of Finance is hosting a **two-day conference of Finance Ministers and Finance Secretaries of States and Union Territories with legislatures** in **New Delhi on 18–19 September 2026**, themed **"Financing India's Journey towards Viksit Bharat."** The Union Finance and Corporate Affairs Minister participates; **Smt. Anuradha Thakur, Secretary, Department of Economic Affairs (DEA)**, outlines the objectives at the inaugural session, and **Shri N. K. Singh** — President and Life Trustee of the Institute of Economic Growth, and Chairman of the **Fifteenth Finance Commission** — delivers the keynote.

**Why it matters.** This is fiscal federalism in its *cooperative*, non-statutory form. India's constitutional machinery for Centre–State finance is largely formal and periodic — the **Finance Commission** under **Article 280**, the **GST Council** under **Article 279A**, borrowing consent under **Article 293(3)**. A conference like this occupies the space *between* those institutions: it is a consultative, executive-led forum with no constitutional standing, whose value lies in aligning State budget priorities with national goals ahead of the **Sixteenth Finance Commission's** award period. For Mains, it is a ready illustration of the argument that Indian fiscal federalism increasingly runs on **informal coordination platforms** (NITI Aayog Governing Council, GST Council, such conferences) rather than on constitutional bodies alone — with the standard critique that these forums lack enforceability and can dilute State autonomy.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Dates and venue:** 18–19 September 2026, New Delhi. **Theme:** "Financing India's Journey towards Viksit Bharat."
- **Participants:** Finance Ministers and Finance Secretaries of States and UTs with legislatures; several Chief Ministers expected.
- **Three thematic sessions:** (i) **macroeconomic developments**; (ii) **financing agricultural transformation**; (iii) **financing the energy transition**.
- **Keynote:** N. K. Singh, Chairman of the **15th Finance Commission** (award period 2021–26).
- **Context on the agenda:** the revenue impact of the **GST 2.0 rate rationalisation** on State finances, and the Centre–State financing roadmap for **Viksit Bharat @2047**.
- **Convening department:** Department of Economic Affairs (DEA).

**Constitutional anchors to revise alongside this**

- **Article 280** — Finance Commission; **Article 275** — statutory grants-in-aid; **Article 282** — discretionary/other grants (the constitutional basis for most Centrally Sponsored Schemes).
- **Article 279A** — GST Council; **Article 293(3)** — State borrowing requires Union consent where the State is indebted to the Union.
- **Sixteenth Finance Commission** — constituted under Article 280 for the award period beginning 1 April 2026.

**Notebook:** GS2 Polity (federalism, Centre–State financial relations, Finance Commission) + GS3 Economy (public finance, fiscal consolidation).

## 2. Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare (DARE / ICAR): **India's first soil-carbon payments — ₹2.9 crore transferred by DBT to 2,550 smallholder farmers in Punjab and Haryana**

**What it is.** At a programme at **Punjab Agricultural University (PAU), Ludhiana**, **Dr. M. L. Jat, Secretary, Department of Agricultural Research and Education (DARE) and Director General, ICAR**, initiated a **Direct Benefit Transfer of over ₹2.9 crore to 2,550 smallholder farmers** in **Punjab and Haryana** as payment for **verified greenhouse-gas reductions and soil-carbon gains on their own fields**. The government describes this as the **first instance in India of farmers being paid for carbon stored in their soil**.

**Why it matters.** This is a genuinely new category of farm income and a very likely prelims/Mains hook. Until now, Indian farmers have been paid for **output** (MSP, procurement) or for **inputs and assets** (subsidy, PM-KISAN); this pays them for an **ecosystem service** — carbon sequestered in soil — that is measured, third-party verified and monetised as tradable **carbon credits**. It links four syllabus threads at once: **regenerative/conservation agriculture** (Direct Seeded Rice, reduced tillage, crop-residue management instead of stubble burning), **climate mitigation in agriculture**, **carbon markets** (India's **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, 2023** under the Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act, 2022, with an offset mechanism where agriculture is a listed sector), and **farmer income diversification**. Note the co-benefit that matters politically in this geography: paying for residue management is a **market-based alternative to penalising stubble burning** in the NCR airshed.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Amount:** over **₹2.9 crore**, disbursed through **DBT**. **Beneficiaries:** **2,550 smallholder farmers** in **Punjab and Haryana**.
- **Per-farmer payment:** roughly **₹3,000 – ₹15,000**.
- **Coverage of the first issuance:** about **30,000 acres** and more than **50,000 carbon credits**.
- **Programme:** **'Aadi'**, a farmer carbon programme run by **Grow Indigo**, launched in **2019** with **technical guidance from ICAR**.
- **Practices adopted (2019–2022):** **Direct Seeded Rice (DSR)**, **reduced/zero tillage**, **crop residue management**.
- **Verification:** GHG reductions and soil-carbon increases were **measured and independently verified** before credits were issued.
- **Venue of the handover:** Punjab Agricultural University, Ludhiana.

**Notebook:** GS3 Agriculture (sustainable/regenerative agriculture, farmer income) + GS3 Environment (carbon markets, climate mitigation, stubble burning).

## 3. Ministry of Health & Family Welfare: **Advisory to States/UTs on regulation of stem cell therapy — autism treatment restricted to approved clinical trials**

**What it is.** The Union Health Ministry has issued an **advisory to all States and Union Territories on the regulation of stem cell therapy**. It states that stem cell treatments may be offered as **standard care only for disease conditions/indications on the Ministry-approved list**, and that **unproven stem cell interventions must not be offered as routine, standard or commercial clinical services**. Specifically on **Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD)**, the therapeutic use of **any type of stem cell is restricted to duly approved clinical trials**, in line with the **National Guidelines for Stem Cell Research, 2017**, issued jointly by the **Indian Council of Medical Research (ICMR)** and the **Department of Biotechnology (DBT)**.

**Why it matters.** Two examinable threads. First, **regulatory governance of emerging biomedicine**: stem cell therapy in India sits in a grey zone between the **Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 / New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019** (which treat most cell-based products as new drugs requiring CDSCO approval) and **non-binding ICMR–DBT guidelines** — a classic case of a **guideline-based regime being hardened into enforceable conduct rules**. The advisory does this by declaring unauthorised administration, prescription, promotion or advertisement of stem cell therapy beyond approved indications to be **professional misconduct** (which brings the National Medical Commission's ethics machinery into play). Second, **vulnerable-group protection**: the ASD restriction directly targets the commercial exploitation of desperate parents by clinics marketing unproven cures — relevant to disability rights under the **Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016**.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Issued by:** Ministry of Health & Family Welfare, to all **States and UTs** (advisory dated mid-September 2026).
- **Core rule:** stem cell therapy as standard care **only for Ministry-approved indications**; everything else is **experimental** and belongs in approved trials.
- **Autism Spectrum Disorder:** stem cell use of **any type** permitted **only within duly approved clinical trials**.
- **Governing framework cited:** **National Guidelines for Stem Cell Research, 2017 (ICMR + DBT)**.
- **Consequence:** unauthorised administration, prescription, promotion or advertisement beyond approved indications constitutes **professional misconduct**.
- **Judicial backdrop:** a **Supreme Court judgment of 30 January 2026** holding that non-compliance with the statutory mandate must attract consequences.
- **Established vs experimental:** haematopoietic stem cell transplantation for certain blood disorders and malignancies is established; most neurological and developmental indications remain experimental.

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance & Social Justice (health regulation, protection of vulnerable groups, medical ethics) + GS3 Science & Technology (biotechnology, stem cells).

## 4. Ministry of Commerce & Industry (Department of Commerce): **25th Meeting of SCO Ministers responsible for Foreign Economic and Trade Activities, Dushanbe**

**What it is.** The **25th Meeting of Ministers of SCO Member States Responsible for Foreign Economic and Trade Activities** was held in **Dushanbe, Tajikistan, on 17 September 2026**, chaired by **Abdurahmon Abdurahmonzoda**, Tajikistan's Minister of Economic Development and Trade. **India was represented by Shri Yashvir Singh, Additional Secretary, Department of Commerce.** The Ministers agreed on the **"Action Plan for 2026–2030 for the Implementation of the Programme of Multilateral Trade and Economic Cooperation of the SCO Member States"**, to be placed before the **SCO Council of Heads of Government (Prime Ministers)** for approval.

**Why it matters.** The SCO's economic pillar is where India's position is most distinctive — and most constrained. India pressed the familiar **three pillars of "Security, Connectivity and Opportunity"** (the formulation PM Modi used at the SCO Summit in Bishkek) and emphasised **digital and cross-border payments** and **trade finance for MSMEs and start-ups**, which is India's comparative-advantage pitch (UPI-style rails) inside a grouping whose connectivity agenda is otherwise dominated by China's Belt and Road framing — an initiative India has consistently declined to endorse in SCO documents on sovereignty grounds relating to CPEC. Remember the institutional split: the **Council of Heads of State (CHS)** is the summit-level body, while trade and economic programmes such as this Action Plan are cleared by the **Council of Heads of Government (CHG)**.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Meeting:** 25th Meeting of SCO Ministers responsible for Foreign Economic and Trade Activities, **Dushanbe, 17 September 2026**.
- **India's representative:** Shri **Yashvir Singh**, Additional Secretary, Department of Commerce.
- **Main outcome:** agreement on the **Action Plan 2026–2030** implementing the SCO Programme of Multilateral Trade and Economic Cooperation; to be approved by the **CHG**.
- **India's emphases:** intra-SCO trade, lower trade costs, resilient supply chains, connectivity; **digital and cross-border payments**; **trade finance for MSMEs and start-ups**.
- **India's framing:** the **"Security, Connectivity, Opportunity"** triad.
- **SCO basics for revision:** founded **2001** (from the Shanghai Five of 1996); **Secretariat in Beijing**; **RATS (Regional Anti-Terrorist Structure) in Tashkent**; India and Pakistan joined as full members in **2017**, Iran in **2023**, Belarus in **2024**.

**Notebook:** GS2 International Relations (regional groupings, India and SCO, connectivity) + GS3 Economy (trade facilitation, supply chains).

## 5. Department of Food and Public Distribution (Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution): **Sugar stockholding limit for bulk consumers relaxed from 15 days to 30 days — but the extra stock must be imported sugar**

**What it is.** The Government has **relaxed the stockholding limit for bulk sugar consumers from 15 days' consumption to 30 days' consumption**, with a crucial rider: the quantity held **beyond the original 15-day limit must be sourced exclusively from sugar imported under the Advance Authorisation Scheme (AAS) and the Tariff Rate Quota (TRQ)**. Stock purchased from the **open (domestic) market remains capped at 15 days' consumption**. This modifies the **Sugar (Stockholding Limit of Bulk Consumers) Order, 2026**, notified in August 2026 and in force from **1 September to 30 November 2026**, which had capped bulk consumers — those using **more than 10 MT of sugar per month** as raw material — at 15 days' stock after sugar prices touched record highs.

**Why it matters.** This is a textbook illustration of how the State manages an essential commodity using **quantitative controls rather than price controls**, and of the trade-offs involved. The original order was a **hoarding-control measure** under the **Essential Commodities Act, 1955**; the relaxation is a **supply-side accommodation** that simultaneously (i) relieves confectionery, beverage and food-processing units facing input shortages, and (ii) **channels demand towards imported sugar**, thereby protecting domestic mill realisations and the cane-price chain (FRP/SAP) while pulling in the 10 lakh MT of raw sugar allowed in under TRQ. The split-source design — 15 days domestic, 15 days imported — is the elegant bit worth quoting in an answer on **administered markets**.

**Key facts and figures**

- **New limit:** **30 days'** consumption (up from **15 days**), for bulk consumers.
- **Condition:** stock beyond 15 days must come **only** from sugar imported under **Advance Authorisation Scheme (AAS)** and **Tariff Rate Quota (TRQ)**; **open-market purchases remain capped at 15 days**.
- **Parent order:** **Sugar (Stockholding Limit of Bulk Consumers) Order, 2026**, effective **1 September – 30 November 2026**.
- **Who is a "bulk consumer":** an entity using/consuming **more than 10 MT of sugar per month** — confectioners, soft-drink makers, food processors, sweetmeat sellers, institutional buyers.
- **Import window in the background:** **10 lakh MT** of raw sugar permitted, with DGFT modalities for TRQ allocation and a one-time conversion facility from **AA to TRQ**.
- **Statutory basis:** the Essential Commodities Act, 1955 (stock-limit powers over essential commodities).

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (food/commodity price management, essential commodities, trade policy instruments) + GS3 Agriculture (sugar sector, cane pricing).

The NGMV contract was concluded with CSL in **March 2023**; the ships are to carry state-of-the-art weapons and sensors.

**Why it matters.** Steel cutting is the **first physical milestone** in the warship-construction sequence — **steel cutting → keel laying → launch → sea trials → commissioning** — and that sequence is itself a favourite prelims/interview detail. Substantively, the NGMV fills a specific capability gap: a **fast, heavily armed anti-surface platform** intended to succeed the Veer/Prabal-class missile vessels, designed for offensive anti-surface operations and sea denial in the littorals. It also sits squarely inside the **Aatmanirbhar Bharat defence-industrialisation story** — indigenous design, an Indian public-sector yard, and domestic content requirements under **Defence Acquisition Procedure (DAP) 2020** categories such as **Buy (Indian–IDDM)**.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Event:** steel cutting of the **second NGMV**, **CSL Kochi**, **17 September 2026**. First NGMV's steel cutting was also at CSL, earlier in this build programme.
- **Contract:** signed with **Cochin Shipyard Limited in March 2023**.
- **Role:** offensive anti-surface warfare, sea denial and littoral operations; successor to the older missile-vessel fleet.
- **Chief guest:** **Rear Admiral Kapil Mehta**, ACWP&A.
- **CSL context:** India's largest shipbuilding and maintenance yard; builder of **INS Vikrant**, India's first indigenous aircraft carrier.
- **Related terms to revise:** **Buy (Indian–IDDM)** under DAP 2020; the **Defence Acquisition Council (DAC)**; **Positive Indigenisation Lists**.

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: over **₹2.9 crore** was transferred by **DBT** to **2,550 smallholder farmers** after the greenhouse-gas reductions and soil-carbon increases were **measured and independently verified**, covering about **30,000 acres** and more than **50,000 carbon credits**; the qualifying practices adopted between 2019 and 2022 were **Direct Seeded Rice, reduced tillage and crop residue management**. Statement 3 is wrong — the payments came from the **'Aadi' farmer carbon programme** run by Grow Indigo with technical guidance from **ICAR**, not from PMFBY, which is a **crop insurance** scheme and pays out against yield loss, not ecosystem services.

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is the trap and is wrong: the relaxation is **source-conditional** — purchases from the **domestic open market remain capped at 15 days' consumption**, and only sugar imported under **AAS/TRQ** may be held for the additional 15 days. The parent **Sugar (Stockholding Limit of Bulk Consumers) Order, 2026** runs from **1 September to 30 November 2026** and defines a bulk consumer as one using **more than 10 MT per month**.

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct. The SCO **Secretariat is in Beijing** and **RATS is in Tashkent** — the two permanent bodies, and a very common prelims pairing. Trade and economic programmes, including the **Action Plan 2026–2030** agreed at Dushanbe on 17 September 2026, go to the **Council of Heads of Government (CHG)** for approval, while the **Council of Heads of State (CHS)** is the annual summit-level body. India was represented at Dushanbe by **Shri Yashvir Singh, Additional Secretary, Department of Commerce**.
