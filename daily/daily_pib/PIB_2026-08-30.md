## 1. Department of Consumer Affairs notifies the **Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules, 2026** — "One Nation, One Time" becomes law

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution — Department of Consumer Affairs (with CSIR-NPL and ISRO)

**What it is.** The Rules make **Indian Standard Time (IST) the common time reference** across India for legal, administrative, commercial and other official purposes. They were **notified on 27 August 2026** and **come into force 180 days** after publication in the Official Gazette — a transition window for departments, businesses and institutions to modify systems. A draft version had been floated in January 2025; this is the final notification.

**Why it matters.** India's critical digital infrastructure — banking and UPI timestamps, telecom and internet networks, railways and airports, power grid synchronisation, government and legal records, emergency services — depends on precise, synchronised time. At present many Indian systems draw timing from **foreign satellite sources**; the Rules push a domestic alternative, which is a strategic-autonomy question as much as a metrology one.

**Key facts:**

- Rules provide for use of **NavIC**, India's regional satellite navigation system, and other **approved Indian timing sources** for secure time dissemination.
- Department of Consumer Affairs project with **CSIR-NPL and ISRO** to disseminate IST at **millisecond-to-microsecond accuracy** from **five Legal Metrology laboratories**.
- A **White Rabbit Technology-based IST Dissemination Demonstration Network** was commissioned at the **Regional Reference Standard Laboratory (RRSL), Bengaluru, in July 2026**.
- Secure IST dissemination demonstrated between **RRSL Bengaluru and NSE Chennai**, with CSIR-NPL, ISRO, SEBI, NSE and BSNL.
- IST is set at **UTC + 5:30**, referenced to the **82°30′E meridian** (Mirzapur, UP).

**Destination notebook:** GS3 Science & Technology (space applications, standards) + GS2 Governance (legal framework, regulatory reform).

## 2. Vice-President to release **"Mission Rangeen Machhli 2031"** — Strategic Action Plan for Ornamental Fisheries

**Ministry/Body:** Department of Fisheries, Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying

**What it is.** At a Ministry Outreach Programme at **Agatti, Lakshadweep on 31 August 2026**, Vice-President C. P. Radhakrishnan will release **"Mission Rangeen Machhli 2031"**, a strategic action plan for developing India's **ornamental (aquarium) fisheries** sector. It builds on the earlier *Rangeen Machhli* app and gives the sector its first uniform national framework.

**Why it matters.** Ornamental fisheries is a high-value, low-volume, employment-intensive niche — well suited to island and coastal communities and to women's self-help groups — but India has so far captured a tiny share of the global aquarium trade because of inconsistent quality, biosecurity and traceability standards. This plan targets exactly that gap.

**Key features of the plan:**

- Uniform framework for **scientific management, biosecurity, quality assurance and regulatory compliance** across the ornamental fisheries value chain.
- **SOPs** standardising broodstock management, breeding, hatchery operations, production systems, **quarantine, health management, traceability**, aquarium operations, handling, transport and trade.
- Aims at **certification and market access**, stakeholder capacity building and globally competitive growth.

**Supporting figures (from the Ministry):**

- India's coastline **≈11,099 km**; **EEZ ≈24 lakh sq km**; marine fisheries potential in the EEZ **≈58.6 lakh MT**, supporting **≈50 lakh fishers**.
- Seafood exports **≈₹73,890 crore in FY 2025-26**.
- **Lakshadweep**: EEZ of nearly **4 lakh sq km ≈ 17% of India's total EEZ**; lagoon area **over 4,200 sq km**; notified as a dedicated **Seaweed Cluster**; tuna (Yellowfin, Skipjack) is the mainstay.
- Most fishing is concentrated within **40–50 nautical miles** of the coast; the **Guidelines for Sustainable Harnessing of Fisheries in the High Seas by Indian-Flagged Fishing Vessels, 2025** govern deeper-water expansion.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 Economy (Blue Economy, fisheries) + GS1/GS3 Geography (EEZ, island territories).

## 3. **India–Chile CEPA negotiations move to the next phase** — critical minerals at the centre

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Commerce & Industry (Department of Commerce)

**What it is.** Commerce Secretary **Rajesh Agrawal** met **Paula Estévez Weinstein, Vice-Minister of International Economic Relations of Chile**, in **Santiago on 26 August 2026** to advance the proposed **Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement (CEPA)**. India reiterated its commitment to openness, sustained engagement and a balanced approach; both sides aim to **conclude negotiations by end-2026**.

**Why it matters.** Chile is the world's largest copper producer and part of the "lithium triangle". For India, a CEPA is less about the trade balance and more about **securing critical-mineral supply chains** for EV batteries, electronics and the energy transition — the same logic behind the Critical Minerals Mission and the KABIL-type overseas acquisition route. India already has a **Preferential Trade Agreement (PTA) with Chile** (2007, expanded 2017); CEPA would widen it to services, investment and rules.

**Key facts:**

- **Four formal rounds** of CEPA negotiations held so far; talks had paused after Chile's elections and resumed after the new government's review.
- India–Chile bilateral trade rose from **US$3.84 billion (2024)** to **US$5.38 billion (2025)**.
- Focus areas: **market access, critical minerals (lithium, copper)**, and reliable supply chains.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 International Relations (bilateral agreements, Latin America) + GS3 Economy (trade agreements, critical minerals).

## 4. Scheme-in-focus: **Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY)** — PIB Research Unit explainer, 29 August 2026

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare

**Name & launch:** Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana, launched **18 February 2016** (from Kharif 2016).

**Objective:** Provide affordable, comprehensive crop insurance from pre-sowing to specified post-harvest losses, so that farmers can absorb income shocks, repay loans and reinvest in the next season.

**Key features:**

1. **Farmer premium capped** at **2% (Kharif)** and **1.5% (Rabi)** for foodgrain and oilseed crops, and **5%** for commercial and horticultural crops; the balance premium is subsidised by Centre and State in **50:50**, and **90:10** for **North-Eastern and Himalayan States/UTs**.
2. **Risks covered:** yield losses on a notified-area basis (drought, dry spells, floods, inundation, cyclones, hailstorms, lightning, pests, diseases); **prevented sowing** (claims up to **25% of sum insured**); **post-harvest losses** for crops in "cut-and-spread" condition up to **14 days** after harvest; **localised calamities** (hailstorm, landslide, inundation, cloudburst, natural fire). **Excluded:** war, nuclear risks, riots, theft and preventable/man-made risks.
3. **Both loanee and non-loanee farmers**, including **tenant farmers and sharecroppers**; on average about **50%** of enrolments over ten years have been voluntary non-loanee farmers.
4. **Technology stack:** National Crop Insurance Portal (NCIP), **DigiClaim** (since Kharif 2022, over **₹55,000 crore** in claims calculated and paid digitally), **CCE-Agri App**, **CLAP**, **AIDE**, **YES-TECH** (remote-sensing yield estimation — paddy/wheat from Kharif 2023, soybean from Kharif 2024, weightage raised from 30% up to **50%** in some states), **WINDS** (Automatic Weather Stations/Rain Gauges at block and gram panchayat level), **CROPIC** (geo-tagged crop photos), and the **Krishi Rakshak Portal & Helpline — toll-free 14447** (26.12 lakh grievances resolved, **99.66%** resolution rate).

**Coverage / beneficiaries:**

- Since inception (Kharif 2016 to Rabi 2025-26): **over 92.46 crore farmer applications insured**; **over 26.33 crore farmer applications paid claims exceeding ₹2.06 lakh crore**.
- **Union Budget 2026-27 allocation: ₹12,200 crore.** Implemented by **25 States/UTs in Kharif 2026**.
- **Kharif 2026 (as on 27 August 2026): 241.38 lakh farmers insured over 278.12 lakh hectares** — already above Kharif 2025 (229.77 lakh farmers, 269.38 lakh ha).
- Kharif 2025 claims: **₹9,837.61 crore paid to 60.89 lakh farmers**. Peak enrolment year **2024-25**: **15.23 crore farmer applications**, over **4 crore farmers**, **623 lakh hectares**.
- **States returning to PMFBY:** Andhra Pradesh (Kharif 2022), Jharkhand (Kharif 2024), **West Bengal (Kharif 2026)**, **Bihar (from Rabi 2026-27)**.
- Companion scheme **RWBCIS** (weather-index based, "Area Approach" on Reference Unit Areas) covered **25.95 lakh farmer applications over 12.31 lakh hectares in Kharif 2026**.

**Significance:** The world's largest crop insurance programme by farmer enrolment, and now a live case study in how **remote sensing + parametric triggers + DBT** can compress claim-settlement cycles. Note also the 2025-26 dip driven by Maharashtra and Andhra Pradesh discontinuing "universalisation" (100% state-borne premium) — a useful mains point on the fiscal sustainability of state-funded premium waivers versus genuine risk pooling.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 Agriculture (crop insurance, agri-risk management, climate resilience).

## 5. **₹1,503 crore of PMGSY-IV rural roads launched in J&K** — 110 roads, waste plastic in construction

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Rural Development (project execution) — foundation laid by **Dr Jitendra Singh**, MoS (IC) Science & Technology and Earth Sciences, MoS PMO/Personnel, on 29 August 2026

## 6. **Operation Vajra 2.0:** CBN seizes 66.80 lakh psychotropic tablets in an inter-state pharmaceutical-diversion case

**Ministry/Body:** Central Bureau of Narcotics (CBN), Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance

**What it is.** Acting on intelligence developed by its **Neemuch (Madhya Pradesh)** unit, CBN intercepted a truck on the **Chandigarh–Baddi** route on **27 August 2026** and seized **66,79,980 psychotropic tablets** moving without the required statutory documentation. **One person arrested**; investigation continues into the wider syndicate.

**Why it matters.** This is **diversion of licit pharmaceutical production**, not smuggling across a border — the tablets are legally manufactured medicines leaked out of the regulated supply chain. It goes to the heart of CBN's dual mandate: it both **licenses and supervises legal opium/psychotropic manufacture** and enforces against diversion under the **NDPS Act, 1985**.

**Key facts:**

- Seizure composition: **59,08,100 Alprazolam**, **7,47,780 Tramadol**, **23,700 Nitrazepam**, **400 Clonazepam** tablets.
- CBN is headquartered at **Gwalior** and functions under the **Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC)**, Department of Revenue.
- Follow-on to the earlier **Operation Vajra**, under which CBN seized about **12 crore Tramadol tablets** destined for conflict zones.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 Internal Security (drug trafficking, NDPS enforcement, licit-to-illicit diversion).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the **Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules, 2026**, consider the following statements:

1. The Rules establish Indian Standard Time as the common time reference for legal, administrative and commercial purposes across India.
2. The Rules provide for the use of NavIC and other approved Indian timing sources for time dissemination.
3. The Rules came into force with immediate effect from the date of their notification.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** "**Mission Rangeen Machhli 2031**", seen recently in the news, is associated with:

- (a) A national programme for conservation of coral reefs in the Gulf of Mannar and the Andaman Sea
- (b) A strategic action plan for the development of ornamental fisheries, released under the Department of Fisheries
- (c) A scheme of the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change for tagging and tracking migratory fish species
- (d) A dye and colourant standardisation initiative of the Ministry of Textiles for handloom clusters

---

**Q3.** With reference to the **Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojana (PMFBY)**, consider the following statements:

1. The maximum premium payable by a farmer is 2% of the sum insured for Kharif foodgrain and oilseed crops and 5% for commercial and horticultural crops.
2. Under the scheme, YES-TECH uses remote sensing-based methods for crop yield estimation, while WINDS provides hyperlocal weather data through automatic weather stations and rain gauges.
3. The scheme covers losses arising from theft and from riots affecting standing crops.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the Rules make IST the common time reference and expressly provide for NavIC and other approved Indian timing sources. Statement 3 is wrong — the Rules were notified on 27 August 2026 but come into force **180 days** after publication in the Official Gazette, to give stakeholders a transition window.

**Q2 — (b).** "Mission Rangeen Machhli 2031" is a **Strategic Action Plan for Ornamental Fisheries Development** of the Department of Fisheries, Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying, to be released at Agatti, Lakshadweep. It provides SOPs on broodstock management, breeding, quarantine, traceability and trade.

**Q3 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statements 1 and 2 correctly state the premium caps (2% Kharif / 1.5% Rabi foodgrains and oilseeds; 5% commercial and horticultural) and the roles of YES-TECH and WINDS. Statement 3 is wrong — PMFBY explicitly **excludes** losses from war, nuclear risks, **riots, theft** and other preventable or man-made risks.
