## 1. Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution: **Import duty cut on all major edible oils, effective 24 September 2026**

**Issuing body:** Department of Food and Public Distribution (DFPD), with the duty change notified by the Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance.

**What it is.** The Centre has reduced customs duties across the crude and refined edible-oil basket, with effect from 24 September 2026, and simultaneously issued an advisory asking edible-oil associations and industry to *immediately* revise their Price to Distributors (PTD) and Maximum Retail Price (MRP) in line with the lower landed cost. This is a supply-side, tariff-led intervention against food inflation rather than a subsidy.

**Why it matters (GS3 Economy / Agriculture).** India imports roughly 55–60% of its edible-oil requirement, so the import tariff is the single fastest lever on retail cooking-oil prices. But the same tariff is also the protection wall for domestic oilseed growers and for the National Mission on Edible Oils. Every duty cut is therefore a live *consumer-versus-producer* trade-off — a classic UPSC question framing.

**Key facts / figures (as reported):**

| Oil | Duty before | Duty after |
|---|---|---|
| Crude sunflower oil | 10% | **Nil** |
| Crude palm oil | 10% | 5% |
| Crude soybean oil | 10% | 5% |
| Refined palm oil | 32.5% | 27.5% |
| Refined soybean oil | 32.5% | 27.5% |
| Refined sunflower oil | 32.5% | 22.5% |

- PIB states the Government has **maintained an import duty differential of 19.25% between crude and refined edible oils** — the deliberate wedge that protects domestic refining capacity and keeps refining value-addition inside India.
- Context: retail food inflation was reported at a 20-month high of about **7.1% in August 2026**; the August 2026 WPI (flagged in the 14 September briefing) had risen to 9.92%.
- The DFPD advisory has no statutory enforcement mechanism — it relies on industry compliance, which is why the Department also monitors retail price data.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (inflation management, tariff policy) with a cross-link to GS3 Agriculture (oilseeds, import dependence).

## 2. Ministry of Road Transport & Highways (NHAI): **Guidelines to strengthen technology integration and sustainability in access-controlled high-speed National Highway corridors**

**Issuing body:** National Highways Authority of India (NHAI), under MoRTH.

**What it is.** NHAI has issued a consolidated set of guidelines standardising how Detailed Project Reports (DPRs) are prepared for *access-controlled high-speed National Highway corridors*. Until now there was no separate standard for this category — access-controlled corridors were being designed off ordinary NH norms, producing inconsistent widths, interchange spacing and signage across States. Today's release extends the framework to technology integration and sustainability in corridor development.

**Why it matters (GS3 Infrastructure).** "Access-controlled" is the technical distinction between an expressway-grade corridor and an ordinary highway: entry and exit only at designated interchanges, no ribbon development, no at-grade crossings. Standardising DPRs is what makes the **Viksit Bharat 2047 target of about 50,000 km of access-controlled high-speed corridors** buildable at scale, and it feeds directly into the logistics-cost reduction goal of PM GatiShakti and the National Logistics Policy.

**Key facts / figures:**

- Lane-and-width standardisation keyed to projected traffic (PCUs/day):
  - **Up to 15,000** → 4 lanes, **60 m** right-of-way
  - **15,000–25,000** → 4 lanes expandable to 6, **70 m**
  - **25,000–40,000** → 6 lanes, **70 m**
- Special provisions for **advance notification of exits and entries**, so users can anticipate access/egress points safely.
- Interchanges with ramps to connect expressways, State Highways and major traffic generators — **ports, airports, Multi-Modal Logistics Parks (MMLPs), industrial areas and tourist destinations**.
- Target context: ~**50,000 km** of access-controlled high-speed corridors under the Viksit Bharat 2047 roadmap.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Infrastructure (roads, logistics); cross-link to GS3 Economy (logistics cost, PM GatiShakti).

## 3. Ministry of Health and Family Welfare: **India showcases UHC, hepatitis elimination and sickle cell care at UNGA-81; joins the global OneSCD partnership**

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Health and Family Welfare; India represented by Union Health Secretary **Smt. Punya Salila Srivastava**.

**What it is.** India took part in three global health engagements on the margins of the 81st UN General Assembly: the **7th Annual Ministerial Meeting of the Group of Friends of Universal Health Coverage and Global Health**; a side event on **"Advancing Global Action to Eliminate Hepatitis"**; and the launch of **OneSCD — a global partnership to advance equity and transform sickle cell care**.

**Why it matters (GS2 Health + IR).** This is India positioning its domestic delivery numbers as an exportable model of health diplomacy — the same play as vaccine diplomacy and the International Solar Alliance, but in public-health systems. For Mains, it is the cleanest recent illustration of "India as a norm-shaper in global health governance", and OneSCD gives sickle cell disease — long a neglected tropical-belt and tribal-health issue — a formal global platform for the first time.

**Key facts / figures presented by India:**

- Over **1.8 lakh Ayushman Arogya Mandirs** functional across the country.
- Over **900 million citizens** hold unique digital health identities (ABHA, under the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission).
- More than **497 million teleconsultations** delivered through **eSanjeevani**.
- **National Viral Hepatitis Control Programme (launched 2018):** nearly **225 million beneficiaries screened**; services at about **1.80 lakh** primary healthcare facilities; **1,039** treatment centres. India is working to the WHO goal of **eliminating viral hepatitis as a public health threat by 2030**.
- **National Sickle Cell Anaemia Elimination Mission (launched 2023, target 2047):** **7.29 crore people screened**; over **20 lakh carriers** identified; **2.5 lakh** diagnosed; **4.93 crore Sickle Cell Cards** distributed.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Health/Governance and International Relations (India in global institutions).

## 4. Ministry of Science & Technology (TDB–DST): **₹63.84 crore RDI support to GalaxEye for next-generation multisensor satellite technology**

**Issuing body:** Technology Development Board (TDB), Department of Science and Technology, under the **Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) Scheme**.

**What it is.** TDB–DST has signed an agreement with Bengaluru-based space start-up **GalaxEye** extending **₹63.84 crore** of RDI support to develop next-generation multisensor satellite technology. It is one of the first concrete disbursement-stage agreements showing the RDI Scheme's money actually reaching a private deep-tech firm.

**Why it matters (GS3 Science & Technology / Economy).** India's R&D spend has historically been ~0.6–0.7% of GDP and overwhelmingly government-performed. The RDI Scheme is the structural answer: instead of grants, it routes **long-tenure, low-interest, collateral-free capital** to private firms through fund managers, so that the *private sector* does the research. GalaxEye is the demonstration case — India's largest privately built Earth-observation satellite, already in orbit.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) Scheme**

- **Ministry/Body:** Department of Science and Technology (DST); **Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF)** governance layer, with a **Special Purpose Fund (SPF)** at the apex and TDB acting as a **Second Level Fund Manager (SLFM)**.
- **Approved by Cabinet:** 1 July 2025.
- **Objective:** Move India from government-funded to **private-sector-led** R&D by supplying patient, risk-tolerant growth capital to deep-tech firms in sunrise and strategic sectors.
- **Key features (4):**
  1. Outlay of **₹1 lakh crore over six years**, with **₹20,000 crore** in FY 2025-26.
  2. Two-tier fund management — SPF → first-level fund managers → second-level fund managers (TDB among them).
  3. Instruments: **long-tenure, collateral-free loans at 3–4% interest for 12–15 years, capped at 50% of project cost**; equity up to 25% of assistance sought; and hybrid debt convertible to equity on follow-on rounds.
  4. Priority sunrise sectors: energy security, renewables and climate action; deep tech (quantum, robotics, **space**); AI for Indian applications; biotech and medtech; digital economy and agri-tech — plus strategic/Atmanirbharta technologies.
- **Beneficiaries:** Private deep-tech companies, start-ups and technology entities.
- **Significance:** Converts the Budget announcement of a ₹1 lakh crore R&D corpus into a working financing pipeline, and treats deep-tech risk as a financing problem rather than a grant problem.

**Background on the recipient — GalaxEye:** launched **Mission Drishti** on **3 May 2026** aboard a SpaceX Falcon 9 from Vandenberg Space Force Base. Drishti is billed as the **world's first commercial satellite carrying both Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) and Electro-Optical (EO) sensors on a single platform ("OptoSAR")** — SAR sees through cloud and darkness, EO supplies optical detail. Mass ~**190 kg** (India's largest privately built EO satellite), resolution **1.2–3.6 m**, onboard AI processing, and a proprietary "SyncFused" technique that co-registers both sensors in a single pass. A constellation of 8–12 satellites is planned by 2029. Applications: border surveillance, disaster assessment, crop monitoring, maritime tracking.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (space, deep tech, R&D financing).

## 5. Ministry of Defence (Indian Navy): **INS Trishul arrives at Toulon, France for the 24th edition of bilateral maritime Exercise VARUNA**

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Defence / Indian Navy.

**What it is.** The Indian Navy's guided-missile frigate **INS Trishul** has arrived at **Toulon**, the principal base of the French Navy's Mediterranean fleet, for the **24th edition of Exercise VARUNA**. The engagement covers a harbour phase (cross-deck visits, professional exchanges, sporting fixtures) and a sea phase of operational drills.

**Why it matters (GS2 IR / GS3 Security).** VARUNA is the oldest continuous naval exercise in the India–France defence relationship, and France is one of only a handful of partners with which India holds exercises across all three services (VARUNA – navy, GARUDA – air force, SHAKTI – army). The deployment to the Mediterranean, rather than the Indian Ocean, signals an Indian Navy that is operating well beyond its immediate neighbourhood — relevant to any question on India's extended maritime reach and the Indo-Pacific–Mediterranean linkage.

**Key facts / figures:**

- **24th edition**; the India–France bilateral naval exercise began in **1993** and was christened **VARUNA** in **2001**.
- Venue: **Toulon**, southern France (Mediterranean).
- Participating Indian unit: **INS Trishul**, a guided-missile frigate.
- India–France trilateral: the two also run a trilateral format with the **UAE**, and cooperate in the Indian Ocean through France's territories (Réunion, Mayotte) and the **Indian Ocean Commission**, where India is an observer.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (bilateral: India–France); GS3 — Internal/External Security (defence exercises).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the edible oil import duty changes notified with effect from 24 September 2026, consider the following statements:

1. The customs duty on crude sunflower oil has been reduced to nil.
2. The duty on crude palm oil and crude soybean oil has been reduced from 10% to 5%.
3. The Government has removed the duty differential between crude and refined edible oils.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Consider the following statements regarding the Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) Scheme:

1. It was approved by the Union Cabinet with a total outlay of ₹1 lakh crore spread over six years.
2. Support under the scheme is extended only as outright grants to government research laboratories.
3. The Technology Development Board functions as a Second Level Fund Manager under the scheme.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** Exercise VARUNA, recently in the news, is a bilateral maritime exercise conducted between India and which one of the following countries?

(a) United Kingdom
(b) France
(c) Russia
(d) Japan

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a)** 1 and 2 only.
Statement 3 is incorrect: PIB states the Government has *maintained* an import duty differential of 19.25% between crude and refined edible oils — refined palm and soybean oil were cut from 32.5% to 27.5% and refined sunflower to 22.5%, precisely so that the wedge protecting domestic refining survives. Statements 1 and 2 match the notified rates effective 24 September 2026.

**Q2 — (b)** 1 and 3 only.
Statement 2 is incorrect and is the trap: the RDI Scheme's defining feature is that it is *not* a grant scheme for government labs. It supplies long-tenure, collateral-free loans at 3–4% interest for 12–15 years (capped at 50% of project cost), equity up to 25%, and convertible hybrid instruments, aimed at **private-sector-led** deep-tech R&D. Cabinet approval came on 1 July 2025 with ₹1 lakh crore over six years, and TDB acts as a Second Level Fund Manager.

**Q3 — (b)** France.
VARUNA is the India–France bilateral naval exercise; the series began in 1993 and was named VARUNA in 2001. The 24th edition is being held at Toulon with INS Trishul participating. For contrast: **INDRA** is with Russia, **KONKAN** with the UK, and **JIMEX** with Japan.
