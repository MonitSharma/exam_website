## 1. Ministry of Health and Family Welfare: **Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY completes eight years — over 60 crore people covered, 13.25 crore hospital admissions funded**

**What it is.** **Ayushman Bharat – Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY)** completed **eight years on 23 September 2026**. The Prime Minister and Union Health Minister **J. P. Nadda** marked the day — themed by the Ministry as **"Sankalp for Seva"** — with a consolidated release of coverage, utilisation and expenditure data by the **National Health Authority (NHA)**.

**Why it matters.** PM-JAY is the world's largest government-funded health assurance scheme and the flagship instrument of India's **financial-protection** approach to Universal Health Coverage. Its eighth-year data set is the standard evidence base for GS2 questions on out-of-pocket expenditure, catastrophic health spending and the public–private mix in health delivery. The two recent extensions — **all citizens aged 70+ irrespective of income** and **frontline health workers** — are the exam-relevant design shifts, because they move PM-JAY away from a purely deprivation-based (SECC 2011) targeting logic.

**Key facts.**
- Launched **23 September 2018 at Ranchi, Jharkhand**; provides **₹5 lakh per family per year** of secondary and tertiary hospitalisation cover.
- **Over 60 crore people** now covered; **48.54 crore+ Ayushman cards** created.
- **13.25 crore hospital admissions** funded; treatment value **over ₹2.03 lakh crore**.
- Network of **39,000+ empanelled public and private hospitals**; **1,961 procedures** across **27 medical specialities**.
- **1.36 crore Ayushman Vay Vandana cards** issued to senior citizens aged 70+ (as of 21 September 2026).
- **44.81 lakh Ayushman cards** issued to **ASHAs, anganwadi workers and anganwadi helpers**.
- Sits alongside **Ayushman Arogya Mandirs** (primary care), the **Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM)** and **PM-ABHIM** in the Ayushman Bharat architecture.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Ayushman Bharat – PM-JAY**

- **Ministry:** Health and Family Welfare; implementing body **National Health Authority (NHA)**, with State Health Agencies.
- **Objective:** reduce catastrophic out-of-pocket health expenditure by providing cashless, paperless, portable secondary and tertiary hospitalisation cover to the poorest 40% of households, plus the newly added categories.
- **Key features:** (i) **₹5 lakh per family per year**, no cap on family size or age; (ii) **cashless and portable across India** at any empanelled hospital; (iii) **no premium from the beneficiary** — funded on a **60:40 Centre–State** sharing ratio (90:10 for North-Eastern and Himalayan States, 100% for UTs without legislature); (iv) beneficiary base originally drawn from **SECC 2011** deprivation criteria, since expanded to **all 70+ citizens (Vay Vandana)** and frontline health workers.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** 60 crore+ individuals; 48.54 crore cards; 1.36 crore senior-citizen cards.
- **Significance:** the largest single test of whether demand-side financing can substitute for weak public hospital supply — the core of the GS2 debate on health as a public good versus insurance-based purchasing.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance / Health (welfare schemes, UHC); cross-tag GS3 Economy (social-sector expenditure).

## 2. Prime Minister's Office / Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change: **'Project Rhino' announced on World Rhino Day**

**What it is.** On **World Rhino Day, 22 September 2026**, the Prime Minister announced the initiation of **Project Rhino**, a dedicated central programme to support rhino **range states** with **financial and technical assistance** for **protection, habitat management, genetic improvement and the study of population dynamics** of the **greater one-horned rhinoceros (*Rhinoceros unicornis*)**.

**Why it matters.** This adds a fourth species-specific flagship to India's conservation architecture alongside **Project Tiger (1973)**, **Project Elephant (1992)** and **Project Dolphin (2020)**. The species-project model matters for GS3 because it bundles habitat protection, anti-poaching enforcement and Centre-to-State fiscal transfer into one instrument — and because the greater one-horned rhino is the textbook case of a **single-population-concentration risk**: the overwhelming share of the world's animals sits in a handful of Assam protected areas, so one epidemic or one flood year is a systemic threat.

**Key facts.**
- Species: **greater one-horned (Indian) rhinoceros**, IUCN Red List status **Vulnerable**; listed in **Schedule I** of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 and **Appendix I of CITES**.
- India holds roughly **three-quarters of the global population**; **Assam alone accounts for about 80% of the world's greater one-horned rhinos**.
- Indian range states: **Assam, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh** (successful reintroduction at **Dudhwa**) and **Bihar**.
- Population recovery: from roughly **1,500 animals in the 1980s** to **over 4,000 by 2024**.
- Key habitats: **Kaziranga**, Orang, Pobitora and Manas (Assam); **Jaldapara and Gorumara** (West Bengal); **Dudhwa** (UP); **Valmiki** (Bihar).
- Builds on the **National Conservation Strategy for the Greater One-Horned Rhinoceros (2019)** and Assam's anti-poaching drive at Kaziranga.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Project Rhino**

- **Ministry:** Environment, Forest and Climate Change (announced by the PMO).
- **Objective:** secure and expand the greater one-horned rhino population by supporting range states beyond their own budgets.
- **Key features:** (i) **financial and technical support to range states**; (ii) **protection and anti-poaching** strengthening; (iii) **habitat management**, including grassland restoration; (iv) **genetic improvement and population-dynamics monitoring** — i.e. translocation to new habitats to break single-site concentration.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** four range states — Assam, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar.
- **Significance:** a direct precedent-driven answer for the standard GS3 question, "should India continue with species-specific flagship projects or shift to landscape-level conservation?"

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Environment (biodiversity, conservation projects).

## 3. Ministry of External Affairs: **First-ever V4+India Foreign Ministers' Meeting held on the UNGA-81 sidelines**

**What it is.** On **22 September 2026**, on the margins of the **81st session of the UN General Assembly in New York**, External Affairs Minister **Dr S. Jaishankar** took part in the **first V4+India Foreign Ministers' Meeting** — India's inaugural engagement in this format with the **Visegrád Group (V4)**.

**Why it matters.** India's Europe policy has historically run through Brussels, Paris, Berlin and London. A dedicated Central Europe platform is a **minilateral diversification** move: all four V4 states are **EU and NATO members**, so the format gives India four additional votes inside EU decision-making on trade, technology and migration/mobility. The headline deliverable is political — **V4 backing for India's permanent UN Security Council seat**.

**Key facts.**
- **V4 / Visegrád Group** members: **Czech Republic (Czechia), Hungary, Poland, Slovakia**. Formed in **1991 at Visegrád, Hungary**; all four joined the **EU in 2004**.
- Agreed outcomes: **annual Foreign Ministers' meetings**; an **annual V4+India Business Forum**; cooperation in **technology and innovation, defence, research, education and culture, space and connectivity**.
- **Slovak Foreign Minister Juraj Blanár** announced V4 support for **India's bid for permanent membership of the UN Security Council**.
- Held on the sidelines of **UNGA-81**.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India and groupings, bilateral/regional fora, UNSC reform).

## 4. Ministry of Communications (Department of Telecommunications): **Third Investors' Roundtable in Bengaluru for the Gwalior Telecom Manufacturing Zone — about ₹5,500 crore in proposals**

**What it is.** The **Department of Telecommunications (DoT)** hosted its **third Investors' Roundtable** in **Bengaluru on 23 September 2026** to draw manufacturers into India's **first integrated Telecom Manufacturing Zone (TMZ)** at **Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh**. Investment interest of about **₹5,500 crore** was reported at the meet, and the Ministry said the first phase of investor outreach is complete with roughly **24 companies** on board.

**Why it matters.** The TMZ is the physical-infrastructure leg of India's telecom self-reliance push, which until now has rested mainly on the **PLI scheme for telecom and networking products** and on design-led incentives. A dedicated zone — land, common facilities, testing, supply-chain clustering — targets the part of the problem that subsidies alone do not solve. It is also a working example of **Centre–State co-investment** in industrial policy, with the Union Government and Madhya Pradesh sharing the promotion effort.

**Key facts.**
- **India's first integrated Telecom Manufacturing Zone**, located at **Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh**.
- Bengaluru roundtable (**23 September 2026**) was the **third** in the series; earlier rounds were held to build the initial investor pipeline.
- Investment proposals of about **₹5,500 crore** reported; about **24 companies** committed at the close of Phase 1 of outreach.
- Stated priorities: raising **domestic value addition**, creating **intellectual property within India**, and **exporting Indian-designed and Indian-made telecom products**.
- The Minister committed to resolving investor queries relating to the TMZ **within 48 hours** — framed as responsive, single-window governance.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (industrial policy, manufacturing, electronics/telecom); cross-tag GS2 Governance (Centre–State cooperation).

## 5. Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions (DARPG): **Special Campaign 6.0 — preparatory phase on; nationwide drive from 2 to 31 October 2026, with e-waste as the new headline focus**

**What it is.** Ministries and departments across the Union Government are in the **preparatory phase (through 30 September 2026)** of **Special Campaign 6.0**, the sixth edition of the **DARPG-coordinated government-wide drive** to *institutionalise Swachhata and minimise pendency*. The **implementation phase runs 2–31 October 2026**. Through 22–23 September, department after department — Power, Coal, MSME, Textiles, Statistics & Programme Implementation, Agriculture, Fisheries, Drinking Water & Sanitation, CBDT — issued their preparatory-phase releases.

**Why it matters.** This is the most concrete recurring instance of **administrative reform as a measurable annual exercise** — a rare case where "good governance" is reduced to countable outputs (files weeded, square feet freed, grievances disposed, scrap revenue earned). The new element in 6.0 is the explicit **scientific e-waste management** mandate under the **E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022**, which ties the campaign to circular-economy policy rather than just office cleanliness.

**Key facts.**
- **Nodal department:** Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (**DARPG**), Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions. Campaign series running annually since **2021**.
- **Preparatory phase:** mid-September to **30 September 2026**; **implementation phase: 2–31 October 2026**.
- Pendency categories targeted: **MP references, State Government references, Parliamentary Assurances, PMO references, inter-ministerial references and public grievances**.
- New focus in 6.0: **e-waste collection, segregation and scientific disposal** under the **E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022**, plus space management and scrap disposal.
- Illustrative Special Campaign 5.0 output (**Ministry of Coal**, the top-ranked ministry on "Space Freed"): **over 1.13 crore sq ft** of space cleaned, **14,017+ MT of scrap** disposed, **₹56.85 crore** revenue earned, **1,28,527 files** closed and **100% public-grievance disposal**.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance (administrative reform, citizen-centric governance, grievance redressal); cross-tag GS3 Environment (e-waste, circular economy).

## 6. Ministry of Heavy Industries: **European Parliament's ITRE Committee delegation holds industrial-cooperation talks in New Delhi**

**What it is.** On **22 September 2026**, Union Minister for Heavy Industries and Steel **H. D. Kumaraswamy** held talks with an **eight-member delegation of the European Parliament's Committee on Industry, Research and Energy (ITRE)**, including MEPs **Raffaele Stancanelli** and **Angelika Winzig**. The discussion covered **industrial development, clean energy, electric mobility and industrial decarbonisation**.

**Why it matters.** This is the industrial-policy follow-through on the India–EU relationship after the conclusion of **FTA negotiations at the India–EU Summit earlier in 2026**. The substantive ask on India's side is **European participation in battery manufacturing** — a sector where India's **Advanced Chemistry Cell (ACC)** ambitions still depend on imported cell technology. Note also the decarbonisation angle: EU instruments such as **CBAM** make "greening" Indian steel a trade-access question, not only a climate one.

**Key facts.**
- **ITRE** is the European Parliament's standing committee on **industry, research and energy**.
- Indian schemes highlighted: **PLI for Automobiles and Auto Components**, **PLI for Advanced Chemistry Cell (ACC) Battery Storage**, and **PM E-DRIVE** for the EV ecosystem.
- **Global bidding for 10 GWh of ACC manufacturing capacity** is open, with a bid deadline of **13 October 2026**; European industry was invited to participate.
- Steel-sector cooperation discussed through **recycling and circular-economy** routes ("greening" of steel).
- Context: the **India–EU Summit of January 2026** concluded FTA negotiations and adopted a broader strategic-partnership agenda.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (industrial policy, EV/battery manufacturing); cross-tag GS2 International Relations (India–EU).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the **Ayushman Bharat – Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (AB PM-JAY)**, consider the following statements:

1. It provides a health cover of ₹5 lakh per family per year, with no cap on family size or age.
2. The scheme is implemented by the National Health Authority, and beneficiaries pay no premium.
3. Coverage under the Ayushman Vay Vandana component is available to all citizens aged 70 years and above, irrespective of income.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Consider the following statements about the **greater one-horned rhinoceros**, in the news recently:

1. It is listed as *Endangered* on the IUCN Red List.
2. In India, wild populations are found in Assam, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh and Bihar.
3. Assam accounts for the largest share of the global population of the species.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** The **Visegrád Group (V4)**, with which India held its first Foreign Ministers' Meeting recently, consists of:

(a) Austria, Croatia, Slovenia and Serbia
(b) Czech Republic, Hungary, Poland and Slovakia
(c) Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania and Finland
(d) Bulgaria, Romania, Greece and Cyprus

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.**
All three are correct. PM-JAY gives **₹5 lakh per family per year** for secondary and tertiary hospitalisation with **no cap on family size, age or gender**; it is implemented by the **National Health Authority** with State Health Agencies and is **fully government-funded** (60:40 Centre–State, 90:10 for NE/Himalayan States), so the beneficiary pays no premium; and the **Ayushman Vay Vandana** extension covers **all citizens aged 70+ irrespective of income** — 1.36 crore such cards had been issued as of 21 September 2026.

**Q2 — (b) 2 and 3 only.**
Statement 1 is **incorrect**: the greater one-horned rhinoceros (*Rhinoceros unicornis*) is classified **Vulnerable**, not Endangered, on the IUCN Red List (it is in Schedule I of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 and CITES Appendix I). Statement 2 is correct — the four Indian range states are **Assam, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh (Dudhwa) and Bihar**. Statement 3 is correct — **Assam holds roughly 80% of the world's population** of the species, which is exactly the concentration risk **Project Rhino** (announced on World Rhino Day, 22 September 2026) seeks to address through habitat expansion and population-dynamics work.

**Q3 — (b).**
The **Visegrád Group (V4)** comprises the **Czech Republic, Hungary, Poland and Slovakia**. It was formed in **1991 at Visegrád, Hungary**, and all four members joined the EU in 2004. India held the **first V4+India Foreign Ministers' Meeting** on the sidelines of **UNGA-81** on 22 September 2026, where the group backed India's bid for permanent UNSC membership.
