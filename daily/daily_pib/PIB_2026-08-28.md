## 1. PM Modi to visit Uzbekistan on 29–30 August — fourth visit to Tashkent

**Issuing body:** Ministry of External Affairs / PMO (announced 28 August 2026)

The Prime Minister travels to Tashkent tomorrow on a two-day visit at the invitation of President **Shavkat Mirziyoyev**, for delegation-level talks aimed at deepening the India–Uzbekistan strategic partnership. This is his **fourth visit** to Uzbekistan — after 2015, 2016 and 2022 — while President Mirziyoyev visited India in 2018 and 2019.

**Key facts**

- Agenda spans **trade and investment, defence, digital connectivity, energy, education and people-to-people ties**.
- The two leaders will align their national development visions: **Viksit Bharat 2047** and **Uzbekistan-2030**.
- Uzbekistan is one of only **two doubly landlocked countries** in the world (the other is Liechtenstein) — which is exactly why India's connectivity route to Central Asia runs through **Chabahar Port and the INSTC**, bypassing Pakistan.
- Both are members of the **SCO**; India–Central Asia engagement also runs through the India–Central Asia Dialogue and the India–Central Asia Summit format.

**Why it matters:** Central Asia is India's "extended neighbourhood" — the standard UPSC framing. The binding constraint is not political will but **physical connectivity**, and every India–Uzbekistan engagement should be read against that constraint.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India and its neighbourhood / groupings)

## 2. India to host the 18th BRICS Summit on 12–13 September at Bharat Mandapam

**Issuing body:** Ministry of External Affairs (announced 27 August 2026)

India will host the **18th BRICS Summit** in New Delhi, bringing together leaders of BRICS member and partner countries along with outreach invitees. This is the capstone of India's fourth BRICS Chairship, assumed on **1 January 2026**.

**Key facts**

- Theme of India's Presidency: **"Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability"**, framed around the "Humanity First" approach.
- **Over 350 meetings** and high-level engagements have been held across **more than 25 cities** during the Chairship year.
- Cooperation runs on three pillars: **political and security; economic and financial; cultural and people-to-people**.
- India is a **founding member** and has previously chaired BRICS in **2012, 2016 and 2021**. 2026 marks **20 years** since BRICS was established.

**Why it matters:** Connects directly to the ministerial outcomes already banked this month — the Chennai Declaration on science and technology (23 Aug), the 12th Environment Ministers' Meeting (18 Aug), the Communications Ministers' Meeting at Pune (21 Aug) and the Youth Summit at Gandhinagar (20 Aug). Expect a Delhi Declaration consolidating all of these.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (bilateral, regional and global groupings)

## 3. MEA's Nepal flood response: 290 Indians uncontactable, 84 rescued, 24-hour control room

**Issuing body:** Ministry of External Affairs (28 August 2026)

*New development since yesterday's briefing, which covered only the PM's call to Nepal's Prime Minister.* India has moved into a full **rescue-and-relief posture** after the 26 August Nepal–Tibet flash floods.

**Key facts**

- **290 Indian nationals** in Nepal remain uncontactable; tracing and rescue coordination is underway.
- **84 nationals rescued** so far, including **63 workers from the Trishuli-I run-of-river hydroelectric project** and **21 people from Tamil Nadu**.
- A **24-hour control room** has been set up; EAM **S. Jaishankar** is coordinating with the Nepal government, other Union ministries, state governments and tour operators.
- India has dispatched a **second tranche of 37.5 tonnes** of Humanitarian Assistance and Disaster Relief (HADR) material, medicines and food packets (27 August).
- Combined Nepal–Tibet death toll reported at **469**, with about **1,545 missing** on both sides of the border.

**Why it matters:** A textbook case of India's **HADR-led "first responder" doctrine** in the neighbourhood — the same template as Operation Dost (Türkiye), Operation Karuna (Myanmar) and the 2015 Nepal earthquake response. Also a live illustration of **GLOF/flash-flood risk in the Himalayan cryosphere** and the exposure of run-of-river hydel projects.

**Notebook:** GS2 — IR (India–Nepal, HADR diplomacy) | GS3 — Disaster Management

## 4. BIS expands the HUID-based silver hallmarking ecosystem ahead of the festive season

**Issuing body:** Bureau of Indian Standards, Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution (28 August 2026)

BIS has scaled up the **Hallmark Unique Identification (HUID)** system for silver, the same traceability architecture already mandatory for gold jewellery.

**Key facts**

- **341 silver Assaying and Hallmarking Centres (AHCs)** now operating across **102 districts**.
- **More than 25,000 silver jewellers** registered with BIS.
- Since the **Silver HUID portal** was launched on **1 September 2025**, over **77 lakh silver jewellery pieces** have been hallmarked.
- The **six-digit alphanumeric HUID** assures traceability of each silver article; consumers can verify it on the **BIS CARE app**.
- BIS advisory: buy only hallmarked silver from BIS-registered jewellers, and retain a proper invoice.

**Why it matters:** Consumer protection through **standards and traceability** rather than price control — an increasingly common regulatory instrument. Remember the institutional chain: **BIS is the National Standards Body**, established under the **BIS Act, 2016**, under the Department of Consumer Affairs.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (consumer protection, standards, regulatory bodies)

## 5. PIB Fact Check Unit debunks a viral "Bhakra Nangal Dam tilt" video

**Issuing body:** PIB Fact Check Unit, Ministry of Information and Broadcasting (28 August 2026)

A video circulating on social media claimed the **Bhakra Dam wall had developed a tilt of 1.77 inches**, and that a breach could flood cities including Delhi. The **PIB Fact Check Unit** declared the claim **false**.

**Key facts**

- The **actual recorded deflection** cited by PIB was **0.86 inches** (as recorded on 27 August, per the government clarification).
- All monitored parameters — **stress, strain, pore pressure and seepage** — are within prescribed permissible limits; the dam is stated to be completely safe.
- PIB urged the public not to circulate sensational claims that create panic.
- Context: **Bhakra Dam** is on the **Sutlej** at Bhakra village, Bilaspur district, Himachal Pradesh; the **Nangal Dam** lies downstream in Punjab. Both are managed by the **Bhakra Beas Management Board (BBMB)**, a statutory body under the **Punjab Reorganisation Act, 1966**. Nehru famously called such projects the "temples of modern India".

**Why it matters:** Two exam-relevant threads at once — **misinformation and the government's institutional fact-check machinery** (note the Bombay High Court's 2024 striking down of the IT Rules amendment that would have made an FCU's word binding on intermediaries), and **dam safety**, now governed by the **Dam Safety Act, 2021** with the NDSA and NCDS.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (transparency, misinformation, statutory bodies) | GS3 — Disaster Management (dam safety)

## 6. NHRC issues suo motu notice to Maharashtra over newborn deaths in an Amravati hospital fire

**Issuing body:** National Human Rights Commission (27 August 2026)

The NHRC has issued a notice to the **Chief Secretary of Maharashtra** over the deaths of **three newborns** and injuries to **four others** in a fire at the **Sick Newborn Care Unit (SNCU)** of the District Women's Hospital, Amravati.

**Key facts**

- The Commission took **suo motu cognisance** of media reports — one of its core powers.
- The NHRC is a **statutory body** created under the **Protection of Human Rights Act, 1993**; it is *not* a constitutional body.
- Composition: a Chairperson (a former CJI or Supreme Court judge) plus members, including the **Chairpersons of the NCM, NCSC, NCST, NCW, NCPCR, NCBC and the CCPD as deemed members**.
- Its recommendations are **not binding**; it can approach the Supreme Court or a High Court for directions, and cannot inquire into a matter more than one year old.

**Why it matters:** Recurrent fires in neonatal ICUs (Jhansi 2024, Delhi 2024) point to a **fire-safety and hospital-infrastructure governance gap** under the National Health Mission's SNCU programme — a good GS2 health-governance example, and a clean prelims hook on NHRC's powers.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (statutory bodies, health governance) | GS2 — Polity (NHRC)

**Q1.** With reference to BRICS, consider the following statements:

1. India is a founding member of BRICS and assumed its fourth Chairship in January 2026.
2. India had earlier held the BRICS Chairship in 2012, 2016 and 2021.

**Q2.** With reference to Uzbekistan, consider the following statements:

1. It is one of only two doubly landlocked countries in the world.
2. It shares a land border with China.
3. It is a member of the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 and 3 only
- (b) 2 only
- (c) 1 and 2 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** Consider the following statements regarding the Hallmark Unique Identification (HUID) system recently in the news:

1. It is administered by the Bureau of Indian Standards, which functions under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry.
2. The HUID is a six-digit alphanumeric code that enables traceability of an individual jewellery article.
3. Consumers can verify a HUID number using the BIS CARE mobile application.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

## Answers

**Q1 — (d).** All three are correct. India is a founding member of BRIC (2006; South Africa joined in 2010 to make it BRICS), assumed its fourth Chairship on 1 January 2026, chaired earlier in 2012, 2016 and 2021, and its Presidency theme is "Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability". The 18th Summit is scheduled for 12–13 September 2026 at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi.

**Q2 — (a).** Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Uzbekistan and Liechtenstein are the world's only two **doubly landlocked** countries (every neighbour is itself landlocked). Statement 2 is wrong — Uzbekistan borders **Afghanistan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and Turkmenistan**, but **not China**. It is a full SCO member.

**Q3 — (b).** Statements 2 and 3 are correct. Statement 1 is wrong on the ministry: **BIS, the National Standards Body established under the BIS Act, 2016, functions under the Department of Consumer Affairs, Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution** — not Commerce and Industry. The HUID is a six-digit alphanumeric code and is verifiable on the BIS CARE app.
