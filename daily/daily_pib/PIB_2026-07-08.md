# Daily PIB Briefing — 8 July 2026

## 1. Ministry of Tribal Affairs launches TribeX; National Workshop on Tribal Research Institutes adopts the Bhubaneswar Declaration
**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Tribal Affairs

A two-day National Workshop on Strengthening Tribal Research Institutes (TRIs) concluded in Bhubaneswar, Odisha (7–8 July), inaugurated by Union Tribal Affairs Minister Jual Oram. It reviewed the functioning of TRIs across states and charted a roadmap to turn them into hubs for tribal knowledge, culture and policy support. The Minister launched **TribeX**, a digital learning platform for tribal arts, culture, languages and traditional knowledge, and an MoU was signed with Sampurnanand Sanskrit Vishwavidyalaya, Varanasi. The workshop closed with the **Bhubaneswar Declaration**, a shared vision for reforming TRIs into future-ready, evidence-based development institutions.

**Key facts:** ~200 participants (TRIs, state tribal welfare departments, academia, NGOs, industry); Minister of State Durgadas Uikey and NITI Aayog member Dr R. Balasubramaniam also participated.

**Destination notebook:** GS1 Society/Culture; GS2 Governance (welfare institutions).

### Scheme-in-focus: TribeX
- **Ministry:** Ministry of Tribal Affairs
- **Objective:** A dedicated digital learning platform to document, preserve and disseminate tribal arts, culture, languages and traditional knowledge systems.
- **Key features:** (1) Digital repository of tribal knowledge and heritage; (2) Learning modules linked with Tribal Research Institutes; (3) Academic partnership (MoU with Sampurnanand Sanskrit Vishwavidyalaya) for research and language documentation; (4) Feeds into the broader TRI-strengthening roadmap under the Bhubaneswar Declaration.
- **Beneficiaries/Coverage:** Tribal communities, researchers, students, and the 25+ state/UT Tribal Research Institutes network.
- **Significance:** First dedicated digital knowledge platform of its kind for tribal heritage; supports evidence-based policymaking and safeguards intangible tribal culture.

## 2. Government notifies Navi Mumbai Airport as a port for import of drugs under the Drugs Rules, 1945
**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (CDSCO)

The Health Ministry amended Rule 43A of the Drugs Rules, 1945 to add Navi Mumbai (NMIA) to the list of airports through which drugs/pharmaceutical consignments can be legally imported into India. The notification follows consultation with the Drugs Technical Advisory Board under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940, and is aimed at easing pharma trade logistics and giving importers an additional entry point.

**Key facts:** Total notified ports of entry for drug imports (air/sea/road/rail) now stands at 42.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 Governance/Health regulation; GS3 Economy (pharma supply chain).

## 3. Department of Commerce holds Chintan Shivir on seafood export opportunities under India–EFTA TEPA
**Ministry/Body:** Department of Commerce, Ministry of Commerce & Industry

On the sidelines of Seafood Expo Bharat 2026 in Chennai, the Commerce Department convened a high-level brainstorming session ("Chintan Shivir") to help exporters tap tariff concessions for seafood under the India–European Free Trade Association (EFTA) Trade and Economic Partnership Agreement (TEPA) — India's first trade pact with Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland.

**Key facts:** TEPA aims to attract USD 100 billion in investment and create 1 million direct jobs over 15 years; Iceland has scrapped its 55% duty on fish feed, Switzerland has cut duty on fish fats/oils from 18.05% to zero, and Norway has eliminated its 13.16% duty on fish/shrimp feed.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 Economy (trade agreements, agri-exports).

## 4. India and Rwanda hold 2nd Joint Defence Cooperation Committee (JDCC) meeting in New Delhi
**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Defence / Ministry of External Affairs

The 2nd India–Rwanda JDCC meeting (6–7 July) was co-chaired by Joint Secretary (International Cooperation) Amitabh Prasad and Brig Gen Louis Kanobayire. Both sides agreed to deepen cooperation in military training, joint exercises, medical collaboration and defence industry engagement, and adopted an implementation plan with timelines. The Indian delegation (Defence, Defence Production, DRDO, MEA, Armed Forces Medical Services, HQ IDS and Services) also briefed Rwanda on India's defence-industrial ecosystem.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 International Relations; GS3 Defence/Internal Security.

## 5. Fisheries Ministry reviews Odisha schemes ahead of VP's launch of High-Seas Fishing Authorisation and Odisha Deep Sea Fishing Mission
**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying

Union Minister Rajiv Ranjan Singh chaired a review meeting (8 July) on implementation of central fisheries and aquaculture schemes in Odisha, ahead of a national launch event on 9 July in Bhubaneswar. Vice-President C.P. Radhakrishnan is set to launch the **Letter of Authorisation (LoA) for Sustainable Harnessing of Fisheries in the High Seas** — a digitised, time-bound authorisation process for Fish Farmer Producer Organisations (FFPOs) and cooperatives to access deep-sea and high-seas fishing — along with Odisha's **Deep Sea Fishing Mission (2026–2036)** document, a Blue Economy initiative approved by the Odisha Cabinet (~₹2,295 crore) to build the state into a deep-sea fishing and marine-export hub.

**Key facts:** ~1,000 fish farmers/fishers expected at the launch; LoAs to be distributed to fisheries cooperatives and vessel owners.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 Economy/Agriculture (Blue Economy, allied sector).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** Consider the following statements regarding the India–European Free Trade Association (EFTA) Trade and Economic Partnership Agreement (TEPA):
1. EFTA comprises Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland.
2. TEPA is India's first trade agreement with the EFTA bloc.
3. Under TEPA, EFTA states have committed to facilitate USD 100 billion in investment into India over 15 years.

How many of the statements given above are correct?
(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) All three
(d) None

**Q2.** The "Letter of Authorisation (LoA) for Sustainable Harnessing of Fisheries in the High Seas," recently in the news, is associated with which of the following?
(a) Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways, for regulating coastal shipping routes
(b) Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying, to enable FFPOs/cooperatives to access deep-sea and high-seas fishing
(c) Ministry of Earth Sciences, for deep-sea mineral exploration licensing
(d) Ministry of External Affairs, for maritime boundary agreements with neighbouring states

**Q3.** With reference to the Drugs Rules, 1945, consider the following statements:
1. The Rules are framed under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940.
2. They specify the ports (air/sea/road/rail) through which drugs may be legally imported into India.
3. Amendments to add new ports of entry require prior consultation with the Drugs Technical Advisory Board.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1. (c) All three** — EFTA = Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland; TEPA (signed 2024, provisions being operationalised through 2026) is India's first pact with EFTA; it targets USD 100 billion investment and 1 million jobs over 15 years.

**Q2. (b)** — The LoA scheme, launched by the Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying (with the VP launching it nationally from Bhubaneswar), gives Fish Farmer Producer Organisations and cooperatives a digitised, time-bound authorisation to access high-seas and deep-sea fishing resources.

**Q3. (d) 1, 2 and 3** — The Drugs Rules, 1945 are subordinate legislation under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940; Rule 43A lists the notified ports of entry for drug imports (now 42 after adding Navi Mumbai Airport); such amendments follow consultation with the Drugs Technical Advisory Board.
