## 1. Prime Minister's Office / Ministry of Railways: **PM dedicates projects worth over ₹35,000 crore at Vadodara; the 2,800 km Dedicated Freight Corridor is complete**

**Ministry/Body:** Prime Minister's Office; projects executed largely through the Ministry of Railways and DFCCIL. Venue: Maharaja Sayajirao University ground, Vadodara, Gujarat — the *'Namo for Viksit Bharat'* programme.

**What it is.** On 8 September 2026 the Prime Minister laid foundation stones for, inaugurated and dedicated to the nation development projects worth **more than ₹35,000 crore** in Vadodara. The headline announcement was the **completion of the 2,800-kilometre Dedicated Freight Corridor (DFC)** — the Eastern and Western corridors together — with the final links connected in the Vadodara region.

**Why it matters.** The DFC is the single largest freight-infrastructure project of the post-2014 period and the physical backbone of the PM Gati Shakti logistics agenda. Its whole logic is **segregation of freight from passenger traffic** on saturated trunk routes, which raises average freight speed, frees passenger paths on the parallel Golden Quadrilateral rail routes, and lowers logistics cost as a share of GDP — the standing target that ties this to the **National Logistics Policy**.

**Key facts and figures (from the release):**

- Projects dedicated/inaugurated/foundation-stoned: **over ₹35,000 crore**.
- **2,800 km** Dedicated Freight Corridor now complete (Eastern + Western DFC).
- Segregating passenger and freight traffic allows **over 430 cargo trains to run daily** on the corridors.
- A journey that took a truck **30 hours** is cut to **10–12 hours** by DFC freight.
- The PM framed the projects under the **'Saptadhara'** (seven streams) resolution announced from the Red Fort, and around the Gati Shakti connectivity push; he described Vadodara as the city of **Gati Shakti Vishwavidyalaya**, where the final links of the corridor were joined. Framing line: *"From chips to ships, India is now building a new manufacturing future."*

**Static hooks worth revising (reference, not from the release):** DFCCIL — Dedicated Freight Corridor Corporation of India Ltd — is a PSU under the Ministry of Railways set up to build and operate the corridors. The **Western DFC** runs broadly from **JNPT (Mumbai) to Dadri**, the **Eastern DFC** from **Ludhiana to Sonnagar**; the EDFC includes the Khurja–Bhaupur stretch and the world-class **Sonnagar–Dankuni** extension planning. Gati Shakti Vishwavidyalaya at Vadodara is the former **National Rail and Transportation Institute**, redesignated as a central university for transportation in 2022. Link this to **PM Gati Shakti National Master Plan (2021)** and the **National Logistics Policy (2022)**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (infrastructure, logistics, freight); GS3 Environment (modal shift from road to rail and emissions); GS1 Geography (industrial corridors and freight geography).

## 2. Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship: **PM-SETU — 5th National Steering Committee approves Strategic Investment Plans worth ₹735.70 crore; cumulative sanction reaches ₹2,171 crore**

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE), Directorate General of Training (DGT). The 5th National Steering Committee (NSC) meeting was chaired by **Smt. Debashree Mukherjee, Secretary, MSDE**, and attended by DG (DGT) Shri Dilip Kumar, State government officials, multilateral development bank representatives and industry partners.

**What it is.** The NSC of **PM-SETU — Pradhan Mantri Skilling and Employability Transformation through Upgraded ITIs** — approved **Strategic Investment Plans (SIPs)** with an aggregate outlay of **₹735.70 crore** for three ITI clusters, in **Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh and Telangana**. This takes the total sanctioned investment under PM-SETU to **₹2,171 crore across 9 ITI clusters**.

**Why it matters.** PM-SETU is the clearest current example of an **industry-led, cluster-based public institution reform**: rather than the Centre funding ITIs directly, an *anchor industry partner* co-invests, co-designs courses and takes responsibility for outcomes in a **Hub-and-Spoke** cluster of government ITIs. That structure — Centre + State + industry as equity-like partners in a public training institution — is the examinable idea, and it maps directly onto the long-standing GS2/GS3 question of why Indian vocational training has weak employability outcomes.

**Key facts and figures:**

| Cluster | Anchor / proposer | Outlay | Hub → Spokes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bhiwadi, Rajasthan** | M/s H.G. Infra Engineering Ltd (HGIEL) | **₹241.00 crore** | Govt ITI Bhiwadi (Hub) → Alwar, Neemrana, Tijara, Kishangarh Bas |
| **Medchal, Telangana** | M/s ZEN Technologies Ltd | **₹254.30 crore** | Govt ITI Medchal (Hub) → Alwal, Shameerpet, Kukunoorpally, Siddipet |
| **Meerut, Uttar Pradesh** | National Skill Development Corporation (NSDC) | **₹240.40 crore** | Govt ITI Saket, Meerut (Hub) → Ghaziabad, Hapur, Gautam Buddha Nagar, Baghpat |

- The Telangana SIP had been **deferred at the 4th NSC** pending clarifications on operational and financial aspects; it was approved after the anchor industry partner satisfied the Committee on course structure, financial calculations, eligibility, documentation and civil works.
- A **Shareholders' Agreement (SHA)** was signed among **H.G. Infra Engineering Ltd, the Government of Rajasthan and MSDE** — making **Rajasthan the first State in the country to execute an SHA under PM-SETU**, and formally commencing implementation for the Bhiwadi cluster.
- SIPs are first evaluated and recommended by the respective **State Steering Committees** before going to the National Steering Committee — a two-tier approval chain worth remembering.

### Scheme-in-focus: **PM-SETU (Pradhan Mantri Skilling and Employability Transformation through Upgraded ITIs)**

- **Ministry:** Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE), implemented through the Directorate General of Training.
- **Objective:** transform the Industrial Training Institute (ITI) ecosystem through industry partnerships, modern infrastructure, upgraded training programmes and improved employability outcomes.
- **Key features:**
  1. **Industry-led, cluster-based Hub-and-Spoke model** — one Hub ITI supported by a network of Spoke ITIs, enabling coordinated investment and delivery of industry-relevant skills.
  2. **Strategic Investment Plans (SIPs)** submitted by anchor industry partners or institutional partners such as NSDC, cleared first by a State Steering Committee and then by the National Steering Committee.
  3. Funding covers **infrastructure modernisation, upgradation of existing trades, new long-term and short-term courses, and strengthening operational capabilities**.
  4. Formal **Shareholders' Agreements** between the industry partner, the State government and the Centre lock in commitments; multilateral development banks participate in the governance process.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage so far:** **9 ITI clusters**, cumulative sanctioned investment **₹2,171 crore**; the three clusters cleared on 7–8 September cover **15 government ITIs** in Rajasthan, Telangana and Uttar Pradesh.
- **Significance:** shifts ITI reform from a grant-based model to a co-investment model in which the employer has skin in the game, and is the operative answer to the "skills mismatch" critique in GS3 Economy.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance (Centre–State–private institutional design, human resource development); GS3 Economy (skilling, employment, manufacturing competitiveness).

## 3. Ministry of Communications (Department of Telecommunications): **Financial Fraud Risk Indicator has prevented suspected cyber-fraud transactions of over ₹5,000 crore**

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Communications, **Department of Telecommunications (DoT)** — under its **Digital Intelligence Platform (DIP)**.

**What it is.** DoT announced that its **Financial Fraud Risk Indicator (FRI)** has helped prevent suspected cyber-fraud losses of **more than ₹5,000 crore** in the **fifteen months** since its launch on **22 May 2025**. FRI is a real-time risk-assessment framework that tells a bank, payment app, insurer or securities intermediary how likely it is that a given **mobile number** is associated with cyber-crime or financial fraud — *before* the transaction goes through.

**Why it matters.** This is the cleanest available illustration of the shift from **reactive recovery to preventive interdiction** in cyber-fraud policy, and of **telecom intelligence being fused into financial-sector risk systems**. Money stopped at source never enters the mule-account chain, so there is no FIR, no freeze order, no tracing. For Mains, it is a ready example of a "whole-of-government", cross-regulator architecture (DoT + RBI + NPCI + SEBI + I4C) built on a citizen-reporting front end.

**Key facts and figures:**

- Cumulative suspected fraud prevented: **₹5,043.73 crore as of August 2026**, up from **₹139.16 crore in August 2025** and **₹660 crore in the first six months** — roughly an **eight-fold scaling**.
- **Over ₹2,000 crore** of that was prevented in the **four months from April 2026** alone.
- FRI classifies mobile numbers into **three risk categories — Medium, High and Very High** — based on the likelihood of involvement in financial fraud.
- Inputs come from **citizen reports on Sanchar Saathi**, the **National Cybercrime Reporting Portal (NCRP)** of the **Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C)**, and intelligence shared by telecom operators, banks and financial institutions, plus other telecom parameters.
- **1,600+ organisations** are on the Digital Intelligence Platform; **25+ training sessions** have been conducted covering **1,500 banks, financial institutions and regulators**.
- Coverage is being extended beyond banking to **securities market intermediaries** (trading and demat accounts), **insurance entities** (policy issuance and claims) and **pension ecosystem stakeholders**.
- Coordination partners named: **RBI, NPCI, SEBI** and other financial-sector institutions.
- Companion citizen-facing tools in DoT's stack: **Sanchar Saathi**, **Chakshu** (reporting suspected fraud communication), **CEIR** (blocking/tracing lost or stolen handsets) and **ASTR**. Cyber-fraud helpline: **1930**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security (cyber security, cyber-crime architecture); GS3 Economy (digital payments integrity); GS2 Governance (inter-agency and public-private coordination, citizen participation / *Jan Bhagidari*).

## 5. NITI Aayog: **PACT and the Zero Emission Truck (ZET) Marketplace launched at the 5th e-FAST India Summit**

**Ministry/Body:** NITI Aayog. Inaugural address by **Shri Rajiv Gauba, Member, NITI Aayog**, at the **5th e-FAST India Summit 2026** (7 September 2026).

**What it is.** Two linked platforms were launched: the **Platform for Aggregating Clean Transport (PACT)** and the **Zero Emission Truck (ZET) Marketplace**. Both are designed to unblock the electrification of **heavy-duty freight** through **demand aggregation, financing innovation and corridor-based projectisation**.

**Why it matters.** Freight decarbonisation is the hardest part of India's transport transition, and this release supplies the exact numbers that make the argument: a tiny slice of the fleet produces a disproportionate share of emissions, while sales of electric heavy trucks are still negligible. The diagnosis offered — **fragmentation among logistics operators, high financing costs, and absence of coordinated corridor planning** — is a ready-made structure for a Mains answer on why EV policy has not reached trucking.

**Key facts and figures:**

- **Heavy trucks are only 3–4% of the vehicle fleet but contribute over one-third of transport-sector carbon emissions.**
- **Around 800 heavy-duty electric trucks** were sold in India in **2025**.
- **70% of India's freight moves by road**, making corridor electrification the strategic lever.
- Existing policy stack cited: **PLI schemes for Automobiles and for Advanced Chemistry Cells**, charging infrastructure expansion by Oil Marketing Companies, and demand-side schemes — **FAME, PM e-DRIVE, PARIVARTAN and PM e-Bus Sewa**.
- Instruments called for: **blended finance, leasing models and stronger data systems**, to bring the cost of capital for electric trucks closer to diesel equivalents.
- Framing: electrification as an **economic, environmental and strategic imperative** tied to **Net Zero by 2070** and **Viksit Bharat @2047**; the state's role described as building systems in which markets discover outcomes rather than allocating outcomes directly.

**Static hook (reference):** **e-FAST India** — the Electric Freight Accelerator for Sustainable Transport – India — is a NITI Aayog-anchored national electric freight platform, running since 2022; Shri Gauba noted it has evolved over **four years** from a dialogue forum into a vehicle of coordinated action.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Environment (transport emissions, net-zero pathway); GS3 Economy (logistics, EV industrial policy, blended finance).

## 6. PIB Backgrounder (Ministry of Textiles / National Jute Board): **Jute — India's Golden Fibre**

**Ministry/Body:** PIB Research Unit backgrounder, 7 September 2026; the sector is administered by the **Ministry of Textiles** through the **National Jute Board (NJB)** and the **Jute Corporation of India (JCI)**.

**What it is.** A consolidated backgrounder on India's jute economy — production, trade, history, the geotextile application, and the full stack of government interventions. This is exactly the kind of PIB document Prelims questions get lifted from.

**Why it matters.** Jute sits at the intersection of three examinable themes: a **small and marginal farmer crop** in the eastern region, an **MSP-and-mandatory-packaging protected industry**, and a **biodegradable substitute for plastics** with a technical-textile future in geotextiles.

**Production and trade (FY 2025-26 unless stated):**

- **76 lakh bales** of raw jute produced; **12.80 lakh MT** of jute goods produced.
- **Jute and mesta production rose from 88.02 lakh bales (2024-25) to 94.03 lakh bales (2025-26)** — Third Advance Estimate of Foodgrain Production 2025-26.
- India is the **world's largest producer of raw jute** and the leading producer of jute goods, accounting for about **75% of estimated world production** (as of July 2026).
- **Exports:** raw jute **22,700 MT worth ₹225.11 crore**; jute goods **1,71,300 MT worth ₹3,155.27 crore**.
- **Domestic consumption** of jute goods **12.05 lakh MT = 94%** of total production — India is *Atmanirbhar* in jute goods.
- **120 composite jute mills** as of December 2025, of which **West Bengal has 89** — the highest of any State.
- Employment: about **3.70 lakh workers** in organised mills and diversified jute units; livelihoods of nearly **40 lakh farm families**.

**Crop geography and agronomy:**

- Leading States: **West Bengal**, followed by **Bihar, Assam, Odisha and Jharkhand**.
- Sown **March–April**, harvested in **100–110 days**; needs hot, humid conditions with **700–1,500 mm** rainfall in the growing period; predominantly **rainfed**; grown mainly by **small and marginal farmers**.
- **Mesta** is a bast fibre grown as a cost-effective jute alternative in **drier agro-climatic regions**; jute and mesta are together classified as **raw jute** for trade purposes.

**History (useful for GS1/GS3 linkage):** jute traces to the third century BC but became commercial under colonial rule, with East India Company mills concentrated in Bengal. Partition in 1947 left roughly **80% of jute-growing area in East Pakistan** while most mills remained in India; the **"Grow More Jute" campaign (September 1949)** more than doubled raw jute production from **6.7 lakh tonnes (1951-52) to 14.56 lakh tonnes (1961-62)**.

**Government interventions — the examinable list:**

1. **Minimum Support Price:** raw jute MSP fixed at **₹5,925 per quintal for FY 2026-27**, giving a **61.8% return** over the all-India weighted average cost of production — up about **147%** from **₹2,400/quintal in FY 2014-15**. MSP payments to jute farmers rose from **₹441 crore (FY 2004-05 to 2013-14)** to **₹1,342 crore (FY 2014-15 to 2025-26)**.
2. **Jute Packaging Materials (Compulsory Use in Packing Commodities) Act** — mandates jute packaging for **100% of foodgrains and 20% of sugar**.
3. **Jute Corporation of India (JCI)** — sole nodal agency implementing the MSP for raw jute; procures directly from farmers through about **110 Departmental Purchase Centres (DPCs)** in West Bengal, Assam, Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha and Tripura.
4. **National Jute Development Programme (NJDP)** — umbrella scheme implemented by the National Jute Board, outlay **₹485.58 crore for FY 2021-22 to FY 2025-26**, with two components: **JUTE-ICARE** and the **Jute Diversification Scheme**.
   - **JUTE-ICARE (Improved Cultivation and Advanced Retting Exercise)**, launched **FY 2015-16** — subsidised certified seed, field demonstrations, mechanisation and improved retting, with **CRIJAF** and **JCI**; retting ponds built through convergence with **PMKSY** and **RKVY**.
   - **Jute Diversification Scheme** — **Jute Raw Material Bank (JRMB)** supplying raw jute at mill-gate price plus transport to artisans, women SHGs and MSMEs; **30% capital subsidy** on plant and machinery for jute mills and MSMEs making jute diversified products (JDPs); **Jute Resource-cum-Production Centres**; **Jute Retail Outlets** with **25% sales assistance up to ₹9 lakh per unit**; and a **production-linked incentive for JDP exports**.
5. **Jute Mark India logo** — certifies quality compliance and verifies jute content in blended products.
6. **Market promotion:** over **57 regional jute fairs** in FY 2025-26 including India International Trade Fair, Surajkund Mela and Bharat Tex.

**Two showcase items:**

- **Jute Geotextile (JGT)** — a technical textile used for slope protection on embankments, hill cuttings, riverbanks and canals; road, haul-road and railway formation (improving load distribution and controlling settlement); concealed drainage in hilly regions; and soft-soil stabilisation via prefabricated vertical drains. It is **fully biodegradable**, lowers soil temperature and aids seed germination — the NJB provides design, procurement, testing and installation support.
- **Commonwealth Games 2026** — the Indian contingent wore **jute-viscose blended apparel** developed by the **National Jute Board** and designed by **NIFT, New Delhi**, showcasing 100% biodegradable jute-viscose fabric.
- **Jute leaves as food:** consumed as a vegetable in Africa, West Asia and Southeast Asia and as a health food in Japan; rich in **vitamins, carotenoids, calcium, potassium and dietary fibre**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (commercial crops, MSP, cropping geography); GS3 Economy (MSMEs, exports, technical textiles); GS3 Environment (biodegradable alternatives to plastics); GS1 Geography (eastern India agro-climatic regions).

## Answers

**Q1 — (b) 2 and 3 only.**
Statement 1 is incorrect: FRI is an initiative of the **Department of Telecommunications (DoT)**, Ministry of Communications, launched on **22 May 2025** under its Digital Intelligence Platform. RBI, NPCI and SEBI are collaborating institutions that consume the FRI signal, not its developers. Statements 2 and 3 are correct — FRI classifies mobile numbers into **Medium, High and Very High** risk bands, drawing on Sanchar Saathi citizen reports, the **NCRP of I4C**, and telecom-operator and financial-institution intelligence. Cumulative suspected fraud prevented reached **₹5,043.73 crore by August 2026**.

**Q2 — (c) 1 and 3 only.**
Statement 1 is correct: India accounts for about **75%** of estimated world jute-goods production and is also the largest producer of raw jute. Statement 2 is incorrect: **JUTE-ICARE** is a component of the **National Jute Development Programme**, implemented by the **National Jute Board under the Ministry of Textiles**, in collaboration with CRIJAF and the Jute Corporation of India — not by the Ministry of Agriculture. Statement 3 is correct: of **120 composite jute mills** in India (as of December 2025), **89 are in West Bengal**.

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.**
All three are correct. **PACT** and the **ZET Marketplace** were launched by **Shri Rajiv Gauba, Member, NITI Aayog**, at the **5th e-FAST India Summit 2026** on 7 September. They are designed to close the gap created by fragmentation among logistics operators, high financing costs and the absence of coordinated corridor planning, through **demand aggregation, financing innovation and corridor-based projectisation**. Heavy trucks are **3–4%** of the fleet but contribute **over one-third** of transport-sector carbon emissions; only about **800 heavy-duty electric trucks** were sold in India in 2025, and **70%** of India's freight moves by road.
