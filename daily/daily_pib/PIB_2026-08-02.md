## 1. PM launches the 'Nasha Mukt Yuva for Viksit Bharat Sankalp Abhiyan'

**Issuing body:** Prime Minister's Office; implemented with the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports and the Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment (which runs the parent Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan).

**What it is:** On 2 August 2026 the Prime Minister launched, via video conferencing, a nationwide *Jan Bhagidari* (people's participation) campaign against substance abuse, framed around youth as agents of social change on the road to Viksit Bharat 2047.

**Why it matters:** This is the largest youth-facing demand-reduction push yet mounted by the Centre, and it shifts the anti-drug effort from an awareness *programme* to a sustained, calendarised *movement*. For UPSC it sits at the intersection of GS2 (government policies and interventions for vulnerable sections; role of civil society) and GS3 (internal security — the narcotics-trafficking dimension addressed elsewhere by NCB and the NDPS Act).

**Key features**

- Duration: a **100-week** nationwide campaign.
- Rhythm: activities every **Sunday** across the country.
- Activity basket: sports events, walkathons, meditation sessions, cultural programmes, nukkad nataks (street plays), awareness drives, art competitions, discussion forums and community engagement.
- Stated scale: aims to bring together **over 1 crore (10 million) youth across roughly 10,000 locations** in a collective pledge against substance abuse.
- Framing: "a drug-free youth is essential for Viksit Bharat."

**Static linkage worth revising:** Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan (launched 2020, Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment); NDPS Act, 1985; Narcotics Control Bureau (MHA); National Action Plan for Drug Demand Reduction (NAPDDR).

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance & Social Justice (also usable as GS1 Society material on youth and social change).

## 2. National Biodiversity Authority releases ₹8.22 crore for rice under Access and Benefit Sharing

**Issuing body:** National Biodiversity Authority (NBA), a statutory body under the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change.

**What it is:** The NBA has disbursed **₹8.22 crore** collected as Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) dues to three research institutions and **33 State Biodiversity Boards / UT Biodiversity Councils**. Reports describe it as one of the **largest single ABS disbursements for rice** in India.

**Where the money came from:** The amount was paid by **M/s Pioneer Overseas Corporation**, an international seed company, for accessing Indian **rice (*Oryza sativa*) varieties** to develop and commercialise hybrid seeds. This is the ABS principle in live operation — a commercial user of Indian genetic resources paying back into the conservation chain.

**How it was split**

| Head | Amount |
|---|---|
| To research institutions that conserved/shared the varieties | **₹2.47 crore** |
| — of which **ICAR-Indian Institute of Rice Research, Hyderabad** | **₹2.43 crore** |
| To 33 State Biodiversity Boards / UT Biodiversity Councils (apportioned by **rice-growing area**) | **₹5.75 crore** |

Top State shares: **Uttar Pradesh** (highest, over ₹73 lakh), **West Bengal** ₹64.95 lakh, **Telangana** ₹50.60 lakh, **Odisha** ₹48.09 lakh, **Chhattisgarh** ₹45.91 lakh, **Punjab** ₹37.15 lakh, **Bihar** ₹36.93 lakh, **Madhya Pradesh** ₹36.16 lakh, **Assam** ₹27.79 lakh, **Andhra Pradesh** ₹25.88 lakh.

**Why it matters:** ABS is the third objective of the **Convention on Biological Diversity (1992)** and the operative core of the **Nagoya Protocol (2010)**, domesticated in India through the **Biological Diversity Act, 2002** (amended 2023). Money actually flowing to State Boards — and being distributed by rice acreage rather than by political weight — is the clearest evidence yet that India's three-tier biodiversity architecture (NBA → State Biodiversity Boards → Biodiversity Management Committees) can convert a legal obligation into conservation finance.

**Static linkage:** NBA is headquartered at **Chennai**; Section 21 of the Biological Diversity Act governs benefit sharing; BMCs maintain **People's Biodiversity Registers (PBRs)**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Biodiversity (also GS3 Agriculture, for the germplasm/seed-industry angle).

## 3. Coal production grows 7.51% in July 2026; dispatch surges 17.34%

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Coal (provisional data).

**What it is:** Monthly provisional coal statistics for July 2026.

**Key figures**

- **Production (July 2026): 69.75 million tonnes (MT)** — up **7.51%** over July 2025 (64.88 MT).
- **Dispatch (July 2026): 86.33 MT** — up **17.34%** over July 2025 (73.57 MT).
- **Coal India Limited (CIL): 50.35 MT**, up **8.42%** year-on-year.
- **Cumulative FY 2026-27 (April–July): production 302.24 MT; dispatch 354.70 MT**, the latter up **5.87%** year-on-year.

**Why it matters:** Dispatch growing more than twice as fast as production is the analytically interesting bit — it signals **destocking at pitheads** and improved rail evacuation, which is what actually determines whether power plants hold adequate stock through the monsoon. Coal still supplies the bulk of India's electricity generation, so these numbers feed directly into energy-security and import-substitution arguments, and sit awkwardly alongside the renewable-capacity narrative — a tension examiners like.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure & Energy).

## 4. Handloom Hackathon 2.0 concludes at IIT Delhi

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Textiles (Office of the Development Commissioner for Handlooms), in collaboration with the **Foundation for Innovation and Technology Transfer (FITT), IIT Delhi**.

**What it is:** The second edition of the Handloom Hackathon — themed **"Weaving Innovations"** — concluded at FITT, IIT Delhi, showcasing technology-led solutions for the handloom sector.

**Key facts**

- Over **2,500 participants** registered; the **top 100 shortlisted teams**, comprising **280 participants**, reached the finale.
- Problem statements covered **loom modernisation, design innovation, sustainability, market access and weaver livelihoods**.
- Development Commissioner (Handlooms) **M. Beena, IAS** framed it as combining heritage with technology: "Handloom represents our rich heritage, whereas technology represents the future."

**Why it matters:** Handloom is India's largest cottage industry after agriculture and is overwhelmingly female-employing. The sector's chronic problems are productivity, yarn cost and market access — not demand. A hackathon route to loom modernisation is an attempt to raise weaver incomes without industrialising the craft out of existence. Note the timing: this lands days before **National Handloom Day (7 August)**, which commemorates the launch of the **Swadeshi Movement in 1905**.

**Static linkage:** National Handloom Development Programme; Raw Material Supply Scheme; Handloom Mark; Handlooms (Reservation of Articles for Production) Act, 1985.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Textiles/MSME) and GS1 — Indian Society & Culture (crafts, Swadeshi).

## 5. India's first Telecom Manufacturing Zone at Gwalior — DoT signs MoU with Madhya Pradesh

**Issuing body:** Department of Telecommunications, Ministry of Communications (MoU signed 30 July 2026 at Vigyan Bhavan, New Delhi).

**What it is:** The DoT and the Government of Madhya Pradesh signed an MoU for **Phase-I of India's first dedicated Telecom Manufacturing Zone (TMZ)** at Gwalior.

**Key facts**

- Conceived as an integrated **350-acre plug-and-play industrial cluster** for telecom and electronics manufacturing, R&D, innovation and allied activities.
- The Centre has approved **100% financial assistance of ₹500 crore** for Phase-I infrastructure.
- Phase-I expectation: **₹2,000 crore of investment and 4,500+ jobs**; the next stage targets at least **₹10,000 crore**.
- Signed in the presence of Union Minister for Communications **Jyotiraditya M. Scindia**, MP Chief Minister **Dr Mohan Yadav**, and MoS Communications **Dr Chandra Sekhar Pemmasani**.

**Why it matters:** India remains dependent on imported network hardware even as it exports telecom software and services. A dedicated zone — as distinct from a generic PLI subsidy — is an attempt to build **supply-chain depth** (components, testing, R&D) rather than just final assembly. It also fits the trust-and-security agenda: domestically manufactured network equipment is a national-security asset as much as an industrial one.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Industrial Policy, Manufacturing) with an Internal Security overlay (telecom equipment security).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) mechanism recently in the news, consider the following statements:

1. The National Biodiversity Authority is a statutory body established under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002.
2. Access and Benefit Sharing is one of the three objectives of the Convention on Biological Diversity.
3. Benefit-sharing amounts collected by the National Biodiversity Authority are credited exclusively to the Consolidated Fund of India and cannot be passed on to State Biodiversity Boards.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** The 'Nasha Mukt Yuva for Viksit Bharat Sankalp Abhiyan', launched in August 2026, is best described as:

(a) A statutory regulatory framework replacing the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985
(b) A 100-week nationwide public-participation campaign against substance abuse, with weekly activities held on Sundays
(c) A centrally sponsored scheme providing financial assistance to State-run de-addiction centres
(d) An international cooperation framework for interdiction of narcotics trafficking in the Indian Ocean Region

---

**Q3.** Consider the following statements regarding India's first Telecom Manufacturing Zone (TMZ):

1. It is being set up at Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh, under a MoU between the Department of Telecommunications and the State Government.
2. The Centre has approved 100 per cent financial assistance for Phase-I infrastructure development.
3. It is envisaged as a plug-and-play industrial cluster covering telecom and electronics manufacturing, R&D and allied activities.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
The NBA is indeed a statutory body under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002, headquartered at Chennai, and ABS is the third objective of the CBD (alongside conservation and sustainable use). Statement 3 is wrong — and the 2 August news is the proof: the NBA disbursed ₹8.22 crore, of which ₹5.75 crore went to 33 State Biodiversity Boards and UT Biodiversity Councils apportioned by rice-growing area.

**Q2 — (b).**
It is a campaign (*abhiyan*), not a law or a funding scheme: a 100-week Jan Bhagidari drive with Sunday activities across roughly 10,000 locations, targeting over 1 crore youth. The NDPS Act, 1985 remains fully in force; option (a) is a distractor.

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.**
All three are correct. The 350-acre Gwalior TMZ was agreed via a DoT–Madhya Pradesh MoU (30 July 2026), with ₹500 crore of 100% central assistance for Phase-I, and is designed as a plug-and-play cluster for telecom/electronics manufacturing, R&D and allied activity.
