## 1. Raksha Mantri approves **Transfer of Technology (ToT) of DRDO-developed missile systems** to Indian defence industry

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Defence / DRDO

Defence Minister Rajnath Singh has approved the transfer of technology of **all conventional missile systems developed by DRDO** to Indian industry, allowing domestic firms — public and private — to take up series production, subject to the applicable qualification, certification and regulatory requirements. This is the single largest opening-up of India's missile technology base to industry so far.

**Why it matters:** India's homegrown missile programmes have historically stayed inside a DRDO-to-DPSU production channel. Widening the production base to private industry is meant to compress delivery timelines, reduce import dependence in a category where India has been a persistent buyer, and create a scalable manufacturing base that can serve both the armed forces and the export market. It is the clearest recent instance of the *design-here, build-at-scale-here* logic of Aatmanirbharta in defence, and pairs with the 6th Positive Indigenisation List notified on 18 August.

**Key facts:**

- Covers **conventional** DRDO missile systems — the family includes systems such as **Akash** (medium-range SAM), **QRSAM** (Quick Reaction SAM), **Pralay** (quasi-ballistic surface-to-surface) and the **Pinaka** guided rocket system.
- Production by industry is conditional on qualification, certification and regulatory clearances.
- Announced by the Ministry of Defence on **25 August 2026**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security (defence indigenisation, Aatmanirbhar Bharat in defence) / Science & Technology.

## 2. **CSIR-NAL unveils indigenous micro and small gas turbine engines — NJ-05, NJ-50 and NJ-100**

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Science & Technology — CSIR / National Aerospace Laboratories (NAL), Bengaluru

CSIR-NAL unveiled a family of three indigenous gas turbine engines at CSIR Headquarters, New Delhi: the **NJ-05 (5 kgf thrust)**, **NJ-50 (50 kgf)** and **NJ-100 (100 kgf)**. These are compact propulsion systems for tactical unmanned aerial vehicles, drone interceptors, loitering munitions and compact cruise/missile platforms. Air Marshal Tejinder Singh, Chief of Integrated Defence Staff to the Chairman COSC, was the chief guest.

**Why it matters:** Small gas turbines are a classic chokepoint technology — India has been importing them for exactly the class of unmanned and expendable systems that recent conflicts have made central. The NJ-100 in particular fills the mid-range gap between micro-UAV engines and full turbojets, where imported engines dominated. Read this alongside Item 1: engines plus missile ToT together describe an attempt to indigenise both the platform and its propulsion.

**Key facts:**

- **NJ-05 / NJ-50 / NJ-100** — designations broadly track thrust in kilogram-force (kgf).
- Applications: tactical UAVs, drone interceptors, loitering munitions, compact cruise missiles.
- Developer: **CSIR-National Aerospace Laboratories**, a constituent laboratory of CSIR, headquartered at Bengaluru; India's premier civil aeronautics R&D lab (also behind SARAS and HANSA aircraft).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (indigenisation, dual-use aerospace tech).

## 3. India at **UNCCD COP17**: a call to shift from **drought response to drought resilience**

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Environment, Forest & Climate Change

At the **Ministerial Dialogue on 'Accelerating Drought Resilience'** at UNCCD COP17 in **Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia**, Union Minister Bhupender Yadav argued for a global shift "from reactive relief to proactive, technology-enabled resilience". India's framing: drought is no longer an occasional event but a **defining developmental challenge** cutting across water security, food systems, biodiversity and economic stability, and must be managed as a *predictable, preventable risk* rather than a recurring emergency.

> **What's new since yesterday's briefing:** the 24 August entry covered India's land-restoration numbers and its finance ask at COP17. This is the separate **drought-resilience ministerial track** — a distinct agenda item at COP17 and a different argument.

**Why it matters:** Drought has been the weakest limb of the UNCCD's three-part mandate (desertification, land degradation, drought). A binding global drought protocol has been contested at successive COPs, with developing countries pushing for one and several developed countries preferring a voluntary framework. India's position — integrated early warning + mitigation + relief + community resilience, backed by diversified, adequate, predictable and sustained international finance — situates it firmly on the "structured, financed" side of that debate.

**Key facts:**

- **UNCCD** — one of the three Rio Conventions (with UNFCCC and CBD), adopted 1994; the only legally binding instrument linking environment, development and sustainable land management.
- **COP17** is being hosted at **Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia**; India hosted **COP14 at New Delhi (2019)**, where it raised its restoration ambition to **26 million hectares by 2030**.
- India's stated approach: coordinated multi-institutional early warning, mitigation, relief and community resilience.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (UNCCD, drought, land degradation neutrality); GS2 — International Relations (multilateral environmental negotiation).

## 4. **India–Japan investment partnership**: Commerce Minister invites greater Japanese institutional capital in Tokyo

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Commerce & Industry

Commerce and Industry Minister **Piyush Goyal** is leading **India's largest-ever business delegation to Japan** — over 200 representatives across manufacturing, semiconductors, clean energy, steel, automotive, financial services, healthcare and start-ups — on a visit running **24–27 August 2026**. In Tokyo he held discussions with senior leaders of major Japanese financial and investment institutions — **MUFG, Development Bank of Japan, Mizuho, Morgan Stanley, Nomura and Nippon Life** — on long-term capital flows and expanding Japanese institutional investment in India, and addressed a roundtable with **Keidanren (Japan Business Federation)**, which represents over 1,500 Japanese enterprises.

**Why it matters:** The pitch has moved from Japanese *corporate* FDI (autos, industrial parks) to Japanese *institutional* capital — pension funds, banks, insurers — which is patient, long-tenor money suited to infrastructure and energy transition. Dedicated sessions on semiconductors and AI, start-ups and foreign institutional investors signal where India wants the next tranche of Japanese money to go.

**Key facts:**

- Delegation size: **200+** business representatives; visit dates **24–27 August 2026**.
- Institutions engaged: MUFG, DBJ, Mizuho, Morgan Stanley, Nomura, Nippon Life.
- **Keidanren** — Japan Business Federation, 1,500+ member enterprises.
- Focus sectors: semiconductors, AI, infrastructure, energy, frontier tech, start-ups.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India–Japan Special Strategic and Global Partnership); GS3 — Economy (FDI, institutional capital).

## 5. **'Parishrama Adalat', Hyderabad** clears 65 delayed-payment cases worth ₹8.29 crore for micro and small enterprises

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises (with Commissionerate of Industries and Commerce, Government of Telangana)

A **Conciliation Conclave** styled 'Parishrama Adalat', held at **FTCCI, Hyderabad**, resolved **65 delayed-payment disputes involving ₹8.29 crore**, restoring cash flow to micro and small enterprises. It brought together MSEs, buyers, public sector enterprises, **MSE Facilitation Councils**, ADR institutions and officials. Separately, MSME Minister **Jitan Ram Manjhi** stressed promoting MSMEs in **hill areas** as a route to local employment and to checking out-migration.

**Why it matters:** Delayed payments are the single largest working-capital drag on Indian MSEs, and the statutory route through MSE Facilitation Councils has been slow. The Rajya Sabha in early August 2026 passed an MSME Bill prescribing timelines for faster adjudication of such disputes and empowering courts to order payment of at least 50% of the awarded amount where a set-aside application stays pending beyond six months. The Adalat model is the administrative complement — conciliation before litigation.

**Key facts:**

- **65 cases**, **₹8.29 crore**, at FTCCI Hyderabad.
- Institutional anchor: **MSE Facilitation Councils** under the MSMED Act, 2006 (Section 15 mandates buyer payment within 45 days).
- Grievance channel: the **MSME Samadhaan** portal for delayed-payment complaints.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (MSMEs, working capital, dispute resolution); GS2 — Governance (ADR mechanisms).

## 6. WCD Ministry pushes **child-marriage reporting on helpline 1098** under *Bal Vivah Mukt Bharat*

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Women & Child Development

Union Minister **Annpurna Devi** urged citizens to report instances of child marriage on **Child Helpline 1098** and to help realise the pledge of a **Bal Vivah Mukt Bharat** (child-marriage-free India). Minister of State **Savitri Thakur** separately marked ten years of UPI, noting that the phone has become every Indian's wallet.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Bal Vivah Mukt Bharat**

- **Ministry:** Women & Child Development.
- **Objective:** eliminate child marriage in India; the national campaign was launched by the WCD Minister in **November 2024** at Vigyan Bhawan, alongside the **Child Marriage Free Bharat Portal**.
- **Key features:** (i) a citizen-facing portal to report child marriages and file complaints; (ii) **Child Helpline 1098** — toll-free, 24×7×365, working with police, Child Marriage Prohibition Officers and District Child Protection Units; (iii) district- and panchayat-level pledge and awareness drives; (iv) convergence with **Mission Shakti** and its app, which routes users to women- and child-safety helplines.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** girls and boys below the legal age of marriage; awareness programmes under the campaign have reached over **6 crore citizens**.
- **Significance:** child marriage sits at the intersection of education drop-out, maternal and child health (anaemia, low birth weight) and female labour-force participation — which is why it recurs in GS1 Society and GS2 vulnerable-sections answers.

**Legal backbone:** the **Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006** — marriage age 21 (male) and 18 (female); a child marriage is **voidable at the option of the contracting party who was a child**, and void only in specified circumstances (e.g. where the child is taken away by enticement or force, or sold/trafficked for marriage).

**Notebook:** GS1 — Society (women, social empowerment); GS2 — Governance (welfare schemes for vulnerable sections).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to missile and rocket systems developed by the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO), consider the following statements:

1. Akash is a medium-range surface-to-air missile system.
2. QRSAM stands for Quick Reaction Surface-to-Air Missile.
3. Pralay is a quasi-ballistic surface-to-surface missile.
4. Pinaka is a guided anti-tank missile.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) Only three
- (d) All four

---

**Q2.** The NJ-05, NJ-50 and NJ-100, recently in the news, are best described as:

- (a) Small modular nuclear reactors designed by BARC for captive industrial power
- (b) Indigenous micro and small gas turbine engines developed by CSIR-National Aerospace Laboratories
- (c) A series of navigation satellites launched under the NavIC augmentation programme
- (d) Ultra-high-yield rice hybrids released by the Indian Council of Agricultural Research

---

**Q3.** Consider the following statements:

1. The 'Bal Vivah Mukt Bharat' campaign is run by the Ministry of Women and Child Development.
2. Childline 1098 is a toll-free, round-the-clock emergency outreach service for children in distress, including for prevention of child marriage.
3. Under the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006, every child marriage is void *ab initio*.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (c) Only three.** Statements 1, 2 and 3 are correct. Statement 4 is incorrect: **Pinaka is a multi-barrel rocket launcher system** (in guided and unguided variants), not an anti-tank missile; DRDO's anti-tank systems are Nag/HELINA/MPATGM. All these conventional systems fall within the scope of the ToT approved on 25 August 2026.

**Q2 — (b).** CSIR-NAL, Bengaluru, unveiled the **NJ-05 (5 kgf), NJ-50 (50 kgf) and NJ-100 (100 kgf)** micro and small gas turbine engines for tactical UAVs, drone interceptors, loitering munitions and compact cruise missiles — an indigenous substitute for imported engines in this thrust class.

**Q3 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is incorrect: under the PCMA, 2006 a child marriage is generally **voidable at the option of the contracting party who was a child** (petition maintainable up to two years after attaining majority); it is void only in specified circumstances such as where the child is enticed or taken away, or sold/trafficked for the purpose of marriage.
