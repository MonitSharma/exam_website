## 1. Union Cabinet approves a **bench of the High Court of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh at Ladakh**

**Issuing body:** Union Cabinet; announced by Union Home Minister Shri Amit Shah (20 August 2026).

**What it is.** The Cabinet has cleared the establishment of a bench of the High Court of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh in the Union Territory of Ladakh. The High Court presently sits at Srinagar and Jammu; the Ladakh bench will be its **third** seat. The Home Minister framed it as reducing the travel time and cost that Ladakhi litigants currently bear to reach Srinagar or Jammu, and as part of the Centre's broader commitment on constitutional safeguards for Ladakh.

**Why it matters.** Access to justice in high-altitude, low-density, weather-cut-off territories is a recurring GS2 theme. The J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019 created **one common High Court** for the two successor Union Territories of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh — Ladakh does not have a separate High Court of its own. A circuit/permanent bench is therefore the constitutionally available route, and it follows the pattern used for other remote regions (e.g. the Gauhati High Court's benches in the North-East).

**Key facts to retain**

- Bench count after establishment: **3** (Srinagar, Jammu, Ladakh).
- Parent statute for the common High Court: **Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019**.
- Establishment of High Court benches is an executive decision taken by the Central Government in consultation with the Chief Justice of the concerned High Court; the High Court itself is constituted under **Article 214** read with Article 216.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Judiciary, Union Territories).

**Issuing body:** Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce & Industry — released 20 August 2026 (provisional estimates for July 2026, final index for June 2026, **base year 2022-23**).

**What it is.** The Index of Core Industries (ICI) rose **5.4% year-on-year in July 2026**, easing from **6.0% in June 2026**. Cumulative growth for **April–July 2026** stands at **4.3%**.

## 5. Government backgrounder: **India's Makhana Sector — traditional crop to global superfood**

**Issuing body:** PIB Research Unit / Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare — 19 August 2026.

**What it is.** A government backgrounder placing India's fox-nut (*makhana*) economy on record for the first time with consolidated production and export numbers, against the policy scaffolding of the **National Makhana Board**.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Production (2025-26, Second Advance Estimates): 80,590 tonnes**, up from **63,910 tonnes** in 2024-25 — a **26.1%** jump in one season.
- **Productivity** rose from **2.03 t/ha (2024-25) to 2.34 t/ha (2025-26)**.
- **Exports (2025-26): 7,264.89 tonnes** of makhana products — about **9.01%** of production.
- India is the **world's largest producer**, with roughly **80%** of global output; **Bihar** is the production heartland (Mithila belt — Darbhanga, Madhubani, Purnea, Katihar, Saharsa).
- Botanical name: ***Euryale ferox*** — an aquatic crop grown in ponds and low-lying wetlands. **Mithila Makhana** carries a **GI tag**.

### Scheme-in-focus: **National Makhana Board** and its Central Sector Scheme

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare (Department of Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare) |
| **Announced in** | Union Budget 2025-26; Board inaugurated by the Prime Minister at **Purnea, Bihar, 15 September 2025** (HQ Purnea) |
| **Outlay** | **₹476.03 crore** Central Sector Scheme, **2025-26 to 2030-31** |
| **Objective** | Whole-value-chain development of makhana — production, post-harvest, processing, branding and export |

**Key features (4)**

1. **Production and technology** — promotion of improved varieties and modern pond/field cultivation practices; consolidated state seed demand met through **SAU Sabour** and **CAU Samastipur** (Bihar).
2. **Research and training** — the **National Research Centre (NRC) for Makhana, Darbhanga** anchors research and trains master trainers from other states.
3. **Post-harvest and value addition** — infrastructure for grading, drying, popping and packaging, the historically labour-brutal part of the chain.
4. **Market, export and branding** — FPO-led market linkage, brand development and export promotion (APEDA has already facilitated a first sea-route export of GI-tagged Mithila Makhana).

**Beneficiaries/coverage:** makhana growers, pond lessees and traditional *Mallah* fisher-cultivator communities of the Mithila region, FPOs and processors, with the scheme designed to be extended to makhana-growing pockets beyond Bihar (West Bengal, Assam, Odisha, Manipur, Meghalaya, Tripura, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh).

**Significance:** a rare case of a single traditional crop getting its own statutory-style board — a template for niche, geography-locked, high-value crops. Also a livelihood-diversification and wetland-utilisation story rather than a land-expansion one.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (cropping patterns, value addition, agri-exports).

## 6. India launches its **first Guide to Grasslands and Other Open Natural Ecosystems (ONEs)** at UNCCD COP17

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC), with ATREE — launched at **UNCCD COP17, Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, 17 August 2026**.

**What it is.** India's first consolidated guide to its **Open Natural Ecosystems** — grasslands, savannas, scrublands, rocky plateaus, ravines, deserts and coastal open systems. It classifies **24 ecosystem types** across the country and documents their geography, ecological character, biodiversity, and the pastoral and tribal communities that depend on them, with thematic sections on **pastoralism, fire ecology and carbon sequestration**. Prepared by the **Ashoka Trust for Research in Ecology and the Environment (ATREE)** with MoEFCC and partners; Union Minister Shri Bhupender Yadav addressed the launch virtually.

**Why it matters.** ONEs have been India's blind spot in conservation policy: revenue records and afforestation targets have long classified them as "wastelands," making them the default site for plantation drives, solar parks and industrial land banks — which destroys, rather than restores, them. A national classification is the first step towards protecting species like the Great Indian Bustard, the wolf, the caracal and the blackbuck, and towards recognising pastoralist tenure. Directly relevant to India's **Land Degradation Neutrality** commitments.

**Key facts to retain**

- **UNCCD COP17**: Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, **17–28 August 2026**; theme **"Restoring Land, Restoring Hope."** COP16 was at Riyadh (2024); **India hosted COP14 at New Delhi in 2019**.
- UNCCD is one of the **three Rio Conventions** from the 1992 Earth Summit (with UNFCCC and CBD); adopted **1994**, in force **1996** — the only one with a legally binding land focus.
- Central unresolved issue at COP17: a **global drought protocol/framework**, left hanging at COP16, plus the gap on restoring **1 billion hectares** of degraded land by 2030.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (conservation, land degradation, international conventions).

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the Cabinet cleared a Ladakh bench of the High Court of J&K and Ladakh, which will be its third bench after Srinagar and Jammu. Statement 3 is incorrect — the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019 provided a **common High Court** for both successor Union Territories; Ladakh has no separate High Court, which is precisely why a bench was needed.

**Q2 — (d) All four.**
Makhana (fox nut) comes from *Euryale ferox*, an aquatic crop, and India accounts for roughly 80% of world output. The National Makhana Board, inaugurated at Purnea in September 2025, is headquartered there and runs a ₹476.03 crore Central Sector Scheme (2025-26 to 2030-31). The NRC for Makhana is at Darbhanga, and Mithila Makhana holds a GI tag.

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.**
All three are correct. India's first Guide to Grasslands and ONEs — prepared by ATREE with MoEFCC — was launched on 17 August 2026 at UNCCD COP17 in Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, and classifies 24 ecosystem types. The UNCCD, adopted in 1994, is one of the three Rio Conventions along with the UNFCCC and the CBD.
