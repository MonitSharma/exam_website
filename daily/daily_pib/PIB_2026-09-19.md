## 1. Ministry of Rural Development: **DDU-GKY 2.0 training batches to begin across most States/UTs from October 2026 — 1,164 batches, 34,920 rural youth**

**What it is.** The Ministry of Rural Development has announced that **Deen Dayal Upadhyaya–Grameen Kaushalya Yojana (DDU-GKY) 2.0** training batches will commence across most States and UTs **from October 2026**. Preparedness was reviewed in a first-phase video conference with **14 States/UTs** — Odisha, Mizoram, Sikkim, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, West Bengal, Himachal Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Puducherry, Karnataka, Telangana and Bihar. **1,164 batches covering 34,920 rural youth** will start in October, against a sanctioned target of **3,57,112 rural youth to be trained by 31 December 2027** in these 14 States/UTs.

**Why it matters.** DDU-GKY is India's flagship **placement-linked** rural skilling programme, and "2.0" is its relaunch on a **two-year target cycle (2026–28)** with an **integrated digital portal** replacing the older fragmented workflow. Two examinable angles. First, the **delivery-architecture** angle: DDU-GKY runs through **Project Implementing Agencies (PIAs)** selected by State Rural Livelihood Missions — a public-funded, private-delivered model whose recurring criticisms are weak placement retention, inflated placement claims and delayed payments to PIAs. The 2.0 design answers the last of these directly by **integrating the Grameen Kaushal Portal with State Treasury portals** so payments flow online. Second, the **federal-implementation** angle: this is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme where the Centre allocates targets but States must sub-allocate to PIAs on time — hence the hard deadlines the Ministry has set.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Batches starting October 2026:** 1,164, covering **34,920 rural youth** in the 14 first-phase States/UTs.
- **Sanctioned target for those 14 States/UTs:** **3,57,112 rural youth by 31 December 2027**.
- **All-India:** targets allocated to all **31 participating States/UTs** for **2026–28**; **5.6 lakh targets** across **1,200+ projects** already allocated by **24 States/UTs**; more than **5 lakh targets** already passed down to PIAs.
- **Deadlines set:** States/UTs to complete **Treasury-portal integration by 30 September 2026**; allocation of targets to PIAs for batch commencement **by 31 October 2026**.
- **Regional PIA workshops (hybrid), October 2026:** **Shillong (5 Oct)**, **Vijayawada (7 Oct)**, **Mumbai (9 Oct)**, **Dharamshala (16 Oct)**.
- **Chaired by:** Joint Secretary (Skills) and Director (Skills), Ministry of Rural Development, with State CEOs/COOs.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Deen Dayal Upadhyaya–Grameen Kaushalya Yojana (DDU-GKY) 2.0**

- **Ministry:** Rural Development. **Type:** Centrally Sponsored Scheme; the **placement-linked skill development component of DAY-NRLM** (Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana – National Rural Livelihoods Mission).
- **Objective:** convert the rural demographic surplus into a skilled, wage-employed workforce by providing **free, residential/non-residential, placement-linked training** to **poor rural youth**, with a guaranteed placement obligation on the training agency.
- **Key features (2.0):** (i) **training duration of 3 to 12 months**; (ii) delivery through **Project Implementing Agencies** under State Rural Livelihood Missions, on **two-year target cycles (2026–28)**; (iii) an **Integrated DDU-GKY 2.0 Portal** covering the **end-to-end project life cycle** — mobilisation, training, placement tracking and payments — with **digital monitoring**; (iv) **integration with State Treasury portals** to enable online payments through the **Grameen Kaushal Portal**.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** poor rural youth (conventionally **15–35 years**, with relaxation for vulnerable groups); **31 participating States/UTs**; **5.6 lakh targets** allocated so far.
- **Significance:** it is the only large central skilling scheme where **placement**, not merely certification, is the unit of success — which makes it the natural comparator in any answer on PMKVY, PM-SETU or the National Skill Development Mission.

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (Centrally Sponsored Schemes, welfare delivery, Centre–State implementation) + GS3 Economy (employment, skilling, rural livelihoods).

## 2. Ministry of Law and Justice: **President appoints 14 judicial officers as Judges and Additional Judges of four High Courts**

**What it is.** In exercise of the power conferred by the Constitution and **after consultation with the Chief Justice of India**, the President has appointed **14 judicial officers** as Judges/Additional Judges of the **Delhi**, **Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh**, **Jharkhand** and **Karnataka** High Courts. **Five of the 14 are women.** All 14 come from the **District Judiciary** — this is the "service quota" route to the High Court bench, not elevation from the Bar.

**Why it matters.** Straightforward prelims material on **Articles 217 and 224**, and useful Mains material on two live debates: **judicial appointments** (the collegium, the unsettled Memorandum of Procedure, and the perennial question of pendency versus vacancies) and **diversity on the bench**. Note the structural point the notification illustrates: a High Court bench is filled from **two streams** — advocates from the Bar and members of the **State Judicial Service** — and the composition of any batch tells you which stream the collegium is drawing on. Note also the constitutional anomaly on display: an **Additional Judge** under **Article 224** is appointed for a **fixed term not exceeding two years** to clear temporary arrears, a device that has become near-routine and is criticised for creating a probationary tier of judges whose confirmation depends on later executive and collegium action.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Total appointed:** 14 judicial officers across **four High Courts**; **five women** among them.
- **Delhi High Court:** **Smt. Nivedita Anil Sharma** and **Smt. Nisha Sahay Saxena** as **Judges**; **Sanjay Sharma, Bharat Parashar, Dr. (Smt.) Aditi Choudhary, Dinesh Bhatt** and **Arun Bhardwaj** as **Additional Judges**.
- **J&K and Ladakh High Court:** **Yash Paul Bourney** as a **Judge**.
- **Jharkhand High Court:** **Manoj Prasad, Akhil Kumar** and **Ram Sharma** as **Judges**.
- **Karnataka High Court:** **Smt. Usharani, K.S. Bharath Kumar** and **Smt. Nerale V. Bhavani** as **Additional Judges**.
- **Issued by:** Department of Justice, Ministry of Law and Justice (notification signed by Narayan Prasad, Director).

**Constitutional anchors to revise alongside this**

- **Article 214** — a High Court for each State; **Article 231** — a common High Court for two or more States/UTs (the **J&K and Ladakh High Court** is the common High Court for the **UT of Jammu & Kashmir and the UT of Ladakh**; the **Delhi High Court** exists under the **Delhi High Court Act, 1966**).
- **Article 216** — composition: a Chief Justice and such other judges as the President may from time to time deem necessary (**no fixed strength**).
- **Article 217** — appointment: by the **President**, after consultation with the **CJI**, the **Governor of the State**, and (for judges other than the Chief Justice) the **Chief Justice of that High Court**. **Qualifications:** citizen of India **and** either ten years' judicial office in India **or** ten years as an advocate of a High Court. **No minimum age is prescribed.** **Retirement age: 62 years.**
- **Article 224** — **Additional Judges** (temporary increase in business/arrears, for a period **not exceeding two years**) and **Acting Judges**. **Article 224A** — retired judges sitting at sittings of a High Court.
- **Article 222** — transfer of a judge from one High Court to another. **Article 218 read with 124(4)** — removal, on the Supreme Court judge's grounds and procedure.
- **Collegium:** not in the text of the Constitution — evolved through the **Second Judges Case (1993)** and **Third Judges Case (1998)**; the **99th Amendment and the NJAC Act were struck down in the Fourth Judges Case (2015)**.

**Notebook:** GS2 Polity (judiciary, appointment of High Court judges, collegium, separation of powers).

## 3. Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change / NGT: **PM inaugurates the NGT's International Conference on 'The Future of Environment and Climate Dynamics'; NGT Mobile Application released**

**What it is.** The Prime Minister inaugurated the **National Green Tribunal's two-day International Conference on 'The Future of Environment and Climate Dynamics'** in New Delhi on **19 September 2026**, and **released the NGT Mobile Application**. The inaugural session was attended by **Chief Justice of India Justice Surya Kant**, **Attorney General for India Shri R. Venkataramani**, **Union Environment Minister Shri Bhupender Yadav** and **NGT Chairperson Justice Prakash Shrivastava**.

**Why it matters.** Three usable threads. First, **environmental jurisprudence** — the CJI used the platform to restate the doctrinal spine of Indian environmental law: the **right to a pollution-free environment as a fundamental right**, the **precautionary principle**, **polluter pays**, **absolute liability** and the **public trust doctrine**, and the newer move of bringing **climate-related rights** into constitutional focus (equality, livelihood, health). Second, the **institution** — the NGT, a **statutory** specialised tribunal, hosting an international judicial conference and simultaneously digitising litigant access through an app, is a clean example of tribunalisation plus e-governance. Third, **India's climate positioning ahead of COP31**, with the Environment Minister's framing of **equity, implementation and the needs of vulnerable countries** — the standard Indian negotiating triad of CBDR-RC, climate finance and adaptation.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Event:** NGT's two-day International Conference, New Delhi, inaugurated **19 September 2026**; **NGT Mobile Application** released.
- **Non-fossil share of installed electricity capacity: 54.18%** — the **50% target for 2030 met nine years ahead of schedule**.
- India ranks **third globally in renewable energy installed capacity** and is the **world's second-largest solar growth market**.
- **Forest and tree cover: 8.27 lakh sq km**; per the **UN FAO Global Forest Resources Assessment 2025**, India retains **third position globally in annual net forest-area gain**.
- Conservation examples cited: the **Cheetah reintroduction programme** and the **conservation breeding programme for the Great Indian Bustard**.
- **Next climate COP:** **UNFCCC COP31 at Antalya, Türkiye**.
- Framing quoted by the Minister: the choice is **not between development and environment, but between sustainable development and development that cannot be sustained**.

**On the National Green Tribunal (revise alongside)**

- Established under the **National Green Tribunal Act, 2010**; India was among the **first countries after Australia and New Zealand** to set up a dedicated environmental court.
- **Composition:** Chairperson (a sitting or retired Supreme Court judge or Chief Justice of a High Court), **Judicial Members** and **Expert Members**; **Principal Bench at New Delhi**, with zonal benches at **Bhopal, Pune, Kolkata and Chennai**.
- **Jurisdiction:** civil cases involving a **substantial question relating to the environment** arising from the enactments in **Schedule I** of the Act (Water Act 1974, Air Act 1981, Environment (Protection) Act 1986, Forest (Conservation) Act 1980, Biological Diversity Act 2002, among others). **Not** the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.
- It is **not bound by the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908** or the Indian Evidence Act; it follows **principles of natural justice** and is mandated to apply **sustainable development, the precautionary principle and the polluter pays principle**. Appeals lie to the **Supreme Court**. Applications are to be disposed of, as far as possible, **within six months**.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment (climate policy, conservation, India's climate diplomacy) + GS2 Polity (tribunals, environmental jurisprudence, judicial review).

## 4. Ministry of Communications (TRAI): **Telecom Commercial Communication Customer Preference (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2026 notified — A2P calls defined, AI/ML spam detection made mandatory, complaint threshold cut to three**

**What it is.** The **Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI)** has notified the **Telecom Commercial Communication Customer Preference (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2026**, amending the **TCCCPR, 2018**, to curb **Unsolicited Commercial Communications (UCC)**. The amendment follows a **draft issued for consultation on 13 March 2026**, comments and counter-comments up to **19 April** and **4 May 2026**, and an **Open House Discussion on 3 June 2026**.

**Why it matters.** This is a model example of how an **independent statutory regulator** actually makes law — consultation paper, comments, counter-comments, open house, then binding regulation under delegated legislative power. Substantively it is the first Indian regulation to treat **AI-generated and automated calling (A2P, robocalls, synthetic voice)** as a distinct regulated category, and it uses an unusual instrument — a **per-minute termination charge** — as an economic deterrent rather than relying on penalties alone. Read it against the **Telecommunications Act, 2023** and the **DPDP Act, 2023** (consent architecture): the amendment's expansion of "explicit consent" to cover **digitised legacy consents** is exactly the consent-portability problem the DPDP regime raises.

**Key facts and figures**

- **AI/ML detection made binding:** new **Regulation 21A** requires TSPs to identify sender CLIs with a **high probability of being used for UCC** and to **share that intelligence across operators** (giving regulatory force to TRAI's **Direction of 27 February 2026**).
- **Graded action trigger:** where **five or more CLIs** of a sender are flagged **within ten days**, access providers must escalate — **KYC re-verification, physical verification, barring of outgoing services, and disconnection** for repeat misuse.
- **Complaint threshold lowered:** action now triggers at **3 or more unique complaints in ten days** (down from **5**) **when corroborated** by AI/ML flagging of the sender's CLI.
- **A2P calls defined:** voice calls initiated by an **application, software system or automated platform without direct human dialling**, including **autodialling, robo-calls and pre-recorded/artificial voice**. Entities must **pre-declare** A2P use and CLIs to their TSP; **undeclared A2P calls are treated as UCC**.
- **Termination charge:** up to **₹0.05 per minute** on A2P calls, levied by the **terminating access provider** on the **originating access provider**; calls on designated commercial series and Authority-authorised calls are **exempt**.
- **Protected series:** **140xx, 1600xx and 1601xx** shall **not** be flagged as suspected spam, so legitimate commercial and government communications are not lost.
- **Inquiry-based communication:** permitted for only **seven days** from a customer's inquiry, and the inquiry must be **in writing or digital and kept in verifiable form**.
- **Consumer appeal mechanism (new):** appeal against the resolution of a UCC complaint within **15 days** to the **Appellate Authority**, disposed of under the **Telecom Consumers Complaint Redressal Regulations, 2012**; routes include the **TRAI DND app**, TSP apps/portals and **call or SMS to 1909**.
- **Explicit consent widened:** **legacy consents** recognised only if obtained through **verifiable means** and **registered on the TSP's Digital Platform**.

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (statutory regulators, regulation-making process, consumer protection) + GS3 Science & Technology (AI/ML in enforcement, telecom regulation, cyber fraud).

## 6. Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment: **AVYAY — ₹1,089.58 crore released under IPSrC and SAPSrC; 10.76 lakh senior citizens supported since 2014-15** *(UPDATE — AVYAY appeared as a one-line mention on 13 September; what is new is the first consolidated expenditure and beneficiary data for its two institutional-care components)*

**What it is.** The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment has released consolidated figures for the two institutional-care components of **Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana (AVYAY)** — the **Integrated Programme for Senior Citizens (IPSrC)** and the **State Action Plan for Senior Citizens (SAPSrC)**. Approximately **₹1,089.58 crore** has been released across the two; **more than 10.76 lakh senior citizens** have benefited under IPSrC from **FY 2014-15 to FY 2025-26**. The statement was made by **Shri Sudhansh Pant, Secretary, Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment**.

**Why it matters.** India's ageing transition is one of the most reliably examined demographic themes, and AVYAY is the single umbrella scheme to cite. The structural point worth carrying into an answer: elderly welfare in India is **overwhelmingly institutional and NGO-delivered** — IPSrC funds old-age homes, day-care centres and continuous-care facilities largely run by **NGOs, Panchayati Raj Institutions and urban local bodies** — whereas the demographic reality is that the vast majority of older Indians live **at home**, with declining family co-residence. SAPSrC is the partial answer: untied money for States to design **locally relevant** interventions. Pair this with the **Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007**, the **National Policy on Older Persons, 1999**, and **Article 41** (Directive Principle: right to public assistance in old age).

**Key facts and figures**

- **Total released under IPSrC + SAPSrC:** approximately **₹1,089.58 crore**.
- **IPSrC expenditure, FY 2014-15 to FY 2025-26:** approximately **₹953.53 crore**; **more than 10.76 lakh senior citizens** benefited.
- **SAPSrC:** launched in **FY 2019-20**; **₹136.05 crore** released to date.
- **IPSrC interventions:** shelter, food, medical care and recreational support; capacity building of government institutions, **PRIs**, **urban local bodies**, NGOs and community-based organisations.
- **SAPSrC interventions:** awareness campaigns, sensitisation programmes, **cataract surgeries** and other State-specific measures.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana (AVYAY)**

- **Ministry:** Social Justice and Empowerment (Department of Social Justice and Empowerment). **Type:** Central Sector umbrella scheme for senior citizens (the erstwhile **National Action Plan for Senior Citizens** was renamed AVYAY in 2021).
- **Objective:** promote **healthy, productive and dignified ageing** by strengthening **institutional and community-based support systems**, covering shelter, food, healthcare, assistive devices, helpline services, capacity building, caregiver training and active ageing.
- **Key components:** (i) **Integrated Programme for Senior Citizens (IPSrC)** — grants for old-age homes, day-care and continuous-care homes run by NGOs/PRIs/ULBs; (ii) **State Action Plan for Senior Citizens (SAPSrC)** — financial assistance to States/UTs for State-specific interventions; (iii) **Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana (RVY)** — **free physical aids and assisted-living devices** for BPL senior citizens; (iv) **Elderline — the National Helpline for Senior Citizens (14567)**; (v) **Training of Geriatric Caregivers**. Allied verticals include **SACRED** (employment portal for elderly), **SAGE** (Seniorcare Ageing Growth Engine — start-ups in the eldercare economy) and **AGRASR** (elderly self-help groups).
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** senior citizens (**60 years and above**), with **BPL** targeting for RVY; delivered nationwide through States/UTs, NGOs, PRIs and ULBs.
- **Significance:** the principal central instrument for an **elderly population projected at about 20% of India's total by 2050** — the demographic counterpart to the "demographic dividend" argument, and the standard example of welfare delivery through **non-state implementing partners**.

**Notebook:** GS1 Society (ageing, demographic change, family structure) + GS2 Governance (welfare schemes for vulnerable sections, Article 41, MWPSC Act 2007).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the appointment of Judges of High Courts in India, consider the following statements:

1. Additional Judges of a High Court are appointed under Article 224 of the Constitution for a period not exceeding two years.
2. A Judge of a High Court is appointed by the President after consultation with the Chief Justice of India and the Governor of the State concerned.
3. The Constitution prescribes a minimum age of 45 years for appointment as a Judge of a High Court.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** With reference to the Telecom Commercial Communication Customer Preference (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2026, consider the following statements:

1. Application-to-Person (A2P) calls have been defined to include autodialled calls, robo-calls and calls made using pre-recorded or artificial voice.
2. The threshold for complaint-based action against a sender has been reduced from five to three unique complaints within ten days, where the sender's CLI is also flagged by an AI/ML-based system.
3. Numbers in the 140xx, 1600xx and 1601xx series will henceforth be flagged as suspected spam for recipients.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 1 and 2 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** Consider the following:

1. Integrated Programme for Senior Citizens (IPSrC)
2. Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana
3. Elderline – National Helpline for Senior Citizens
4. Atal Pension Yojana

How many of the above are components of the Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana (AVYAY)?

(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) Only three
(d) All four

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statement 1 is correct: **Article 224** allows the President to appoint **Additional Judges** when a High Court's business is temporarily increased or arrears have accumulated, **for a period not exceeding two years**. Statement 2 is correct: under **Article 217(1)**, a High Court judge is appointed by the **President** after consultation with the **CJI**, the **Governor of the State**, and — for a judge other than the Chief Justice — the **Chief Justice of that High Court**. Statement 3 is wrong: the Constitution prescribes **no minimum age** for a High Court judge. Article 217(2) prescribes only citizenship plus **ten years of judicial office** or **ten years as an advocate of a High Court**; the retirement age is **62**. Today's notification appointed **14 judicial officers** — all from the District Judiciary, **five of them women** — to the Delhi, J&K and Ladakh, Jharkhand and Karnataka High Courts.

**Q2 — (b) 1 and 2 only.** Statements 1 and 2 are correct exactly as notified: **A2P calls** are defined as voice calls initiated by an application, software system or automated platform **without direct human dialling**, expressly including **autodialling, robo-calls and pre-recorded/artificial voice**; and the complaint trigger has been cut from **five to three unique complaints in ten days** *when corroborated* by AI/ML flagging. Statement 3 inverts the regulation and is the trap: the special series designated for commercial communications — **140xx, 1600xx and 1601xx** — shall **NOT** be flagged as suspected spam, precisely so that legitimate commercial and government messages are not missed. Also worth remembering: undeclared A2P calls are **treated as UCC**, a termination charge of up to **₹0.05 per minute** applies, and a new **15-day consumer appeal** to the Appellate Authority has been created.

**Q3 — (c) Only three.** **IPSrC**, **Rashtriya Vayoshri Yojana** and **Elderline (14567)** are all components of **AVYAY**, along with the **State Action Plan for Senior Citizens (SAPSrC)** and **Training of Geriatric Caregivers**. **Atal Pension Yojana** is the odd one out — it is a **guaranteed pension scheme administered by the PFRDA under the Ministry of Finance**, not a component of AVYAY, which is a **Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment** umbrella scheme. The name "Atal" in both is the trap. Today's release put **₹1,089.58 crore** released under IPSrC and SAPSrC together, with **10.76 lakh+** senior citizens supported under IPSrC since FY 2014-15.
