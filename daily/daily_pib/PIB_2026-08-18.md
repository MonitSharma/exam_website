## 1. Aatmanirbhar Bharat: DDP notifies the **6th Positive Indigenisation List** of 405 strategically important items

**Ministry/Body:** Department of Defence Production (DDP), Ministry of Defence

The Department of Defence Production has notified the **sixth Positive Indigenisation List (PIL)** covering **405 strategically important items** with an estimated business potential of **₹3,070 crore**. A PIL is an embargo list: once the notified indigenisation timeline expires, these items can be procured **only from Indian industry**, converting an assured order pipeline into a policy instrument for domestic defence manufacturing.

**Key facts**

- **405 items** in total — **389 items of Defence PSUs** and **16 items of the Indian Coast Guard**.
- Composition: Line Replaceable Units (LRUs), sub-systems, sub-assemblies, spares, components and raw materials.
- Platforms covered include **Advanced Light Helicopter (ALH), Light Utility Helicopter (LUH), Su-30MKI, Light Combat Aircraft (LCA) and the AL-31FP engine** (IAF), and armoured platforms **T-72, T-90 and BMP-II** (Army).
- Each item carries an **indicative timeline** for indigenisation; after that date import is barred.
- **Sequence recap for prelims:** this is the **6th DPSU-side PIL**; the 5th PIL (346 items) preceded it. The DDP list series (for DPSUs/OFB) runs in parallel with the **DMA (Department of Military Affairs) lists** of services' capital-acquisition items — two distinct series that are frequently confused.

**Why it matters:** Indigenisation of spares and LRUs — not just full platforms — is where import dependence actually bites, because legacy Russian-origin fleets (Su-30MKI, T-90, BMP-II) are sustained through recurring spares purchases. Notebook: **GS3 — Internal Security / Defence Production & Indigenisation**; overlaps with GS3 Economy (Make in India, MSME order flow).

## 2. UIDAI crosses **2 crore Mandatory Biometric Updates** for schoolchildren; ~1.6 lakh schools covered

**Ministry/Body:** Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI), Ministry of Electronics & IT

UIDAI has completed **over 2 crore Mandatory Biometric Updates (MBU)** for schoolchildren under a special school-camp campaign launched in **September 2025**, covering **more than 1.56 lakh schools** across States and UTs. Aadhaar enrolled below age 5 captures no biometrics; the law requires **MBU at age 5 and again at age 15**, failing which the Aadhaar number can be **deactivated** — which is why the school route matters.

**Key facts**

- **Over 2 crore MBUs completed**; **~1.56 lakh schools** covered since September 2025 (it was ~1.2 crore across ~1.03 lakh schools in March 2026 — the drive has roughly doubled in five months).
- Delivered through **school-level camps** with State/UT education departments.
- Backed by **UIDAI–UDISE+ integration** (Unified District Information System for Education Plus), letting schools and authorities track student-wise MBU status.
- **Fee waiver:** MBU made free for the **7–15 age group from 1 October 2025 for one year**; the 5–7 and 15–17 brackets were already free. Net effect — **MBU is free for all children aged 5–17 until 30 September 2026**.

**Why it matters:** A clean example of **convergence of two databases (Aadhaar + UDISE+)** to close a last-mile compliance gap, and directly relevant to DBT integrity in scholarships, PM POSHAN and exam registration. Notebook: **GS2 — Governance (e-governance, Aadhaar architecture, service delivery)**.

## 4. **Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan** completes six years; National Mass Pledge Against Drug Abuse on 18 August

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment (Department of Social Justice and Empowerment)

A **nationwide National Mass Pledge Against Drug Abuse** was taken on **18 August 2026** to mark the **6th anniversary** of the Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan, mobilising citizens through physical and online participation. Implementation was coordinated by a high-level inter-ministerial meeting chaired by the **Joint Secretary (Drug Prevention)**, with pledge ceremonies across Railways, Defence, Police, CAPFs, AYUSH, Rural Development and Revenue formations, PSUs, autonomous bodies and educational institutions, and **nodal officers** nominated under a **uniform reporting mechanism**.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan (NMBA)**

- **Name / Ministry:** Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan — Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment.
- **Launched:** **15 August 2020**, initially in **272 vulnerable districts**; since expanded **nationwide**.
- **Objective:** **Demand reduction** for drugs — the awareness-and-community arm, complementing supply-side enforcement by NCB/police.
- **Key features:** (i) community outreach through youth clubs, women's groups and higher educational institutions; (ii) **volunteer cadre — "Nasha Mukti Mitr"** registered through the Department's website; (iii) **toll-free de-addiction helpline 14446**; (iv) convergence across Narcotics Bureau, State and district administrations, police, hospitals and NGOs, with treatment, rehabilitation and aftercare.
- **Coverage/achievement (as on date):** reached **over 29.26 crore individuals**, including **11.17 crore youth** and **7.90 crore women**; **28.30 lakh persons** treated and rehabilitated; outreach in **21.34 lakh educational institutions**.
- **Significance:** India's substance-abuse response is split between **supply control** (NDPS Act, 1985; NCB) and **demand reduction** (NMBA; the umbrella **NAPDDR** scheme). NMBA is the flagship of the second limb.

**Notebook:** **GS2 — Governance / Social Sector Schemes**; **GS1 — Society (social issues, youth)**.

## 5. PIB Backgrounder: **India's Coconut Sector** — first in global production, third in area

**Ministry/Body:** PIB Backgrounder; Coconut Development Board (CDB), Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare

An official factsheet released on 18 August places India **first in the world in coconut production** and **third in area under cultivation**, and sets out the policy scaffolding built around the crop — MSP for copra, CDB schemes, and the new Coconut Promotion Scheme.

**Key facts**

- **Global share:** **31.24% of world production** (rank 1); **17.90% of world area** (rank 3).
- **Domestic output (2024-25):** **over 20,301 million nuts** from **2.19 million hectares**; average productivity **9,249 nuts per hectare**.
- **State pattern (high-yield prelims fact):** **Keralam — largest area** (~**7.54 lakh ha**, ~5,149.87 million nuts); **Tamil Nadu — highest production**; **Andhra Pradesh — highest productivity**, followed by West Bengal and Tamil Nadu. *(Area leader ≠ production leader ≠ productivity leader.)*
- **Area expansion:** ~**2,175 ha** brought under new cultivation in **2025-26**; in 2024-25, **₹209.24 lakh** was used to bring **2,979 ha** under new cultivation, benefiting **13,068 farmers**. **₹632.77 lakh** provided for plantation establishment and maintenance in 2025-26.
- **CDB funding:** **₹147.21 crore** released for CDB activities in 2025-26.
- **MSP link:** **Copra** — the dried kernel — is one of the **22 mandated crops** under the MSP regime. *(Coconut itself is not; copra is.)*
- **New scheme:** the **Coconut Promotion Scheme** announced in **Union Budget 2026-27** for productivity, sustainability, exports and farmer incomes.
- **Value addition:** export basket has moved from raw/traditional products to **virgin coconut oil, coconut milk and activated carbon**.

**Why it matters:** A textbook case of **horticultural diversification** — where the income gain comes from processing and exports, not acreage. Notebook: **GS3 — Agriculture (horticulture, MSP, value chains, commodity boards)**.

## 6. **CIC launches AppCoMS 2.0** — upgraded portal for RTI second appeals and complaints (17 August)

**Ministry/Body:** Central Information Commission (CIC)

The Central Information Commission launched the upgraded **Appeal and Complaint Management System (AppCoMS 2.0)** on **17 August 2026**, released by Chief Information Commissioner **Raj Kumar Goyal**. It digitises end-to-end processing of **second appeals and complaints under the RTI Act, 2005**.

**Key facts**

- **AppCoMS** was first introduced in **September 2016**; 2.0 is the upgrade, delivered on the prescribed project timeline.
- **For citizens:** individual accounts linked to **email ID and mobile number** to file second appeals/complaints, **track case timelines**, access documents, download **hearing notices**, and obtain decisions and orders.
- **For Public Authorities:** user accounts for **CPIOs** to view case details and submit case documents through the portal.
- **RTI recall:** first appeal lies with the **First Appellate Authority** within the public authority; **second appeal** lies to the **CIC/State IC** — under **Section 19(3)**, within **90 days**. Complaints lie under **Section 18**.

**Why it matters:** CIC pendency is the standing critique of the RTI regime; case-tracking and CPIO-side digitisation attack turnaround time rather than the law itself. Notebook: **GS2 — Polity & Governance (RTI Act, transparency institutions)**.

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the **Positive Indigenisation Lists (PILs)** in the defence sector, consider the following statements:

1. Once the indicative timeline notified against an item expires, that item can be procured only from Indian industry.
2. The sixth Positive Indigenisation List notified in August 2026 covers items of Defence PSUs and the Indian Coast Guard.
3. The lists cover only complete weapon platforms and not spares, components or raw materials.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(b) 2 and 3 only

**Q2.** With reference to India's coconut sector, consider the following statements:

1. India ranks first in the world in coconut production and third in area under coconut cultivation.
2. The State with the largest area under coconut cultivation is also the State with the highest coconut production in India.
3. Copra is included among the mandated crops for which Minimum Support Price is announced.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q3.** Consider the following statements regarding appeals under the **Right to Information Act, 2005**:

1. A second appeal lies to the Central Information Commission or the State Information Commission, as the case may be.
2. The Appeal and Complaint Management System (AppCoMS) is a portal of the Central Information Commission for filing and processing second appeals and complaints.
3. The first appeal lies directly to the Central Information Commission against a decision of the Central Public Information Officer.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** A PIL functions as a phased import embargo: after the indicative indigenisation date, procurement must be from Indian industry. The 6th PIL (notified 18 August 2026) has **405 items — 389 of Defence PSUs and 16 of the Indian Coast Guard**. Statement 3 is wrong: the lists explicitly cover **line replaceable units, sub-systems, sub-assemblies, spares, components and raw materials**, not whole platforms.

**Q2 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** India accounts for **31.24% of world coconut production (rank 1)** and **17.90% of world area (rank 3)**. Statement 2 is wrong — **Keralam has the largest area (~7.54 lakh ha)** but **Tamil Nadu leads in production**, while **Andhra Pradesh has the highest productivity**. **Copra**, the dried kernel, is one of the **22 mandated crops under the MSP regime**.

**Q3 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Under **Section 19(3)** of the RTI Act, a **second appeal** lies to the CIC/State IC within 90 days; the **first appeal** lies to the **First Appellate Authority — an officer senior to the CPIO within the same public authority** — not to the CIC, so statement 3 is wrong. **AppCoMS**, introduced in September 2016 and upgraded to **AppCoMS 2.0 on 17 August 2026**, is the CIC's portal for second appeals and complaints.
