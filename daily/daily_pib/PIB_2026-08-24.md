## 1. **UPI completes 10 years** — recognised as the world's largest real-time payments system

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Finance (Department of Financial Services); system operated by the **National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI)** under **RBI** regulatory oversight | **Date:** 24 August 2026

The Unified Payments Interface — piloted by NPCI in **April 2016** and opened to the public through banking apps on **25 August 2016** — has completed a decade. The government's release frames UPI not as a payment app but as the **retail payments layer of India's Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)**, sitting alongside Aadhaar and the Account Aggregator framework, and now the single largest real-time payment system in the world by transaction volume.

**Why it matters:** This is the highest-yield "India model" item of the year for both prelims and mains — DPI as a *domestic* financial-inclusion instrument and as an *export* of governance technology (UPI linkages with Singapore, UAE, France, Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Mauritius). It also anchors the standard GS3 debate: interoperability and zero-MDR drove adoption, but the revenue model for the system's plumbing remains unresolved.

**Key facts / figures:**

- **Volume:** from **1.78 crore** transactions in 2016-17 to over **24,162 crore** in 2025-26 — roughly a **13,000-fold** rise.
- **Value:** from about **₹0.07 lakh crore** to about **₹314 lakh crore** over the same period — a **4,000-fold-plus** rise.
- **Daily throughput:** over **66 crore** transactions a day, against roughly **90,000** in UPI's debut month.
- **Banks live on UPI:** **44** (2016-17) → **703** (2025-26).
- **Global share:** roughly **49%** of world real-time payment transaction volume; the **IMF** has acknowledged UPI as the largest real-time payment system by volume.
- Architecture points worth memorising: built on **IMPS** rails, uses a **Virtual Payment Address (VPA)** so bank account numbers are never exposed, **interoperable** across apps and banks, and works on a **four-party** (payer PSP, payee PSP, remitter bank, beneficiary bank) model.

**Notebook:** **GS3 — Economy** (banking, financial inclusion, digital payments); overlaps **GS2 — Governance** (Digital Public Infrastructure, DBT plumbing).

## 2. India at **UNCCD COP17**: **21.76 million hectares** restored in 2011–2020; call for predictable land-restoration finance

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC) — Union Minister **Shri Bhupender Yadav** at **UNCCD COP17, Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia** | **Date:** 24 August 2026

*What's new since the 21 August briefing:* that item covered the **launch of India's first Guide to Grasslands and Other Open Natural Ecosystems** at the same COP. Today's two releases are a **different development** — India's headline restoration numbers under the **Bonn Challenge**, and a formal Indian intervention on **international financing** for land restoration and drought resilience.

India told COP17 that it brought **21.76 million hectares** of degraded and deforested land under restoration during **2011–2020**, against its **Bonn Challenge pledge of 26 million hectares by 2030** — roughly **84%** of the commitment already met. The Minister emphasised that restoration in India is simultaneously an **employment programme**, and in a second intervention India called for **"diverse, adequate, predictable and sustainable" international financing** for land restoration and drought resilience.

**Why it matters:** Land degradation is the one Rio Convention agenda where India can credibly claim delivery rather than pledges — and it lets you tie MGNREGS-type watershed and plantation works to a *treaty* commitment. The financing demand is the recurring North–South fault line: COP16 (Riyadh, 2024) failed to agree a **global drought protocol**, and COP17's central unresolved question is money.

**Key facts:**

- **21.76 million ha** restored (2011–2020); pledge **26 million ha by 2030** under the **Bonn Challenge**.
- Restoration activity generated about **1.22 billion person-days** of employment — the jobs-and-ecology linkage India argues at every land forum.
- **UNCCD COP17**: Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, **17–28 August 2026**; theme **"Restoring Land, Restoring Hope."**
- **UNCCD** is one of the **three Rio Conventions** (with UNFCCC and CBD); adopted **1994**, in force **1996**; the only one with a legally binding land mandate. **India hosted COP14 in New Delhi (2019)**, where it raised its **Land Degradation Neutrality (LDN)** ambition to **26 million ha by 2030**.
- The **Bonn Challenge** (2011) is a *voluntary* global goal — **150 million ha by 2020** and **350 million ha by 2030** — not a UNCCD instrument; it is hosted by **IUCN** and the German government, later endorsed by the **New York Declaration on Forests**.

**Notebook:** **GS3 — Environment** (land degradation, desertification, international conventions); usable in **GS2 — IR** (climate finance, North–South bargaining).

## 3. Health Ministry proposes **Medical Devices (Amendment) Rules, 2026**

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (through **CDSCO**) | **Date:** 24 August 2026

The Ministry has put out **draft amendments to the Medical Devices Rules, 2017**, aimed at **Ease of Doing Business (EoDB)** — simplifying compliance, standardising testing fees, and speeding market access for eligible devices. Three changes carry the weight.

**Why it matters:** India imports a large majority of its medical devices by value; the regulatory architecture is the binding constraint the government keeps trying to loosen alongside the PLI for medical devices and the National Medical Devices Policy, 2023. It is also a clean illustration of **regulatory reform via subordinate legislation** — rules framed under the **Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940**, since medical devices in India are notified as "drugs".

**Key facts — the three amendments:**

1. **Outsourced sterilisation simplified** — manufacturers may use specialised third-party sterilisation facilities with lighter documentation, fewer approvals and shorter timelines, cutting cost without a separate licence chain.
2. **Uniform testing fees** — a new **Ninth Schedule** is inserted into the Medical Devices Rules, 2017, prescribing standard fees for testing by **Medical Device Testing Laboratories**, ending lab-to-lab variation.
3. **EU added as a recognised jurisdiction** — **Rule 63** is amended to include the **European Union** among stringent regulatory jurisdictions whose approval permits a **waiver of clinical investigation** for devices without a predicate device (previously the recognised set was USA, UK, Australia, Canada and Japan).

**Notebook:** **GS2 — Governance** (regulation, statutory bodies — CDSCO/DCGI); **GS3 — Economy / Science & Tech** (manufacturing, import substitution, health technology).

## 4. Vice-President releases **India's first non-GMO high-expansion popcorn maize hybrid seed**

**Issuing body:** Vice-President's Secretariat — **Shri C. P. Radhakrishnan** at Musunuru, **Eluru district, Andhra Pradesh** | **Date:** 24 August 2026

The Vice-President released what is described as **India's first indigenously developed non-GMO, high-expansion popcorn maize hybrid seed**, at a programme at the Gourmet Popcornica facility at Musunuru, where he also felicitated farmers. The hybrid was bred in the **Nuzvid region** for Indian soil and climatic conditions, and the stated purpose is to cut dependence on **imported popcorn maize seed**.

**Why it matters:** "High-expansion" popcorn maize is a **specialty/high-value maize** segment where India has been import-dependent for planting material. The item connects three live GS3 threads — **seed sovereignty**, **crop diversification away from paddy–wheat**, and **maize as the feedstock of the ethanol blending programme**. Note the GM angle: **Bt cotton remains the only GM crop approved for commercial cultivation in India**, so a *non-GMO* hybrid sidesteps the GEAC approval pathway entirely.

**Key facts:**

- **"Expansion volume"** (or popping expansion ratio) is the technical quality parameter for popcorn maize — the ratio of popped to unpopped kernel volume; high-expansion types command a premium.
- India is the world's **fourth-largest maize producer**; maize is India's **third most important cereal** after rice and wheat, and demand is being pulled up by **poultry feed and ethanol**.
- Andhra Pradesh's **Eluru/Nuzvid belt** is a major seed-production cluster; the state is among India's leading hybrid-seed producing regions.
- Regulatory contrast for prelims: **GEAC** (under MoEFCC) approves GM organisms; **non-GM hybrids** are handled through varietal release/notification under the **Seeds Act, 1966** and the **Central Seed Committee**.

**Notebook:** **GS3 — Agriculture** (seeds, crop diversification, agri-value chains); touches **GS3 — Science & Technology** (biotechnology and GM regulation).

## 5. MeitY launches **GENESIS Entrepreneur-in-Residence (EiR) Cohort-3**

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), through **MeitY Startup Hub (MSH)**; launched at the **GENESIS NextGen Startup Summit — Coimbatore Edition** | **Date:** 24 August 2026

MeitY has opened **Cohort-3 (EiR 3.0)** of the Entrepreneur-in-Residence component of **GENESIS**, a national call inviting aspiring entrepreneurs, innovators, researchers, students and early-stage founders to convert ideas into technology ventures. Selected innovators receive **up to ₹10 lakh** in financial assistance through participating GENESIS incubators, plus incubation and mentorship. **Applications close 3 September 2026.** The summit theme was **"Igniting Innovation Beyond the Metros."**

**Why it matters:** GENESIS is the government's explicit answer to the geographic concentration of India's startup ecosystem in a handful of metros — the Economic Survey's recurring point that innovation capacity is spatially lopsided. Pair it with **Startup India**, the **Fund of Funds for Startups (SIDBI)** and **Atal Incubation Centres** when a question asks about the startup support architecture.

### Scheme-in-focus: **GENESIS — Gen-Next Support for Innovative Startups**

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY) |
| **Implementing agency** | **MeitY Startup Hub (MSH)**, working through Implementing Agencies (incubators), **preferably located in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities** |
| **Objective** | Discover, support, grow and scale **tech startups outside the metros**, bridging the gap between innovation and commercialisation |
| **Outlay** | About **₹490 crore** over five years |
| **Target** | Roughly **1,600 tech startups** across the network of implementing agencies |

**Key features (4):**

1. **Ideation Support (the Entrepreneur-in-Residence component)** — a stipend-style grant of **up to ₹10 lakh** to de-risk the pre-company stage for an individual innovator; this is the component whose **Cohort-3** opened today.
2. **Pilot / Proof-of-Concept Support** — funding of **up to ₹40 lakh** for startups to run pilots with corporates, PSUs and large enterprises, solving the "first customer" problem.
3. **Deep-Tech Startup Support** — dedicated assistance for hardware-heavy and long-gestation deep-tech ventures that conventional seed capital avoids.
4. **Matching Investment Funding** — government money that matches private/angel investment, crowding in rather than substituting for private capital.

**Beneficiaries / coverage:** aspiring and early-stage tech entrepreneurs, with a deliberate bias towards **Tier-2 and Tier-3 city** ecosystems.

**Significance:** Shifts startup policy from *national schemes administered from Delhi* to *distributed incubation capacity*, addressing the regional-inequality critique of India's innovation economy.

**Notebook:** **GS3 — Science & Technology** (innovation ecosystem, startups); **GS3 — Economy** (employment, entrepreneurship, regional development).

## 6. **Operation Black Hawk** — Central Bureau of Narcotics busts inter-state heroin syndicate

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Finance (Department of Revenue) — **Central Bureau of Narcotics (CBN)**, Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh units | **Date:** 24 August 2026

In back-to-back operations near **Lucknow**, CBN teams seized a total of **over 18.6 kg of crude heroin** and arrested **three key members** of an inter-state trafficking network. Joint teams laid a cordon on the **Sultanpur–Lucknow highway** on the intervening night of **20–21 August**, intercepted an **Assam-registered car**, and recovered **25 waterproof packages holding 16.46 kg** of crude heroin from a specially fabricated cavity; the balance came from a separate CBN-MP seizure. The consignment had originated in a **North-Eastern state** and was destined for consumption in **Uttar Pradesh**.

**Why it matters:** The routing is the exam-relevant detail — it points to the **Golden Triangle** (Myanmar–Laos–Thailand) corridor feeding heroin into India through the **North-East**, as distinct from the **Golden Crescent** (Afghanistan–Pakistan–Iran) route that feeds Punjab and the western seaboard. Also note the agency: the **CBN sits under the Department of Revenue**, not the MHA, and is distinct from the **Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB)**, which is the MHA's nodal drug-law-enforcement agency.

**Key facts:**

- Total seizure: **18.6 kg+** crude heroin (16.46 kg in the primary interception); **3 arrests**.
- Governing statute: **Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances (NDPS) Act, 1985**, which classifies offences by **small / intermediate / commercial quantity**.
- **CBN** (HQ **Gwalior**, Department of Revenue) chiefly regulates **licit opium poppy cultivation** and licit narcotics trade, and also enforces against illicit traffic; **NCB** (HQ New Delhi, MHA) is the national coordinating agency created in 1986.
- Context: the item lands during the sixth-anniversary month of the **Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan** (covered 18 August) — the demand-reduction half of the same policy.

**Notebook:** **GS3 — Internal Security** (drug trafficking, transnational organised crime, security challenges in border areas).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the Unified Payments Interface (UPI), consider the following statements:

1. It is operated by the National Payments Corporation of India under the regulatory oversight of the Reserve Bank of India.
2. It is built on the Immediate Payment Service (IMPS) infrastructure and uses a Virtual Payment Address so that bank account details need not be shared.
3. As of 2025-26, the International Monetary Fund has recognised it as the world's largest real-time payment system by transaction volume.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q2.** Consider the following statements about the Bonn Challenge and India's land restoration commitments:

1. The Bonn Challenge is a legally binding target adopted under the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification.
2. India raised its land restoration ambition to 26 million hectares by 2030 at the UNCCD COP held in New Delhi.
3. India reported at UNCCD COP17 that it had brought about 21.76 million hectares of degraded land under restoration during 2011–2020.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** The "GENESIS" programme, seen in the news recently, is associated with which one of the following?

- (a) A Ministry of Environment scheme for restoring degraded grasslands and open natural ecosystems
- (b) A Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology initiative to support technology startups in Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities
- (c) An ICMR programme for genome sequencing of endemic disease pathogens
- (d) A Ministry of Agriculture mission for the development and release of non-GMO hybrid seeds

## Answers

**Q1 — (d) 1, 2 and 3**
All three are correct. UPI is operated by NPCI under RBI oversight, is built on the IMPS rails and uses a Virtual Payment Address (VPA) so account numbers are never exposed, and the IMF has acknowledged it as the world's largest real-time payment system by transaction volume — with roughly a 49% share of global real-time payment volume.

**Q2 — (b) 2 and 3 only**
Statement 1 is wrong: the **Bonn Challenge (2011) is a voluntary global goal**, hosted by IUCN and the German government, not a legally binding UNCCD instrument. Statement 2 is correct — India enhanced its restoration ambition to 26 million hectares by 2030 at **UNCCD COP14, New Delhi (2019)**. Statement 3 is correct — the figure reported at COP17, Ulaanbaatar, was **21.76 million hectares** for 2011–2020, generating about 1.22 billion person-days of employment.

**Q3 — (b)**
**GENESIS — "Gen-Next Support for Innovative Startups"** — is a **MeitY** initiative implemented through MeitY Startup Hub, with an outlay of about ₹490 crore over five years, targeting roughly 1,600 startups through incubators located preferably in **Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities**. Its Entrepreneur-in-Residence (EiR) Cohort-3, launched on 24 August 2026, offers up to ₹10 lakh per selected innovator.
