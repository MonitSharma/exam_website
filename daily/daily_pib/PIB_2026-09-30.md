## 1. Ministry of Environment, Forest & Climate Change: **First PRAMAAN certificates issued under India's own forest and wood certification scheme** (29 Sep)

**What it is:** Union Environment Minister **Bhupender Yadav** presented the first certificates under **PRAMAAN**, the label of the **Indian Forest and Wood Certification Scheme (IFWCS)**. The Ministry launched the scheme in **December 2023**. The first two certificates went to:
- the **Andhra Pradesh Forest Development Corporation (APFDC)**, for about **33,000 ha** of plantations;
- **Govind Mohapatra**, an individual bamboo farmer linked to the **Odisha Bamboo Development Agency (OBDA)**.

**Why it matters:** Buyers such as the EU now require proof that timber and wood products are **deforestation-free** (see the **EU Deforestation Regulation, EUDR**). Until now, Indian producers depended on foreign private labels such as FSC and PEFC for this proof. PRAMAAN is a government-backed domestic alternative with third-party audits. It covers **trees outside forests (agroforestry)**, which is where much of India's wood supply comes from, and it aims to include smallholders, bamboo growers and women's SHGs.

**Key facts:**
- **Full form:** Programme for Recognition and Accreditation of sustainable Management practices for Agroforestry And Natural forestry resources.
- **Scheme operator:** **Indian Institute of Forest Management (IIFM), Bhopal**, under MoEFCC.
- **Three certification streams:** (i) **Forest Management (FM)**, (ii) **Trees Outside Forests (TOF) Management**, (iii) **Chain of Custody (CoC)**, which gives traceability through the supply chain.
- **Standard:** the Indian Forest Management Standard, part of the **National Working Plan Code (NWPC) 2023**. Audits are done by independent certification bodies accredited by the **National Accreditation Board for Certification Bodies (NABCB)**, not by self-declaration.
- The scheme is benchmarked against international certification frameworks so that certified products can meet export due-diligence rules.

**Notebook:** GS3 – Environment (forest conservation, agroforestry, trade–environment linkages). GS2 – IR (EUDR as a non-tariff measure).

## 2. Ministry of Labour & Employment: **"Shram Samvad" — 90-day awareness campaign on the four Labour Codes across 316 districts** (28 Sep)

**What it is:** Labour Minister **Mansukh Mandaviya** announced **Shram Samvad** at the CII–EFI National Conference on Industrial Relations in New Delhi. Ministry teams will visit **industrial clusters in 316 districts across all 36 States/UTs over 90 days**. They will meet industry, lawyers, chartered accountants and other intermediaries to clear up confusion about the new labour-law framework. The Minister said stakeholders are still confused about the Codes.

**Why it matters:** The Codes are law, but implementing them depends on States framing their own rules and on employers actually complying. Shram Samvad shows the Ministry moving to outreach and consultation to close that implementation gap. This is useful GS2 material on how a law's design differs from its implementation, and on labour as a Concurrent List subject.

**Key facts:**
- **The four Codes:** Code on Wages, 2019; Industrial Relations Code, 2020; Code on Social Security, 2020; Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020. Together they consolidated **29 central labour laws**.
- **Figures cited by the Ministry:** social-security coverage rose from **19% (2015) to 68%** of the population (about **101 crore** people). Employment elasticity was about **1.11** for 2017–23.
- Released at the event: an **"Employer's Guide under the New Labour Codes Framework: Fixed-Term Employment in India"**.
- Labour Secretary **Chandra Bhushan Kumar** said the **National Classification of Occupations** is being updated to add emerging roles, including AI-related jobs.

**Notebook:** GS2 – Governance (implementation of legislation, Centre–State roles). GS3 – Economy (employment, labour reforms, informal sector).

## 3. Ministry of Steel: **Steel Industry Safety Council (SISC) set up as an apex safety body, backed by a Steel Industry Safety Directorate** (28–30 Sep)

**What it is:** The Ministry of Steel has set up an apex **Steel Industry Safety Council**, chaired by the **Secretary, Ministry of Steel** (**Sandeep Poundrik**). Its members are:
- senior Ministry officials;
- CEOs of integrated steel producers;
- representatives of the **Directorate General of Mines Safety (DGMS)**, the **Petroleum and Explosives Safety Organisation (PESO)** and the **National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA)**;
- steel technology institutions.

**Why it matters:** Blast furnaces, coke ovens, gas lines and molten-metal handling make steel one of the most accident-prone process industries. India's steel capacity is expanding fast under the **National Steel Policy 2017** target of about 300 MT capacity by 2030–31. Until now, safety regulation was spread across factory, mines and explosives regulators. This is the first sector-specific apex body that sets standards and studies accidents, which links to GS3 disaster management (industrial disasters) and GS2 occupational safety.

**Key facts:**
- **SISC functions:**
  - assess safety conditions across the industry;
  - facilitate studies and improvements in safety practice;
  - **approve safety standards**;
  - examine serious accidents and **near-miss incidents**;
  - issue guidance on safety awareness, training and prevention.
- **Steel Industry Safety Directorate (SISD):** the technical arm. It implements SISC decisions, runs **periodic safety audits**, investigates serious incidents, drafts **Steel Industry Safety Standards suited to Indian conditions**, and keeps accident data. Its expertise covers process safety, blast furnaces, steel melting shops, rolling mills, coke ovens, electrical and mechanical safety, occupational health, fire engineering and disaster management.
- **Context:** finished-steel production was **15.9 MT in January–August 2026, up about 6%**, according to reports of the release. *(Steel demand data was covered in the 25 Sep briefing. The safety institution is new.)*

**Notebook:** GS3 – Industry, Disaster Management (industrial safety). GS2 – Statutory, regulatory and quasi-judicial bodies.

## 4. Ministry of Skill Development & Entrepreneurship: **India finishes 10th at WorldSkills Shanghai 2026 with 6 silver medals and 20 Medals for Excellence** (27–28 Sep) *(UPDATE)*

**What's new:** The 9 Sep briefing previewed India's entry. The results are now in. India finished **10th**, up from **13th at WorldSkills Lyon 2024**.

**Why it matters:** WorldSkills is one of the few international benchmarks for a country's vocational training (TVET) system. Useful for GS2/GS3 answers on skilling, the demographic dividend and Skill India.

**Key facts:**
- **48th WorldSkills Competition**, **Shanghai, China, 22–27 September 2026**. About **1,400+ competitors from ~70 countries/regions** took part.
- **Team India:** **70 competitors** (its largest ever) in **63 skills**, including **11 skills entered for the first time**. **NSDC** ran India's participation, with support from Sector Skill Councils.
- **Silver medallists:** Smriti Nambiar (Bakery), Mohammed Salam (Dental Prosthetics), Kovid Ajay Gangrade (Digital Interactive Media Design), Parth Vohra (Industrial Design Technology), Mehrunissa Begum (Logistics), Saniya Joshi (Retail Sales).
- **Trajectory:** 29th (2015) → 13th (2024) → **10th (2026)**. WorldSkills is held every two years.

**Notebook:** GS2 – Welfare / HRD (skilling). GS3 – Economy (employment, human capital).

## Prelims MCQs

**Q1.** With reference to PRAMAAN, recently in the news, consider the following statements:
1. It is the label of the Indian Forest and Wood Certification Scheme, and the Indian Institute of Forest Management, Bhopal is its scheme operator.
2. It certifies only natural forests managed by State Forest Departments; trees outside forests and agroforestry are excluded.
3. Certification audits are done by independent bodies accredited by the National Accreditation Board for Certification Bodies (NABCB).

Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 1 and 3 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q2.** The Ministry of Labour & Employment's "Shram Samvad" campaign covers the four Labour Codes. Which one of the following is **NOT** one of the four Labour Codes?
(a) Code on Wages, 2019
(b) Industrial Relations Code, 2020
(c) Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020
(d) Code on Employment Guarantee and Minimum Livelihood, 2020

**Q3.** With reference to the Steel Industry Safety Council (SISC), recently constituted in India, which one of the following statements is correct?
(a) It is a statutory body under the Factories Act, 1948, chaired by the Union Labour Secretary.
(b) It is chaired by the Secretary, Ministry of Steel, includes representatives of DGMS, PESO and NDMA, and is supported by a Steel Industry Safety Directorate.
(c) It is an international body set up under the World Steel Association to certify Indian steel plants.
(d) It replaces the Bureau of Indian Standards as the authority for notifying steel quality standards.

---

## Answers

1. **(b)** — Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Statement 2 is wrong because PRAMAAN has a separate **Trees Outside Forests** stream as well as Forest Management and Chain of Custody. One of the first two certificates went to an individual bamboo farmer in Odisha.
2. **(d)** — The four Codes are the Code on Wages (2019), the Industrial Relations Code (2020), the Code on Social Security (2020) and the OSH&WC Code (2020). Option (d) does not exist.
3. **(b)** — The SISC was set up by the Ministry of Steel, is chaired by the Steel Secretary, and includes DGMS, PESO and NDMA. The SISD is its technical arm for audits, investigations and standards. It is not a Factories Act body and does not replace BIS.
