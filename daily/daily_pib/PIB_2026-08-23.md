## 1. National Workshop on "Mission Zero — Ending Fatalities in Sanitation Work" held at Bhubaneswar

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment (through the National Safai Karamcharis Finance and Development Corporation, NSKFDC), in association with the Government of Odisha | **Date:** 22 August 2026

A national workshop themed **"Mission Zero — Ending Fatalities in Sanitation Work"** was held at the State Convention Centre, Lokseva Bhawan, Bhubaneswar, alongside the **second National GARIMA Conclave 2026**, inaugurated by Odisha Chief Minister Shri Mohan Charan Majhi. The stated purpose was to strengthen institutional mechanisms, promote appropriate technologies, ensure operational safety and expand **mechanised sanitation services** across cities.

**Why it matters:** Manual scavenging is prohibited by the **Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Act, 2013**, yet deaths during hazardous sewer and septic-tank cleaning continue. "Zero fatalities" is the headline outcome indicator of the NAMASTE scheme, and this workshop is the operational push behind it — a classic GS2 "vulnerable sections / welfare scheme implementation gap" theme.

**Key facts:**

- Participants: senior officials, **urban local bodies**, sanitation experts, technology providers, equipment manufacturers, civil society and sanitation workers themselves.
- Focus: mechanisation, advanced technology and safer working practices to reach **zero fatalities** in sanitation operations.
- The Supreme Court in *Dr Balram Singh v. Union of India* (2023) had directed the Centre and States to work towards complete eradication of manual scavenging and enhanced compensation for sewer deaths — useful linkage in mains answers.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance / Welfare Schemes for Vulnerable Sections (also usable in GS1 Society — caste and dignity of labour).

### Scheme-in-focus: **NAMASTE — National Action for Mechanised Sanitation Ecosystem**

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment, jointly formulated with the Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs |
| **Implementing agency** | National Safai Karamcharis Finance and Development Corporation (NSKFDC) |
| **Objective** | Eliminate hazardous cleaning, achieve **zero fatalities** among sewer and septic-tank workers (SSWs), and ensure their safety, dignity and sustainable livelihood |
| **Coverage** | All **4,800+ Urban Local Bodies** in the country |
| **Outlay** | **₹349.70 crore** for the three years up to 2025-26 |

**Key features (4):**

1. **Profiling and identification** of all sewer and septic-tank workers, and issue of PPE kits and safety devices; occupational safety training.
2. **Health insurance** cover under Ayushman Bharat–PM-JAY for identified workers and their families.
3. **Capital subsidy and skilling** for sanitation workers to become **Sanitation Response Units / sanipreneurs** operating mechanised equipment — the erstwhile **Self Employment Scheme for Rehabilitation of Manual Scavengers (SRMS)** stands subsumed in NAMASTE.
4. **Waste pickers added as a target group in June 2024**, with a Waste Picker Enumeration App and an aim to profile about **2.5 lakh waste pickers**.

**Beneficiaries:** sewer and septic-tank workers, and (since 2024) urban waste pickers — overwhelmingly from Scheduled Castes and other marginalised communities.

**Significance:** Shifts the State's posture from *rehabilitation after the fact* to *engineering the hazard out of existence* — mechanisation plus formalisation plus enterprise ownership by the workers themselves.

## 2. BRICS unanimously adopts the **'Chennai Declaration'** on science, technology and innovation

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Science & Technology (Department of Science and Technology) | **Date:** 22 August 2026

At the closing session of the **14th BRICS Science, Technology and Innovation (STI) Ministerial Meeting** at Chennai, chaired by Union Minister of State (Independent Charge) for Science & Technology **Dr Jitendra Singh**, member countries unanimously adopted the **Chennai Declaration**, reaffirming a commitment to an "effective, resilient and future-ready" STI framework. The meeting was held under **India's BRICS Chairship 2026**.

**Why it matters:** This is India's flagship science-diplomacy outcome of its chairship year and pairs with the other 2026 BRICS tracks India has hosted (Environment Ministers, Communications Ministers, Youth Ministers). Expect prelims questions on "which declaration came out of which BRICS ministerial in India's chairship year".

**Key facts:**

- Thematic sweep: **artificial intelligence and high-performance computing, quantum technologies, advanced materials, biotechnology, health, climate and sustainability**.
- The Declaration puts a **"people-centred and humanity-first" approach** at the core of BRICS STI cooperation.
- It **welcomes the BRICS Youth Startup Platform** and records consideration of a proposed **BRICS Startup Innovation Fund** to catalyse financing for early- and growth-stage startups.
- Framed by the **"Vishwa Bandhu Bharat"** vision articulated by the Prime Minister.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (groupings India is part of); GS3 — Science & Technology (indigenisation, innovation ecosystem).

## 3. National Conference on **AgriStack** and the **Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses**

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare | **Date:** 22 August 2026, ICAR Convention Centre, Pusa, New Delhi

Union Agriculture and Rural Development Minister **Shri Shivraj Singh Chouhan** chaired a national review of **AgriStack** under the Digital Agriculture Mission and of the **Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses**, telling States that scheme benefits must show up "in the fields and in farmers' pockets", not merely in official reports, and pressing for **100% saturation** under both.

**Why it matters:** AgriStack is the plumbing for almost every future farm-facing DBT, crop insurance and advisory service; the pulses mission is India's answer to a persistent import dependence in tur, urad and masoor. Together they are the two most examinable strands of GS3 agriculture right now.

**Key facts:**

- **Farmer ID** is the anchor registry — a unique digital identity linking a farmer to land and other assets.
- Roughly **40% of India's sown area is still not linked to Farmer IDs**, and most States are yet to sync the farmer registry with the **Record of Rights (RoR)** — the binding constraint flagged at the conference.
- AgriStack's three foundational registries: **Farmers' Registry, Geo-referenced Village Maps, and Crop Sown Registry** (built through the Digital Crop Survey).
- Under the pulses mission, a target of **528 processing units** across States by **2026-27**.
- States represented included Haryana, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh and Nagaland; MoS Shri Bhagirath Choudhary and Agriculture Secretary Shri Atish Chandra participated.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (e-technology in the aid of farmers; food security; cropping patterns and pulses self-sufficiency).

## 4. Home Minister reviews **Siliguri Corridor** security; push for a "triangular grid"

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Home Affairs | **Date:** 22 August 2026, Sukna (near Siliguri), West Bengal

Union Home Minister **Shri Amit Shah** chaired a security review of the **Siliguri Corridor** — the "Chicken's Neck" — on the concluding day of his West Bengal visit, and separately met stakeholders and representatives of the hill areas of West Bengal in the presence of the Chief Minister of West Bengal.

**Why it matters:** The corridor is the single land link between the North-Eastern States and the rest of India, pinched between Nepal, Bangladesh and Bhutan and close to the Chumbi Valley. Its security is simultaneously a geography question, an internal-security question and a border-management question.

**Key facts:**

- The **Multi Agency Centre (MAC)**'s **Standing Focus Group on Siliguri Corridor (SFG-SC)** presented progress and recommendations.
- The Home Minister pushed a **"triangular security grid"** anchored on new garrisons at **Dhubri (Assam), Kishanganj (Bihar) and Chopra (West Bengal)**.
- Directions to agencies: **increase institutional footprint** in the corridor, keep it **free of encroachments**, and act to **stop illegal immigration**; the State was urged to strengthen **Civil Defence and Home Guards** in the corridor.
- Issues discussed: demographic change, illegal immigration, PIO-linked activity and border affairs.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security (border management, illegal migration); GS1 — Geography of India (strategic chokepoints).

## 5. IAF concludes **Exercise Pitch Black 2026** (Australia) and **Exercise Udara Shakti 2026** (Malaysia)

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Defence | **Date:** 22 August 2026

The Indian Air Force concluded a **back-to-back dual-nation overseas deployment**: first **Exercise Pitch Black 2026**, hosted by the **Royal Australian Air Force**, and then **Exercise Udara Shakti 2026**, a bilateral exercise with the **Royal Malaysian Air Force** at **Subang** air base.

**Why it matters:** Multinational air exercises are a standard prelims staple (which exercise, with which country). The Indo-Pacific framing also feeds GS2 answers on India's defence diplomacy.

**Key facts:**

- **Pitch Black 2026:** IAF deployed frontline **Rafale** aircraft; **over 20 participating nations**; complex **Large Force Employment (LFE)** missions in a high-threat, multi-domain setting.
- **Udara Shakti 2026:** bilateral with Malaysia; high-intensity combat sorties, joint tactical manoeuvres, greater operational flexibility. (Malaysia also operates the Su-30MKM, making this exercise unusually useful for interoperability.)
- The contingent moved **directly from Australia to Malaysia**, demonstrating sustained expeditionary reach.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Security (defence exercises, military modernisation); GS2 — IR (Indo-Pacific partnerships).

## 6. India observes the **third National Space Day**, 23 August 2026

**Issuing body:** Department of Space / ISRO; greetings issued by the Prime Minister | **Date:** 23 August 2026

India marked its **third National Space Day** on 23 August 2026, with the national event hosted at **Ahmedabad**. The theme for 2026 is **"Towards Viksit Bharat — Innovating, Collaborating and Aspiring to Global Space Leadership."** The Prime Minister also released the text of his interaction with **CEOs and founders of space start-ups**, held in New Delhi on 21 August.

**Why it matters:** National Space Day is the government's chosen anchor for space-sector outreach, and the start-up interaction signals the continuing shift from an ISRO-monopoly model to a regulated private ecosystem under IN-SPACe and NSIL.

**Key facts:**

- **23 August** was designated National Space Day to mark the soft landing of **Chandrayaan-3's Vikram lander** on the lunar surface on **23 August 2023**, at the site named **Shiv Shakti Point**, near the lunar south pole.
- India became the **fourth country** to soft-land on the Moon and the **first to land in the lunar south polar region**.
- The **first** National Space Day was observed in **2024**; 2026 is the third edition.
- 2026 activities: expert sessions, live demonstrations and student–scientist interactions across the country.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (space programme, achievements of Indians in S&T).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**1.** With reference to the **NAMASTE (National Action for Mechanised Sanitation Ecosystem)** scheme, consider the following statements:

1. It has been jointly formulated by the Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment and the Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs.
2. The National Safai Karamcharis Finance and Development Corporation (NSKFDC) is its implementing agency.
3. Waste pickers were included as a target group under the scheme in 2024.
4. It replaced the Self Employment Scheme for Rehabilitation of Manual Scavengers (SRMS), whose components were subsumed into it.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) Only three
(d) All four

**2.** The **'Chennai Declaration'**, seen in the news recently, is associated with:

(a) The outcome document of the 14th BRICS Science, Technology and Innovation Ministerial Meeting held under India's BRICS Chairship
(b) A World Health Organization resolution on antimicrobial resistance adopted at a regional committee session in India
(c) A joint statement of the Indian Ocean Rim Association on maritime search-and-rescue cooperation
(d) The concluding statement of the G20 Digital Economy Working Group under India's presidency

---

**3.** With reference to the **Siliguri Corridor**, consider the following statements:

1. It is the narrow stretch of Indian territory that connects the North-Eastern States with the rest of India.
2. It lies between the international borders of Nepal, Bhutan and Bangladesh.
3. It falls entirely within the State of Assam.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**1. (d) All four.** NAMASTE is a joint MoSJE–MoHUA scheme implemented through NSKFDC, covering all 4,800+ ULBs with an outlay of ₹349.70 crore up to 2025-26; waste pickers were added as a target group in June 2024, and SRMS components (PM-JAY health cover, skilling, capital subsidy) were subsumed into it.

**2. (a).** The Chennai Declaration was adopted unanimously at the closing session of the 14th BRICS STI Ministerial Meeting chaired by Dr Jitendra Singh under India's BRICS Chairship 2026; it welcomes the BRICS Youth Startup Platform and records consideration of a BRICS Startup Innovation Fund.

**3. (a) 1 and 2 only.** The corridor — the "Chicken's Neck" — is India's only land link to the North-East and is squeezed between Nepal, Bhutan and Bangladesh. Statement 3 is wrong: it lies in **West Bengal** (around Siliguri, Jalpaiguri and Darjeeling districts), not Assam — the proposed "triangular grid" garrisons at Dhubri, Kishanganj and Chopra span Assam, Bihar and West Bengal respectively.
