## 1. Health Ministry expands QR-code "Track & Trace" to vaccines, antimicrobials, anti-cancer & narcotic drugs

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (MoHFW) / CDSCO | **Date:** 25 June 2026 | **Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (health regulation) · GS3 — Science & Technology / Health

**What & why it matters:** The MoHFW notified amendments to the **Drugs Rules, 1945**, expanding **Schedule H2** to bring **vaccines, antimicrobials, anti-cancer (oncology) medicines and narcotic drugs** under a mandatory **QR-code-based track-and-trace framework**. Manufacturers must affix QR/barcodes carrying product data (unique product identification, batch number, manufacture/expiry dates) so any medicine can be **authenticated across the supply chain**. The aim is to **curb counterfeit and spurious drugs** and to strengthen India's fight against **Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)**. A clean prelims + mains hook (drug safety regulation, "pharmacy of the world," AMR/One Health).

**Key facts:**
- Builds on the existing barcode mandate that since **August 2023** covered the **top 300 pharma brands** (Schedule H2); this widens the net to high-risk categories.
- **Phased compliance:** vaccines and narcotic drugs by **July 2027**; antimicrobials by **July 2028**.
- Regulator: **CDSCO** (Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation); apex authority is the **Drugs Controller General of India (DCGI)**; the rules flow from the **Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940** and Drugs Rules, 1945.

## 2. India to launch its first-ever Index of Services Production (ISP)

**Issuing body:** National Statistical Office (NSO), Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation (MoSPI) | **Date:** announced 24 June 2026 | **Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Reports & Indices / data infrastructure)

**What & why it matters:** MoSPI announced that India's **first Index of Services Production (ISP)** — a high-frequency indicator tracking short-term output of the **services sector** — will see its first (trial) release on **14 July 2026**. It fills a long-standing data gap: the services sector contributes **over 50% of India's Gross Value Added (GVA)** yet had no monthly volume index, unlike industry which has the **Index of Industrial Production (IIP)**. The ISP **complements (does not replace)** the IIP and will sharpen GDP nowcasting and policy responsiveness.

**Key facts:**
- **Proposed base year:** **2024-25 (FY25)**.
- **Data backbone:** mainly **GST data** on outward supplies (trade, real estate, telecom, professional services); **administrative records** for railways, air transport, banking, insurance; and the **Annual Survey of Incorporated Services Sector Enterprises (ASISSE)** for health & education.
- **Release cadence:** **monthly with ~60-day lag**; regular indices to be published on the **29th of each month**.
- **Initial coverage:** trade, transport, banking, insurance, telecom, hospitality, real estate, professional services, entertainment.

> **Initiative snapshot — Index of Services Production (ISP)**
> - **Body:** NSO, MoSPI · **Type:** high-frequency statistical index (services counterpart to the IIP).
> - **Objective:** measure short-term volume changes in services output; improve growth tracking of India's largest GVA contributor.
> - **Significance:** better data for monetary/fiscal policy; supports the "data-driven governance" and credible-statistics agenda.

## 4. Six states reach consensus on the long-pending Kishau Multipurpose Project

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Jal Shakti; meeting chaired by Union Home Minister & Minister of Cooperation **Amit Shah** | **Date:** consensus 16 June 2026 (MoU to follow) | **Notebook:** GS1 — Geography (drainage/water resources) · GS3 — Infrastructure / Energy / Environment

**What & why it matters:** Six states agreed to sign an **MoU** to implement the **₹15,000-crore Kishau Multipurpose Project**, ending an **~8-year deadlock**. It is central to **Yamuna rejuvenation** and Delhi-region water security, and is a textbook example of **cooperative federalism** in inter-state river management. After the MoU, the project goes to the **Union Cabinet** for approval.

**Key facts:**
- **Location/river:** on the **Tons River** — the **largest tributary of the Yamuna** — on the **Dehradun (Uttarakhand)–Sirmour (Himachal Pradesh)** border.
- **Design:** a **236-metre-high dam**, **660 MW** of hydropower plus large **water storage**.
- **Six states:** Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Rajasthan.
- **Cost-sharing:** **90%** of the **water-component** cost by the **Centre** (Central Assistance), **10%** by the six states; HP's water share is reallocated to **Delhi & Rajasthan** in exchange for sharing HP's power-component cost.
- **Static link:** Tons → Yamuna → Ganga (the Yamuna is the largest tributary of the Ganga).

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** ISP is **MoSPI's** first high-frequency services index (first release **14 July 2026**, proposed base year **FY25**) and relies largely on **GST data** on outward supplies. Statement 3 is wrong — it **complements, not replaces,** the **IIP**: the IIP measures industrial output while the ISP measures services output.

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All correct. The Kishau dam is on the **Tons** (largest tributary of the **Yamuna**), on the **Dehradun (Uttarakhand)–Sirmour (Himachal Pradesh)** border; the **236-m** dam will yield **660 MW** plus large water storage. Six states and the Centre reached consensus in **June 2026** (90:10 Centre–state cost-sharing on the water component).

**Q3 — (b) Authenticate medicines and curb counterfeit and spurious drugs.** The **MoHFW** expanded **Schedule H2** to bring **vaccines, antimicrobials, anti-cancer medicines and narcotics** under QR-code track-and-trace — to authenticate medicines, curb counterfeits, and support the fight against **Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)**.
