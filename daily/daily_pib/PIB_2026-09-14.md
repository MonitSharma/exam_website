## 1. Ministry of Power: **Export of up to 654 MW of power to Nepal approved after flood damage to Nepali hydropower**

**What it is.** The Ministry of Power has approved the export of electricity to the **Nepal Electricity Authority (NEA)** for **18 hours a day (00:00 to 18:00 hours)**, valid from **13 September 2026 to 31 December 2026**. The quantum to be exported from January 2027 will be reviewed in December 2026.

**Why it matters.** This is a reversal of the recent direction of the India–Nepal power trade, in which Nepal has increasingly been a *seller* of surplus monsoon hydropower to India. Recent floods damaged Nepal's hydropower infrastructure, cutting its domestic generation and creating a deficit. It is a good illustration of how **cross-border electricity trade functions as a resilience mechanism**, not merely a commercial one — and of India's "Neighbourhood First" energy diplomacy in practice.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Up to 600 MW** through the **Muzaffarpur (Bihar) – Dhalkebar (Nepal) 400 kV double-circuit line** — the principal India–Nepal cross-border transmission link.
- **Up to 54 MW** through the **Tanakpur (Uttarakhand) – Mahendranagar (Nepal) 132 kV single-circuit line**.
- Total approved: **up to 654 MW**, for 18 hours a day.
- Trigger: flood damage to Nepal's hydropower infrastructure reducing domestic generation capacity.

**Prelims hooks.** D/c = double circuit; S/c = single circuit. Muzaffarpur–Dhalkebar is 400 kV; Tanakpur–Mahendranagar is 132 kV. Nepal Electricity Authority is the counterparty.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India–Nepal / Neighbourhood First); GS3 — Infrastructure & Energy.

## 2. Ministry of Commerce & Industry: **WPI inflation rises to 9.92% in August 2026; Fuel & Power group inflation at 22.93%**

**What it is.** The Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT released the **provisional Wholesale Price Index (WPI) estimates for August 2026 (Base Year 2022-23)**. All-India WPI inflation stands at **9.92% year-on-year**, up from **9.78%** in July 2026.

**Why it matters.** WPI in double-digit territory signals sustained producer-level price pressure, driven overwhelmingly by **mineral oils and fuel**. The divergence between the Fuel & Power group (22.93%) and Manufactured Products (8.37%) is the kind of composition question UPSC likes. Note also the **base year revision to 2022-23** and the parallel publication of the **Output PPI (OPPI)** and a **trial Input PPI (IPPI)** — India's gradual shift from a WPI-centric to a PPI-centric producer-price framework.

**Key facts and figures**

| Indicator (August 2026) | Index | YoY inflation | (July 2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **All Commodities** | 110.8 | **9.92%** | 9.78% |
| Primary Articles (wt. 22.76%) | 118.1 | 7.76% | 8.52% |
| Fuel & Power (wt. 14.11%) | 108.3 | **22.93%** | 20.05% |
| Manufactured Products (wt. 63.13%) | 108.8 | 8.37% | 8.29% |
| WPI Food Index (wt. 24.99%) | — | 7.05% | 6.65% |

- **Major drivers:** Mineral Oils (petroleum products), Food Articles, Manufacture of Food Products, Manufacture of Basic Metals, Non-Food Articles, and Manufacture of Chemicals & Chemical Products.
- **Final estimate for June 2026 revised upward:** index 110.2 → 110.3; inflation 9.87% → **9.97%**.
- **Response rates:** June final compiled at 99.3% weighted response; August provisional at 84.4%.
- **Release calendar:** provisional WPI, OPPI and trial IPPI are released on the **14th of the following month** (next working day if a holiday). September 2026 data due **14 October 2026**.

**Prelims hooks.** WPI base year = **2022-23**. WPI does **not** include services; CPI does. The WPI **Food Index** combines 'Food Articles' (from Primary Articles) and 'Manufacture of Food Products' (from Manufactured Products) — weight 24.99%. Manufactured Products carry the largest weight (63.13%).

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Inflation & Price Indices).

## 3. Ministry of Electronics & IT: **SEMICON India 2026 — fifth edition at Yashobhoomi, New Delhi, 17–19 September; theme "Silicon to Systems: Building the Ecosystem"**

**What it is.** The Prime Minister will inaugurate the fifth edition of **SEMICON India** on 17 September 2026 at **Yashobhoomi (India International Convention & Expo Centre), Dwarka, New Delhi**. It is organised jointly by the **India Semiconductor Mission (ISM)** under MeitY and **SEMI**, the global semiconductor industry association.

**Why it matters.** The release doubles as a status report on the **Semicon India Programme** — useful for a prelims-style factual question and for a GS3 answer on semiconductor self-reliance and supply-chain resilience. The theme captures the stated policy shift from chip *fabrication* alone to the full **"Sand to Silicon to Systems"** value chain.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Scale:** 600+ exhibitors including **300 international companies**; delegations from **40+ countries**; 15,000 sq m of exhibition space; 150+ speakers; **6 country pavilions** and **12 State pavilions**.
- **Semicon 1.0:** **12 semiconductor projects approved**; **three have commenced commercial production**.
- **Semicon 2.0:** approved by the Union Cabinet with **six pillars** (outlay **₹1,27,500 crore** under ISM, approved 15 July 2026).
- **Design ecosystem:** EDA (electronic design automation) tools provided to **400+ academic institutions and 105 start-ups**; **24 start-ups approved under the Design Linked Incentive (DLI) Scheme**.
- **Market size:** global semiconductor market ~**USD 796 billion (2025)**, projected to cross **USD 2 trillion by 2030**. India's semiconductor demand ~**USD 50 billion now**, projected ~**USD 103 billion by 2030**.
- Features a Startup Pavilion (~40 start-ups), Student Hackathon, Workforce Development Pavilion and a Women's Forum.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Semiconductors); GS3 — Economy (Industrial policy, PLI-type incentives).

## 4. PIB Backgrounder: **Data Centres in India — installed capacity up from 375 MW (2020) to 1.57 GW (August 2026), projected ~8 GW by 2030**

**What it is.** A PIB Research Unit backgrounder mapping India's data-centre ecosystem: what a data centre is, current capacity, the enabling policy framework, and the energy-and-water sustainability question.

**Why it matters.** Data centres sit at the intersection of three GS3 themes — digital infrastructure, energy demand, and resource sustainability. The backgrounder is unusually rich in **scheme names and numerical facts**, which is exactly the profile of a PIB item that converts into prelims questions.

**Key facts and figures**

- **Capacity:** ~**375 MW (2020)** → **1.57 GW (August 2026)** → projected ~**8 GW by 2030**.
- **Investment:** ~**USD 70 billion** already underway; a further **USD 90 billion** in announced projects.
- **Government data centres:** **NIC** operates National Data Centres at **Delhi (HQ), Pune, Hyderabad and Bhubaneswar**, plus **37 smaller data centres** at State capitals.
- **Infrastructure status:** data centres were added to the **Harmonized List of Infrastructure** in **Union Budget 2022-23**, easing access to long-term credit.
- **Union Budget 2026-27:** a **tax holiday for eligible notified foreign cloud service providers until 2047** (Tax Years 2026-27 to 2046-47) for global operations using India-based data-centre infrastructure.
- **Power demand:** the **Central Electricity Authority (CEA)** projects data-centre demand could reach **17 GW by 2031-32**.
- **Standards:** **BIS** technical committee **LITD 31** (Cloud Computing, IT and Data Centres) has published standards on **PUE** (Power Usage Effectiveness), **CUE** (Carbon Usage Effectiveness), **CER** (Cooling Efficiency Ratio) and **WUE** (Water Usage Effectiveness). **BEE** has notified **ECBC 2017** and **ECSBC 2024**.
- **Linked schemes named:** Semicon 2.0 (₹1,27,500 crore), **IndiaAI Mission (₹10,371.92 crore)**, **Electronics Components Manufacturing Scheme (ECMS)** — allocation raised from **₹22,000 crore to ₹40,000 crore**, and **PLI 2.0 for IT Hardware**.
- **Clean power:** Green Energy Open Access Rules, Green Energy Corridor Scheme, National Green Hydrogen Mission, National Programme on High Efficiency Solar PV Modules, Solar Park Scheme; and the **SHANTI Act** (Sustainable Harnessing and Advancement of Nuclear Energy for Transforming India), which supports Small Modular Reactors and Micro Nuclear Reactors for AI/data-centre power.
- **Cooling technologies flagged:** direct-to-chip liquid cooling, adiabatic cooling, immersion cooling, closed-loop liquid cooling. Groundwater extraction is regulated under **Ministry of Jal Shakti** guidelines.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Digital infrastructure); GS3 — Environment (energy and water efficiency); GS2 — Governance (data sovereignty, technology governance).

## 5. Ministry of Finance (DRI): **'Operation Deep Manifest' — 362+ MT of Pakistan-origin dry dates seized after third-country transshipment through UAE**

**What it is.** The **Directorate of Revenue Intelligence (DRI)** seized **more than 362 metric tonnes** of Pakistan-origin dry dates, intercepting **13 containers** imported by a Mumbai-based firm at **CFS Ambad, Nashik**. Import documents declared the **UAE** as the country of origin.

**Why it matters.** This is a textbook case of **origin misdeclaration through third-country transshipment** — a recurring theme in trade-security and customs-enforcement questions. It also anchors the post-Pahalgam trade prohibition, a factual point worth fixing precisely.

**Key facts and figures**

- The consignments arrived from **Jebel Ali, UAE**. Preliminary investigation found the goods were shipped from **Karachi Port** to Jebel Ali in one set of containers on one vessel, then merely **transshipped** into a different set of containers on a different vessel for onward shipment to India, through entities operated by Pakistani nationals.
- **Legal basis of the ban:** following the **Pahalgam terror attack**, India imposed a **complete prohibition on the direct or indirect import or transit of all goods originating in or exported from Pakistan** with effect from **2 May 2025**, via **DGFT Notification No. 06/2025-26 dated 2 May 2025**.
- 'Operation Deep Manifest' is DRI's continuing operation against Pakistan-origin goods routed through third countries.

**Prelims hooks.** DRI is under the **Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC)**, Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance. CFS = Container Freight Station. Import prohibitions of this kind are notified by **DGFT** under the Foreign Trade (Development and Regulation) Act, 1992.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security (economic offences, trade-route security); GS3 — Economy (External sector, trade policy instruments).

## 6. Ministry of MSME: **Coir as a rural livelihood engine — India produces over 80% of the world's coir fibre; women are ~80% of the workforce in fibre extraction and spinning**

**What it is.** An MSME feature on how the **Coir Board** and the **Prime Minister's Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP)** are converting coconut husk into value-added enterprise — mats, ropes, handicrafts, bio-fertilisers and eco-friendly packaging.

**Why it matters.** Coir is a compact example of the **traditional-industry-to-modern-enterprise** transition that recurs in GS3 (agro-processing, rural industry) and GS1 (women and work). The gendered workforce statistic and the export dominance figure are both high-yield.

**Key facts and figures**

- **India is the world's largest producer of coir**, accounting for **more than 80% of global coir fibre production**.
- **Women account for nearly 80%** of those engaged in **fibre extraction and spinning**.
- Organised coir production in India began in **1859** with the first coir factory at **Alappuzha, Kerala**.
- The **Coir Board** is a **statutory body under the Ministry of MSME**.
- **Central Coir Research Institute (CCRI), Kalavoor (Kerala)** has developed **coir rail wheel axle packaging cups** as an alternative to HDPE plastic cups.
- Case studies cited: a PMEGP-supported unit in **Kadiyam, East Godavari (Andhra Pradesh)** — ₹10 lakh project cost, ₹6.50 lakh bank loan, ~₹35 lakh annual turnover, ~120 women trained; and a coir-pith bio-fertiliser unit in **Kozhikode, Kerala** (est. November 2024) — 20+ tonnes sold, ₹13 lakh turnover.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Coir Vikas Yojana (CVY)**

- **Ministry:** Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises, implemented through the **Coir Board** (statutory body).
- **Objective:** to enable people associated with the coir sector to acquire new skills, adopt better technology, produce quality products and access larger markets.
- **Key features:**
  1. Technology upgradation and **research** support (through CCRI, Kalavoor and the Central Institute of Coir Technology).
  2. **Skill development and dedicated women's training** programmes at Coir Board training centres.
  3. **Domestic and international market promotion** — exhibitions, fairs, showrooms, export promotion.
  4. **Industry development and worker welfare** measures.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** coconut farmers, coir workers (predominantly women), artisans, cooperatives, entrepreneurs, manufacturers and exporters across India's coconut-growing regions.
- **Significance:** converts an agro-waste stream (coconut husk and coir pith) into a value-added, export-earning, largely woman-employing rural industry — while substituting plastic in packaging applications.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (MSME, rural industry, agro-processing); GS1 — Society (women and work).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to data centres in India, consider the following statements:

1. Data centres were included in the Harmonized List of Infrastructure in the Union Budget 2022-23.
2. The Central Electricity Authority has projected that data-centre power demand could reach about 17 GW by 2031-32.
3. The Bureau of Indian Standards has published standards on Power Usage Effectiveness and Water Usage Effectiveness for data centres.

How many of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) All three
- (d) None

---

**Q2.** Consider the following pairs of cross-border electricity transmission links between India and Nepal:

1. Muzaffarpur – Dhalkebar : 400 kV
2. Tanakpur – Mahendranagar : 132 kV

Which of the pairs given above is/are correctly matched?

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 2 only
- (c) Both 1 and 2
- (d) Neither 1 nor 2

---

**Q3.** With reference to the Wholesale Price Index (WPI) in India, consider the following statements:

1. The current WPI series uses 2022-23 as the base year.
2. Among the three major groups of the WPI, 'Manufactured Products' carries the largest weight.
3. The WPI Food Index is drawn only from the 'Primary Articles' major group.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 2 and 3 only
- (c) 1 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (c) All three.** All three are stated in the PIB Backgrounder of 14 September 2026. Infrastructure status was granted in Budget 2022-23, easing access to long-term credit; CEA projects ~17 GW of data-centre demand by 2031-32; BIS committee LITD 31 has published PUE, CUE, CER and WUE standards.

**Q2 — (c) Both 1 and 2.** The Ministry of Power's approval covers up to 600 MW through the **Muzaffarpur–Dhalkebar 400 kV double-circuit line** and up to 54 MW through the **Tanakpur–Mahendranagar 132 kV single-circuit line** — both correctly matched.

**Q3 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statement 3 is incorrect: the WPI Food Index (weight 24.99%) combines **'Food Articles' from Primary Articles** *and* **'Manufacture of Food Products' from Manufactured Products** — it draws on two major groups, not one. Statements 1 and 2 are correct: base year is 2022-23, and Manufactured Products carries the largest weight at 63.13%.
