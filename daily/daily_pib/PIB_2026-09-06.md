## 1. PIB Backgrounder (Ministry of Home Affairs): **Vibrant Villages Programme — VVP-I and VVP-II, combined outlay ₹11,639 crore**

**What it is.** PIB's backgrounder consolidates the status of the Vibrant Villages Programme, the Centre's flagship border-village development scheme. It reframes border settlements not as the country's "last villages" but as its **"first villages"** — treating residents as the "eyes and ears" of the border guarding forces. India shares over **15,000 km** of international land border with seven countries.

**Why it matters.** This is the clearest single-document treatment of a scheme that sits at the intersection of GS3 internal security and GS2 governance/scheme-delivery, and it supersedes the older Border Area Development Programme (BADP), now in its sunset phase.

**Key facts and figures**

| | VVP-I | VVP-II |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | Centrally **Sponsored** Scheme (with States) | Central **Sector** Scheme (100% Centre) |
| Announced / approved | Union Budget 2022-23 | Cabinet approval **2 April 2025** |
| Launched | **10 April 2023, Kibithoo**, Anjaw district, Arunachal Pradesh | **20 February 2026, Nathanpur**, Cachar district, Assam |
| Coverage | Northern border — **662** strategic villages, 46 blocks, 19 districts, 4 States + 1 UT (Arunachal Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh, Sikkim, Uttarakhand, Ladakh); population ~1.42 lakh | All other land borders — **1,954** villages, 334 blocks, **15 States + 2 UTs** |
| Outlay | **₹4,800 crore** (2022-23 to 2026-27), of which **₹2,500 crore** for road connectivity | **₹6,839 crore** (up to 2028-29) |

- Combined target: **more than 2,616 villages**; combined outlay **₹11,639 crore**.
- Longest land border segment: **Bangladesh 4,096.7 km**, then **China 3,488 km**, **Pakistan 3,323 km**.
- Implementation is via **Village Action Plans** and scheme **convergence/saturation**; all-weather roads come through **PMGSY-IV** (Ministry of Rural Development). A **High-Powered Committee chaired by the Cabinet Secretary** may relax scheme guidelines for border-specific conditions. **MHA is the nodal ministry.**
- Progress as of **July 2026** under VVP-I: **1,248 projects sanctioned by MHA worth ₹3,020.32 crore**; 1,655 more sanctioned by other ministries under convergence; **₹953.47 crore released**; 283 projects completed. Arunachal Pradesh leads with 832 sanctioned projects.
- **111 road projects (₹2,481.93 crore)** will connect **134 previously road-less villages**, including **35 long-span bridges**. **327 tourism projects (₹145 crore)** approved. Over **8,800 outreach activities** held up to 31 July 2026.
- BADP context: launched **1986-87**, covered villages within 10 km of the first habitation from the border across 111 districts in 16 States and 2 UTs; over 39,000 works sanctioned since 2004-05.

**Notebook:** GS3 Internal Security (border management) + GS2 Governance (Centrally Sponsored vs Central Sector schemes, convergence model).

## 2. Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change / NBA: **16th National Meet of State Biodiversity Boards and UT Biodiversity Councils, Chandigarh**

**What it is.** The National Biodiversity Authority convened the two-day (**6–7 September 2026**) National Meet of **State Biodiversity Boards (SBBs)** and **UT Biodiversity Councils (UTBCs)** at Chandigarh, presided over by Punjab Governor and Chandigarh Administrator Shri Gulab Chand Kataria and Union Environment Minister Shri Bhupender Yadav. The agenda: aligning State rules and institutions with the amended national biodiversity governance framework.

**Why it matters.** This is the three-tier architecture of the **Biological Diversity Act, 2002** in action — NBA at the Centre, SBBs at State level, and **Biodiversity Management Committees (BMCs)** at the local level — and it links directly to India's Access and Benefit Sharing (ABS) obligations and to the Kunming-Montreal targets.

**Key facts**

- Governance framework updated by the **Biological Diversity (Amendment) Act, 2023** and the **Biological Diversity Rules, 2024**; States/UTs asked to align their rules accordingly.
- **Over 2.76 lakh Biodiversity Management Committees** to be made functional; **People's Biodiversity Registers (PBRs)** to become "dynamic planning instruments" for local bodies rather than static documents.
- **NBA has shared over ₹200 crore of ABS funds** with farmers, local communities, BMCs, SBBs, UTBCs and research institutions.
- India has submitted its **7th National Report to the Convention on Biological Diversity** and its **1st National Report on the Nagoya Protocol** on Access and Benefit Sharing.
- India's **updated National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan (NBSAP) 2024–2030** is aligned with the **Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (KMGBF)**; States urged to contribute to the global **"30-by-30"** conservation target.
- Other initiatives referenced: Mission LiFE, Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam, Amrit Sarovar, Green Credit Programme.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment (biodiversity governance, ABS, KMGBF) + GS2 Governance (statutory bodies, decentralisation to BMCs).

## 3. MoEFCC: **International Vulture Awareness Day 2026 at Panchkula — national workshop 'From Crisis to Recovery – Conserving India's Vultures'**

**What it is.** Union Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav inaugurated a national workshop on vulture conservation at Panchkula, Haryana, and visited the **Jatayu Conservation Breeding Centre (JCBC), Pinjore**. Organised with the **Central Zoo Authority**, Haryana Forest & Wildlife Department, the **Bombay Natural History Society (BNHS)** and JCBC.

**Why it matters.** Vultures are the classic Indian case study of a keystone-scavenger collapse driven by an anthropogenic chemical cause — the veterinary NSAID painkiller in cattle carcasses — and of a species-recovery programme built on conservation breeding plus **Vulture Safe Zones**. India recorded a sharp vulture decline from the **1990s**.

**Key facts**

- **Nine vulture species** (resident and migratory) named in the release: White-rumped, Long-billed, Slender-billed, Red-headed, Egyptian, Himalayan Griffon, Eurasian Griffon, Cinereous and Bearded.
- JCBC Pinjore breeds the three critically endangered *Gyps* species — **White-rumped, Long-billed and Slender-billed vultures**.
- **JCBC performance in 2025–26:** housed **356 vultures**, bred **35 chicks**, released/contributed **87 vultures** for reintroduction, transferred **90 birds** to other conservation-breeding institutions, and trained **1,222 participants from 22 institutions**.
- Minister's ask: continued creation of **Vulture Safe Zones** through vulture-safe veterinary practices, safe food availability and technical capacity building; a "Whole of Government / Whole of Society" approach.
- Coffee-table book released: **'Story of Jatayu: Saving India's Vulture'**.
- Species-conservation framing extended to Big Cats, Sloth Bear, Great Indian Bustard, Dolphins, Gharial and Wild Water Buffalo.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment (species conservation, ecosystem services of scavengers, conservation breeding).

## 4. Ministry of Defence: **Raksha Mantri to visit Sri Lanka, 8–10 September 2026**

**What it is.** Defence Minister Rajnath Singh will make a three-day official visit to Sri Lanka from **8 to 10 September 2026**, leading a delegation of senior Ministry of Defence and Ministry of External Affairs officials, with consultations with the Sri Lankan leadership and an interaction with the Indian diaspora in Colombo.

**Why it matters.** The visit is framed explicitly around two doctrines UPSC tests directly: **'Neighbourhood First'** and **MAHASAGAR — Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions**, the successor framing to SAGAR for India's Indian Ocean outreach.

**Key facts**

- Focus areas: regional security, defence and maritime partnership, economic and trade cooperation, people-to-people ties.
- Recent context cited by the Ministry: India's assistance to Sri Lanka during its economic crisis, and India as **one of the first responders after Cyclone Ditwah in Sri Lanka in 2025**.

**Notebook:** GS2 International Relations (India–Sri Lanka, Neighbourhood First, MAHASAGAR, Indian Ocean maritime security).

## 5. Ministry of Communications (Department of Posts): **Dr. Vinaya Prakash Singh re-elected Secretary-General of the Asian-Pacific Postal Union**

**What it is.** **Dr. Vinaya Prakash Singh**, a **1988-batch Indian Postal Service** officer, was re-elected **unopposed by acclamation** as Secretary-General of the **Asian-Pacific Postal Union (APPU)** at the **14th APPU Congress in Bangkok on 4 September 2026** — a second consecutive four-year term running **January 2027 to December 2030**.

**Why it matters.** A clean prelims-style organisation question: APPU is the **restricted/regional union of the Universal Postal Union (UPU)** for the Asia-Pacific, and the UPU is a **United Nations specialised agency** for the postal sector. It also marks continued Indian leadership in a multilateral technical body.

**Key facts**

- **APPU:** intergovernmental organisation, **headquartered in Bangkok**, **32 member countries**, functions as a **Regional Union of the UPU**.
- Dr. Singh first took office in **January 2023** after election at the **13th APPU Congress in 2022**.
- Earlier posts: Member, Postal Services Board; Senior Deputy Director General and Chief Vigilance Officer, Department of Posts; Chief Postmaster General, Uttar Pradesh Circle; Chief General Manager, India Post HQ. Also served with UPSC, UNDP in Afghanistan and the Army Postal Service Corps.

**Notebook:** GS2 International Relations (international organisations, Indians in multilateral bodies) — also a standard "Persons in News" entry.

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the Vibrant Villages Programme (VVP), consider the following statements:

1. VVP-I is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme covering villages along the northern border, while VVP-II is a Central Sector Scheme extending to the other international land borders.
2. VVP-II was approved by the Union Cabinet in April 2025 and was formally launched from Nathanpur village in Assam.
3. The Ministry of Defence is the nodal ministry for the implementation of both phases of the programme.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Consider the following statements regarding India's biodiversity governance framework:

1. Biodiversity Management Committees are constituted at the local body level under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002.
2. India's updated National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan 2024–2030 is aligned with the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework.
3. The Nagoya Protocol deals with access to genetic resources and the fair and equitable sharing of benefits arising from their utilisation.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** The Asian-Pacific Postal Union (APPU), recently seen in the news, is best described as:

(a) A specialised agency of the United Nations dealing exclusively with cross-border e-commerce logistics
(b) A regional union of the Universal Postal Union, headquartered in Bangkok
(c) An organ of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) dealing with postal tariffs
(d) A private sector consortium of courier companies operating in the Asia-Pacific region

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: VVP-I is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme for the northern border (launched at Kibithoo, Arunachal Pradesh, on 10 April 2023), while VVP-II is a 100% centrally funded Central Sector Scheme approved on 2 April 2025 and launched on 20 February 2026 at Nathanpur, Cachar district, Assam. Statement 3 is wrong — the **Ministry of Home Affairs**, not Defence, is the nodal ministry.

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct. BMCs are the local-level tier of the three-tier structure (NBA → State Biodiversity Boards → BMCs) under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002; the updated NBSAP 2024–2030 is aligned with the KMGBF; and the Nagoya Protocol, adopted under the Convention on Biological Diversity, governs access and benefit sharing (ABS) for genetic resources.

**Q3 — (b).** APPU is an intergovernmental organisation of 32 member countries, headquartered in Bangkok, and functions as a Regional (restricted) Union of the Universal Postal Union — the UPU itself being the UN specialised agency for the postal sector. Dr. Vinaya Prakash Singh of the Indian Postal Service was re-elected its Secretary-General at the 14th APPU Congress on 4 September 2026.
