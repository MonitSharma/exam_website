## 1. 12th National Handloom Day — President confers Sant Kabir and National Handloom Awards 2025

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Textiles (Office of the Development Commissioner for Handlooms); Rashtrapati Bhavan

The Ministry of Textiles observed the **12th National Handloom Day on 7 August 2026** at the Rashtrapati Bhavan Cultural Centre (RBCC), New Delhi, with **President Droupadi Murmu** as Chief Guest. The day commemorates the launch of the **Swadeshi Movement on 7 August 1905**, and was first observed in 2015. It matters because handloom is India's second-largest rural employment sector after agriculture and a live case study in artisan livelihoods, GI protection and Aatmanirbharta in textiles.

**Key facts**

- **22 awards** conferred for the year 2025: **3 Sant Kabir Handloom Awards** + **19 National Handloom Awards**.
- Sant Kabir Handloom Award — the **highest honour in the handloom sector** — went to **Ram Meher, Runima Chetiya Choudhury and Shah Muhammad Ansari**, all for saree weaving.
- Awardees span **13 States/UTs**: Odisha, Assam, Uttar Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Madhya Pradesh, West Bengal, Manipur, Rajasthan, Gujarat and Sikkim.
- Categories cover weavers, designers, cooperatives and entrepreneurs.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Art & Culture (crafts, textiles); GS3 — Economy (rural livelihoods, MSME/handicraft clusters)

> *Repeat check:* the 2 Aug (Handloom Hackathon 2.0) and 5 Aug (GI & Beyond 2.0 Summit / Digital Handloom Atlas) briefings both **previewed** National Handloom Day. **What's new here:** the day itself was observed on 7 August and the Handloom Awards 2025 were actually conferred — names, categories and numbers below are new information, not a restatement.

## 2. Jal Shakti releases ₹6,150 crore to States under Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 in FY 2026-27

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Jal Shakti — Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation

On **6 August 2026**, the Ministry announced that the Centre has released **over ₹6,150 crore** to States/UTs under JJM in FY 2026-27 so far, and Union Minister **C. R. Patil** chaired a state-wise progress review (followed by a review with Haryana MPs on 7 August). This is the first substantial tranche after the Cabinet's approval of **JJM 2.0 in March 2026**, and is the key test of whether the mission shifts from *connection counting* to **service delivery** (reliable, adequate, safe water).

**Key facts**

- **₹5,289.09 crore** released as **reimbursement to 10 States**; **₹865.87 crore** routed through the **SNA SPARSH** platform to **13 States**.
- Largest reimbursement: **Uttar Pradesh ₹2,711.52 crore**; then **Karnataka ₹795.74 crore**, **Maharashtra ₹594.44 crore**, **Rajasthan ₹537.72 crore**.
- Top SNA SPARSH recipient: **Assam, ₹669.92 crore**.
- **Over 82%** of rural households now reported to have tap water connections.

### Scheme-in-focus: Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0

- **Name / Ministry:** Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0 — Ministry of Jal Shakti, Department of Drinking Water & Sanitation. Approved by the Union Cabinet on **10 March 2026**.
- **Objective:** Move beyond household tap connections to **assured, adequate and safe drinking water service delivery** in every rural household, sustained through 2028.
- **Key features:**
  1. Mission extended till **December 2028**.
  2. Total outlay **₹8.69 lakh crore**, of which **Central assistance is ₹3.59 lakh crore** (rest is State share + convergence).
  3. Emphasis on **source sustainability, water quality testing and O&M** of existing schemes rather than fresh connections alone.
  4. Funds routed through **SNA SPARSH** (Single Nodal Agency — a Public Financial Management System module) for real-time, just-in-time transfer and expenditure tracking.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** All rural households across States and UTs; **>82%** already covered with tap connections.
- **Significance:** Directly touches SDG 6, women's time poverty, and waterborne disease burden; the SNA SPARSH routing is an example of **Direct Benefit Transfer-style fiscal plumbing applied to a Centrally Sponsored Scheme**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (welfare schemes, Centre-State fiscal transfers); GS3 — Economy (infrastructure), Environment (water security)

## 3. NITI Aayog releases "Reimagining Care: Strategies for Empowering Caregivers in Viksit Bharat@2047"

**Ministry/Body:** NITI Aayog

NITI Aayog has released a report proposing an institutional architecture for India's **caregiving economy** — care for the elderly, persons with disabilities, and those needing long-term care. It was released by **Vice Chairperson Ashok Kumar Lahiri**, in the presence of **Member Dr. R. Balasubramaniam** and **CEO Nidhi Chhibber**. This is directly relevant to the demographic-transition and "care economy" debate: an ageing population, nuclearising families and rising disability prevalence against a largely **informal, unpaid and female** care workforce.

**Key facts / recommendations**

- Recommends a **National Policy on Caregiving (NPC)**.
- Recommends a **National Caregiver Council (NCC)** to handle training, accreditation, certification, quality standards and professional development.
- Proposes a **National Caregiver Registry** and a digital caregiving portal.
- Positions caregiving as an **employment and export opportunity** — India as a supplier to the global caregiving economy.
- Frames caregiving as a pillar of inclusive growth under **Viksit Bharat@2047**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (welfare of vulnerable sections, policy design); GS1 — Society (ageing, family structure, gendered labour)

## 4. Ministry of Tourism and Netflix launch "As Seen on Netflix" on the Incredible India portal

**Ministry/Body:** Ministry of Tourism (Union Minister for Culture & Tourism, **Gajendra Singh Shekhawat**)

The Ministry launched a dedicated **"As Seen on Netflix"** section on the **Incredible India** website, mapping destinations and cultural experiences featured in Indian productions on the platform. Announced around Netflix's completion of **a decade in India**, it operationalises an earlier Ministry–Netflix **MoU (2025)** and is a textbook example of **soft power / screen tourism** being used as a destination-marketing instrument.

**Key facts**

- Launch event at the **National Gallery of Modern Art (NGMA), New Delhi**, attended by Netflix Co-CEO **Ted Sarandos**.
- The section lets viewers discover **filming locations and cultural experiences** from Indian titles.
- Two new videos on **India's wildlife and cultural heritage** premiered at the event.
- Part of a broader Ministry push to use streaming and digital platforms (including YouTube) for tourism promotion.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Art & Culture (heritage promotion); GS2 — IR (soft power); GS3 — Economy (tourism as employment/forex earner)

## 5. ACC clears one-year extensions for the Cabinet Secretary and Home Secretary; senior officers posted to IMF, ADB and ICAO

**Ministry/Body:** Appointments Committee of the Cabinet (ACC) / Department of Personnel and Training (DoPT), Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances & Pensions

The **ACC, chaired by the Prime Minister**, approved a **one-year extension** for Cabinet Secretary **Dr. T. V. Somanathan** (IAS), whose tenure was to end on **30 August 2026**, and for Union Home Secretary **Govind Mohan** (1989-batch IAS, Sikkim cadre) beyond **22 August 2026**. A parallel set of ACC approvals placed senior officers in multilateral institutions. Useful for the Prelims-facts layer on **civil services tenure rules and ACC's role**.

**Key facts**

- Extensions granted by relaxing **Fundamental Rule 56(d)** and **Rule 16(1A) of the All India Services (Death-cum-Retirement Benefits) Rules, 1958**.
- Govind Mohan continues as Home Secretary till **22 August 2027**.
- Foreign postings (3-year terms each): **Rahul Jain** as Senior Adviser to the Executive Director (India), **IMF**; **Baldeo Purushartha** as Senior Adviser to the Executive Director, **ADB (Manila)**; **Satyajit Mohanty** as India's Representative on the **Council of ICAO**.

## 6. Annapurni Subramaniam wins the COSPAR Vikram Sarabhai Medal 2026 — first Indian woman to do so

**Ministry/Body:** Indian Institute of Astrophysics (IIA), Bengaluru — an autonomous institute of the **Department of Science & Technology**; medal awarded jointly by **COSPAR and ISRO**

**Prof. Annapurni Subramaniam**, Director of IIA Bengaluru, received the **COSPAR Vikram Sarabhai Medal 2026** "in recognition of her outstanding contributions to space research in developing countries", presented at the **46th COSPAR Scientific Assembly in Florence, Italy** (3 August 2026). She is the **first Indian woman** and only the **third woman globally** to win it.

**Key facts**

- She is the **4th Indian** recipient. Earlier Indians: **U. R. Rao (1996)**, **Gurbax Singh Lakhina (2014)**, **Anil Bhardwaj (2024)**.
- The medal was **instituted in 1990** in honour of **Vikram Sarabhai**, and is awarded **biennially**, **jointly by COSPAR and ISRO**.
- **COSPAR** (Committee on Space Research) is affiliated to the **International Science Council (ISC)** and works with the UN through **COPUOS**.
- Her work covers **stellar populations, blue straggler stars, the UVIT payload on AstroSat**, and next-generation telescope projects. She also heads the **Centre for Nano and Soft Matter Sciences (CeNS)**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (space, institutions, awards); GS1 — Society (women in science)

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to **Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0**, consider the following statements:

1. It was approved by the Union Cabinet in March 2026 and extends the Mission up to December 2028.
2. It is implemented by the Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation under the Ministry of Rural Development.
3. Central assistance under the Mission is routed to States through the SNA SPARSH platform.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 and 2 only
- (b) 1 and 3 only
- (c) 2 and 3 only
- (d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Consider the following statements about the **Sant Kabir Handloom Award**:

1. It is conferred by the Ministry of Textiles and is the highest recognition in India's handloom sector.
2. National Handloom Day, on which the awards are presented, commemorates the launch of the Swadeshi Movement of 1905.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

- (a) 1 only
- (b) 2 only
- (c) Both 1 and 2
- (d) Neither 1 nor 2

---

**Q3.** The **Vikram Sarabhai Medal**, in the news recently, is awarded

- (a) annually by the Indian National Science Academy for contributions to atmospheric sciences
- (b) biennially, jointly by COSPAR and ISRO, for contributions to space research in developing countries
- (c) every five years by the Department of Space to Indian scientists under the age of 40
- (d) annually by UNESCO for the peaceful use of outer space

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** JJM 2.0 was approved on 10 March 2026, extended to December 2028, with an outlay of ₹8.69 lakh crore (Central share ₹3.59 lakh crore); funds flow via SNA SPARSH. Statement 2 is wrong — the Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation is under the **Ministry of Jal Shakti**, not Rural Development.

**Q2 — (c) Both 1 and 2.** The Sant Kabir Handloom Award is the highest honour in the handloom sector, conferred by the Ministry of Textiles; National Handloom Day (7 August) marks the Swadeshi Movement launched on 7 August 1905.

**Q3 — (b).** Instituted in 1990, the COSPAR Vikram Sarabhai Medal is given **every two years jointly by COSPAR and ISRO** for outstanding contributions to space research in developing countries; Prof. Annapurni Subramaniam is the 2026 recipient and the first Indian woman to receive it.
