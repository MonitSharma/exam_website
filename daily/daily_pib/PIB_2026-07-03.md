# Daily PIB Briefing — 2026-07-03

## 1. MANAS — National Narcotics Helpline crosses 2.65 lakh drug-related inputs, 16,200+ actionable leads
**Issuing body:** Narcotics Control Bureau (NCB), Ministry of Home Affairs | **Date:** update reported 3 July 2026 (helpline launched 18 July 2024) | **Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (citizen-centric digital service delivery) · GS3 — Internal Security (narco-terrorism, drug trafficking)

MANAS (Madak Padarth Nishedh Asoochna Kendra) is a secure, round-the-clock platform — helpline 1933, web portal, UMANG app, email — that lets citizens anonymously report drug trafficking/peddling/cultivation or seek de-addiction counselling, in support of the Nasha Mukt Bharat vision. It matters because it is a live example of digital-public-infrastructure being used for internal-security intelligence gathering rather than just service delivery.
**Key facts:** launched 18 July 2024 under NCB (constituted 1986 under the NDPS Act); tech partner Digital India Corporation; as of June 2026 — ~10 lakh connection attempts, 2,65,673 drug-related inputs logged, 16,200+ actionable leads escalated, 47,500+ counselling calls connected; tips flow to 30 NCB Zonal Units and 36 State/UT Anti-Narcotics Task Forces (ANTFs); counselling calls transfer to MoSJE Helpline 14446; sits alongside the 4-tier NCORD mechanism (Apex–Executive–State–District, formed 2016, restructured 2019).

## 2. MoRTH proposes AIS-125 amendments — Neonatal & Multi-stretcher ambulance categories, mandatory rescue kits
**Issuing body:** Ministry of Road Transport & Highways (MoRTH) | **Date:** draft notification GSR 382(E), 14 May 2026 (in public-comment window, current as of early July 2026) | **Notebook:** GS3 — Infrastructure/Disaster & Emergency Management · GS2 — Health infrastructure & welfare schemes

Draft amendments to the Automotive Industry Standard (AIS)-125, which governs road-ambulance construction and medical equipment, add two new ambulance categories and mandate rescue equipment — directly linked to India's "Golden Hour" road-safety push. Relevant because it pairs a technical standard with the financial-protection scheme PM-RAHAT, illustrating how India's road-safety architecture is being built in two complementary layers.
**Key facts:** AIS-125 (Part 1) first notified via GSR 868(E), 8 September 2016; 2026 draft adds Neonatal Road Ambulance and Multi-stretcher Road Ambulance categories, mandatory extraction/rescue tools for Class B/C/D ambulances, and dedicated power provisions for e-ambulances; India recorded 4,87,707 road accidents and 1,77,175 fatalities in 2024 (MoRTH data); ~50% of road deaths are considered preventable via Golden Hour treatment; PM-RAHAT offers cashless treatment up to ₹1.5 lakh for 7 days via the Motor Vehicle Accident Fund, integrated with ERSS-112, eDAR and TMS 2.0.

## 3. Employees' Provident Fund Scheme, 2026 notified — replaces the 1952 Scheme under the Code on Social Security
**Issuing body:** Ministry of Labour & Employment / EPFO | **Date:** notified in the Gazette, effective 29 June 2026 | **Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (labour codes, social security) · GS2 — Governance (digital compliance/service delivery)

*(See Scheme-in-focus below — first major PF-framework overhaul in seven decades, part of the shift to the four new labour codes.)*

### Scheme-in-focus: Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) Scheme, 2026
- **Ministry:** Ministry of Labour & Employment (administered via EPFO)
- **Objective:** Migrate the provident-fund framework from the EPF Scheme, 1952 to the legal architecture of the Code on Social Security, 2020, while strengthening digital compliance and administrative efficiency.
- **Key features:** (1) Mandatory contribution unchanged at 12% of wages each from employer and employee (10% continues for notified establishments); (2) updated, simplified rules for partial withdrawals for medical treatment, education, marriage, housing and other specified needs, subject to minimum-balance conditions; (3) heavy emphasis on electronic filings, online claims, e-passbooks and Universal Account Number (UAN) linkage; (4) stricter compliance rules for exempted (privately managed) PF trusts.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** All existing and future EPF-covered formal-sector employees and employers currently governed by the EPF Scheme, 1952.
- **Significance:** First full legal re-basing of India's provident-fund regime since 1952; operationalises the Code on Social Security, 2020 for PF specifically, ahead of similar rollouts already seen for the EPS and other social-security instruments; a recurring "labour code implementation" theme likely to keep generating PIB items through 2026.

## 4. NITI Aayog releases "Strategic Roadmap for Making Ayurveda Global"
**Issuing body:** NITI Aayog (Health Division), with Ministry of Ayush and Ministry of External Affairs | **Date:** 2 July 2026, New Delhi | **Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology/Health · GS2 — International Relations (soft power) · GS1 — Society/Culture

A NITI Aayog report, prepared with PwC, lays out a long-term strategy — aligned with Viksit Bharat@2047 — to make Ayurveda a globally recognised, evidence-based healthcare system. It matters for its "soft power meets economic policy" framing: traditional medicine treated as an export, workforce and diplomacy asset, not just a health topic.
- **Key facts:** released by Dr Ashok Kumar Lahiri (Vice Chairman, NITI Aayog) in presence of Ministry of Ayush Secretary Vaidya Rajesh Kotecha; three-pillar framework — Availability, Acceptability, Propagation; priority areas include global workforce expansion, exports/manufacturing, research & education, regulatory alignment, insurance coverage, medical value travel and brand visibility; framed under the "One Earth, One Health" vision.

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** With reference to the Employees' Provident Fund (EPF) Scheme, 2026, notified by the Central Government, consider the following statements:
1. It replaces the EPF Scheme, 1952, and has been notified under the Code on Social Security, 2020.
2. The mandatory EPF contribution rate has been raised from 12% to 15% of wages.
3. The scheme places strong emphasis on electronic filings, e-passbooks and Universal Account Number (UAN) linkage.

How many of the statements given above are correct?
(a) Only one
(b) Only two
(c) All three
(d) None

**Q2.** Consider the following statements about MANAS (Madak Padarth Nishedh Asoochna Kendra):
1. It is the National Narcotics Helpline operated by the Narcotics Control Bureau under the Ministry of Home Affairs.
2. It can be accessed via helpline 1933, a web portal, and the UMANG app.
3. Counselling requests received on MANAS are transferred to the Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment's Helpline 14446.

Which of the statements given above are correct?
(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

**Q3.** The "Strategic Roadmap for Making Ayurveda Global," released by NITI Aayog in July 2026, is built around which three-pillar framework?
(a) Access, Affordability, Accountability
(b) Availability, Acceptability, Propagation
(c) Production, Promotion, Protection
(d) Research, Regulation, Revenue

## Answers

**Q1. (b) Only two.** Statements 1 and 3 are correct — the EPF Scheme, 2026 replaces the 1952 Scheme under the Code on Social Security, 2020, with heavy emphasis on digital compliance (e-filings, e-passbooks, UAN linkage). Statement 2 is wrong: the mandatory contribution rate remains unchanged at 12% of wages each from employer and employee (10% for notified establishments).

**Q2. (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three statements are correct — MANAS is run by the NCB (MHA), accessible via helpline 1933/web portal/UMANG app, and counselling requests are routed to MoSJE's Helpline 14446.

**Q3. (b) Availability, Acceptability, Propagation.** NITI Aayog's report is structured around these three pillars to guide Ayurveda's global expansion in workforce, exports, research, regulation and medical value travel, aligned with Viksit Bharat@2047.
