## 1. Ministry of Commerce & Industry (DPIIT): **Make in India completes 12 years — PIB stocktake of manufacturing, FDI and PLI outcomes**

Make in India was launched on **25 September 2014** to turn India into a global hub for manufacturing, design and innovation. On its 12th anniversary, the government put out a consolidated scorecard, and the Prime Minister summed it up as "more made in India, more investment, more exports".

**Why it matters:** This is the government's own balance sheet for its flagship industrial policy. Mains questions on deindustrialisation, PLI effectiveness and "Make in India vs. import dependence" need these baseline numbers. The release also shows the policy's new shape: sector-specific incentives such as PLI, semiconductors and rare-earth magnets now sit on top of the original ease-of-doing-business agenda.

**Key facts**
- Now called **Make in India 2.0**, it covers **27 sectors (15 manufacturing + 12 services)**.
- **Cumulative FDI of about US$ 843 billion** from 2014-15 to 2025-26.
- **Electronics production** rose from **₹1.9 lakh crore (2014-15) to ₹13.11 lakh crore (2025-26)**. India is the **world's 2nd-largest mobile phone manufacturer**.
- **Defence production** rose from **₹46,429 crore (2014-15) to ₹1.78 lakh crore (2025-26)**. **Crude steel** rose from **81.7 MT to 170 MT**, and India has been the 2nd-largest producer since 2018.
- **PLI schemes** cover **14 sectors**. Reported production and sales exceed **₹22 lakh crore** and jobs exceed **14 lakh** (sources give slightly different cut-off figures).
- Enablers: the **National Single Window System** (327 Central + 3,452 State approvals), the **India Industrial Land Bank** (4,220 parks mapped), **PM GatiShakti** and **Startup India** (about 2.54 lakh DPIIT-recognised start-ups).

### Initiative-in-focus: **Make in India**
- **Ministry:** Commerce & Industry, through **DPIIT**. **Launched:** 25 September 2014.
- **Objective:** Promote investment, foster innovation, build skills and create world-class manufacturing infrastructure.
- **Key features:** (i) four pillars: new processes, new infrastructure, new sectors and a new mindset; (ii) liberalised FDI, with the automatic route in most sectors; (iii) regulatory simplification through NSWS and ease-of-doing-business reforms; (iv) since 2020, sector-specific **PLI schemes** plus semiconductor and electronics-component missions.
- **Coverage:** 27 sectors under Make in India 2.0.
- **Significance:** Measures both the *scale* of output gains (electronics, defence, steel) and the *open questions* around domestic value addition and manufacturing's share of GDP. Use it to write a balanced Mains answer.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (industrial policy, manufacturing, FDI).

## 2. Ministry of Jal Shakti: **India and the Netherlands sign two MoUs on the Kalpasar project; 9th India International Water Week concludes** *(UPDATE — IIWW was flagged as upcoming on 20 September. What is new is the Kalpasar MoUs signed there and the week's outcomes.)*

On **24 September 2026**, during IIWW, two tripartite MoUs were signed for **technical cooperation and capacity building on the Kalpasar project**. The signatories were the **Central Water Commission (CWC)**, the **Kalpasar Department of the Government of Gujarat** and two Dutch institutions, **Deltares** and **TU Delft**. Kalpasar (the Gulf of Khambhat Development Project) proposes a dam across the **Gulf of Khambhat**, from **Ghogha (Bhavnagar) to Hansot (Bharuch)**. The dam would create a large coastal **freshwater reservoir** for semi-arid **Saurashtra**.

**Why it matters:** The Dutch have a century of experience with closure dams; the **Afsluitdijk (1932)** turned the saltwater Zuiderzee into the freshwater IJsselmeer. The MoUs bring that expertise to one of India's most ambitious coastal water projects. They also make the **India–Netherlands Strategic Partnership on Water (2021)** concrete.

**Key facts**
- Reservoir inflows would come from the **Sabarmati, Mahi and Dhadhar** rivers, supplemented by **Narmada** water. The plan also includes about **2,470 MW of hybrid renewable energy** on reclaimed land and a road-rail link over the dam.
- The Gulf of Khambhat is funnel-shaped, with a **spring tidal range above 10 m**, which makes the engineering hard.
- Cooperation areas: hydraulic and hydrological modelling, coastal and structural engineering, climate-resilience assessment and capacity building.
- **IIWW 2026** was held **22–25 September** at Bharat Mandapam on the theme **"Climate Resilient Water Management"**. It drew **7,224+ delegates, 227 exhibitors and 119 papers**, and **eight capacity-building modules** for rural water delivery were launched. It also hosted an **India–Africa Roundtable on WASH** attended by ambassadors from 22 African countries.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Environment/Infrastructure (water resources, coastal reservoirs); cross-tag GS2 IR (India–Netherlands) and GS1 Geography (Gulf of Khambhat).

## 3. Ministry of Tourism: **World Tourism Day 2026 — theme "Digital Agenda and Artificial Intelligence to Redesign Tourism"; PIB releases a sector backgrounder**

The Ministry of Tourism issued a backgrounder on **26 September 2026** ahead of **World Tourism Day (27 September)**. The global host this year is **El Salvador**. India's national ceremony in New Delhi is slated to launch a **National Digital Tourism Stack** and **Dekho Apna Desh 2.0**, which promotes lesser-known destinations. Full design details will come with the launch.

**Why it matters:** Tourism is an employment-heavy services sector and a source of foreign exchange. The UN Tourism theme matches India's push for digital public infrastructure in the sector, including e-visa, the NIDHI+ registry and AI-based promotion.

**Key facts (as given in the backgrounder)**
- **Domestic tourist visits: 428.55 crore**; **international arrivals: 2.53 crore**; **foreign exchange earnings: ₹2,76,831 crore**.
- Tourism's share of GDP was **5.22% (2023-24)**, and the sector supports about **8.46 crore jobs**.
- **e-Visa** is available to nationals of **172 countries**. About **78%** of visas are now issued digitally.
- **Swadesh Darshan 2.0:** 53 projects worth ₹2,207.08 crore. **PRASHAD:** 54 pilgrimage projects worth ₹1,726.74 crore.
- **Cruise passengers** rose from **1.08 lakh to 4.62 lakh**, an increase of about 328%.
- World Tourism Day has been observed on 27 September since **1980** by **UN Tourism** (formerly UNWTO), marking the adoption of its Statutes in 1970.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (services, tourism); cross-tag GS1 Culture (heritage and pilgrimage circuits).

## 4. Ministry of Health & Family Welfare (at the UN): **India at the UN High-Level Meeting on Pandemic Prevention, Preparedness and Response — calls for an equitable PABS annex**

At the UNGA-81 **High-Level Meeting on Pandemic Prevention, Preparedness and Response** in New York on **25 September 2026**, Union Health Secretary **Punya Salila Srivastava** described pandemic preparedness as a *shared responsibility*. She called the **WHO Pandemic Agreement (adopted May 2025)** a significant milestone, but said its credibility depends on a fair **Pathogen Access and Benefit-Sharing (PABS)** system.

**Why it matters:** This is the live fault line in global health governance. The PABS annex is still being negotiated, and until it is settled the Agreement cannot open for signature. India's position is typical of the Global South: countries that share pathogen samples and genetic data should be guaranteed access to the vaccines, diagnostics and therapeutics developed from them. It also stresses national sovereignty and flexibility in implementing **One Health**.

**Key facts**
- India cited its COVID-19 record: about **300 million vaccine doses to around 100 countries and two UN entities**, and medical supplies to **150+ countries**.
- India highlighted its whole-of-government, whole-of-society approach: expanding lab networks, emergency coordination, health-workforce capacity and diagnostics.
- The **WHO Pandemic Agreement** was adopted by the **World Health Assembly in May 2025**. The **PABS annex** is being negotiated separately.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (global health governance, WHO, UNGA); cross-tag GS2 Health.

## 5. Ministry of Communications (TRAI): **Digital Connectivity Rating (DCR) platform and mobile app launched — buildings to be rated for in-building connectivity**

On **25 September 2026**, TRAI launched an integrated **Digital Connectivity Rating platform (dcrp.trai.gov.in)** and a DCRA mobile app. They put into operation the **TRAI (Rating of Properties for Digital Connectivity) Regulations, 2024**, amended in **May 2026**, and the **Rating Manual 2026**. Property managers register their buildings, **Digital Connectivity Rating Agencies (DCRAs)** carry out field assessments, and the platform issues **eSigned certificates with QR codes** that the public can look up.

**Why it matters:** Most mobile data is consumed indoors, yet building design has largely ignored connectivity. A public rating turns connectivity into a feature of the property that developers compete on, much like energy-efficiency ratings. This makes it an example of regulation by disclosure rather than by mandate.

**Key facts**
- Parameters: **fibre readiness, indoor 4G/5G coverage, in-building solutions and Wi-Fi**, including common areas, lifts and basements.
- Covers residential, commercial, government and public properties, both under construction and completed.
- Timeline: TRAI recommendations **February 2023**; regulations **October 2024**; amendment **13 May 2026**; Rating Manual **9 June 2026**; platform launch **25 September 2026**.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance (regulatory bodies, TRAI); cross-tag GS3 Infrastructure (digital connectivity).

## Prelims MCQs

**Q1.** With reference to the **Kalpasar project**, recently in the news for India–Netherlands MoUs, consider the following statements:

1. It proposes a dam across the Gulf of Khambhat to create a coastal freshwater reservoir for the Saurashtra region.
2. The Dutch partners in the MoUs are Deltares and the Delft University of Technology (TU Delft).
3. The proposed dam would close off the Gulf of Kutch between Kandla and Okha.

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** Consider the following statements regarding **World Tourism Day 2026**:

1. World Tourism Day is observed every year on 27 September.
2. El Salvador is the global host country for the 2026 observance.
3. The global theme for 2026 is "Tourism and Sustainable Transformation".

Which of the statements given above are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** In the context of the **WHO Pandemic Agreement**, the term **"PABS"**, which India emphasised at the UN High-Level Meeting on Pandemic Prevention, Preparedness and Response, refers to:

(a) Pathogen Access and Benefit-Sharing
(b) Pandemic Alert and Biosurveillance System
(c) Public-health Assistance for Biological Security
(d) Preparedness Assessment Baseline Standard

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Kalpasar would dam the **Gulf of Khambhat** (Ghogha–Hansot) to create a freshwater reservoir for Saurashtra, and the Dutch MoU partners are Deltares and TU Delft. Statement 3 is wrong because the project is in the Gulf of Khambhat, not the Gulf of Kutch.

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** World Tourism Day falls on 27 September, and El Salvador hosts in 2026. The 2026 theme is "Digital Agenda and Artificial Intelligence to Redesign Tourism". "Tourism and Sustainable Transformation" was the **2025** theme.

**Q3 — (a) Pathogen Access and Benefit-Sharing.** The PABS annex to the WHO Pandemic Agreement (adopted May 2025) will govern how pathogen samples and sequence data are shared and how the resulting vaccines, diagnostics and therapeutics are shared fairly. India said the Agreement's credibility depends on it.
