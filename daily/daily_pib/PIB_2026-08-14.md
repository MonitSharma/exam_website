## 1. Ministry of Heavy Industries receives 20 bids under the Sintered Rare Earth Permanent Magnet (REPM) Scheme

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Heavy Industries (MHI)

MHI has received **20 bids through a global tender on the Central Public Procurement (CPP) portal** for selecting manufacturers to set up integrated facilities for **Sintered NdFeB Rare Earth Permanent Magnet** manufacturing in India. Technical bids were opened on 13 August 2026 in the presence of bidders. This is the first concrete step from policy to plant in India's attempt to break out of near-total import dependence on China for magnets that go into EV traction motors, wind turbines, defence electronics and aerospace.

**Key facts**

- Scheme approved by the **Union Cabinet on 26 November 2025**; outlay **₹7,280 crore**; notified by MHI on 15 December 2025.
- **RFP floated 20 March 2026**; pre-bid conference 7 April 2026; bid submission closed 12 August 2026.
- Target: **6,000 MTPA** of integrated REPM capacity, allocated to **five beneficiaries** (up to **1,200 MTPA each**) via competitive bidding.
- Implementation period: **7 years** from award — 2 years to build the integrated facility, then 5 years of incentive disbursal linked to REPM sales.
- Value chain covered: from **NdPr oxide processing to finished magnets** within India.
- Bidders reported include Coal India, Larsen & Toubro, Attero Recycling, Lohum, Proterial (India) and others.

### Scheme-in-focus: Scheme to Promote Manufacturing of Sintered Rare Earth Permanent Magnets

- **Name & ministry:** Scheme to Promote Manufacturing of Sintered REPM — Ministry of Heavy Industries.
- **Objective:** Build an end-to-end domestic REPM value chain, reduce import dependence, and position India in the global magnet market.
- **Key features:** ₹7,280 crore outlay; 6,000 MTPA integrated capacity; five beneficiaries selected by global competitive bidding; sales-linked incentives over five years after a two-year gestation.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** Domestic and global manufacturers setting up integrated units in India; downstream users in EVs, renewables, electronics, aerospace and defence.
- **Significance:** Rare earth magnets are a chokepoint input for the energy transition. Domestic capacity de-risks supply chains and supports the net-zero-by-2070 and Viksit Bharat@2047 goals.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (industrial policy, critical minerals, supply-chain security); overlaps GS2 IR (resource geopolitics).

## 2. MoHFW convenes two-day National Workshop on Anemia Mukt Bharat; AMB app and portal launched

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW) / National Health Mission

A two-day **National Workshop on the Anemia Mukt Bharat (AMB) Abhiyaan** was held in New Delhi on **12–13 August 2026** with **over 160 representatives from all States and UTs**, ministries, experts and development partners. The workshop operationalised the **revised AMB Operational Guidelines** — a genuine design change, from a supplementation-first programme to a test-treat-track programme.

**Key facts**

- Revised AMB Operational Guidelines were **released by Union Health Minister J.P. Nadda on 29 June 2026** at the Conference of Central Health and Family Welfare (CCHFW).
- Strategy shifts from the earlier **6×6×6** framework to a **7×7×7** framework (7 interventions, 7 target beneficiary groups, 7 institutional mechanisms).
- New approach adds **testing, case management, treatment adherence and dietary diversity** to supplementation, with digital tracking.
- **Anemia Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan Application and Portal launched** at the workshop for beneficiary tracking, follow-up and gap identification.
- Speakers: Health Secretary **Punya Salila Srivastava**; AS & MD (NHM) **Aradhana Patnaik**; DG-ICMR **Dr Rajiv Bahl**; **Dr M. Srinivas** (NITI Aayog).

### Scheme-in-focus (UPDATED): Anemia Mukt Bharat

- **Name & ministry:** Anemia Mukt Bharat — MoHFW, delivered through the National Health Mission.
- **Objective:** Accelerate reduction in anaemia prevalence across the life course, in line with POSHAN Abhiyaan targets.
- **Key features (revised):** 7×7×7 strategy; universal testing and severity-based treatment; adherence monitoring; dietary diversity and iron-rich food promotion; digital beneficiary tracking via the new AMB app/portal.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** Children 6–59 months, school-going children and adolescents, women of reproductive age, pregnant and lactating women — with supplementation now being extended to younger cohorts under a life-course approach.
- **Significance:** Anaemia remains one of India's most stubborn public-health indicators (NFHS-5 showed worsening in several groups). The pivot from blanket supplementation to diagnosis-led management is the substantive point for answer-writing.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance/Social Sector (health schemes); GS1 — Society (women and child health).

## 3. NITI Aayog releases "Key Sectors to Position India as a Global Manufacturing Hub"

**Issuing body:** NITI Aayog (released 13 August 2026)

The report applies a structured, data-driven framework to identify manufacturing sectors where India can win globally. **This edition analyses four sectors in depth — Chemicals, Textiles, Telecom & Networking Equipment, and Solar PV** — and will be **followed by reports on eight more sectors**.

**Key facts**

- Four-phase methodology: (i) shortlisting by relative attractiveness, (ii) three-pronged assessment of market potential/competitiveness/strategic relevance, (iii) international benchmarking, (iv) actionable recommendations.
- **Textiles:** ~2% of GDP, **11% of manufacturing GVA**, **9% of merchandise exports**; second-largest employer after agriculture with **45 million+ livelihoods**; FY2025 exports **USD 37.7 billion**; **4.1% of global T&A exports**, **6th-largest** exporter.
- **Telecom:** world's **second-largest** telecom market, **1.2 billion+ subscribers**, ~85% telecom penetration, ~75% internet usage; **National Telecom Policy 2025 (NTP-25)** targets doubling sectoral GDP contribution and exports and creating **1 million jobs** by 2030.
- **Solar PV:** **106 GW installed by March 2025**; needs ~**174 GW more** to reach the **280 GW solar target by 2030**; domestic PV market ~**₹32,400 crore (USD 3.7 bn)**, projected **17–20% CAGR** to FY2030.
- **Chemicals:** three consumption segments — petrochemicals & organic, specialty, inorganic; emphasis on downstream value addition and feedstock utilisation.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (manufacturing, industrial policy, exports); useful data bank for essay and GS3 answers.

## 5. Government outlines India's space debris strategy: Debris Free Space Missions, IADC and IN-SPACe norms

**Issuing body:** Department of Space / ISRO (Government reply in Parliament, Dr Jitendra Singh)

Responding on the risks from proliferating **Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite constellations**, the Government set out India's approach to space debris and **Space Situational Awareness (SSA)**. The exam-relevant framing: orbital congestion is now a sustainability-of-the-commons problem, not just an engineering one.

**Key facts**

- **'Debris Free Space Missions'** is ISRO's initiative to protect space assets and curb debris generation.
- Mega-constellations run into thousands of satellites; most carry autonomous collision-avoidance, but **failed satellites cannot manoeuvre**, and **small untrackable debris** can neither be avoided nor shielded against — a single strike can end a mission and multiply debris.
- **ISRO has been a member of the Inter-Agency Space Debris Coordination Committee (IADC) since its formation in 1996**; India also engages at **UN-COPUOS**.
- The **Indian Space Policy** mandates adherence to internationally accepted end-of-life disposal guidelines; **IN-SPACe's Norms, Guidelines and Procedures (2024)** push Indian space entities toward proper disposal or limited post-mission orbital lifetimes.
- Pillars of sustainable operation flagged: **manoeuvrability, trackability, and a robust regulatory framework**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (space); GS2 — IR (global commons, space governance).

## 6. Ayush and Health Ministries renew the 'One Herb, One Standard' MoU for three more years

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Ayush (PCIM&H) and Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (IPC), 13 August 2026

The **Pharmacopoeia Commission for Indian Medicine & Homoeopathy (PCIM&H)** under Ayush and the **Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission (IPC)** under MoHFW have renewed their inter-ministerial MoU for **three more years** to continue the **'One Herb, One Standard'** initiative — a single, uniform quality standard for each medicinal plant across systems of medicine.

**Key facts**

- Original MoU signed in **2022**; the first phase produced **50 monographs** of medicinal plants.
- Goal: harmonise standards for **single drugs of plant origin** used across **Ayurveda, Siddha, Sowa-Rigpa, Unani and Homoeopathy** — and align them with the Indian Pharmacopoeia used in modern medicine.
- Rationale: the same herb has historically carried different quality benchmarks in different pharmacopoeias, weakening regulatory certainty and export credibility.
- Union Minister of State for Ayush **Prataprao Jadhav** framed it as strengthening the quality and credibility of traditional medicines.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (regulation, inter-ministerial coordination); GS3 — Economy (standards, exports, IPR in traditional knowledge).

## 3 Prelims MCQs (based on today's PIB items)

**Q1.** Consider the following statements regarding the 'Scheme to Promote Manufacturing of Sintered Rare Earth Permanent Magnets (REPM)', which was in the news recently:

1. It was approved by the Union Cabinet in November 2025 with a financial outlay of ₹7,280 crore.
2. It targets an integrated manufacturing capacity of 6,000 MTPA, to be allocated among five beneficiaries through global competitive bidding.
3. It is implemented by the Ministry of Mines.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q2.** With reference to the Anemia Mukt Bharat (AMB) Abhiyaan, consider the following statements:

1. It is implemented by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare through the National Health Mission.
2. Its revised operational guidelines shift the programme from a 6×6×6 strategy to a 7×7×7 strategy.
3. The revised approach relies exclusively on iron-folic acid supplementation and does not include testing or treatment components.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 only
(b) 1 and 2 only
(c) 2 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

**Q3.** With reference to India's efforts on space debris and space situational awareness, consider the following statements:

1. ISRO has been a member of the Inter-Agency Space Debris Coordination Committee (IADC) since its formation in 1996.
2. The 'Debris Free Space Missions' initiative is an ISRO initiative aimed at debris-free space operations by Indian actors.
3. IN-SPACe has issued Norms, Guidelines and Procedures that encourage Indian space entities to adopt end-of-life disposal measures or limited post-mission orbital lifetimes.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

---

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The scheme is run by the **Ministry of Heavy Industries**, not the Ministry of Mines. Statements 1 and 2 match the release: ₹7,280 crore approved on 26 November 2025, 6,000 MTPA split among five beneficiaries at up to 1,200 MTPA each.

**Q2 — (b) 1 and 2 only.** Statement 3 is the trap. The revised AMB framework deliberately moves **beyond** supplementation to include testing, severity-based case management, treatment adherence and dietary diversity, supported by the new AMB application and portal.

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are as stated in the Government's reply — IADC membership since 1996, the Debris Free Space Missions initiative, and IN-SPACe's 2024 Norms, Guidelines and Procedures on end-of-life disposal. India also engages through UN-COPUOS.
