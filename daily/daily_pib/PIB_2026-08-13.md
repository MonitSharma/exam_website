## 1. Monsoon Session, 2026 of Parliament adjourns *sine die* — 12 Bills passed by both Houses

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs (PIB Delhi, 13 Aug 2026)

The 8th Session of the 18th Lok Sabha and the 271st Session of the Rajya Sabha, which began on **20 July 2026**, were adjourned *sine die* on **13 August 2026**. This is the definitive official record of the session's legislative output — the single most reliable place UPSC picks up "which Bill was passed when" questions from.

**Key facts and figures**

- **19 sittings** spread over **25 days**.
- **11 Bills introduced in Lok Sabha, 2 in Rajya Sabha**; 12 Bills passed by Lok Sabha, 12 by Rajya Sabha; **total Bills passed by both Houses = 12**.
- **Productivity: Lok Sabha ~19%, Rajya Sabha ~39%** — a heavily disrupted session.
- Two Bills **replaced Ordinances** promulgated by the President before the session: the **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Ordinance, 2026** and the **Income-Tax (Amendment) Ordinance, 2026**.
- **Demands for Excess Grants for 2022-23** were taken up; the related Appropriation Bill passed Lok Sabha on 04.08.2026 and was returned by Rajya Sabha on 06.08.2026.
- The **Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026** was **referred to a Joint Committee of the Houses** — not passed.

**The 12 Bills passed by both Houses**

1. Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Bill, 2026
2. Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026
3. Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Bill, 2026
4. Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026
5. Appropriation (No. 3) Bill, 2026
6. MSME Development (Amendment) Bill, 2026
7. Bankers' Books Evidence Bill, 2026
8. Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026
9. Kerala (Alteration of Name) Bill, 2026
10. National Co-operative Development Corporation (Amendment) Bill, 2026
11. Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026
12. Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026

**What each of the headline Bills does (exam-relevant one-liners)**

- **Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Amendment Bill, 2026** — amends the 2024 parent Act. Enables States/UTs to designate **Special Fast Track Courts** and appoint Special Public Prosecutors; mandates **investigation within 2 months and trial within 3 months**; allows the Centre to constitute **Special Task Forces**; enhances penalties; provides a time-bound appellate mechanism before a **Division Bench of the High Court**. Already **assented to by the President**.
- **Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Bill, 2026** — brings ***Vande Mataram***, the **National Song**, within **Section 3** of the 1971 Act; makes it an offence to intentionally prevent its singing or disturb an assembly engaged in singing it.
- **Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Bill, 2026** — tightens delayed registration under the 1969 Act: registration **after 1 year and up to 2 years** requires approval of the competent **Executive Magistrate**; **beyond 2 years**, of a **Judicial Magistrate First Class**.
- **MSME Development (Amendment) Bill, 2026** — national digital platform for free, voluntary MSME registration; faster adjudication of delayed-payment disputes of micro and small enterprises; **decriminalises** certain contraventions, replacing conviction-based fines with **graded penalties** (warning at first instance).
- **Bankers' Books Evidence Bill, 2026** — widens "bankers' books" to cover physical, electronic, digital, virtual and **cloud-based** records; technology-neutral, recognises manual/digital/electronic authentication.
- **Kerala (Alteration of Name) Bill, 2026** — amends the **First Schedule** to the Constitution to rename "Kerala" as **"Keralam"**, under **Article 3**.
- **Tribunals Reforms Bill, 2026** — uniform qualifications, appointment and service conditions for Chairpersons/Members of tribunals; provides for a **National Tribunals Commission**.
- **Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026** — amends the 1957 Act for certainty, stability and predictability in the **fiscal regime** of the mineral sector.
- **National Co-operative Development Corporation (Amendment) Bill, 2026** — amends the 1962 Act; expands the definition of "foodstuffs" and removes the geographical restriction on industrial goods.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Parliament, legislative process, Articles 3 & 123, tribunals) | GS3 — Economy (MSME, mining fiscal regime)

## 2. India and the Southern African Customs Union (SACU) sign Terms of Reference for a Preferential Trade Agreement

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Commerce & Industry (signed at Vanijya Bhawan, New Delhi, 12 Aug 2026)

India and **SACU** signed the **Terms of Reference (ToR)** that set the framework for formal negotiations towards a **Preferential Trade Agreement (PTA)**. If concluded, this would be **India's first major trade agreement with an African regional bloc**.

**Key facts and figures**

- **SACU members (5):** **South Africa, Botswana, Namibia, Lesotho, Eswatini**. SACU is the **world's oldest functioning customs union** (founded 1910).
- Combined market of roughly **65 million people**.
- Formal negotiations expected to **begin in about a month**, with both sides aiming to **conclude within a year**.
- This **revives** talks that ran through **five rounds between 2002 and 2010** without a deal.
- India's likely asks: duty concessions on **automobiles and auto components, pharmaceuticals, machinery, electrical equipment, chemicals and textiles**; and more reliable access to SACU **critical minerals — platinum-group metals, manganese, copper**.

**Why it matters:** A PTA (tariff concessions on a limited set of lines) is a lower rung than an FTA/CECA — a useful distinction for Prelims. Strategically it plugs into India's Africa outreach, the critical-minerals security push (KABIL, National Critical Mineral Mission) and the wider India–Africa trade architecture alongside the AfCFTA.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India–Africa, regional groupings) | GS3 — Economy (trade agreements, critical minerals)

## 3. Digital India BHASHINI Division and NITI Aayog sign a Statement of Intent on multilingual AI for governance

**Issuing body:** Digital India BHASHINI Division (DIBD), under Digital India Corporation, **Ministry of Electronics & IT** — with **NITI Aayog** (12 Aug 2026)

A **Statement of Intent (SoI)** — not an MoU — to build **multilingual, voice-first AI solutions** for public service delivery under the **BHASHINI for Seva / Sanchalan** initiative.

**Key features**

- Integrates BHASHINI's language-AI stack across **NITI Aayog's digital platforms and services**, so citizens can access government information in their preferred Indian language.
- **Reference applications and voice bots** for selected NITI Aayog sectors — voice-based interaction as part of digital public-service delivery.
- **BHASHINI Sahayogi** will use **BhashaDaan** to crowdsource, curate and share linguistic data; officials and citizens contribute text, audio and video samples to improve models across **languages, dialects and specialised domains**.

**Background worth knowing:** BHASHINI = *Bhasha Interface for India*, launched 2022 under the **National Language Translation Mission**; it is a **Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)** layer for language, alongside Aadhaar, UPI and ONDC. Recent BHASHINI tie-ups include the Ministry of Ayush and Punjab National Bank.

## 4. World Elephant Day 2026 national celebration held at Visakhapatnam; Gaj Gaurav Awards 2026 conferred

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (12 Aug 2026)

The **national-level celebration of World Elephant Day 2026** was held at **Visakhapatnam, Andhra Pradesh**, where the **Gaj Gaurav Awards 2026** were presented for exemplary contribution to elephant conservation and management. Andhra Pradesh was a deliberate choice — wild elephants have been migrating into **Parvathipuram Manyam** district in north Andhra, making human–elephant conflict a live regional issue.

**Key facts and figures**

- **World Elephant Day: 12 August** (observed globally since 2012).
- India hosts close to **60% of the world's wild Asian elephant population**; the Asian elephant is **Endangered** on the IUCN Red List and is in **Schedule I** of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 and **CITES Appendix I**.
- **33 Elephant Reserves** notified; around **150 elephant corridors** identified and verified.
- The **Gaj Gaurav Award** was instituted in **2022** to recognise conservationists and frontline workers.

### Scheme-in-focus: **Project Elephant**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Project Elephant |
| **Ministry** | Environment, Forest and Climate Change |
| **Launched** | **1992**, as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme |
| **Objective** | Protect elephants, their habitats and migratory corridors; address human–elephant conflict; ensure welfare of captive elephants |

**Key features (4)**

1. **Financial and technical assistance to States** for elephant habitat and corridor management, notification of **Elephant Reserves**, and anti-poaching operations.
2. **Human–elephant conflict mitigation** — early-warning systems, barriers, rapid response teams and ex-gratia for crop/property/life loss.
3. **Welfare of captive elephants** — guidelines on care, transfer and health, plus the **Gaj Gaurav Awards** and awareness campaigns such as *Gaj Utsav*.
4. **Monitoring and science** — periodic **synchronised elephant population estimation**, MIKE (Monitoring the Illegal Killing of Elephants) sites, and the elephant-corridor mapping exercise.

**Beneficiaries/coverage:** Elephant-bearing States across the North-East, Eastern, Central, North-West and Southern elephant ranges; forest-fringe communities exposed to conflict; and captive-elephant keepers.

**Significance:** The elephant is India's **National Heritage Animal** (2010) and a **keystone/umbrella species** — protecting its corridors protects entire forest landscapes. Since 2023-24 elephant conservation funding largely flows through the umbrella **Integrated Development of Wildlife Habitats** framework, so exam questions increasingly test the *scheme architecture*, not just the name.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Ecology (species conservation, human–wildlife conflict)

## 5. Home Minister launches the 'Gramin Gyan Setu' app to interconnect rural libraries

**Issuing body:** Ministry of Home Affairs / Ministry of Cooperation (launched by the Union Home Minister via video conferencing, 12 Aug 2026)

A digital library network for the **Gandhinagar Lok Sabha constituency, Gujarat**, connecting village libraries to large central collections — launched on the birth anniversary of **Padma Shri S. R. Ranganathan**, observed as **National Library Day**.

**Key facts and figures**

- Links **63 rural libraries** with **4 central libraries** holding a combined **2.8 lakh books**.
- Village libraries in **Gandhinagar, Kalol and Sanand** stocked with roughly **4,500–6,000 books each**.
- **4 mobile library vans**, funded from the **MP Local Area Development (MPLAD) grant**, to visit villages **every 15 days**.
- Stated objective: a library in **every village**, preferably located near a school.

**Why it matters:** A usable model answer example for questions on rural knowledge infrastructure, learning poverty and the **National Mission on Libraries**; also a clean illustration of MPLADS being deployed for social infrastructure. **S. R. Ranganathan**, the father of library science in India, is himself a fair Prelims target — his **Five Laws of Library Science** and the **Colon Classification**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance (public service delivery, education infrastructure) | GS1 — Society

## 6. National Commission for Scheduled Castes submits its Annual Report 2024-25 to the President

**Issuing body:** National Commission for Scheduled Castes (NCSC) / Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment (Rashtrapati Bhavan, 12 Aug 2026)

The NCSC presented its **Annual Report for 2024-25** to **President Droupadi Murmu**, discharging its constitutional duty to report on the working of safeguards for the Scheduled Castes.

**Key constitutional facts (high-yield)**

- The NCSC is a **constitutional body under Article 338**, in its present form since the **65th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1990** (which converted the earlier single Special Officer/Commissioner into a multi-member Commission); the **89th Amendment, 2003** bifurcated it into separate Commissions for SCs (Art. 338) and STs (**Art. 338A**).
- **Composition:** a Chairperson, a Vice-Chairperson and **three other Members**, appointed by the **President by warrant under his hand and seal**.
- **Article 338(5)** duties: investigate and monitor safeguards, inquire into specific complaints, advise on planning for socio-economic development, and report **annually and at such other times as it deems fit** to the President.
- **Article 338(8):** while investigating, the Commission has **all the powers of a civil court** trying a suit — summoning witnesses, requiring discovery of documents, receiving evidence on affidavits.
- The President causes the reports to be **laid before each House of Parliament**, along with a memorandum on action taken on the recommendations and reasons for non-acceptance of any of them.
- Under **Article 338(9)**, the Union and every State government must **consult the Commission on all major policy matters** affecting Scheduled Castes.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (constitutional bodies, safeguards for weaker sections)

**Q3.** With reference to Project Elephant, consider the following statements:

1. It was launched in 1992 as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme.
2. The Gaj Gaurav Award is conferred under it to recognise contributions to elephant conservation.
3. India is home to the largest population of wild Asian elephants in the world.

(a) 1 and 2 only
(b) 2 and 3 only
(c) 1 and 3 only
(d) 1, 2 and 3

## Answers

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The Kerala (Alteration of Name) Bill, 2026 amends the **First Schedule** (names of States and their territories), not the Fourth Schedule (allocation of Rajya Sabha seats), and does so under **Article 3**.

**Q2 — (a) 1, 2, 4, 5 and 6 only.** SACU has **five members** — South Africa, Botswana, Namibia, Lesotho and Eswatini. **Zimbabwe is not a member** (it belongs to SADC and COMESA).

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct: Project Elephant was launched in 1992 as a Centrally Sponsored Scheme; the Gaj Gaurav Award was instituted in 2022 under it; and India holds close to 60% of the global wild Asian elephant population — the largest of any country.
