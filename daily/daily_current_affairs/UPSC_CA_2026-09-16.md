## Part A — Briefing

### 1. Zero-MDR ends for large UPI merchant payments — a 0.4% fee, capped at ₹300, on P2M above ₹2,000 — Economy

**Headline.** On **15 September 2026** the Centre notified a new UPI charging framework under the **Payment and Settlement Systems (PSS) Act, 2007**, settled by the **UPI Steering Committee**: a **Merchant Discount Rate (MDR) of 0.4%** on person-to-merchant (P2M) payments **above ₹2,000**, capped at **₹300** per transaction. All person-to-person (P2P) transfers of any amount stay free, as do P2M payments up to ₹2,000 and all receipts of small merchants in the **P2PM** category taking up to **₹1 lakh per month** via UPI QR. It reverses the blanket zero-MDR regime in force since 1 January 2020.

**Exam angle.** This is the best available case study of the **financing problem of Digital Public Infrastructure**: a public good built at scale on a zero-price mandate, whose operating costs (fraud control, uptime, rural acceptance) were carried by budgetary incentives rather than by users. Note the examiner-friendly triad — **who pays, who regulates, who operates**: the merchant pays, **RBI** regulates under the PSS Act, **NPCI** (a Section 8 not-for-profit, promoted by RBI and IBA in 2008) operates. Second, the **₹300 cap makes the effective rate fall as ticket size rises** — a ₹2,00,000 payment pays 0.15%, not 0.4% — a neat illustration of a *regressive-in-reverse* fee structure you can deploy in any answer on user charges. Third, the political economy: the Opposition has framed the notification as a consumer charge, which it formally is not.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **Statutory basis:** PSS Act, 2007; RBI's oversight arm is the **Board for Regulation and Supervision of Payment and Settlement Systems (BPSS)**. Zero MDR rested on **Section 10A of the PSS Act** (inserted by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2019), effective **1 January 2020** for UPI and RuPay debit cards.
- **Rates:** general P2M above ₹2,000 — **0.4%, capped at ₹300** (the cap binds from **₹75,000**). **Essential/thin-margin sectors** (railways, telecom, insurance, fuel, agricultural inputs) — **flat ₹5** per transaction. **Capital markets** (mutual funds, securities, brokers) — **0.02%, capped at ₹300**.
- **Free tiers:** all P2P; P2M ≤ ₹2,000; **P2PM small merchants up to ₹1 lakh/month**.
- **Coverage:** government estimates **~96% of P2M transactions** unaffected (only about **4%** attract MDR); **P2P is about 70% of UPI value**.
- **Consumer protection:** banks *advised* to stop merchants passing MDR to customers; UPI apps **prohibited** from platform or hidden fees; no monthly quotas on free use.
- **Small-merchant fund:** **5% of total MDR collections** earmarked for UPI adoption among small merchants — compare with RBI's **Payments Infrastructure Development Fund (PIDF, 2021)** for Tier-3 to Tier-6 acceptance infrastructure.
- **Lineage:** Budget July 2019 (₹50-crore-turnover rule) → zero MDR from 1 Jan 2020 → RBI **Discussion Paper on Charges in Payment Systems, August 2022** → NPCI allows **1.1% interchange on PPI-over-UPI above ₹2,000 (April 2023)** → incentive outlays (**₹2,600 crore for 2022-23**, **₹1,500 crore for 2024-25**) → September 2026 framework.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Economy (Banking, digital payments, Digital Public Infrastructure).

**Static anchor.** Mrunal's Pillar 1 — Banking & Financial Intermediaries, the sub-section on payment systems and NPCI products. *Update:* build a one-page "UPI charging tree" (P2P → free; P2M ≤ ₹2,000 → free; P2PM ≤ ₹1 lakh/month → free; above ₹2,000 → 0.4% / ₹5 flat / 0.02%), and cross-link it to the UPI phone-number-masking note filed on 6 September so the whole NPCI-regulation cluster sits in one place.

### 2. Gujarat gets its own Great Indian Bustard action plan — and Project Cheetah is now at 52 birds' worth of scrutiny — Environment

**Headline.** At **Bhuj, Kachchh on 15 September 2026**, the Union Environment Minister released the **Gujarat Action Plan for Great Indian Bustard Conservation** — prepared by the **Wildlife Institute of India** with the Gujarat Forest Department — and inaugurated a **GIB Soft Release Centre** near **Naliya** for captive-bred birds brought from Rajasthan. The same review covered national grassland restoration and **Project Cheetah**, whose population now stands at **52**.

**Exam angle.** Two distinct exam hooks. First, the **species-vs-landscape** argument: the GIB plan is explicitly framed around a *minimum viable population* over a contiguous habitat block, not around individual birds — the standard conservation-biology answer to small-population risk (inbreeding depression, stochastic extinction). Second, **grasslands as India's orphaned biome**: they cover nearly a quarter of India's land area but are classed as "wasteland" in much revenue-record practice, are thinly represented in the protected-area network, and are the habitat that both the GIB and the cheetah need. A Mains answer that connects GIB + cheetah + Banni + the "wasteland" classification problem writes itself.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **Great Indian Bustard (*Ardeotis nigriceps*)** — **IUCN Critically Endangered**; **Schedule I**, Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972; State bird of **Rajasthan**.
- **Target in the Gujarat plan:** a minimum viable population of **30+ birds** over **200–500 sq. km** of contiguous habitat *(government target)*. Priority landscape — **Lala–Rawa, Kachchh (~200 sq. km)**.
- **On the ground:** ~**11 sq. km** of *Prosopis juliflora*-infested grassland restored at Naliya; **30 km of predator-proof fencing** over 15 sq. km; a **Soft Release Centre** near Naliya.
- **Breeding intervention:** a **"jump-start" technique** — infertile wild-laid eggs swapped for viable eggs from the conservation-breeding programme; the **second such chick** has survived past its critical first three months.
- **Grasslands:** nearly **24% of India's land area** *(government figure)*; a proposed **five-year national grassland assessment** by WII, **ICFRE** and **FSI**, supported by **National CAMPA**, is to feed a national grassland-management framework (Banni included).
- **Project Cheetah:** Asiatic cheetah declared **extinct in India in 1952**; translocation from **Namibia and South Africa** began **September 2022** at **Kuno National Park (MP)**. **29 founders → 52 today**; **49 cubs born in India** cumulatively; **~68% cub survival** at Kuno. Managed habitat now **3,428 sq. km** across Kuno and **Gandhi Sagar**, with **Banni Grasslands (Gujarat)** and **Nauradehi WLS (MP)** as future release sites.
- **Wildlife Institute of India** — Dehradun, autonomous body under MoEFCC; technical author of both the GIB plan and the grassland proposal. **MISHTI** — Mangrove Initiative for Shoreline Habitats & Tangible Incomes, reviewed alongside at **Jakhau**.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Environment (Conservation, species recovery programmes).

**Static anchor.** Shankar IAS *Environment* — the chapters on Biodiversity Conservation and on Grassland Ecosystems. *Update:* add a GIB fact-box (IUCN status, Schedule, Desert National Park + Kachchh as the two surviving strongholds, power-line collision as the dominant mortality cause and the Supreme Court's bird-diverter directions) and cross-link it to the Project Cheetah page so "grassland" reads as one landscape argument, not two species stories.

### 3. India and Vietnam move from exchanges to co-production — and park the next Defence Policy Dialogue at Aero India 2027 — International Relations

**Headline.** On **13 September 2026 in New Delhi**, on the sidelines of the **18th BRICS Summit**, Defence Secretary **Rajesh Kumar Singh** met Vietnam's Deputy Minister of National Defence, **Senior Lieutenant General Nguyen Truong Thang**. The two sides agreed to move from training-and-exchange cooperation to **joint production of defence equipment and technology transfer**, emphasised **ADMM-Plus** and its Experts' Working Groups, and fixed the **next Defence Policy Dialogue in India on the sidelines of Aero India 2027**.

**Exam angle.** The value here is the **ladder of partnership nomenclature**, which UPSC likes to test as a matching or statement question: Strategic Partnership (2007) → Comprehensive Strategic Partnership (2016) → **Enhanced Comprehensive Strategic Partnership (May 2026)**. Second, this is India's **Act East policy translated into defence-industrial terms** — the shift from *selling and gifting* (INS Kirpan, patrol boats on Lines of Credit) to *co-producing*, which is the same logic India is pursuing with the Philippines and Indonesia. Third, note the **ASEAN-centric multilateral frame**: India works Vietnam through ADMM-Plus rather than bilaterally alone, which is the diplomatic hedge against a purely China-facing reading.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Diplomatic relations established **1972**; **Strategic Partnership 2007**; **Comprehensive Strategic Partnership 2016**; **Enhanced Comprehensive Strategic Partnership May 2026**. **2027 to be observed as the Year of India–Vietnam Friendship**, marking **55 years**.
- Defence track record: **INS Kirpan** transferred **2023**; **12 high-speed patrol boats** under a **US$100 million Line of Credit**; a further **US$300 million** LoC for defence modernisation.
- **ADMM-Plus** — ASEAN Defence Ministers' Meeting-Plus, established **2010**, the ten ASEAN members plus eight dialogue partners (India, China, Japan, South Korea, Australia, New Zealand, Russia, USA); it works through **Experts' Working Groups** on maritime security, counter-terrorism, HADR, peacekeeping, military medicine, humanitarian mine action and cyber security.
- Vietnam is a participant in India's **Indo-Pacific Oceans Initiative (IPOI)**; India's maritime doctrine for the region is now framed as **MAHASAGAR** (successor framing to SAGAR).
- Bilateral trade target of **US$25 billion by 2030**.

**Notebook.** GS2 — International Relations (India and its neighbourhood/ASEAN; bilateral groupings and agreements).

**Static anchor.** Your GS2 IR notebook's Act East Policy page. *Update:* convert the partnership-nomenclature ladder into a single table across India's partners (Strategic / Comprehensive Strategic / Enhanced Comprehensive Strategic / Comprehensive Global Strategic) with the year each was signed, and add a second column for what *defence-industrial* stage each has reached — sale, LoC-funded transfer, or co-production.

### 4. Meta agrees to report child sexual abuse material straight to India's I4C, bypassing the NCMEC route — Society

**Headline.** Meta has told Indian authorities it will report **child safety cases directly to the cybercrime portal run by the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C)**, rather than only to the **US-based National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (NCMEC)**. The commitment came around the **NCPCR** proceedings that followed a **BBC Eye** investigation alleging Instagram carried paid advertisements promoting CSAM; Meta India's leadership was summoned and appeared before the Commission on **9 September 2026**. Meta is the first large intermediary to commit to a direct pipeline to Indian law enforcement.

**Exam angle.** Read this as a question about **jurisdiction over evidence**, not about content moderation. Under the existing arrangement, a report generated in India travels to a US non-profit and returns to Indian police through the **NCRB–NCMEC MoU of 2019** — adding latency to time-critical investigations. The direct pipeline compresses that, but raises the reciprocal question the examiner will want: what **statutory basis, retention limits and due-process safeguards** govern a foreign platform pushing personal data straight into a government portal? That is the **DPDP Act, 2023** and IT Rules territory. Also useful for the perennial GS1 Society theme — children and the digital environment.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **I4C** — Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre, under **MHA**, operationalised 2020; runs the **National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in)** and the **1930 helpline**; declared an **"office" of the Central Government** in 2024. Its verticals include the **National Cybercrime Threat Analytics Unit**, **National Cybercrime Forensic Laboratory**, **CyTrain** and the **Joint Cybercrime Coordination Teams**.
- **NCMEC** — US non-profit (founded 1984) that runs the **CyberTipline**; US law requires platforms to report apparent child sexual exploitation to it. India's channel is the **NCRB–NCMEC MoU, 2019**; over **69 lakh CyberTipline reports** had been shared with States/UTs by March 2024.
- **NCPCR** — National Commission for Protection of Child Rights, a **statutory** body under the **Commissions for Protection of Child Rights (CPCR) Act, 2005**, functioning under the Ministry of Women and Child Development; it has the powers of a civil court in inquiries and can monitor **POCSO** implementation.
- Indian law on the offence: **POCSO Act, 2012** (Sections 13–15 on use of a child for pornographic purposes and storage/possession, strengthened by the 2019 amendment) read with **Section 67B, IT Act, 2000**. In 2024 the Supreme Court held that *viewing and storing* such material is itself an offence and urged Parliament to replace the term "child pornography" with **CSEAM** — child sexual exploitative and abuse material.

**Notebook.** GS1 — Society (Children, social issues) with a strong GS2 Governance / GS3 Internal Security (cyber security) overlap.

**Static anchor.** Your GS3 Internal Security notebook — the "Cyber Security Architecture of India" page (CERT-In, NCIIPC, I4C, NCCC, Cyber Swachhta Kendra). *Update:* add a one-line entry for the Meta–I4C direct-reporting channel under "international cooperation", and cross-link to the POCSO page in GS1 Society so the institution and the offence are not filed apart.

### 5. Unemployment at 5.0% in August — rural joblessness at a seven-month low, female LFPR up — Reports & Indices

**Headline.** The **monthly Periodic Labour Force Survey (PLFS)** bulletin released by **MoSPI** on **16 September 2026** puts the all-India unemployment rate for persons aged **15 years and above** at **5.0% in August 2026**, down from 5.1% in July. **Rural unemployment fell to 4.1%** — the lowest since January this year, and down from 4.3% a year earlier — while **urban unemployment edged up to 6.8%** from 6.7%. Overall **female LFPR rose to 34.8%** from 34.4%, driven by rural female LFPR climbing to **39.4%** from 38.8%.

**Exam angle.** The number is less examinable than the **measurement apparatus** behind it, which UPSC has repeatedly probed: PLFS uses **Current Weekly Status (CWS)** for its monthly estimates and both **Usual Status (ps+ss)** and CWS for annual ones; the **monthly** bulletin has existed only since the **January 2025 revamp**. Learn the three ratios and how they interlock — **LFPR** (labour force ÷ population), **WPR** (workers ÷ population), **UR** (unemployed ÷ labour force) — because the trap question is exactly this: a *rising* unemployment rate alongside a *rising* LFPR is not unambiguously bad news, since discouraged workers re-entering the labour force are counted as unemployed. The urban–rural divergence this month is the illustration.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **August 2026 (age 15+, CWS):** all-India UR **5.0%** (July: 5.1%); **rural 4.1%** (lowest since January 2026; 4.3% a year earlier); **urban 6.8%** (July 6.7%).
- **LFPR:** rural **58.2%** (from 58.0%); urban **50.4%** (unchanged).
- **Female LFPR:** all-India **34.8%** (from 34.4%); **rural female 39.4%** (from 38.8%).
- **PLFS** launched by **NSO** in **April 2017**, replacing the quinquennial **NSSO Employment–Unemployment Surveys**; first annual report for 2017-18. Quarterly bulletins were urban-only until the **January 2025 revamp**, which introduced **monthly** estimates and rural quarterly coverage.
- **Definitions:** *Usual Status (ps+ss)* — reference period of 365 days; *Current Weekly Status* — 7 days; *Current Daily Status* — day/half-day intensity. **LFPR = (employed + unemployed) ÷ population**; **WPR = employed ÷ population**; **UR = unemployed ÷ labour force**.
- Nodal body: **Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI)**, through the **National Statistical Office**.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Economy (Employment, growth and development) / Reports & Indices.

**Static anchor.** Mrunal's Pillar 6 — Human Resources & Employment; Economic Survey chapter on employment. *Update:* write the three ratios as formulas on one card with a worked numerical example, and add a two-line note on why LFPR must be quoted alongside UR — that is the pairing UPSC tests, not the headline rate.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Governance / Social Justice, 250 words, 15 marks)

**"Protecting children online requires not only faster reporting channels between platforms and the police, but also a statutory framework that governs how such reports are handled." Examine this statement in the light of recent developments in India's online child-safety architecture.**

*Model-answer skeleton:*

1. **Frame the problem.** India is among the largest sources of CSAM reports globally; the existing route sends platform reports through NCMEC under the NCRB MoU of 2019, adding latency to investigations where evidence is volatile. Meta's commitment to report directly to I4C's cybercrime portal is the first direct platform-to-law-enforcement pipeline.
2. **What the faster channel fixes.** Cuts the intermediary hop and time-to-first-response; strengthens I4C as the single nodal point (portal + 1930 helpline + forensic and analytics verticals); improves the chances of victim identification, which is time-bound; sets a template other intermediaries can be pressed to follow.
3. **What it does not fix — the statutory gap.** A direct pipeline moves personal data from a private foreign intermediary into a government portal without a dedicated statute specifying purpose limitation, retention, access control, audit, redress for wrongful flagging, or oversight of automated detection. Existing anchors — POCSO (Sections 13–15), Section 67B of the IT Act, IT Rules 2021 due-diligence obligations, and the DPDP Act, 2023 — are scattered rather than integrated. The Supreme Court's 2024 ruling that viewing and storing such material is itself an offence, and its call to adopt the term CSEAM, signals judicial impatience with the current framing.
4. **Way forward.** A single reporting-and-handling protocol with statutory backing; mandatory transparency reporting by intermediaries; capacity at the State police level (the reports are useless without trained investigators and forensic throughput); demand-side work through school curricula and parental digital literacy; and mutual legal assistance reform so that cross-border evidence keeps pace with a domestic pipeline.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** Statement 2 is wrong: all person-to-person transfers remain completely free regardless of amount; the ₹2,000 threshold applies only to eligible person-to-merchant transactions. Statements 1 and 3 restate the notified framework — 0.4% capped at ₹300 on general P2M above ₹2,000, and zero MDR for P2PM small merchants receiving up to ₹1 lakh a month.

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct. The plan was authored by the Wildlife Institute of India with the Gujarat Forest Department, names Lala–Rawa (~200 sq. km) in Kachchh as the priority landscape, and was released alongside the inauguration of a GIB Soft Release Centre near Naliya for captive-bred birds brought from Rajasthan.

**Q3 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statement 3 is wrong on two counts: the Comprehensive Strategic Partnership dates from 2016, and the relationship was already elevated to an *Enhanced* Comprehensive Strategic Partnership in May 2026 — the September 2026 meeting was a Defence Secretary-level engagement on the BRICS Summit sidelines, not an elevation of partnership status.

**Q4 — (b).** All-India unemployment eased to 5.0% from 5.1%; rural fell to 4.1% (lowest since January 2026) while urban edged up to 6.8% from 6.7%. Option (d) is the reverse of the actual movement — female LFPR rose to 34.8%, led by rural female LFPR at 39.4%.

**Q5 — (b) Ministry of Home Affairs.** I4C is an MHA initiative, operationalised in 2020, which runs the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal and the 1930 helpline. MeitY houses CERT-In; the NCPCR sits under the Ministry of Women and Child Development.

**Q6 — (c) All three.** CERT-In is the national nodal agency for cyber incident response under MeitY, given statutory status by **Section 70B** of the IT Act, 2000. NCIIPC was designated under **Section 70A** and functions as a unit of the **National Technical Research Organisation**. The National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (cybercrime.gov.in) is run by **I4C under the Ministry of Home Affairs**. The trap here is assuming that one of India's cyber bodies must sit outside these three homes — in fact the architecture is deliberately split between MeitY (incident response), NTRO (critical infrastructure) and MHA (crime reporting and investigation).

**Q7 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** Statement 1 is wrong: the NCPCR is a **statutory** body set up under the Commissions for Protection of Child Rights Act, 2005 — Article 338B creates the National Commission for Backward Classes. The Commission does have civil-court powers in inquiries and is the designated monitoring authority for the POCSO Act, 2012.
