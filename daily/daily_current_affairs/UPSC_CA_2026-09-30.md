### 1. India sits at Pakistan's first SCO table: the Council of National Coordinators meets in Islamabad — International Relations

India's delegation was led by **MEA Additional Secretary Alok Dimri**. It is Pakistan's first major SCO event since it took the **2026–27 chairmanship from Kyrgyzstan on 1 Sept 2026**, after the Bishkek summit. The theme is ***"Turning Vision into Action: Connectivity, Innovation and Shared Prosperity."*** The **Heads of State summit is due in Islamabad in August 2027**. ### 2. Green Energy Corridor-III: ₹1.86 lakh crore for intra-state lines plus 50 GWh of batteries — Economy
**Exam angle:** The constraint on renewable energy is shifting from building generation to evacuating and storing the power. This is the first GEC phase to put **battery storage inside the scheme**. It fits GS3 infrastructure and energy-transition questions and pairs well with the "curtailment" problem.
**Memorise:** Approved by the Union Cabinet on **30 Sept 2026**. Total outlay is **₹1,86,405 crore**:
- **₹1,36,378 crore** for **intra-state transmission (InSTS)**
- **₹50,000 crore** for **50 GWh of BESS**

Central Financial Assistance is **₹54,082 crore** (about 29% of the outlay). It will evacuate **135 GW** of RE, with completion by **FY 2032-33**. **Greenfield** InSTS goes through **Tariff-Based Competitive Bidding (TBCB)**, with private providers building on a build-own-operate-maintain basis. **Brownfield** upgrades are on a **cost-plus** basis through the **State Transmission Utilities**. Targets it serves: **500 GW** non-fossil capacity by 2030 and **900 GW** by 2035. India's solar capacity was **162.15 GW** in June 2026. Background: **GEC-I** (2015, KfW-supported) and **GEC-II** (Jan 2022: about ₹12,031 crore, 33% CFA, 7 states, about 20 GW).
**Notebook:** GS3 – Economy (Infrastructure: Energy) and Environment (Climate mitigation).
**Static anchor:** Mrunal Economy, *Infrastructure → Power sector (Electricity Act 2003, CERC/SERC, transmission)*; Shankar IAS, *Renewable Energy*. *Nudge:* add a GEC-I → II → III comparison table (year, outlay, CFA, GW, storage) and cross-link to your "RE curtailment / grid-scale storage (BESS VGF, pumped storage)" note.

### 3. Preventive detention can't rest on a custodial confession: SC quashes NSA order in the Sambhal case, with ₹10 lakh costs — Polity & Governance
**Exam angle:** This tightens what can count as material for the "subjective satisfaction" behind a detention order. The ₹10-lakh cost against a State is unusual and makes it a strong case study for Mains questions on Article 21/22 and the misuse of preventive detention.
**Memorise:** ***Mulla Afroz v. Union of India*** was decided on **29 Sept 2026** by **Justices Dipankar Datta and Sheel Nagu**. The detenu was an alleged organiser of the **Nov 2024 Sambhal violence** that followed a mosque survey. The High Court had granted him bail, but an **NSA detention (Oct 2025)** kept him in jail, and the **Allahabad HC upheld it**. The SC set aside both the detention and the HC judgment. **Holding:** a confession made in police custody cannot on its own form the basis of the detaining authority's satisfaction. The State must show **cogent material** pointing to a real apprehension of future prejudicial activity, and pending criminal cases or the chance of reoffending are not enough. Law link: confessions to police are inadmissible under **Section 23 of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023** (formerly §§25–26 of the Evidence Act). NSA, 1980 background: detention allowed "to prevent" acts prejudicial to the **security of the State or public order**, for a maximum of **12 months**.
**Notebook:** GS2 – Polity (Fundamental Rights; Judiciary); GS3 – Internal Security (legal framework).
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth, *Fundamental Rights → Article 22 (Preventive detention)*. *Nudge:* add "custodial confession ≠ sufficient ground (2026)" under the case-law list for Art. 22, next to the rule that bail by itself does not justify detention.

### 4. India's first PRAMAAN certificates: a home-grown forest and wood certification label — Environment
**Exam angle:** India now has its own certification route for sustainably managed plantations, agroforestry and bamboo. This matters for exporters facing due-diligence rules such as the **EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR)**. It is a new Prelims term and a Mains example of "trees outside forests" and farmer livelihoods.
**Memorise:** On **30 Sept 2026**, Environment Minister **Bhupender Yadav** handed over the first certificates. **PRAMAAN** stands for *Programme for Recognition and Accreditation of sustainable Management practices for Agroforestry And Natural forestry resources*. It runs under the **Indian Forest & Wood Certification Scheme (IFWCS)**, which MoEFCC launched in **Dec 2023**.
- **Operating agency:** **IIFM, Bhopal**
- **Audits:** third-party audits by **NABCB-accredited** certification bodies
- **Standard:** Indian Forest Management Standard under the **National Working Plan Code 2023**
- **First recipients:** **Andhra Pradesh Forest Development Corporation** (about **33,000 ha** of plantations) and **Govind Mohapatra**, a farmer with the **Odisha Bamboo Development Agency**
- **International fit:** benchmarked against the major international certification frameworks to help exporters meet due-diligence rules such as the EUDR. Certification is **voluntary** (IFWCS was launched as a voluntary, market-based scheme).

**Notebook:** GS3 – Environment (Forests; sustainable trade).
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS, *Forest / Agroforestry* chapter (India State of Forest Report, trees outside forests, Green India Mission). *Nudge:* add "IFWCS/PRAMAAN (IIFM, voluntary, EUDR link)" to your *environment certifications and labels* list, beside eco-mark, BEE star labels and GreenCo.

### 5. Places in News: Vasundhara Tal (Chamoli) gets Uttarakhand's first glacial-lake early-warning system — Geography
**Exam angle:** GLOF risk in the Himalaya is a recurring Geography and Disaster Management theme. This item moves it from mapping lakes to instrumenting them. Pair it with the Chamoli disaster of Feb 2021 and the Sikkim South Lhonak GLOF of Oct 2023.
**Memorise:** Installation runs from **29 Sept to 15 Oct 2026**. It was flagged off by Chief Secretary **Anand Bardhan** and is coordinated by the **USDMA**. Other agencies: **Wadia Institute of Himalayan Geology (Dehradun)**, **CBRI (Roorkee)**, **SDRF, NDRF and ITBP**. Equipment:
- water-level sensors
- automatic weather stations (temperature, pressure)
- snow-depth sensors
- **eight sub-surface temperature probes**
- satellite links for real-time alerts

Uttarakhand has **344 glacial lakes**, of which **13 are high-risk** (2023 data): **Pithoragarh 6, Chamoli 4, and one each in Bageshwar, Tehri Garhwal and Uttarkashi**. Vasundhara Tal is a **pilot**, and similar systems are planned for other lakes.
**Notebook:** GS1 – Geography (Glacial landforms; Places in News); GS3 – Disaster Management.
**Static anchor:** NCERT Class 11 *India: Physical Environment*, ch. *Natural Hazards and Disasters*; NCERT Class 11 *Fundamentals of Physical Geography*, ch. *Landforms and their Evolution* (glaciers, moraines). *Nudge:* mark Vasundhara Tal (Chamoli) and the Pithoragarh cluster on your Uttarakhand map, and add "instrumented EWS (2026)" to your NDMA GLOF-mitigation note.

**Mains (GS3, 150 words, 10 marks)**
**Q8.** *"Transmission and storage, not generation, are now the binding constraints on India's energy transition." Examine this statement with reference to the Green Energy Corridor Phase-III.*

*Model-answer skeleton:*
- **Intro:** India's installed RE has grown fast (solar alone was about 162 GW by June 2026) toward targets of 500 GW by 2030 and 900 GW by 2035. GEC-III (₹1.86 lakh crore; 135 GW evacuation; 50 GWh BESS; by 2032-33) is the policy response to the evacuation gap.
- **Evidence the statement holds:** RE-rich zones (Rajasthan, Gujarat, Ladakh) sit far from load centres; curtailment and stranded capacity when lines lag; the solar "duck curve" means peak demand after sunset; the intra-state grid lags the ISTS; right-of-way and forest-clearance delays on lines.
- **How GEC-III responds:** InSTS strengthening; TBCB to bring in private capital; BESS inside a transmission scheme for the first time (peak and non-solar hours); CFA of about ₹54,000 crore to help State utilities with weak balance sheets.
- **Limits / other binding constraints:** DISCOM finances and payment delays; battery cell import dependence (link to ACC-PLI); storage costs and technology risk; land and ROW issues. *Conclusion:* generation is no longer the scarce input. Grid flexibility (storage, market reform, DISCOM health) now decides whether the RE targets are met.

# PART C — ANSWERS

1. **(b)** Pakistan took the 2026–27 chair from Kyrgyzstan on 1 Sept 2026, and the summit is due in Islamabad in Aug 2027. The Islamabad meeting was of the **Council of National Coordinators**, not the CFM, so statement 2 is wrong.
2. **(c)** GEC-III covers **intra-state** transmission (InSTS) through State Transmission Utilities, not the ISTS, so statement 2 is wrong. It includes a ₹50,000-crore component for 50 GWh of BESS, and greenfield works go through TBCB.
3. **(a)** The SC (Datta and Nagu JJ.) held that a custodial confession alone cannot ground the satisfaction needed for NSA detention. It quashed the order and imposed ₹10 lakh costs on U.P. The Act itself was not struck down.
4. **(b)** IFWCS/PRAMAAN is run by IIFM Bhopal with NABCB-accredited auditors. It is **voluntary**, a tool to help exporters meet due-diligence rules like the EUDR, not a mandate.
5. **(a)** Vasundhara Tal is in Chamoli, and Pithoragarh has 6 of the 13 high-risk lakes. The Wadia Institute of Himalayan Geology is in **Dehradun**, not Shimla.
6. **(c)** All three are correct. Section 23 of the BSA, 2023 bars confessions to police, bars custodial confessions unless made in the immediate presence of a Magistrate, and keeps the discovery-of-fact proviso (formerly §§25–27 of the Evidence Act).
7. **(a)** Under the Electricity Act 2003, CERC regulates inter-State transmission tariff (§79) and SERCs regulate intra-State transmission tariff (§86). Electricity is **Entry 38 of the Concurrent List**, not the Union List.
