### 2. Record July exports, but the merchandise trade gap hits a six-month high — Economy

**Headline:** Commerce Ministry data (released 13 August) show **merchandise exports at a record USD 44.24 billion in July 2026** (+19.63% y-o-y, the highest ever for a July), while **merchandise imports rose 17.52% to USD 76.22 billion**, pushing the goods trade deficit to a six-month high of **USD 31.98 billion**.

**Exam angle:** The textbook Indian pattern in one table — **goods deficit offset by a services surplus**. Services exports of USD 35.89 bn against services imports of USD 18.94 bn gave a net services surplus of roughly **USD 16.95 bn**, which cut the **overall (goods + services) deficit to USD 15.03 billion**, though that is still 31.5% wider year-on-year. For Mains, this is the live illustration of why India's **current account deficit stays manageable despite a large trade deficit** — invisibles (services + remittances) do the work. Watch the cumulative number: April–July overall deficit widened to **USD 49.43 bn from USD 32.32 bn** (+52.97%), the pressure point for the rupee and the CAD/GDP ratio.

### 3. Centre restratifies the Ganga's flood plain and drops the "construction-free zone" phrasing — Environment

**Headline:** The Jal Shakti Ministry's **River Ganga (Rejuvenation, Protection and Management) Authorities (Second Amendment) Order, 2026** — issued 7 August, gazetted 10 August — replaces the blanket "construction-free zone" formulation of the 2016 Order with a **graded, flood-frequency-based zoning** of the flood plain drawn from Central Water Commission guidelines.

**Exam angle:** A rare chance to see **subordinate legislation under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** doing heavy lifting. The parent **2016 Order** — itself notified under EPA, 1986 — dissolved the old NGRBA and created the **National Ganga Council (chaired by the PM)**, an **Empowered Task Force** under the Jal Shakti Minister, and made the **National Mission for Clean Ganga (NMCG)** the implementing authority. The 2026 amendment does not repeal the prohibition on construction in the active flood plain; it **redefines what the active flood plain is** (the one-in-five-year flood line), which in practice shrinks the strictly protected belt and moves the rest into approval-based regulation. Classic Mains tension: **flood-plain zoning as ecosystem protection vs. development pressure on riparian land**, with the added twist that flood-plain zoning has been recommended since the 1975 Model Bill but adopted by very few states.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Instrument: **River Ganga (Rejuvenation, Protection and Management) Authorities (Second Amendment) Order, 2026**; parent Order of **2016** under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**.
- New three-tier flood plain, on **CWC** flood-frequency lines:
  - **Active flood plain** — area of the **1-in-5-year** flood;
  - **Regulatory zone** — between the **1-in-5** and **1-in-25**-year flood;
  - **Warning zone** — between the **1-in-25** and **1-in-100**-year flood.
- The "construction-free zone" wording is replaced by a duty to *maintain* banks and flood plains so as to reduce pollution pressures and preserve **natural groundwater recharge** functions.
- 2016 architecture: **National Ganga Council** (PM-chaired) → **Empowered Task Force** (Jal Shakti Minister) → **NMCG**; legal frame for **Namami Gange**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Environmental legislation; river conservation) with a GS2 governance edge.
**Static anchor:** **Shankar IAS — "Environmental Legislations / Environment Protection Act, 1986"** and the **Namami Gange** topic. Add the three-tier flood-plain diagram beside your NGC/NMCG org chart, and cross-link to **NCERT Class XI Physical Geography — fluvial landforms (flood plains, natural levees)**.

### 4. ISRO's improved SSLV first stage clears its static test — about 100 kg more to LEO — Science & Technology

**Headline:** ISRO ground-tested an **improved version of the SS1 solid booster** (the SSLV's first stage) at the static test facility at **SDSC Sriharikota on 11 August 2026**; performance matched prediction, and the upgrade is expected to raise SSLV's payload capability to Low Earth Orbit by **about 100 kg**.

**Exam angle:** Prelims loves the *taxonomy* of Indian launch vehicles, and SSLV is the newest entry — a **three-stage all-solid vehicle with a liquid Velocity Trimming Module (VTM)** for terminal injection, designed for **on-demand, low-turnaround small-satellite launches** into LEO/SSO. The engineering detail is unusually quotable: the improvement came from a **higher propellant burn rate in two motor segments**, an **optimised thermal protection system**, and **nozzle sub-system** process changes that also cut stage mass — i.e., performance gained without new hardware architecture. **More than 600 parameters** were instrumented to capture ignition, ballistics, structural, thermal, dynamic and acoustic behaviour. For your Physics optional this is a free revision of **specific impulse, burn-rate–thrust coupling and mass ratio in the Tsiolkovsky rocket equation**.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Date/site: **11 August 2026**, static test facility, **Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota**.
- Stage: **SS1**, the SSLV's solid first stage. Gain: **~100 kg extra to LEO**.
- Changes: enhanced **propellant burn rate** in two segments; optimised **thermal protection system**; **nozzle** process refinements; **mass reduction**; more production-friendly.
- Instrumentation: **>600 parameters**.
- SSLV configuration: **three solid stages + liquid Velocity Trimming Module (VTM)**; niche = small satellites to LEO on short turnaround.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Space technology).
**Static anchor:** Your **Space Technology** chapter — the **ISRO launch-vehicle table (SLV-3 → ASLV → PSLV → GSLV Mk-II → LVM3 → SSLV)**. Add an "SSLV upgrades" row and cross-link to **Physics optional: rocket equation, solid vs liquid propulsion**.

### 5. The total solar eclipse of 12 August 2026 — and why totality is still the best coronagraph — Geography

**Headline:** A **total solar eclipse on 12 August 2026** swept eastern Greenland, western Iceland and northern Spain (with partial phases far wider; **not visible from India**), and observatories used the few minutes of totality to image the **inner solar corona**, which the photosphere's glare normally hides.

**Exam angle:** UPSC has repeatedly used **"important geophysical phenomena"** for concept-checking rather than date-checking, so hold the mechanism, not the calendar: alignment (**syzygy**) at **new Moon**, the **umbra** giving totality and the **penumbra** a partial eclipse, the **antumbra** giving the annular "ring of fire", and the reason eclipses are not monthly — the Moon's orbit is inclined about **5°** to the ecliptic, so eclipses cluster into **two eclipse seasons a year**. The science payoff is the **coronal heating problem**: the corona exceeds **1 million °C** while the photosphere is only about **5,500 °C**. India relevance is real and quotable — **helium was discovered from the 1868 total solar eclipse observed from India** (Janssen's yellow spectral line; named by Norman Lockyer after *Helios*), the **1919 Eddington eclipse** confirmed light-bending under General Relativity, and today **Aditya-L1's VELC coronagraph** does from L1 what totality does for a few minutes.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **12 August 2026** total solar eclipse — path over **eastern Greenland, western Iceland, northern Spain**; not visible in India.
- **Syzygy**; occurs only at **new Moon**; **umbra** (totality) / **penumbra** (partial) / **antumbra** (annular).
- Moon's orbital inclination ≈ **5°** to the ecliptic → **two eclipse seasons per year**.
- **Corona > 1,000,000 °C** vs **photosphere ≈ 5,500 °C** → coronal heating problem; corona is the source region of the **solar wind** and **CMEs**.
- **Helium discovered during the 1868 total solar eclipse seen from India**; **Eddington 1919**; **Aditya-L1 / VELC**.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Geography (important geophysical phenomena), with a GS3 Sci-Tech spillover.
**Static anchor:** **NCERT Class XI, *Fundamentals of Physical Geography*, Ch. 1–2 (Earth in the Solar System / Motions of the Earth)**. Add a labelled umbra–penumbra–antumbra sketch, and cross-link to your **Aditya-L1** note under Space Technology with the line: *"VELC = permanent artificial eclipse."*

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Environment, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *"Flood-plain zoning is India's least-used and most-needed instrument of flood management."* **Examine** this statement in the light of the recent restratification of the Ganga's flood plain into active, regulatory and warning zones.

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points):**

1. **Frame the instrument.** Flood-plain zoning regulates land use by flood frequency rather than by embankment-building; recommended since the **Model Flood Plain Zoning Bill, 1975**, but adopted by very few states because land is a State subject and riparian land is politically valuable. Contrast with India's structural bias (embankments, dams) flagged by successive Committees.
2. **What the 2026 Order actually does.** Replaces a blanket "construction-free zone" with a **CWC-based three-tier zoning** — active flood plain (1-in-5-year), regulatory zone (5–25 year), warning zone (25–100 year) — under the **2016 Order made under the EPA, 1986**; the 2016 prohibition on construction in the active flood plain survives, but the *definition* of that zone now governs its extent.
3. **The two-sided assessment.** *For*: legal clarity, risk-proportionate regulation, enforceability, alignment with CWC hydrology, retention of groundwater-recharge and pollution-abatement duties. *Against*: a narrower strictly protected belt, discretion shifted to approvals, loss of the deterrent value of a bright-line ban, cumulative-impact blindness, and the fact that flood frequency itself is non-stationary under climate change and upstream land-use change.
4. **Way forward.** Publish flood-hazard maps and enforce them; tie zoning to the **NDMA flood guidelines** and **Namami Gange**; use **e-flows notification compliance** and **CAG/NGT-style audit** of approvals; incentivise States through Finance Commission disaster-mitigation windows; treat riverfront as ecological infrastructure (recharge, sediment, fisheries) rather than as vacant land. Close on the balance between predictability for planners and precaution for the river.

### D. PYQ Mirror

**Theme 1 — Ganga governance.** UPSC has tested the *institutional architecture* of Ganga rejuvenation rather than any single notification.

> **Prelims 2016:** *Which of the following are the key features of the 'National Ganga River Basin Authority (NGRBA)'?*
> 1. River basin is the unit of planning and management. 2. It spearheads the river conservation efforts at the national level. 3. One of the Chief Ministers of the States through which the Ganga flows becomes the Chairman of NGRBA on rotation basis.
> **Answer: (a) 1 and 2 only.**

*Lesson:* the examiner's hook is **who chairs what**. NGRBA (2009, EPA 1986) was chaired by the **Prime Minister**, not a rotating CM; it was dissolved by the **2016 Order**, which created the **PM-chaired National Ganga Council**. Today's amendment sits inside that same 2016 instrument — so revise the chain, not the headline.

**Theme 2 — Launch vehicles.** SSLV is the newest member of a family UPSC has already tested by function.

> **Prelims 2018:** *With reference to India's satellite launch vehicles, consider the following statements:* 1. PSLVs launch the satellites useful for Earth resources monitoring whereas GSLVs are designed mainly to launch communication satellites. 2. Satellites launched by PSLV appear to remain permanently fixed in the same position in the sky, as viewed from a particular location on Earth. 3. GSLV Mk III is a four-staged launch vehicle with the first and third stages using solid rocket motors, and the second and fourth stages using liquid rocket engines.
> **Answer: (a) 1 only.**

*Lesson:* the trap is always **orbit ↔ vehicle ↔ application**. Extend the same logic to SSLV: **all-solid three stages + liquid VTM → small satellites → LEO/SSO, on demand.**
