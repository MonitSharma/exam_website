# UPSC Daily CA Briefing — 2026-10-11

## Part A — Briefing

### 1. SC lets the Tamil Nadu Speaker close anti-defection proceedings against AIADMK MLAs — Polity & Governance
- **Exam angle:** A live test of the Tenth Schedule: what happens when the *party itself* withdraws the petition, and whether the 15-day condonation proviso can be stretched. A third-party challenger was earlier turned away by the Madras HC for lack of standing. Pairs with the Speaker-neutrality and "independent tribunal" debate.
- **Key facts:** On 9 Oct 2026 the Supreme Court declined to interfere (as reported). Background: 25 of 47 AIADMK MLAs defied the whip in the May 2026 trust vote; the Speaker dropped proceedings against 21 after the party withdrew petitions on 9 June; four who resigned and joined TVK still have pending petitions. Para 2(1)(a) = voluntarily giving up membership; 2(1)(b) = voting against whip (condonable within 15 days). Kihoto Hollohan (1992): Para 7 struck down; Keisham Meghachandra (2020): Speakers should decide within ~3 months.
- **Notebook:** GS2 Polity/Governance.
- **Static anchor:** Laxmikanth — *Anti-Defection Law* chapter. Nudge: add a one-line "condonation applies only to 2(1)(b)" box and cross-link to the Speaker's role under Article 93/178.

### 2. USTR opens a comment docket on the EU's CBAM — and asks whether "enforcement action" is warranted — International Relations / Economy
- **Exam angle:** CBAM is the sharpest friction between climate policy and trade rules; even the US is now questioning it, which strengthens India's CBDR-RC and WTO-compatibility argument and its India–EU FTA talking points. Expect Mains on "unilateral carbon border taxes".
- **Key facts:** Notice published 8 Oct 2026 (docket USTR-2026-0661); written comments due ~30 days after publication (≈ 7 Nov by the notice's own formula; press reports cite 9 Nov — verify). Seeks views on CBAM's effects on US producers/exports, on EU proposals to widen scope to downstream steel- and aluminium-intensive goods, and on whether enforcement action is appropriate. No retaliation or Section 301 probe announced. CBAM = Reg. (EU) 2023/956; definitive period from 1 Jan 2026; covers cement, iron & steel, aluminium, fertilisers, electricity, hydrogen.
- **Notebook:** GS2 IR / GS3 Economy & Environment.
- **Static anchor:** Mrunal — *Climate diplomacy & trade* pillar; Shankar IAS — *Climate Change: UNFCCC principles (CBDR-RC)*. Nudge: add CBAM to your India–EU FTA note with the "US is also objecting" line.

### 3. INS Mangrol: third indigenous ASW shallow-water craft from Cochin Shipyard to be commissioned on 12 Oct — Internal Security
- **Exam angle:** Littoral anti-submarine capability and the indigenisation story (Abhay sonar, Maareech decoy). Useful as an example under "Atmanirbharta in defence".
- **Key facts:** Commissioning at Naval Dockyard, Mumbai, on 12 Oct 2026, presided by FOC-in-C Western Naval Command. ASW-SWC class; waterjet propulsion; Abhay hull-mounted sonar, lightweight torpedoes, indigenous ASW rocket launcher, Maareech torpedo decoy; >80% indigenous content (as reported). Named after the Gujarat port town; succeeds the earlier minesweeper INS Mangrol (retired 2004). Not to be confused with INS Surya (fleet support ship, HSL, covered on 9 Oct).
- **Notebook:** GS3 Internal Security.
- **Static anchor:** Spectrum/Shankar IAS — *Indian Navy: commands and platforms*. Nudge: add the Western (Mumbai) / Eastern (Visakhapatnam) / Southern (Kochi) command table.

### 4. SC to ASI: "lost credibility" — Lodhi-era tombs found used as a cow shed and dumping yard — Art & Culture
- **Exam angle:** The gap between *notified* and *un-notified* heritage, and judicial monitoring of the ASI/MCD/DDA. Ties to the Jaipur Walled City WHS item (2 Oct) only loosely — here the issue is domestic protection law.
- **Key facts:** Order of 7 Oct 2026 in *Rajeev Suri v. ASI*, bench of Justices A.S. Amanullah and N.K. Singh; amicus/court commissioner Gopal Sankaranarayanan. Sites: Zamroodpur tombs (Lodhi era), Lal Mahal (13th-c. palace, Nizamuddin; reportedly not notified as protected), Shaikh Ali Gumti. Court impleaded the Culture ministry's department, ordered senior officials (Culture Secretary, ASI DG, MCD Commissioner, DDA VC, etc.) to appear on 16 Oct, and warned of suspending officers. AMASR Act 1958 (amended 2010): 100 m prohibited + 200 m regulated area.
- **Notebook:** GS1 Art & Culture.
- **Static anchor:** Nitin Singhania — *Conservation of heritage / ASI & AMASR Act*. Nudge: add Article 49 + 51A(f) + "notification gap" as a Mains example.

### 5. NSO's draft guidelines to compile GSDP from the expenditure side (base year 2022-23) — Economy
- **Exam angle:** State income accounting is largely production-side; an expenditure-side GSDP would let States and analysts see consumption vs investment — and fix a data gap (no State compiles PFCE).
- **Key facts:** Released 9 Oct 2026 by NSO (MoSPI); comments till 28 Oct. Components: PFCE, GFCE, GFCF, changes in inventories, valuables, net exports. Aims at uniform methodology across States/UTs and capacity building of State Directorates of Economics & Statistics. Complements, does not replace, production/income approaches. (Distinct from the 9 Oct Environmental-Economic Accounts strategy.)
- **Notebook:** GS3 Economy.
- **Static anchor:** Ramesh Singh — *National Income Accounting* (three approaches, GDP = C + I + G + NX). Nudge: add a "State-level data" line and cross-link to the 2022-23 base-year revision.

### B3. Mains question (GS2 · 15 marks · 250 words)

**Q.** *Critically examine* whether vesting the power to decide disqualification under the Tenth Schedule in the Presiding Officer has stood the test of time. Suggest reforms.

**Model-answer skeleton:**
1. **Intro:** 52nd Amendment (1985) aimed to curb defection; Para 6 gives the Speaker the decision; Kihoto Hollohan (1992) upheld it but allowed limited judicial review.
2. **Problems:** Speaker is a partisan office; delays and selective action (TN case — party withdrawal/condonation, resignations vs pending petitions); dilution via Para 4 mergers; 91st Amendment closed splits but not "resort-politics".
3. **Judicial correctives:** Ravi S. Naik (conduct-based inference), Shrimanth Patil (2019), Keisham Meghachandra (2020: decide in ~3 months; independent tribunal suggested).
4. **Way forward:** Independent tribunal / referral to President-Governor on ECI advice (Dinesh Goswami, Law Commission 170th); time-bound decisions; limit disqualification to confidence/money-bill votes; strengthen intra-party democracy. **Conclusion:** balance stability with representative freedom.

## Part C — Answers

- **Q1: (b)** — Statement 1 is correct: the 15-day condonation proviso applies to contrary voting under para 2(1)(b), not to voluntarily giving up membership under 2(1)(a). Statement 2 is wrong: Kihoto Hollohan (1992) struck down Para 7 (the bar on courts); judicial review survives on limited grounds (mala fides, perversity, natural justice). Statement 3 is correct per the case law cited in commentary on the matter. The Supreme Court on 9 Oct 2026 declined to interfere with the Speaker closing proceedings after the party withdrew its petitions (as reported).
- **Q2: (a)** — CBAM is a unilateral EU regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/956), not a UNFCCC COP decision - which is exactly why India and other developing countries object to it on CBDR-RC and WTO grounds. Its definitive period began on 1 Jan 2026. On 8 Oct 2026 the US Trade Representative opened a docket seeking public comments (30 days) on CBAM's effects on US producers and on EU proposals to widen its scope, including whether enforcement action is appropriate.
- **Q3: (a)** — Mangrol is the third ASW Shallow Water Craft (ASW-SWC) built by Cochin Shipyard Ltd, with Abhay hull-mounted sonar, lightweight torpedoes, an indigenous anti-submarine rocket launcher and Maareech torpedo decoys; reported indigenous content is over 80%. (INS Surya, launched on 9 Oct, is a different vessel - a fleet support ship from HSL.)
- **Q4: (b)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct (AMASR Act 1958; 2010 amendment: 100 m prohibited area plus a further 200 m regulated area). Statement 3 is wrong: statutory protection under the Act flows from notification - the lack of notification of sites such as Lal Mahal is precisely the gap in the case. On 7 Oct 2026 the bench of Justices Amanullah and N.K. Singh warned of drastic action, impleaded the Culture ministry's department and summoned senior officials for 16 Oct; the Zamroodpur tombs (Lodhi era) were found used as a cow shed and dumping ground.
- **Q5: (b)** — Per the draft released on 9 Oct 2026 (comments till 28 Oct), the framework uses base year 2022-23 and covers PFCE, GFCE, GFCF, changes in inventories, valuables and net exports; some States compile GFCE/GFCF but none compile PFCE. Statement 3 is wrong - expenditure-side estimates complement, not replace, production and income-side measures.
- **Q6: (d)** — All three are correct. Para 3 (one-third split protection) was deleted by the 91st Amendment (2003), which also capped Council of Ministers size at 15% of the House; Para 4 (merger with two-thirds of the legislature party) remains.
- **Q7: (b)** — Article 49 is a DPSP (non-justiciable, Article 37) and Article 51A(f) is a Fundamental Duty. Courts nonetheless read them with Article 21 and the AMASR Act 1958 to direct protection of monuments, as in the Delhi heritage matter.
