### 1. Cabinet approves ₹62,370 crore NBS subsidy for Rabi 2026-27 (11 Nov) — Agriculture / Economy
**Exam angle:** The Nutrient Based Subsidy (NBS) fixes a per-kg subsidy on nutrients, not on products, and covers P&K fertilisers, not urea. This year the government blames West Asia and Russia–Ukraine price shocks, so the subsidy bill is overshooting the budget.
**Facts to memorize:**
- Period 1 Oct 2026–31 Mar 2027, about ₹24,418 crore more than Rabi 2025-26.
- Rates per kg of nutrient: N ₹94.21, P ₹66.11, S ₹10.42, K ₹2.38 (unchanged).
- DAP retail price held at ₹1,350 per 50-kg bag; SSP freight subsidy continues as a DAP substitute.
- Support for domestic sulphur and sulphuric acid: 20% of procurement cost.
- Total P&K subsidy for 2026-27 is ₹1,03,903 crore, against roughly ₹54,000 crore budgeted for NBS.

### 2. SC: legislature cannot directly annul a final judgment — Srinivasa T. v. State of Karnataka (29 Oct) — Polity & Governance
**Exam angle:** A classic separation-of-powers ruling on validating Acts. A legislature may retrospectively cure the legal *basis* of a judgment, but cannot simply overrule the judgment. Useful Mains line: legislative power cannot be used to rewrite judicial verdicts.
**Facts to memorize:**
- Struck down the Karnataka Civil Services (Validation) Act, 2022, which sought to protect the 2011 KPSC recruitment (362 Group A/B posts) cancelled for corruption.
- Bench: Justices J.B. Pardiwala and K. Vinod Chandran (Chandran J. authored it). Citation 2026 INSC 1173.
- Even untainted appointees fall with the Act; State must hold fresh recruitment, with a one-time age relaxation and no back wages.

**Notebook:** GS2 Polity.
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth, chapters on the Supreme Court and Judicial Review (Art. 141, legislative competence). *Update:* add this case under "Validating Acts" and link to the Basic Structure/separation-of-powers notes.

### B3. Mains question
**Critically examine the limits on a legislature's power to validate actions that courts have declared invalid, with reference to the Supreme Court's ruling in *Srinivasa T. v. State of Karnataka* (2026). (250 words, 15 marks)**

Model-answer skeleton:
1. Intro: the facts, with the 2022 Validation Act on the cancelled 2011 recruitment, and the Court's holding.
2. Principle: legislatures may retrospectively remove the legal basis of a judgment (competence under Arts. 245–246), but cannot directly annul it. That would breach separation of powers and the finality of Art. 141 law.
3. Critique: innocent candidates suffer, but merit and public trust in recruitment (Art. 14, Art. 16) outweigh that. The Court softens the harm via age relaxation.
4. Way forward: fix recruitment institutions (PSC integrity, digital records), and draft validating laws that cure defects rather than overrule courts.

## Answers
1. **(a)** NBS Rabi 2026-27 is about ₹62,370 crore with per-kg nutrient rates. Urea is outside NBS and price-controlled, so 3 is wrong.
2. **(a)** The Act was struck down. A legislature can retrospectively remove a judgment's legal basis, so 2 is wrong. Untainted appointments depended on the Act and fell with it, so 3 is wrong.
3. **(a)** The Philippines chairs ASEAN in 2026, and the visit covers the 23rd ASEAN-India Summit and the EAS. India is a dialogue partner, not an ASEAN member.
4. **(a)** NMBA runs under the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment. NCB (MHA) does enforcement, not NMBA.
5. **(b)** 1, 2 and 3 are correct. Swachh Survekshan belongs to SBM (Urban), not SBM(G).
6. **(b)** Article 141. Art. 136 is SLP, 142 is complete justice, 144 is aid to the Supreme Court.
7. **(c)** Vietnam joined in 1995. Founders were Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Singapore and Thailand.
