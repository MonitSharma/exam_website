### 2. DRDO completes Phase-II Ballistic Missile Defence (AD-1/AD-2) + maiden NASM-MR — Sci-Tech / Defence
**Headline:** DRDO conducted three flight tests on 10–11 June from ITR Chandipur, Odisha, validating its multi-layered Phase-II BMD; also flight-tested the Naval Anti-Ship Missile–Medium Range (NASM-MR).

**Exam angle:** Puts India in the small club of nations with operational-grade, two-layered (exo- + endo-atmospheric) missile defence able to engage Intermediate-Range (and limited ICBM-class) threats. High Prelims value: the AD-1/AD-2 nomenclature, the two-tier (exo/endo) concept, and the test site are all fact-checkable. Mains angle: indigenous deterrence and "Atmanirbharta" in critical defence tech.

**Key facts to memorize:**
- Phase-II BMD interceptors: **AD-1** and **AD-2**; AD-1 does low exo- + endo-atmospheric interception.
- Two-tier logic: Phase-I = endo-atmospheric (lower); Phase-II = exo-atmospheric (higher). Together = layered shield.
- Reported intercept envelope: ballistic missiles up to ~5,000 km (IRBM, limited ICBM-class).
- Test site: Integrated Test Range (ITR), **Chandipur, Odisha**. Plus maiden **NASM-MR** (naval anti-ship) test.

**Notebook:** GS3 Sci-Tech (cross-tag GS3 Internal Security).
**Static anchor:** *Sci-Tech notes → "Missiles of India / Defence R&D."* Update nudge: add an AD-1/AD-2 row under BMD; cross-link to missile-range classification (SRBM/MRBM/IRBM/ICBM) and to Agni/Prithvi families.

### 3. India's Ramsar tally reaches 100 wetlands — Environment
**Headline:** With 2026 additions (incl. Chhari-Dhand, Kutch–Gujarat, and bird sanctuaries in Uttar Pradesh), India's count of Ramsar Sites (Wetlands of International Importance) reaches the 100 milestone.

**Exam angle:** Wetlands/Ramsar is among the most reliably tested Environment micro-topics. The "100" milestone, the global ranking, and the state leaderboard are all clean factual hooks. Pair it with static framework (Ramsar Convention, Montreux Record) for a guaranteed MCQ family. *(Source counts vary slightly across trackers — treat "~100" as the milestone figure and re-confirm against the official MoEFCC/Wetlands of India list.)*

**Key facts to memorize:**
- Ramsar Convention: signed **1971** at **Ramsar, Iran**; entered into force 1975.
- India: **1st in Asia**, **3rd globally** (after UK and Mexico) by number of Ramsar sites; **Tamil Nadu** leads among states (~20).
- Montreux Record (sites whose ecological character is threatened): India has **Keoladeo NP (Rajasthan)** and **Loktak Lake (Manipur)**. Chilika (Odisha) was added in 1993, removed in 2002 after restoration.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment.
**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment → "Wetlands / Ramsar Convention" chapter.* Update nudge: bump India's tally to 100; add Chhari-Dhand (Kutch) to the Gujarat list and re-verify the state-wise count; cross-link to "Wise Use" principle and the National Wetland Conservation Programme.

### 4. RBI deepens bond market via Fully Accessible Route (FAR) expansion — Economy
**Headline:** As part of June 2026 measures to support the rupee and deepen debt markets, the RBI expanded the Fully Accessible Route to include all new issuances of 15-, 30- and 40-year G-secs; alongside, the Centre moved to ease FII taxation and raise NRI equity-investment limits.

**Exam angle:** FAR is a frequently-confused Economy term — it's the channel through which non-residents can invest *without limit* in *specified* government securities. Expect a "what is FAR / which instruments" MCQ. Context for RBI Grade B too. Mains: deepening the G-sec market, capital-flow management, and index-inclusion (JPMorgan/Bloomberg EM bond indices) tailwinds.

**Key facts to memorize:**
- **FAR (Fully Accessible Route):** introduced 2020; lets eligible non-residents invest **without any quantitative ceiling** in designated ("specified") G-secs. Now extended to all new 15/30/40-yr tenors.
- Companion moves: easing of capital-gains/interest-income tax for FIIs; higher NRI equity limits.
- Macro backdrop: repo rate held at **5.25%**; FY25-26 real GDP growth pegged around **7.4%** (fastest among major economies).

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy.
**Static anchor:** *Mrunal Pillar #1 (Money & Banking) → "Government securities / FPI debt routes (MTF, VRR, FAR)."* Update nudge: note the new long-tenor FAR inclusion; cross-link FAR → sovereign-bond index inclusion → rupee/BoP implications.

### 5. "Homemakers are nation builders": SC quantifies unpaid domestic care — Polity / Society
**Headline:** The Supreme Court (judgment of 11 June 2026) held that unpaid domestic work has independent economic value, fixed a notional income of ₹30,000/month for homemakers in motor-accident compensation, and created a distinct head — "Loss of Domestic Care" — with periodic upward revision (~10% every 3 years).

**Exam angle:** Doubly useful — a landmark interpretive ruling (Motor Vehicles Act / "just compensation," Article 21) *and* a Society/care-economy data goldmine. The Court anchored its reasoning in India's **Time Use Survey**. Strong Mains quote line: "homemakers are nation builders." High MCQ potential on the new compensation head and the cited statistics.

**Key facts to memorize:**
- New compensation head: **"Loss of Domestic Care"**; notional income benchmark **₹30,000/month**, revised ~**10% every 3 years**.
- Cited data (Time Use Survey / studies): women do ~**7 hrs/day** of unpaid domestic work, ~**2.6×** more unpaid care than men, contributing an estimated **15–17% of GDP**.
- Statutory hook: Motor Vehicles Act — "just and fair compensation" before Motor Accident Claims Tribunals (MACTs).

**Notebook:** GS2 Polity (cross-tag GS1 Society).
**Static anchor:** *Society notes → "Women & the Care Economy / Time Use Survey"* and *Polity → "Motor Vehicles Act & MACT / Article 21 right to compensation."* Update nudge: log this judgment beside the "care economy" Mains note and cross-link to the National Statistical Office Time Use Survey.

### Mains Question

**Q (GS1 — Society / GS2 — Governance):** *"Recognising the economic value of unpaid domestic and care work is essential both to gender justice and to accurate national accounting." Critically examine in the light of the Supreme Court's recent recognition of homemakers' contribution and India's Time Use Survey.*

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points):**
1. **Frame the invisibility problem** — unpaid care work (~7 hrs/day for women; ~2.6× men; est. 15–17% of GDP) is excluded from GDP/SNA boundaries, rendering it statistically invisible despite real economic value.
2. **Significance of recognition** — the SC's "Loss of Domestic Care" head and ₹30,000 notional income operationalise valuation in law; advances gender justice, fairer compensation, and the "nation builders" principle.
3. **Challenges** — measurement/valuation methods (replacement vs opportunity cost), risk of reinforcing gendered division of labour, fiscal and data-system constraints, no automatic monetisation into incomes.
4. **Way forward** — mainstream Time Use Surveys, satellite accounts for the care economy, invest in care infrastructure (crèches, elder-care) to redistribute and reduce unpaid burdens; link to SDG 5.4.

## ANSWERS

**Q2 — (b).** AD-1 and AD-2 are the Phase-II Ballistic Missile Defence interceptors (exo- + endo-atmospheric), tested 10–11 June 2026 at ITR Chandipur.

**Q7 — (c).** IRBMs are conventionally defined as 3,000–5,500 km range (SRBM <1,000; MRBM 1,000–3,000; ICBM >5,500).
