## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Cabinet extends PM-KISAN to 2030-31 with a ₹3.15 lakh crore outlay — Agriculture

**Headline:** The Union Cabinet on 31 July 2026 approved continuation of the **Pradhan Mantri Kisan Samman Nidhi (PM-KISAN)** for five more years — **2026-27 to 2030-31** — with a total financial outlay of **₹3.15 lakh crore**.

**Exam angle:**
- This is the single largest direct income-support commitment in Indian agriculture and the cleanest live example of the **price-subsidy → income-transfer** shift that UPSC has repeatedly probed (Mains 2015 DBT question, below).
- Prelims trap: PM-KISAN is a **Central Sector Scheme (100% Union funding)**, *not* a Centrally Sponsored Scheme — no State cost-sharing. Candidates routinely get this wrong.
- Mains line: "Income support decouples support from output and price, avoiding the cropping-pattern and WTO-box distortions of input and price subsidies — but it does not by itself correct the market-access and post-harvest failures that keep farm incomes low."

**Facts/figures to memorise:**
- Outlay **₹3.15 lakh crore**; period **FY2026-27 to FY2030-31** (5 years).
- Benefit unchanged: **₹6,000 per year** per eligible landholding farmer family, in **three equal instalments of ₹2,000**, timed to the crop cycle, via **DBT**.
- Launched **February 2019**; Central Sector Scheme; nodal ministry — **Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare**.
- Government-cited evidence: ~**85% of beneficiary farmers reported reduced borrowing**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (Farm income support, subsidies, DBT) + GS2 (Welfare delivery)

**Static anchor:** **Mrunal Pillar 4/5 — Agriculture (Subsidies: input, price and income support)**; also the **DBT/JAM** section of Pillar 2. *Update nudge:* in your subsidy table, replace the old PM-KISAN row (₹6,000/yr, till FY26) with the new 5-year outlay and cross-link it to your MSP-vs-DBT debate page and to the PM-RKVY note from 29 July.

### 2. DoT signs MoU with Madhya Pradesh for India's first Telecom Manufacturing Zone, at Gwalior — Economy

**Headline:** The **Department of Telecommunications** and the **Government of Madhya Pradesh** signed an MoU on **30 July 2026** to set up **India's first Telecom Manufacturing Zone (TMZ)** at **Gwalior** — a 350-acre plug-and-play cluster for mobile phones, telecom gear, optical fibre, semiconductors and 5G/6G products.

**Exam angle:**
- Slots straight into the "cluster-based industrial policy" story alongside Semicon 2.0 (19 July) and the Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme (17 July) — but the *distinctive* feature here is **cooperative federalism in industrial policy**: Centre funds infrastructure, State contributes land.
- Places-in-news value: Gwalior — first telecom manufacturing hub; note the **SADA (Special Area Development Authority)** region and Gwalior IT Park.
- Mains line: plug-and-play clusters attack the *fixed-cost and clearance* barrier to electronics manufacturing rather than only the *variable-cost* barrier that PLI-type incentives address.

**Facts/figures to memorise:**
- **~350 acres**, integrated plug-and-play cluster at **Gwalior, Madhya Pradesh**.
- **Phase-I: 100% Central funding of ₹493 crore** for core infrastructure.
- MP contributes **~170 acres free of cost** (100 acres in SADA + 70 acres in Gwalior IT Park), valued ~**₹596 crore**.
- Expected investment up to **₹9,000 crore**; **10,000+ jobs** projected.
- Signed in the presence of Union Communications Minister **Jyotiraditya Scindia** and CM **Mohan Yadav**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Industrial policy, electronics manufacturing, infrastructure)

**Static anchor:** **Mrunal Pillar 4 — Industry & Infrastructure (Manufacturing: PLI, clusters, NIMZ/SEZ)**. *Update nudge:* add a "TMZ" line to your cluster-schemes table next to NIMZ, SEZ, PM MITRA, Bulk Drug Parks and Medical Device Parks, and cross-link to the Semicon 2.0 note (19 July).

### 4. IMD rules out a cloudburst behind the Upper Assam floods — the distinction matters — Geography / Disaster Management

**Headline:** Meteorological assessment has concluded that the July 2026 flash floods in **Upper Assam** were triggered by **extreme rainfall over the hill catchments of Nagaland**, not by a **cloudburst** — the IMD explicitly rejected the cloudburst label the State had initially invoked.

**Exam angle:**
- This is a definitional Prelims goldmine: **a cloudburst is >100 mm of rainfall in one hour over ~20–30 sq km**. Not every flash flood is a cloudburst; flash floods also arise from saturated soil, dam release, GLOFs, drainage failure and deforestation.
- Mains framing: calling an event a "cloudburst" reclassifies an *administrative and planning failure* as an *unavoidable natural event*. Disaster analysis must weigh **hazard × exposure × vulnerability**, not hazard alone.
- Geography linkage: the **Brahmaputra basin** records the largest share of India's flash-flood events, followed by the **Ganga** and **Krishna** basins; steep Naga Hills catchments discharge rapidly into the Assam plains.

**Facts/figures to memorise:**
- **Cloudburst (IMD):** >**100 mm in 1 hour** over ~**20–30 sq km**. **Flash flood:** flooding developing typically within **<6 hours** of the triggering rainfall.
- Nagaland rainfall in the trigger window: **85 mm average against a normal of 30.3 mm = 181% above normal**; **Mokokchung 238.9 mm (+493%)**, **Zunheboto (+419%)**, **Kiphire (+427%)**.
- Assam impact: nearly **7 lakh people across 12 districts** affected (ASDMA); State flood death toll had risen to **66**.
- Clausius–Clapeyron rule of thumb for Mains: **~7% more atmospheric moisture per 1°C of warming**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Disaster Management + GS1 Geography (Indian drainage, monsoon)

**Static anchor:** **NCERT Class XI *India: Physical Environment* — Drainage System (Brahmaputra basin)** and your **NDMA / DM Act 2005** page. *Update nudge:* add the IMD cloudburst threshold and the flood-vs-flash-flood table to your disaster definitions sheet, and cross-link to the Bagmati–Burhi Gandak channel note (21 July).

### 5. Mega data centres meet local resistance in Visakhapatnam and Thane — the water–energy nexus of digital infrastructure — Environment

**Headline:** Two of India's largest data-centre projects — a **1 GW AI data-centre hub across Visakhapatnam and Anakapalli districts (Andhra Pradesh)** and a **hyperscale campus at Thane (Maharashtra)** — are facing organised local opposition over **water use, power demand, land acquisition and the absence of public consultation**.

**Exam angle:**
- The exam-relevant framing is not "protest" but **the physical footprint of digital infrastructure**: land, freshwater for cooling, and firm round-the-clock power in already water- and grid-stressed regions.
- Ties three syllabus threads together: **environmental governance and public consultation**, **data localisation / digital sovereignty** (RBI 2018 financial-data localisation, DPDP Act 2023, IndiaAI Mission), and **energy security** (why data-centre demand is pulling policy toward nuclear and renewables-plus-storage).
- Quotable Mains line: "Data centres are not invisible digital assets; they are land-, water- and power-intensive industrial plants, and must be appraised as such."

**Facts/figures to memorise:**
- **Visakhapatnam–Anakapalli:** ~**601 acres** allotted across **Adavivaram-Mudasarlova, Tarluvada and Rambilli**; developed as a **1 GW AI data-centre hub**. Ecological flashpoints cited: **Kambalakonda Eco-Forest**, **Rambilli Wildlife Sanctuary**, **Mudasarlova reservoir**. Land-acquisition grievances from **Dalit landholders** at Tarluvada.
- **Thane (Balkum Pada / Majiwada):** **53.73 acres**; reported requirement of **~422 MW** of power plus backup.
- Policy timeline worth memorising: **2018** RBI financial-data localisation → **2020** draft National Data Centre Policy → **2022** data centres given **infrastructure status** → **2023** DPDP Act → **2024** IndiaAI Mission → **2026** tax incentives for foreign firms using India-based data centres.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Environment (EIA, water stress) + GS3 Economy (Digital infrastructure, energy)

**Static anchor:** **Shankar IAS — Environmental Impact Assessment & Public Hearing chapter**; secondary anchor: **Mrunal Pillar 4 — Energy Infrastructure**. *Update nudge:* add a "data centres" case study to your EIA/public-hearing notes as a live example of the **water–energy–land nexus**, and cross-link to the Semicon 2.0 and IndiaAI notes.

### C. Mains-Style Question

**Q. (GS3 — Disaster Management, 250 words, 15 marks)**
*"Not every sudden flood is a cloudburst."* **Examine** how the misclassification of extreme-rainfall events shapes India's disaster response, and suggest measures to move from a relief-centric to a risk-informed flood management framework.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Define precisely.** Cloudburst = IMD threshold of >100 mm/hour over ~20–30 sq km; flash flood = flooding within roughly six hours of the trigger. Establish that cloudbursts are one of several triggers (saturated soil, GLOF, dam release, drainage failure), using the Upper Assam 2026 case where IMD ruled out a cloudburst and attributed the flood to extreme rainfall over Nagaland's hill catchments.

2. **Why misclassification matters.** Labelling an event a cloudburst frames it as an "act of nature", displacing scrutiny from land-use planning, encroached drainage, embankment failure, riverbank erosion and inadequate early warning. It also distorts compensation, insurance and post-event audit, and weakens accountability under the Disaster Management Act, 2005 architecture.

3. **Structural drivers.** ~7% more atmospheric moisture per 1°C of warming; ~three-fourths of India's rainfall concentrated in June–September; Brahmaputra basin as the leading flash-flood basin (followed by the Ganga and Krishna); Himalayan geomorphology, glacial-lake proliferation, and impervious urban surfaces.

4. **Way forward.** Densify Automatic Weather Stations and Doppler radar in hill catchments and adopt catchment-scale (not State-boundary) monitoring; impact-based forecasting and last-mile warning dissemination; risk-informed land use with updated hazard maps and floodplain zoning; nature-based solutions (wetland and catchment restoration); climate-resilient design codes for embankments, roads and drains; community preparedness and inter-State catchment coordination (Nagaland–Assam) through NDMA/SDMA mechanisms.
