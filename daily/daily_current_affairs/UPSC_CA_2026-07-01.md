### 4. Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026 fails in Lok Sabha; Delimitation Bill and UT Laws Bill withdrawn — Polity & Governance

**Exam angle:** A rare, high-value Polity event: the government's package of three linked Bills — the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026 (raising Lok Sabha's maximum strength to 850 and de-linking the Women's Reservation Act's census-trigger), the Delimitation Bill, 2026 (basing the next delimitation on the 2011 Census), and the Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 — failed when the Constitution Amendment Bill got only 298 votes against the 352 needed for the mandatory two-thirds special majority under Article 368. The government then withdrew the two companion Bills since they were contingent on the failed amendment. This is a textbook illustration of the special-majority safeguard actually operating.

**Key facts to memorize:**
- Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026: needed **2/3 of members present & voting + majority of total membership** (Article 368); got **298 votes**, fell **54 short of 352**.
- Proposed Lok Sabha ceiling: **850 seats** (up to 815 from states, up to 35 from UTs).
- Sought to de-link the **Women's Reservation Act, 2023 (106th CAA)**'s "first census after 2023" trigger.
- Delimitation Bill, 2026 would have used the **2011 Census** as the basis for the next delimitation exercise.
- All three Bills introduced 16 April 2026; failed/withdrawn shortly after.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Constitutional Amendment / Delimitation / Women's Reservation).

**Static anchor:** Update the **Amendment of the Constitution (Article 368)** chapter in Laxmikanth — add a cross-link to your Women's Reservation Act, 2023 notes and to the Delimitation Commission chapter, since all three now intersect in this one failed reform package.

### Mains-Style Question

**Q8.** "The failure of the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026 to secure the mandatory special majority illustrates that the amending power of the Indian Parliament, though wide, is not unlimited." Discuss the constitutional amendment process under Article 368 and critically examine the adequacy of the special-majority safeguard in protecting India's federal and representative character. (15 marks, ~250 words)

**Model-answer skeleton:**
- Briefly state Article 368's three amendment routes (simple majority; special majority; special majority + ratification by half the states for federal provisions) and where the 131st CAB fell (special majority route since it touched Lok Sabha's composition/reservation trigger, not the federal list under the proviso).
- Explain why the safeguard "worked": 298 votes fell short of the 352 needed, showing the special-majority requirement forces broad cross-party consensus before altering representative institutions (Lok Sabha strength, delimitation basis, women's reservation timing).
- Balance with the Kesavananda Bharati "basic structure" doctrine as a further, judicially-enforced check beyond the special majority — note that even a passed amendment remains subject to judicial review.
- Conclude: the special majority is a necessary but not sufficient safeguard — it protects against transient majorities, while basic structure protects against a hostile-but-durable majority; both together, not either alone, secure constitutional federalism and representative democracy.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

1. **(a)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct (verified via multiple news reports). Statement 3 is incorrect: bilateral trade was USD 27.5 billion in FY 2025-26, not over USD 50 billion.
2. **(a)** — Statement 1 is correct (5.1% y-o-y, a five-month high). Statement 2 is correct: Mining & Quarrying recorded -1.6% (negative) while Electricity & Gas recorded +9.9% (the highest among the four sectors). Statement 3 is incorrect — the current IIP base year is 2011-12, not 2004-05.
3. **(b)** — SHP is defined as hydro projects up to 25 MW capacity.
4. **(d)** — All three statements are correct: 850-seat ceiling proposed; special majority required and not secured (298 vs 352 needed); the two companion Bills were withdrawn as a consequence.
5. **(a)** — Women's overweight/obesity (30.7% vs 24.0%) and high blood sugar (17.8% vs 13.5%) worsened; spousal violence reporting actually declined (22.3% vs 29.2%), so Statement 2 is incorrect.
6. **(c)** — Article 368 requires a majority of the total membership of the House and a majority of not less than two-thirds of members present and voting, in each House separately.
7. **(a)** — Statement 1 is correct (SHP ≤ 25 MW). Statement 2 is incorrect — in Indian policy, hydropower of any size (including large hydro) is officially classified as a renewable source of energy.
