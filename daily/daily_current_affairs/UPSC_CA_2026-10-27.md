# UPSC Daily CA Briefing — 27 October 2026

*Five fresh items, none repeated from 13–26 Oct. Subject spread: IR, Geography, Polity, Sci-Tech, Environment.*

## Part A — Briefing

### 1. Jaishankar flags the Global South's "4F crisis" — International Relations
**Headline:** EAM says the Global South faces shortages of finance, fuel, food and fertiliser and urges the Global North to see its "equal stake" (New Delhi, 25 Oct).
**Exam angle:** Fits India's "voice of the Global South" positioning. He tied the crisis to the wars in Ukraine and West Asia, chokepoints and trade restrictions. He also called for "de-risking" and diversified options, UN reform, and AI that is not controlled by a few actors. Useful Mains line for IR questions on Global South leadership and the reform of global institutions.
**Memorise:** 4F = Finance, Fuel, Food, Fertiliser. Spoken at the Chinmaya Amrit Mahotsav Global Sammelan, Bharat Mandapam, on UN Day.
**Notebook:** GS2 International Relations.
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS / Mrunal IR pillar "India and the Global South (Voice of Global South Summits, G20 African Union entry)". *Nudge:* add a "4F crisis" box with chokepoints (Hormuz, Red Sea, Black Sea) and cross-link to the food-security and fertiliser-subsidy notes.

### 2. IMD–IITM study: India's climate zones are shifting; Victoria Mode implicated — Geography
**Headline:** A MAUSAM study redraws India's climate map over 30-year blocks (1951–80 to 1991–2020) and finds the Northeast and Gangetic West Bengal moving out of the tropical zone.
**Exam angle:** Climate classification, regional rainfall variability and food-security links. The study blames rising maximum temperatures and the Victoria Mode (a North Pacific sea-surface temperature pattern) for reduced Gangetic-plain rainfall, and recommends including ocean modes in agricultural planning. Good for a "climate change impact on Indian agriculture" Mains answer.
**Memorise:** Northeast tropical savannah ~18% to ~12% of the region; temperate zone now above 80%; Gangetic West Bengal now temperate. Authors: IMD and IITM Pune.
**Notebook:** GS1 Geography (climatology); GS3 Agriculture.
**Static anchor:** NCERT Class XI *India: Physical Environment*, Climate chapter; Spectrum/Majid Husain on Köppen types (Aw, Cwg, BSh). *Nudge:* add the study as a case for "shifting climate zones" and cross-link to ENSO/IOD notes.

### 3. President assents to the Frontier Nagaland Territorial Authority (FNTA) Bill, 2026 — Polity & Governance
**Headline:** Six eastern Nagaland districts get a self-governing territorial authority; implementation awaits a Gazette notification by the State Government.
**Exam angle:** Sub-state autonomy and the Naga political settlement. The bill follows a 5 Feb 2026 Memorandum of Agreement among the Centre, the Nagaland Government and ENPO, and was passed unanimously by the Nagaland Assembly on 3 Sept 2026. Link to Article 371A, Sixth Schedule-type arrangements and the Ladakh hill-council debate you have already studied.
**Memorise:** Districts: Tuensang, Mon, Longleng, Kiphire, Noklak, Shamator. Announced by CM Neiphiu Rio on 25 Oct. Not yet in force.
**Notebook:** GS2 Polity/Governance; GS3 Internal Security (Northeast).
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth, "Special Provisions for Some States" (Art. 371A) and "Tribal Areas". *Nudge:* add a comparison table: Sixth Schedule ADC vs Ladakh hill councils vs FNTA.

### 4. AATAL: MoES opens a source-apportionment lab for Delhi-NCR air — Science & Technology
**Headline:** The Integrated Atmospheric Chemistry and Aerosol Toxicity Analysis Laboratory, billed as a world first, was inaugurated at IITM's Delhi branch on 24 Oct, ahead of smog season.
**Exam angle:** Moves air-quality policy from modelled to measurement-based source identification (vehicles, industry, biomass and crop burning, dust, transported pollution) and feeds evidence to CAQM and GRAP. It links to IITM's AIRWISE decision-support system and sits under Mission Mausam.
**Memorise:** MoES; IITM Delhi branch; Mission Mausam; reported cost Rs 80 crore; inaugurated by MoS (IC) Jitendra Singh.
**Notebook:** GS3 Science & Technology / Environment (pollution).
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS, "Environmental Pollution" (air quality, NCAP, GRAP, CAQM); Spectrum, science institutions. *Nudge:* add AATAL under "Delhi air pollution: institutions" and cross-link Mission Mausam.

### 5. Siang Upper Multipurpose Project: protests turn violent in Pasighat — Environment
**Headline:** The proposed 11,200 MW NHPC project on the Siang in Arunachal Pradesh saw the NHPC office and a police station set ablaze on 23 Oct after a bandh.
**Exam angle:** A classic development-versus-displacement and ecology case: protesters cite loss of ancestral land, seismicity and ecological damage, and complain of inadequate consultation on the pre-feasibility survey. The government frames it as a strategic answer to China's proposed Medog project on the Yarlung Tsangpo, with flood-regulation and water-security benefits. Good for a Mains question on large dams, federalism and transboundary rivers.
**Memorise:** 11,200 MW; proponent NHPC; Siang = Brahmaputra's course in Arunachal Pradesh; counter to Medog (China).
**Notebook:** GS3 Environment; GS2 IR (India–China water); GS1 Geography (Places in News).
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS, "EIA and Environmental Clearances"; Spectrum/NCERT on Brahmaputra drainage. *Nudge:* add a "Siang vs Medog" box and cross-link to Indus Waters Treaty and Brahmaputra data-sharing MoUs.

### B3. Mains question
**Critically examine** the development–ecology–security trade-offs of the Siang Upper Multipurpose Project. How can India balance strategic water-security concerns with the rights and consent of local communities? (250 words)

**Model-answer skeleton:**
1. *Intro:* 11,200 MW NHPC project on the Siang, framed as a response to China's Medog project; protests in Pasighat turned violent on 23 Oct 2026.
2. *Case for:* flood moderation, water security and storage, hydropower for the clean-energy transition, and a strategic hedge against upstream control by China.
3. *Case against:* displacement and loss of ancestral land, Seismic Zone V risk, biodiversity loss, and weak consultation (pre-feasibility survey stage).
4. *Way forward:* free, prior and informed consent through Gram Sabhas, independent cumulative impact assessment, transparent benefit-sharing, transboundary data-sharing with China and Bangladesh, and phased or smaller storage designs.

## Part C — Answers
1. **(c)** — Statements 1 and 2 match his 25 Oct remarks at the Chinmaya Amrit Mahotsav Global Sammelan (Bharat Mandapam). Statement 3 is wrong: he warned AI must not be controlled by a few actors and backed a responsible, inclusive, human-centric approach.
2. **(a)** — Statements 1 and 2 follow the study as reported on 23 Oct 2026. Statement 3 is incorrect: the Victoria Mode is a North Pacific sea-surface temperature pattern.
3. **(b)** — Statements 1-3 are consistent with reports. Statement 4 is wrong: the bill was passed unanimously by the Nagaland Legislative Assembly (3 Sept 2026) and then got the President's assent.
4. **(c)** — AATAL is a MoES facility (IITM Delhi branch, Mission Mausam; inaugurated 24 Oct 2026, reported cost Rs 80 crore). It does source identification and aerosol-toxicity analysis, links to IITM's AIRWISE, and supplies evidence to CAQM and others; it does not enforce GRAP.
5. **(c)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct (protests turned violent in Pasighat on 23 Oct 2026). Statement 3 is wrong: SUMP is on the Siang, the Brahmaputra's course in Arunachal Pradesh; the Dibang is a separate river.
6. **(d)** — Article 371A (13th Amendment, 1962) protects Naga religious or social practices, customary law and procedure, and land ownership and transfer; such Acts of Parliament apply only if the Nagaland Assembly passes a resolution. The Governor also has a special responsibility for law and order.
7. **(b)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: 'Jamuna' is the Brahmaputra's name in Bangladesh, not India.
