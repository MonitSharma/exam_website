## Part A — Briefing

### 1. India and Uzbekistan become Comprehensive Strategic Partners — and a uranium supply line opens — International Relations

**What is new (the 28 August visit was flagged earlier; this is the outcome):** at the Modi–Mirziyoyev talks in Tashkent on 29–30 August 2026 the relationship was **elevated to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership**, **11 agreements** were signed, and the two sides agreed on a **framework for long-term supply of Uzbek uranium to India**.

**Exam angle.** This is the cleanest "Central Asia matters to India" case study available right now, and it hits three GS2 sub-themes at once — extended neighbourhood, civil-nuclear fuel security, and connectivity constrained by Pakistan's transit refusal. The uranium line is the analytically loaded bit: India's domestic uranium is limited and of low ore grade, so the 2008 NSG waiver architecture makes assured imported fuel for IAEA-safeguarded reactors the binding constraint on the 100 GW-by-2047 nuclear target. Pair it with the connectivity problem — no overland route, so trade runs the Chabahar–INSTC multimodal detour — and you have a ready introduction–body–conclusion for any Central Asia question.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Elevated to **Comprehensive Strategic Partnership**; **11 agreements** covering culture, education, tourism, mining, traditional medicine, environment, finance and scientific research.
- Trade target: **US$5 billion annually by 2030**.
- Bilateral **Joint Commission upgraded from Secretary level to a Ministerial-level Coordination Council** co-chaired by the two Foreign Ministers.
- **Uranium**: framework for long-term supply, tied to civil nuclear cooperation.
- **Critical minerals**: joint ventures for exploration, mining and processing (lithium, copper, rare earths) — the KABIL angle.
- **Digital payments**: Indian UPI apps to be able to scan Uzbekistan's **UZQR** for merchant payments.
- **Culture**: Letter of Intent on archaeological restoration and 3D digitisation of the Buddhist monastic sites at **Fayaz Tepa and Karatepa, Termez** (Silk Road).
- **Multilateral**: Uzbekistan reaffirmed support for India's permanent UNSC seat; India welcomed Uzbekistan's prospective entry into the **International Solar Alliance** and the **International Big Cat Alliance**.
- Defence: bilateral **Exercise Dustlik**, latest edition in the **Namangan region, April 2026**; shift from buyer–seller to co-development and co-production.
- PM conferred the **Oliy Darajali Do'stlik Order** ("Order of Highest Friendship"), Uzbekistan's highest honour for foreign leaders.
- Uzbekistan is **doubly landlocked** (one of only two countries in the world, with Liechtenstein) — a favourite Prelims hook.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India and its neighbourhood / bilateral groupings). Cross-tag GS3 Energy Security.

**Static anchor:** your IR notes on **India–Central Asia** (India–Central Asia Dialogue, the 2022 virtual Summit, the "Connect Central Asia" policy) and **Mrunal's external-sector pillar** for the PTA/trade-target thread. *Nudge: add a cross-link from the Central Asia page to your Nuclear Energy page — Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan now both sit on the "assured fuel supply" list alongside Russia, France, Canada and Namibia.*

### 2. Supreme Court moves toward a clarification Bench on the OBC creamy-layer income test — Polity & Governance

**Headline.** The Supreme Court has agreed to consider constituting a dedicated Bench to hear the Centre's plea for clarification of its March 2026 judgment in ***Union of India v. Rohith Nathan***, which struck down the use of parental *salary* alone to deny OBC non-creamy-layer status.

**Exam angle.** This is the rare current-affairs item that lets you write about **Articles 14 and 16 together with the doctrine of intelligible differentia**, and about the difference between a *status* test and an *income* test in reservation law — a distinction UPSC has never asked directly but which sits one step away from the Indra Sawhney framework it asks constantly. The Centre's plea also raises the underrated GS2 theme of **prospective overruling and administrative disruption**: what does a court do when a correct legal finding, applied retrospectively, would unsettle fourteen years of cadre allocations? Keep the numbers — they make the Mains paragraph.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Judgment: ***Union of India v. Rohith Nathan***, delivered **11 March 2026** by Justices **P. S. Narasimha and R. Mahadevan**.
- The creamy-layer doctrine comes from ***Indra Sawhney v. Union of India (1992)***; criteria were fixed by the **Justice R. N. Prasad Committee** and notified in the **DoPT Office Memorandum of 8 September 1993**.
- The 1993 OM has **two mechanisms**: a **status-based test** (Categories I–V: children of constitutional authorities, Group A/Class I and Group B/Class II officers, armed forces personnel, PSU executives) and an **income/wealth test** (Category VI) as a **residual filter**.
- Income ceiling: **₹1 lakh (1993) → ₹8 lakh per annum since 2017**. Crucially, the 1993 OM **excludes income from salaries and from agricultural land** when computing this limit.
- What the Court held: **Paragraph 9 of a 2004 DoPT letter** could not amend the binding 1993 OM by counting salary income of PSU/private-sector employees where **post equivalence** had not been established; treating children of government Group C/D employees as non-creamy-layer while disqualifying PSU/private employees' children on salary alone was **"hostile discrimination" violating Articles 14 and 16**. Remedy: **supernumerary posts** within six months.
- The Centre's grounds for clarification: cascading effect on service allocations from **CSE 2012** onward; **22 judgments** have already applied the *Rohith Nathan* principle; **over 950 recommended CSE 2025 candidates** about to begin the Foundation Course.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Fundamental Rights, Articles 14–16) and Social Justice (mechanisms for the vulnerable).

**Static anchor:** **Laxmikanth, "Right to Equality" (Articles 14–18)** and the chapter on the **National Commission for Backward Classes**. *Nudge: on your Indra Sawhney page, add a two-line box distinguishing the status test from the income test — and note that the ₹8 lakh ceiling has now gone nine years without revision.*

### 3. Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules, 2026 — one legal time for the country — Science & Technology

**Headline.** The Department of Consumer Affairs notified the **Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules, 2026** on **27 August 2026**, making IST the single mandatory reference for all legal, administrative, commercial and official purposes; the Rules come into force **180 days after gazette publication**.

**Exam angle.** Two distinct angles, and Prelims will take the second. First, governance: a uniform legal time is infrastructure for timestamped systems — banking, securities settlement, telecom, power-grid synchronisation, railway and air operations, digital records under evidentiary law. Second, and more testable, **strategic autonomy in timing** — most Indian systems currently pull time from foreign GNSS sources (GPS), and the Rules push dissemination onto **NavIC** and domestic infrastructure. That connects straight to the NavIC atomic-clock reliability question and to critical-infrastructure cybersecurity.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Notified **27 August 2026**; nodal ministry **Consumer Affairs, Food and Public Distribution**; enabling statute is the **Legal Metrology Act, 2009**.
- **180-day** transition before commencement; **alternative time references are barred unless expressly permitted**.
- Government offices and public institutions must synchronise using **NTP (Network Time Protocol)** and **PTP (Precision Time Protocol)**.
- Technical custodian: **CSIR–National Physical Laboratory (NPL), New Delhi**, working with **ISRO** and Legal Metrology laboratories.
- Dissemination to use **NavIC** and other approved Indian sources; **White Rabbit** technology demonstrated for high-precision IST transmission.
- Static pairing: **IST = UTC + 5:30**, based on the **82°30′ E** meridian passing near **Mirzapur, Uttar Pradesh**; India's longitudinal extent (~68°7′E to 97°25′E) spans roughly **30°**, i.e. about **two hours** of solar time — the basis of the recurring "should India have two time zones?" debate.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (indigenisation, critical infrastructure). Cross-tag GS2 Governance.

**Static anchor:** **NCERT Class 11, *Fundamentals of Physical Geography* / *India: Physical Environment*** — local time, standard time and the standard meridian; and your Sci-Tech **NavIC** page. *Nudge: add the White Rabbit + NTP/PTP line to the NavIC page so the timing-dissemination story sits with the navigation story.*

### 4. 219 hanging glaciers mapped in the Alaknanda basin — the hazard India was not counting — Geography

**Headline.** A basin-scale inventory published in *npj Natural Hazards* identifies **219 hanging glaciers** in Uttarakhand's **Alaknanda basin**, and is back in the news as India steps up Himalayan monitoring after the Nepal flood disaster.

**Exam angle.** "Hanging glacier" is the precise term for what actually caused the **February 2021 Chamoli** disaster — a rock-and-ice mass detaching from a steep wall, not a moraine-dammed lake bursting. UPSC likes exactly this kind of definitional discrimination, so learn the distinction: **GLOF** (glacial lake outburst), **LLOF** (landslide-lake outburst), and **ice-rock avalanche from a hanging glacier**, which can *trigger* the other two as secondary hazards. The formation mechanism is also clean cause-and-effect for Mains: warming raises the 0 °C isotherm → permafrost and basal ice cementing the glacier to bedrock thaw → meltwater lubricates the bed → a stable cold-based mass becomes dynamically unstable.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **219 hanging glaciers**, covering **71.7 ± 3.5 km²**, total ice volume **2.39 ± 0.42 km³**, of which the unstable **hanging ice mass is 0.74 ± 0.14 km³**.
- Mean slope of about **33°**; roughly **30% of the hanging mass volume** lies in the **Upper Alaknanda** sub-basin.
- Typology: **117 ramp-slab**, **99 terrace-slab**, **3 terrace-wedge**.
- Simulated avalanche flows in the **Badrinath–Mana sector** could exceed **50 metres** in height.
- Exposed population projected to rise from about **380 to 8,500 by 2030** — pilgrimage traffic, Char Dham road works and hydropower are the drivers.
- Definition: a hanging glacier originates high on a mountain wall and **terminates abruptly at a cliff**, losing mass mainly by **mechanical detachment** (calving, icefall, avalanche) rather than surface melt — it has no gentle ablation zone.
- Static pairing: the **Alaknanda** rises from the **Satopanth and Bhagirath Kharak** glaciers and meets the **Bhagirathi at Devprayag** to form the Ganga; the **Panch Prayag** in downstream order are **Vishnuprayag (Dhauliganga), Nandprayag (Nandakini), Karnaprayag (Pindar), Rudraprayag (Mandakini), Devprayag (Bhagirathi)**.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Geography (geomorphology, Himalayan cryosphere). Cross-tag GS3 Disaster Management.

**Static anchor:** **Shankar IAS Environment — Climate Change / Himalayan ecosystem**, plus **NDMA's Guidelines on GLOF risk management (2020)**. *Nudge: on your Chamoli 2021 page, correct the label if you wrote "GLOF" — add a one-line box separating hanging-glacier detachment from moraine-dam failure.*

### 5. A 'shuddhikaran' at Haldwani revives the history of the Shuddhi movement — Modern History

**Headline.** A political row escalated on 30–31 August 2026 over a **'shuddhikaran' (purification) ritual** — a havan with Gangajal — performed at the Ramlila Maidan in **Haldwani, Uttarakhand**, at the venue where the Congress president, a Dalit leader, had held a rally; the incident has been raised in Parliament with demands for prosecution under the anti-untouchability law.

**Exam angle.** Do not write this up as politics. The examinable content is the **Shuddhi movement** — the Arya Samaj's attempt to build a proselytising and re-admission mechanism inside a tradition that had none, and the **Dalit rejection of the premise that they required purification at all**, which produced the Ad Dharm assertion of *Adi* (original inhabitant) identity. That tension — reform *within* the caste order versus rejection *of* it — is the single most repeated fault line in GS1 social-reform questions. The constitutional overlay (Article 17 is one of only a handful of Fundamental Rights enforceable against private individuals) gives you the GS2 bridge.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **Shuddhi** was popularised by the **Arya Samaj**, founded by **Swami Dayanand Saraswati in 1875** on the principle "Back to the Vedas"; turned into a mass campaign by **Swami Shraddhanand (Munshi Ram)**, who founded the **Bharatiya Hindu Shuddhi Sabha in 1923**.
- **Two objectives**: reconvert those who had adopted Islam or Christianity, and "cleanse" the Depressed Classes of imputed ritual pollution to admit them as full Vedic Aryas.
- Milestones: the **1900 Rahtia controversy** (mass shuddhi of an outcaste Sikh weaver community, causing friction with Sikh bodies); the **1920s mass reclamation of the Malkana Rajputs** of western UP.
- **Dalit counter-current:** **Babu Mangu Ram's Ad Dharm** movement held that Dalits were India's original inhabitants, distinct from Aryans, and needed no purification; Ad Dharmis registered separately in the **1931 Census**.
- Ritual grammar: **hawan/homa**, conferring the **janeu**, and **Panchgavya** as symbolic cleanser.
- Constitutional/legal frame: **Article 17** abolishes untouchability and makes its practice an offence; the enforcing statute is the **Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955** (originally the Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955), supplemented by the **SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989**.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Modern History (socio-religious reform movements) and Indian Society. Cross-tag GS2 (Article 17, Social Justice).

**Static anchor:** **Spectrum, "Religious and Social Reform Movements in British India"** — the Arya Samaj and Shuddhi section. *Nudge: add a two-column box — Arya Samaj's reform-from-within versus Ad Dharm / Adi movements' rejection-of-the-order — and cross-link it to your Ambedkar and Mahad Satyagraha pages.*

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Social Justice, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **"The creamy-layer exclusion was designed as a test of social advancement, but has drifted into a test of income alone." Examine this proposition in the light of recent judicial intervention, and suggest a framework for determining creamy-layer status.**

**Model-answer skeleton (four points):**

1. **Origin and purpose.** *Indra Sawhney* (1992) upheld 27% OBC reservation subject to exclusion of the creamy layer, on the reasoning that reservation is a remedy for *social and educational* backwardness, not a permanent entitlement — advancement removes the justification. The Justice R. N. Prasad Committee translated this into the DoPT OM of 8 September 1993.
2. **The design: status primary, income residual.** Categories I–V exclude on the basis of the *post held* by parents; Category VI applies an income/wealth test only as a residual filter for trade and business, and expressly excludes salary and agricultural income. Income was never meant to be the operative criterion.
3. **The drift and its constitutional cost.** Executive clarifications extended salary into the test for PSU and private-sector employees without establishing post equivalence — producing the anomaly that a Group D government employee's child retained non-creamy-layer status while a private-sector employee's child on comparable income did not. The Supreme Court in *Rohith Nathan* (2026) found this arbitrary and violative of Articles 14 and 16. Counter-point for balance: the Centre's argument that salary is a genuine intelligible differentia, and that excluding it entirely could let very high private incomes claim the benefit.
4. **Way forward.** Complete the **post-equivalence exercise** between PSU/autonomous bodies and government Group A–D scales through the NCBC; a **statutory, standardised rulebook** for computing gross income; **periodic indexation** of the ceiling (unrevised at ₹8 lakh since 2017); and a **prospective-application principle** with supernumerary posts, so that correcting a legal error does not unsettle settled service allocations. Close on the constitutional balance: substantive equality requires both that the genuinely backward are included and that the advanced are not permanently subsidised.

**Theme: India and Central Asia (Item 1).** UPSC has asked this directly and repeatedly.

- **GS2, 2018 (10 marks):** *"A number of outside powers have entrenched themselves in Central Asia, which is a zone of interest to India. Discuss the implications, in this context, of India's joining the Ashgabat Agreement, 2018."*

- The SCO has also been asked in the form *"Critically examine the aims and objectives of the SCO. What importance does it hold for India?"* — **treat the exact year of this one as unverified** and use it only as a template.

**Theme: socio-religious reform (Item 5).** UPSC's standard framing is comparative rather than descriptive.

- **GS1, 2012:** *"In what ways did the reform movements led by Raja Ram Mohan Roy and Swami Vivekananda differ in their approach towards Indian tradition and modern Western thought?"*
- The lesson: the examiner rewards contrast between reformist and revivalist strands, so the Arya Samaj is best written against the Brahmo Samaj on one side and against Ad Dharm / Ambedkarite assertion on the other — exactly the axis Item 5 sits on.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**Q1 — (b).** Statements 1 and 3 are correct: the CSP elevation, the US$5 billion by 2030 target and the uranium supply framework were all announced. Statement 2 inverts the fact — the Joint Commission was **upgraded** from Secretary level to a **Ministerial-level Coordination Council** co-chaired by the Foreign Ministers.

**Q2 — (b).** *Rohith Nathan* (11 March 2026) struck down Paragraph 9 of a 2004 DoPT letter for counting parental salary of PSU and private-sector employees where post equivalence had not been established, holding this "hostile discrimination" under Articles 14 and 16. It did not extend creamy layer to SC/ST, did not revise the ceiling, and did not deal with OBC sub-classification.

**Q3 — (c).** Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Statement 2 is wrong: the Rules were notified on 27 August 2026 but come into force **180 days after** publication in the Official Gazette, to allow systems to be synchronised.

**Q4 — (b).** A hanging glacier sits high on a steep wall and ends at a cliff, shedding mass by calving, icefall and avalanche rather than by a gentle ablation zone. Option (a) describes a moraine-dammed lake, the classic GLOF setting — a different hazard that a hanging-glacier collapse can nonetheless trigger as a secondary effect.

**Q5 — (b).** Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Statement 2 is wrong: Shuddhi had an explicit second objective of "purifying" the Depressed Classes for admission as full Vedic Aryas — which is precisely what the Ad Dharm movement rejected.

**Q6 — (d).** All three are correct. The NCBC was first constituted under the **National Commission for Backward Classes Act, 1993**, enacted pursuant to *Indra Sawhney*; the **102nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2018** inserted **Article 338B** giving it constitutional status; and Article 342A vests the specification of the Central List in the **President**, with any subsequent inclusion or exclusion requiring a law of Parliament.

**Q7 — (b).** Only pairs 1 and 4 are correct. **Karnaprayag** is the confluence with the **Pindar**, and **Rudraprayag** with the **Mandakini** — pairs 2 and 3 are swapped. The correct downstream order is Vishnuprayag (Dhauliganga), Nandprayag (Nandakini), Karnaprayag (Pindar), Rudraprayag (Mandakini), Devprayag (Bhagirathi).
