# UPSC Daily CA Briefing — 24 September 2026

*Five items, five distinct subjects. Nothing here repeats the 10–23 September briefings.*

## Part A — Briefing

### 1. OECD lifts India's FY27 growth call by 80 basis points to 7.1% — the biggest upgrade among the majors — Economy

**Headline:** The OECD's Interim Economic Outlook (23 September 2026) raised India's 2026-27 growth projection from 6.3% to 7.1%, citing resilient domestic demand and policy cushioning against higher energy prices.

**Exam angle:** This is the cleanest available illustration of *why* forecasts diverge — the same economy is being read at 7.1% (OECD), 7.0% (ADB, S&P, Moody's), 6.9% (Fitch) and 6.7% (RBI), which is a ready-made Mains line on forecasting under uncertainty. It also sets up the standard Prelims trap: a growth *upgrade* is not the same as a higher *level* of development, and an 80 bps revision inside one quarter tells you more about the June assumptions on West Asian energy prices than about India's fundamentals. Note the contrast in direction: India up, global growth pegged at a subdued 2.9% for 2026.

**Memorise:**
- India FY 2026-27: **7.1%** (up from 6.3% in the June estimate; **+80 bps**)
- FY 2025-26 (actual/estimate): **7.8%**; FY 2027-28 projection: **6.5%**
- Global growth 2026: **2.9%**; India's average CPI inflation 2026: **4.7%**
- Peer forecasts: ADB/S&P/Moody's ~7.0%, Fitch 6.9%, **RBI 6.7%**
- Publication: **OECD Interim Economic Outlook** (the interim edition, released between the two full Economic Outlook volumes)

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Growth & Development; Indian economy and issues relating to planning, mobilisation of resources).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal — Economy Pillar #1 (National Income, GDP/GNP/NNP, real vs nominal)* and Ramesh Singh Ch. 2 "Economic Growth and Development". **Update nudge:** add a single comparison row to your "who forecasts what" table — OECD / IMF / World Bank / ADB / RBI / Economic Survey — with the release month against each, and cross-link it to the growth-vs-development note, because that distinction is where UPSC has actually set the question.

### 2. A 31 December 2029 deadline for the drug trade — the 3rd National ANTF Conference adopts a 2026–29 Vision Document — Internal Security

**Headline:** The Union Home Minister inaugurated the 3rd National Conference of Anti-Narcotics Task Force heads in New Delhi (22–23 September 2026), built around a Vision Document on Narcotics Control 2026–2029 and a stated target of uprooting the drug trade by 31 December 2029.

**Exam angle:** The substantive shift is from **seizure counts to network disruption** — the emphasis is on financial investigation under the PMLA, backward-and-forward linkage probes, darknet and synthetic-drug channels, rather than tonnage seized. That is the analytically interesting sentence for Mains: narcotics enforcement is being reframed as an organised-crime and money-laundering problem, not a contraband problem. Also note the federal architecture on display — 36 State/UT ANTFs converging on a Union-drafted vision document is cooperative federalism in a Concurrent-List-adjacent space, since "drugs" straddles Union control of narcotics with State police powers.

**Memorise:**
- **3rd National ANTF Conference**, New Delhi, 22–23 September 2026; theme **"United Resolve, Shared Responsibility"**; ANTF heads of **36 States/UTs**; eight technical sessions
- **Vision Document on Narcotics Control 2026–2029**; target date **31 December 2029**
- Foundation stones for new **NCB zonal complexes at Lucknow and Dehradun**; revamped NCB website; four public-awareness TVCs released
- **46,780 Nashamukt Mitras** deployed against a **1 lakh** target
- **Telangana ANTF** adjudged best-performing State/UT unit
- Parent frames: **NDPS Act, 1985**; **NCB (1986)** under MHA; four-tier **NCORD** mechanism; **MANAS** toll-free helpline **1933**; **Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan (2020)**

**Notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security (linkages of organised crime with terrorism; money-laundering and its prevention).

**Static anchor:** *Internal Security notes — "Drug trafficking and organised crime" + Laxmikanth's treatment of the Seventh Schedule.* **Update nudge:** on your NDPS page, add the enforcement stack in one line — NDPS Act → NCB → NCORD (four tiers) → State ANTFs → MANAS/1933 — and cross-link it to your PMLA note, since the 2026–29 document makes the financial-investigation route the primary instrument.

### 3. ISRO breaks ground on an advanced radar research facility at Chandrapur, Assam — Project NETRA gets an eastern eye — Science & Technology

**Headline:** The foundation stone for ISRO's Advanced Radar Research Facility was laid at Chandrapur near Guwahati, Assam on 21 September 2026 — a space-object tracking and space-situational-awareness node tied to Project NETRA.

**Exam angle:** Two separable angles, and UPSC could take either. First, **space situational awareness** as a strategic capability: India currently depends heavily on foreign catalogues (notably US Space Command data) to predict conjunctions for its own assets, and indigenous radar coverage is what reduces that dependence — a sovereignty argument, not merely a technical one. Second, **siting**: a tracking radar's usefulness is a function of geography, and the Northeast extends India's longitudinal coverage eastward, which matters for objects in low-Earth orbit that are visible only in narrow windows. Pair it with the standing themes of orbital debris, the Kessler syndrome and collision-avoidance manoeuvres.

**Memorise:**
- **Advanced Radar Research Facility**, **Chandrapur**, near **Guwahati, Assam**; foundation stone **21 September 2026**
- Linked to **Project NETRA** — *NEtwork for space object TRacking and Analysis* — ISRO's SSA programme (radar + optical telescopes + a control centre)
- Tracking, telemetry and command work sits with **ISTRAC** (ISRO Telemetry, Tracking and Command Network), headquartered at **Bengaluru**
- ISRO's SSA control centre: **IS4OM** (ISRO System for Safe and Sustainable Operations Management), Bengaluru, operational since 2022
- Concept to carry alongside: **Kessler syndrome** — cascading collisions in a debris-saturated orbit

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (awareness in the field of space; indigenisation of technology).

**Static anchor:** *Sci-Tech notes — "ISRO centres and their functions" + "Space debris and SSA".* **Update nudge:** on your ISRO-centres map, mark Chandrapur alongside the existing ISTRAC ground stations, and add one line distinguishing **NETRA (the tracking network)** from **IS4OM (the operations/analysis centre)** — Prelims has a habit of pairing an acronym with the wrong function.

### 4. Planetary Health Check 2026: seven of nine planetary boundaries breached, and the food system is named as the dominant driver — Environment

**Headline:** The Planetary Health Check 2026, released on 21 September 2026 by the Planetary Boundaries Science Lab at the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research and authored by 60-plus scientists, finds seven of the nine planetary boundaries transgressed and the trends worsening.

**Exam angle:** The **planetary boundaries** framework (Rockström and colleagues, 2009, out of the Stockholm Resilience Centre) is high-yield precisely because it is a *list with a structure* — nine processes, each with a quantified "safe operating space" — and UPSC likes lists whose membership can be tested. The exam-relevant news is not "things are bad"; it is (a) **which two remain within the safe space** — stratospheric ozone depletion and atmospheric aerosol loading — and (b) the report's new **food-system boundaries** framing, which argues that food production and consumption drive transgression across *all* nine. That last point is a genuinely quotable Mains line linking agriculture, land-use change, nitrogen–phosphorus flows and biodiversity in one causal chain.

**Memorise:**
- **Planetary Health Check 2026**, released **21 September 2026**; publisher: **Planetary Boundaries Science Lab, Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research (PIK)**; **60+ authors**
- **7 of 9** boundaries transgressed; the two still within the safe operating space: **stratospheric ozone depletion** and **atmospheric aerosol loading**
- The nine boundaries: climate change; biosphere integrity; land-system change; freshwater change; biogeochemical flows (N and P); ocean acidification; novel entities; stratospheric ozone depletion; atmospheric aerosol loading
- **Ocean acidification** was the most recent to be declared breached (2025 edition)
- Framework origin: **Rockström et al., 2009**, **Stockholm Resilience Centre**
- New in 2026: the first data-based **"food system boundaries"** framework — food named as the dominant driver of transgression

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (conservation, environmental pollution and degradation); GS1 for the ozone/aerosol physical-geography link.

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment — "Planetary Boundaries" and the Ozone Depletion chapter.* **Update nudge:** write the nine boundaries as a single mnemonic line in the margin and tick the two safe ones; cross-link ozone depletion to the **Montreal Protocol / Kigali Amendment** page — the one boundary moving in the *right* direction is also the one with the strongest treaty behind it, and that contrast is the best available answer to "do environmental agreements work?"

### 5. Four more Indian properties enter UNESCO's Tentative List — a penal colony, an Ahom capital, a Nicobarese living tradition and Shekhawati's painted havelis — Art & Culture

**Headline:** India has added four cultural properties to UNESCO's World Heritage Tentative List: the Colonial Penal Settlement of the Andaman Islands, the Rangpur–Sivasagar Historic Ensemble in Assam, Nicobarese Cultural Continuity, and the Painted Haveli Towns of Shekhawati in Rajasthan.

**Exam angle:** The **Tentative List is the procedural gate**, and that procedure is itself examinable: under the Operational Guidelines a property must sit on a State Party's Tentative List for **at least one year** before a nomination dossier can be considered, so inclusion is a first step and not an inscription. Note also what the four say about the *kind* of heritage India is now advancing — a colonial carceral site (difficult heritage), a regional dynastic capital, a **living cultural tradition** rather than a monument, and a vernacular urban ensemble. The Nicobarese entry is the interesting one for Mains: UNESCO's cultural-landscape and living-heritage categories reward continuity of practice, which is a very different evidentiary claim from architectural integrity.

**Memorise:**
- **Colonial Penal Settlement of the Andaman Islands** — Sri Vijaya Puram (Port Blair); four linked locations: **Cellular Jail, Viper Island, Chatham Island, Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose Island**; the Cellular Jail built **1896–1906** with **seven radial wings** on a panopticon-derived plan
- **Rangpur–Sivasagar Historic Ensemble**, Sivasagar district, Upper Assam — **Tai-Ahom** capital established at Rangpur in **1704**; the **Sivadol** (~32 m, among India's tallest Shiva temples), the **Rang Ghar** pavilion, and the great tanks
- **Nicobarese Cultural Continuity** — Central Group, Nicobar Islands; stilted beehive huts, women's **coil-and-pinch pottery** on **Chowra Island**, *hentakoi* wooden ritual sculpture
- **Painted Haveli Towns of Shekhawati** — Rajasthan; fresco-painted merchant mansions across towns including Mandawa, Nawalgarh, Fatehpur, Jhunjhunu, Churu, Sikar
- India's **World Heritage Sites: 45** (Sarnath inscribed at the 47th session of the World Heritage Committee, 2026)

**Notebook:** GS1 — Art & Culture (Indian culture: salient aspects of art forms, architecture from ancient to modern times); GS1 Modern History for the penal settlement.

**Static anchor:** *Spectrum — the chapter on revolutionary nationalism and transportation to the Andamans; NCERT Fine Arts (Class XI) for temple typology; your "UNESCO WHS in India" list.* **Update nudge:** split your UNESCO page into two columns — **inscribed (45)** and **Tentative List** — and add one line on the one-year rule, because the commonest Prelims error is treating a Tentative List entry as a World Heritage Site. Cross-link the Sivasagar entry to your **Ahom kingdom** note (Paik system, the Moamoria rebellion, the Treaty of Yandabo 1826).

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Internal Security, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **"Effective narcotics control has shifted from interdicting consignments to dismantling the financial architecture that sustains them." Examine this proposition in the light of India's recent institutional initiatives against drug trafficking.**

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the shift.** Open with the empirical point: seizure volumes measure activity, not capability — a network whose proceeds survive a seizure reconstitutes itself. Anchor in the Vision Document on Narcotics Control (2026–2029) and its stated emphasis on financial investigation, backward-and-forward linkages and a 31 December 2029 target for uprooting the trade.
2. **The financial-architecture argument.** Trace the linkage chain UPSC itself has used — drug trafficking → money laundering → hawala and cryptocurrency rails → gunrunning and terror financing. Bring in the PMLA route, attachment and confiscation of proceeds of crime under the NDPS Act's own forfeiture provisions, and FATF-driven obligations. Note that a financial investigation reaches the organiser, whereas a seizure typically reaches the courier.
3. **Institutional machinery and its gaps.** NCB as the nodal agency; the four-tier NCORD from Centre to district; State ANTFs in all 36 States/UTs; MANAS/1933 for citizen reporting; Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan and Nashamukt Mitras on the demand side (46,780 against a 1 lakh target). Gaps to name honestly: conviction rates, forensic and cyber-forensic capacity for darknet and synthetic-drug cases, inter-state intelligence sharing, and the Union–State split of police powers.
4. **Balanced conclusion.** Supply-side financial disruption is necessary but not sufficient — synthetic drugs manufactured domestically weaken the "border interdiction" model, and demand reduction plus rehabilitation (a treatment-not-punishment reading of NDPS Sections 39 and 64A) must carry equal weight. Close on the coordination requirement that gives the conference its theme: shared responsibility across Union, States and community.

### D. PYQ Mirror — how UPSC has asked these themes before

**Drug trafficking and internal security — asked directly, GS3 Mains 2018:**
> *"India's proximity to two of the world's biggest illicit opium-growing states has enhanced her internal security concerns. Explain the linkages between drug trafficking and other illicit activities such as gunrunning, money laundering and human trafficking. What counter-measures should be taken to prevent the same?"* (250 words, 15 marks)

Note what UPSC actually wanted: not a description of the Golden Crescent and Golden Triangle, but the **linkages** — which is precisely the framing today's ANTF item sharpens. The "two biggest illicit opium-growing states" are Afghanistan (Golden Crescent) and Myanmar (Golden Triangle), and India sits between them.

**Growth versus development — asked as a definitional trap, Prelims 2018:**
> *"Increase in absolute and per capita real GNP do not connote a higher level of economic development, if:"* — with the key being **(c) poverty and unemployment increase**.

This is the exact reasoning the OECD upgrade should trigger. A 7.1% print is a growth statement; whether it is a development statement depends on employment and distribution. Keep the two vocabularies separate in your answers.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**1. (a)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. The OECD Interim Economic Outlook of 23 September 2026 raised India's FY27 projection from 6.3% to 7.1% (+80 bps), which is above the RBI's 6.7%. Statement 3 is wrong: global growth was projected at **2.9%** for 2026, far *below* the India figure, not above it.

**2. (b)** — The Vision Document on Narcotics Control (2026–2029) framed the 3rd National Conference of Anti-Narcotics Task Force heads held in New Delhi on 22–23 September 2026, attended by ANTF heads of 36 States and UTs under the theme "United Resolve, Shared Responsibility".

**3. (c)** — The facility is a space-object tracking node tied to **Project NETRA** (NEtwork for space object TRacking and Analysis), ISRO's space situational awareness programme; the foundation stone was laid at Chandrapur near Guwahati on 21 September 2026. It is not a meteorological, air-defence or geophysical radar.

**4. (a)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct: seven of nine boundaries are transgressed, with stratospheric ozone depletion and atmospheric aerosol loading still within the safe operating space. Statement 3 is wrong: the report is produced by the **Planetary Boundaries Science Lab at the Potsdam Institute for Climate Impact Research (PIK)**, not the IPCC.

**5. (c)** — All three pairs are correctly matched. The fourth property added alongside them is **Nicobarese Cultural Continuity**, in the Central Group of the Nicobar Islands.

**6. (d)** — All three are correct. The framework was advanced in 2009 by Johan Rockström and colleagues associated with the Stockholm Resilience Centre; it quantifies a safe operating space for each Earth-system process; and ocean acidification is one of the nine boundaries — the most recent to be assessed as transgressed.

**7. (d)** — All three are correct. The NCB, constituted in 1986 under the NDPS Act, 1985, functions under the Ministry of Home Affairs; the NCORD mechanism runs at four tiers down to the district level; and MANAS (helpline 1933) allows citizens to report trafficking and seek de-addiction support anonymously.
