### 1. Defence Ministry notifies the 6th Positive Indigenisation List — 405 items, ₹3,070 crore — Internal Security

**Headline:** The Department of Defence Production (DDP) notified the sixth Positive Indigenisation List on 18 August 2026, covering 405 strategically important line-replaceable units, sub-systems, sub-assemblies, spares, components and raw materials with an estimated business potential of ₹3,070 crore.

**Exam angle:** The PIL is the *import-embargo* instrument of Atmanirbharta in defence — distinct from the Defence Acquisition Procedure (DAP) 2020 categories (Buy Indian-IDDM etc.), which govern *how* things are procured. Note the shift in the sixth list: earlier PILs targeted big-ticket *platforms*; this one goes after the component-and-spares layer, which is where import dependence actually persists. Also a ready GS3 example of MSME/startup participation via the SRIJAN portal.

**Key facts to memorise:**

- 405 items; ₹3,070 crore business potential; notified 18 Aug 2026 by DDP, Ministry of Defence.
- Split: **16 items for the Indian Coast Guard**, **389 linked to DPSUs**.
- Platforms covered: ALH, Light Utility Helicopter, **Su-30MKI**, LCA, **AL-31FP engine**; armour — **T-72, T-90, BMP-II**.
- Indicative indigenisation timelines: **December 2026 to December 2029**.
- Cumulative: **5,012 items** under the first five PILs (5th PIL had 346 items); **>33,000 items** offered on the **SRIJAN** portal as of June 2026; **>15,700 items** indigenised; ~**₹9,000 crore** import substitution over five years.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security (Defence indigenisation / Atmanirbhar Bharat).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* — Defence & Internal Security pillar, "Defence Production & Procurement". **Nudge:** on your DAP-2020 page, add a two-line box separating "PIL = negative import list, item-level embargo with a date" from "DAP categories = procurement route hierarchy", and cross-link to iDEX/SRIJAN and the defence exports figure.

### 2. Project Elephant releases a Regional Action Plan on Human–Elephant Conflict for South India — Environment

**Headline:** MoEF&CC's Project Elephant Division and the Wildlife Institute of India released the *Regional Action Plan for Comprehensive Understanding & Management of Human-Elephant Conflict in South India* (Technical Report 2026) around the 23rd Steering Committee of Project Elephant, with detailed press coverage on 18 August.

**Exam angle:** This is the first regional, evidence-first HEC plan — it maps conflict as *spatially concentrated hotspots* rather than a uniform state-wide problem, which flips the standard mitigation logic from blanket fencing to targeted, corridor-and-infrastructure interventions. High-yield for the human–wildlife conflict question that UPSC keeps circling, and a good counterweight to the usual "compensation and solar fencing" answer.

**Key facts to memorise:**

- Released **13 August 2026** at the World Elephant Day national celebration in **Visakhapatnam, Andhra Pradesh** (MoS Kirti Vardhan Singh; Dy CM Pawan Kalyan). Published by **PE Division, MoEF&CC + WII**; ~100 pages.
- Long-term trend analysis covers **five states: Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Odisha and West Bengal**.
- Content: elephant mortality, human casualties, **spatial hotspots**, seasonal patterns, causes.
- Core finding: conflict is **not uniform** — it concentrates in specific landscapes, driven by **habitat fragmentation, railway and road infrastructure, electrical infrastructure, and expanding settlements**.
- Also released in the same set: *The Trumpet* Vol. VI Issue 01; **Model Elephant Conservation Plan for the Nilgiri Elephant Reserve**; *Captive Elephant Husbandry: Science, Welfare and Practice*; Phase-II of the HEC study.
- Baseline to keep alongside: **Project Elephant, 1992**; elephant is the **National Heritage Animal**; Schedule I, WLPA 1972; Appendix I, CITES; IUCN **Endangered** (Asian elephant).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Ecology (Conservation / Human–Wildlife Conflict).

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment* — "Animal Diversity / Conservation Efforts" (Project Elephant, Elephant Reserves, MIKE). **Nudge:** add a sub-head "HEC: from uniform mitigation to hotspot targeting (RAP South India, 2026)" and cross-link it to your Elephant Corridors (Gaj Yatra / 150 corridors) and railway-track mortality notes.

### 3. Iran moves toward New Development Bank membership — International Relations

**Headline:** Central Bank of Iran Governor Abdolnasser Hemmati said Iran is advancing toward official membership of the BRICS **New Development Bank**, with the statement made ahead of a BRICS finance meeting hosted by India; the NDB itself has not confirmed, and Iran must still complete the accession process.

**Exam angle:** Two separable things UPSC loves to conflate — **BRICS membership** (Iran joined in 2024) and **NDB membership** (a separate accession, still pending). The deeper angle is India's balancing act: the NDB has an explicit policy of not lending in sanctioned jurisdictions, so Iran's entry tests whether the bank stays a market-funded MDB or becomes a sanctions-workaround vehicle — a live GS2 question about the credibility of alternative financial architecture.

**Key facts to memorise:**

- **NDB:** conceived at the **4th BRICS Summit, New Delhi (2012)**; agreed at **Fortaleza (2014)**; operational as a legal entity **2015**; **HQ Shanghai**; first regional centre in **Johannesburg**. Initial authorised capital **$100 billion**, initial subscribed capital **$50 billion**, shared **equally** by the five founders.
- Non-founding members admitted so far: **Bangladesh, UAE, Egypt, Algeria, Uzbekistan, Colombia**.
- Iran joined **BRICS in 2024** (the expansion cohort); NDB accession is separate and incomplete.
- Iran's motive: financing outside the dollar system for transport, sanitation, digital and urban infrastructure while under sweeping US sanctions.
- India connection: the first NDB President was **K. V. Kamath** (India).

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (Groupings & agreements involving India / international institutions).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* Economy — "International Organisations: BRICS, NDB, CRA, AIIB". **Nudge:** update the NDB member list to include Uzbekistan and Colombia, and add a one-line contrast row: "NDB (Shanghai, BRICS-founded, equal founder shares) vs AIIB (Beijing, China ~26% voting, India 2nd largest shareholder)".

### 4. PM CARES Fund publishes delayed FY24 and FY25 audited accounts — ₹8,452 crore corpus, ₹87.85 lakh spent — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** The PM CARES Fund released its overdue audited receipts-and-payments statements, showing a closing balance of **₹8,452.07 crore** as on 31 March 2025 against expenditure of just **₹87.85 lakh** in FY 2024-25.

**Exam angle:** The classic GS2 accountability question — PM CARES is a **public charitable trust**, not a Consolidated Fund charge, so it sits outside **CAG audit** and, per the government's stand, outside the **RTI Act**, even though it uses state insignia and the PM is ex-officio Chairperson. Contrast it with the **PMNRF** (also a trust) and with the **Consolidated Fund / Contingency Fund / Public Account** trio. The 96%+ fall in spending against a growing corpus is the quotable data point for "transparency in extra-budgetary instruments".

**Key facts to memorise:**

- Closing balance **₹8,452.07 crore** (31 Mar 2025), up from **₹7,173.03 crore** a year earlier.
- FY25 expenditure **₹87.85 lakh**, down from **₹15.60 crore** in FY24; almost all of it (**₹87.84 lakh**) to the **PM CARES for Children** scheme.
- Composition: **₹7,846.65 crore** in fixed deposits with scheduled banks; **₹605.41 crore** in savings accounts.
- FY25 receipts: **₹479.05 crore** domestic donations + **₹92.83 lakh** foreign contributions (down from ₹681.81 crore and ₹1.13 crore).
- Structure: constituted **27–28 March 2020**; **PM is ex-officio Chairperson**; Ministers of **Defence, Home Affairs and Finance** are ex-officio Trustees; audited by an **independent chartered accountant**, not the CAG.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Transparency & accountability, statutory vs non-statutory bodies).

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth* — Ch. on **Comptroller and Auditor General** (Art. 148–151) and the **RTI/Transparency** chapter. **Nudge:** add a comparison strip — "PM CARES vs PMNRF vs National Defence Fund: legal form, audit authority, RTI applicability, FCRA treatment" — and cross-link to Art. 266/267 (Consolidated & Contingency Funds) so you can place it instantly in a Prelims statement question.

### 5. Ashok Dham stampede in Lakhisarai revives the NDMA crowd-management framework — Society / Disaster Management

**Headline:** Seven devotees were killed and about 14 injured in a stampede outside the **Ashok Dham temple in Lakhisarai, Bihar** on 17 August 2026 during the third *Somwari* of Shravan, after an electric pole collapse triggered a rumour of a live wire and a panic surge in the queues.

**Exam angle:** Religious mass gatherings are India's dominant stampede category, and the causal chain here is textbook NDMA: a **structural failure** (pole and bamboo barricade collapse) plus a **human/communication failure** (rumour, unannounced train drop-offs) inside a **capacity-blind venue**. The 2014 NDMA guide classifies crowd-disaster causes as structural failure, human error and natural causes — quote that classification and you have the skeleton of any answer on this.

**Key facts to memorise:**

- **17 August 2026**, ~6:30 am, near the **Ashok Dham halt railway station**, Lakhisarai district, Bihar; **7 dead**, ~**14 injured**.
- Trigger: an electric pole with cable fell on the men's queue → rumour of a live wire → panic pressure on the adjacent women's queue; barricading gave way. Crowd surge followed the arrival of **two trains between 6:30 and 6:45 am**.
- **₹4 lakh** ex gratia announced by the state.
- **NDMA guide, 2014:** *"Managing Crowd at Events and Venues of Mass Gathering"* — a guide for state governments, local authorities, administrators and organisers. Planning parameters: **type of event, expected crowd profile, crowd motives, venue characteristics, role of other stakeholders**. Causes classified as **structural failure, human error, natural causes**.
- Institutional frame: **Disaster Management Act, 2005**; NDMA chaired by the **Prime Minister**; SDMA by the **Chief Minister**; DDMA by the **District Collector/DM** (co-chaired by the elected district head).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Disaster Management; GS1 — Society (religious mass gatherings, urban/temporal congestion).

**Static anchor:** *Your Disaster Management notes* (NDMA guidelines set) + **DM Act, 2005** structure page. **Nudge:** create a one-page "Stampede" card — NDMA 2014 causal triad, carrying-capacity and unidirectional-flow principles, holding areas, and a running list of Indian stampede case studies with year, place and trigger; cross-link to Art. 25 (freedom of religion) only for the balancing argument, not as the main frame.

### C. Mains-Style Question

**GS3 — Disaster Management (250 words, 15 marks)**

> *"Stampedes at religious mass gatherings in India are administrative failures rather than accidents."* **Examine** this statement in the light of the NDMA's crowd-management framework, and suggest measures to institutionalise carrying-capacity-based crowd regulation.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the claim with evidence.** Open with the recurrence pattern — Lakhisarai (Aug 2026, 7 deaths) as the latest in a long series at temple and festival sites; establish that the immediate trigger (a falling pole, a rumour) is almost always trivial while the fatal variable is pre-existing crowd density and the absence of escape routes. State the position: the trigger is accidental, the vulnerability is manufactured.
2. **Locate the failure inside the NDMA framework.** Use the 2014 guide's triad — structural failure, human error, natural causes — and show that Lakhisarai combined the first two: barricade and pole collapse plus unannounced train drop-offs and rumour propagation. Add the planning parameters ignored: venue topography, expected crowd profile, crowd motive (religious, time-bound, non-substitutable), stakeholder coordination between Railways, police and temple trust.
3. **Identify the structural gaps.** No statutory carrying-capacity limit for religious venues; visual crowd estimation instead of density measurement; bidirectional flow in narrow corridors; ex-post ex gratia substituting for ex-ante regulation; DDMA capacity thin at district level despite the DM Act, 2005; fragmented ownership between temple trusts (often outside municipal building codes) and civil administration.
4. **Way forward — make it concrete.** Statutory carrying-capacity certification for venues above a threshold footfall; unidirectional movement with holding areas and metering of entry; CCTV plus density-analytics and drone-based early warning tied to a defined intervention threshold; synchronisation of transport arrivals with venue absorption capacity; mandatory pre-event joint plan under the DDMA with a named incident commander; anti-rumour communication via PA systems and local language SMS; and a national stampede database for evidence-based auditing. Close by tying carrying capacity to the shift from a reactive-relief to a prevention-and-mitigation paradigm under the DM Act, 2005 and the Sendai Framework's Priority 4 (preparedness for effective response).

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The 6th PIL was notified by the DDP, MoD, on 18 Aug 2026 with 405 items, of which 389 are DPSU-linked and 16 are for the Indian Coast Guard. Statement 3 is wrong: the PIL is an item-level import-embargo list; DAP 2020 continues to govern procurement categories.

**Q2 — (b) Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Odisha and West Bengal.** The PE-WII-MoEF&CC Regional Action Plan (Technical Report 2026) analyses long-term human-elephant conflict trends in these five states.

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Iran joined BRICS in the 2024 expansion but its NDB accession is still in process and unconfirmed by the bank; the NDB is headquartered in Shanghai with its first regional centre in Johannesburg; Uzbekistan and Colombia are among the non-founding members, alongside Bangladesh, UAE, Egypt and Algeria.

**Q4 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** Closing balance ₹8,452.07 crore on 31 Mar 2025 against FY25 expenditure of ₹87.85 lakh; the PM is ex-officio Chairperson. Statement 1 is wrong — PM CARES is a public charitable trust audited by an independent chartered accountant, not the CAG.

**Q5 — (a) Structural failure, human error and natural causes.** This is the three-way classification used in the NDMA's 2014 guide on managing crowds at events and venues of mass gathering.

**Q6 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** Elephant Reserves are administrative units notified by state governments under Project Elephant and have **no** independent statutory status under the WLPA, 1972 — unlike National Parks and Sanctuaries; hence statement 1 is wrong. The Nilgiri Elephant Reserve sits in the Nilgiri Biosphere landscape, and MIKE was mandated by a CITES CoP Resolution (CoP10, 1997).

**Q7 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Under the Disaster Management Act, 2005: NDMA is chaired by the Prime Minister, SDMA by the Chief Minister, and DDMA by the District Collector/DM (with the elected head of the district as co-chair). The NIDM was also constituted under the same Act.
