## PART A — BRIEFING (5 items)

### 1. NavIC can no longer provide standalone navigation — only three operational satellites left — Science & Technology

**Headline:** The government has confirmed that ISRO's NavIC constellation has fallen below the minimum four-satellite threshold and can no longer independently compute a user's position, after the last atomic clock aboard IRNSS-1F failed in March 2026.

**Exam angle:** This is the rare "negative" Sci-Tech story UPSC likes, because it forces you to know *why* four satellites are the minimum — a receiver solves for four unknowns (x, y, z and its own clock bias), so it needs four simultaneous ranging signals. It also revives the strategic origin story (post-Kargil 1999 denial of GPS access) and the atomic-clock reliability problem that has dogged IRNSS since IRNSS-1A. Expect a statement-based Prelims question mixing constellation design, coverage and service types.

**Facts to memorise:**
- NavIC = operational name of **IRNSS**, developed by ISRO; designed as a **7-satellite constellation — 3 in geostationary orbit + 4 in inclined geosynchronous orbit**; **11 satellites launched** so far including replacements.
- Coverage: Indian landmass **+ up to 1,500 km beyond India's borders** (a *regional*, not global, system).
- Two services: **Standard Positioning Service (SPS)** — civilian, open; **Restricted Service (RS)** — encrypted, for authorised strategic users.
- SPS signals are **interoperable** with GPS, GLONASS, Galileo and BeiDou — which is why phones saw no disruption.
- Now **only 3 operational satellites**; ≥4 needed for independent PNT. **NVS-03** is launch-ready; **NVS-04 and NVS-05** in advanced realisation.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Space).

**Static anchor:** *Shankar/Sci-Tech notes → "Indian Space Programme: Satellite Systems"* and your Physics-optional overlap on orbits. **Nudge:** add a one-page comparison table — GPS (USA, 24+, MEO) / GLONASS (Russia) / Galileo (EU) / BeiDou (China, global) / **NavIC (India, regional)** / **QZSS (Japan, regional)** — and cross-link it to the "geostationary vs geosynchronous vs polar/sun-synchronous orbit" page in your NCERT Geography Ch. on remote sensing.

### 2. India proposes the SHANTI framework, with the Bay of Bengal as its pilot theatre — International Relations

**Headline:** Alongside launching its candidature for a **UN Security Council non-permanent seat for the 2028–29 term**, India has proposed **SHANTI — Securing Holistic Advancement through Norms, Trust and Integrity** — a norms-based maritime governance framework, to be operationalised first in the Bay of Bengal before extension across the Indo-Pacific.

**Exam angle:** This completes a three-step doctrinal ladder that UPSC can test as a matching or sequencing question — **SAGAR (2015) → MAHASAGAR (2025) → SHANTI (2026)**. Analytically, the pitch is that the Bay of Bengal is a low-friction "cooperative maritime commons" (no insurmountable maritime boundary disputes, unlike the Western Indian Ocean), which makes it the right laboratory for a rules-based architecture. It is also a clean GS2 Mains hook on India as "preferred security partner and first responder".

**Facts to memorise:**
- **SAGAR** (2015) = *Security and Growth for All in the Region* — articulated the IOR vision. **MAHASAGAR** (2025) = *Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions* — widened the scope to the Indo-Pacific/Global South. **SHANTI** (2026) = the **operational, norms-based framework** for governing shared maritime commons.
- SHANTI is designed to work **through existing institutions** (BIMSTEC, Colombo Security Conclave, IFC-IOR) rather than create new ones — the "normative glue" argument.
- Rationale for Bay of Bengal: guards the **western approach to the Strait of Malacca**; China's push into the Bay (Kyaukphyu, Myanmar) to ease its **"Malacca Dilemma"**; convergence of NTS threats — cyclones/sea-level rise, **IUU fishing**, and trafficking routes overlapping the **Golden Triangle**.
- India's UNSC bid: **non-permanent seat, 2028–29 term**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India & the World / Indo-Pacific).

**Static anchor:** *Your IR notes → "India's Maritime Strategy / Indo-Pacific"* + BIMSTEC page. **Nudge:** append SHANTI as a third row to the SAGAR–MAHASAGAR table and cross-link it to the BIMSTEC page (mention the **BIMSTEC Master Plan for Transport Connectivity** and the **Maritime Transport Cooperation Agreement**) and to the IFC-IOR entry under Maritime Domain Awareness.

### 3. Supreme Court quashes the 2021 Office Memorandum on ex post facto environmental clearances — Environment

**Headline:** The Supreme Court has struck down the Centre's **2021 Office Memorandum** permitting retrospective environmental clearances, holding that **prior EC under the EIA Notification, 2006 is mandatory** and that any amnesty for already-started projects must come through a **statutory notification under Section 3 of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986** — not an administrative order.

**Exam angle:** A textbook GS3 environment-governance judgment with a GS2 administrative-law edge: an executive OM cannot create a "perpetual, parallel amnesty regime". The Court found the OM promoted a **"pollute and then pay"** approach, failing the tests of **proportionality and reasonable classification**, and thereby violating **Articles 14 and 21**. Note the nuance — the Court did *not* impose an absolute bar; it allowed a **"narrow, time-bound, reasoned and supervised window"** on grounds of supervening public interest for projects of immense public utility (hospitals, airports, strategic infrastructure). That balance — precautionary principle tempered by polluter-pays and sustainable development — is the quotable Mains line.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Struck down: the **2021 Office Memorandum**; the enabling statute for any future retrospective route is **Section 3, Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**.
- **Prior EC is mandatory** under the **EIA Notification, 2006** (issued under the EP Act, 1986) — before commencement of construction/operation.
- **Saving clause:** ECs already granted under the 2017 Notification and the 2021 OM **remain valid unless individually challenged**.
- Constitutional pegs: **Article 14** (equality) and **Article 21** (right to life, read to include a clean and healthy environment).
- Doctrinal shift: from an absolutist **precautionary principle** to balancing it with **polluter pays** and **sustainable development**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Environmental Governance); cross-tag GS2 for judicial review of executive instructions.

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS → "Environmental Impact Assessment" chapter*; **Laxmikanth → Ch. on Fundamental Rights (Art. 14, Art. 21)**. **Nudge:** in the Shankar EIA chapter, add a boxed timeline — *EIA Notification 1994 → 2006 → 2017 Notification → 2021 OM → 2026 SC verdict* — and cross-link it to the "Principles of Environmental Law" page (precautionary / polluter-pays / sustainable development) so the doctrinal shift sits next to the definitions.

### 4. Cabinet approves 'Samudra Manthan' — ₹84,084 crore National Offshore Exploration Scheme — Economy

**Headline:** The Union Cabinet on 31 July approved **'Samudra Manthan', the National Offshore Exploration Scheme** — a **Central Sector Scheme of the Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas** with an outlay of **₹84,084 crore up to FY 2030–31** — to accelerate deepwater and ultra-deepwater oil and gas exploration.

**Exam angle:** The classic energy-security question: India imports the overwhelming bulk of its crude, and offshore acreage is the least-explored frontier. Note the *scheme design* — the government part-funds the **high geological risk** of frontier exploration (seismic acquisition, exploratory drilling, common evacuation infrastructure), because private capital will not price that risk alone. Pair it with the sedimentary-basin categorisation and the OALP/HELP framework for a Prelims-plus-Mains combination.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **Central Sector Scheme** (100% Centre-funded), **Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas**; outlay **₹84,084 crore**, implementation **up to FY 2030–31**.
- Target: reserve accretion of **over 600 MMTOE** (Million Metric Tonnes of Oil Equivalent).
- Components: large-scale **seismic data acquisition/processing/interpretation**; **deepwater & ultra-deepwater exploratory drilling**; **scientific drilling in frontier basins**; **common offshore production and evacuation infrastructure**; an integrated **Oil & Gas Manufacturing and Services Zone**.
- Traces to the PM's **Independence Day 2025 (Red Fort)** call for a modern-day *Samudra Manthan*; builds on earlier upstream reforms — opening nearly the entire offshore acreage, and strengthening the **National Data Repository**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure & Energy).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal Pillar 4A/4B — Infrastructure & Energy Security*, plus the **HELP / OALP / Revenue-Sharing Contract** page. **Nudge:** add Samudra Manthan under "Upstream reforms" alongside HELP (2016), OALP and the **Oilfields (Regulation and Development) Amendment Act, 2024**, and cross-link to your Geography note on **Category I / II / III sedimentary basins** and the KG, Mumbai Offshore and Cauvery basins.

### 5. ILO adopts Convention No. 193 on platform work — India abstains — Society

**Headline:** The International Labour Conference has adopted **Convention No. 193, 'Decent Work in the Platform Economy'** — the **first legally binding international labour standard specific to the platform/gig economy** — by **406 votes in favour, 8 against and 36 abstentions, with India among the abstentions**.

**Exam angle:** Two distinct angles. First, the *institutional* one — the ILO's **tripartite** structure means each member state sends **four delegates** (two government, one employer, one worker), so a country's delegation can split its vote; that is exactly the kind of detail UPSC converts into a statement. Second, the *substantive* one — the Convention covers platform workers **whether or not they are in an employment relationship**, including the self-employed, and imposes transparency and review obligations on **algorithmic management**. India's abstention sits against the backdrop of the **Code on Social Security, 2020**, whose gig-worker provisions are still not in force.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **ILO C-193, 'Decent Work in the Platform Economy'** — first binding international standard for the platform economy; covers **both location-based and online** platform work; applies to employed **and** self-employed platform workers.
- Vote: **406 for, 8 against, 36 abstentions (India abstained)**; China, Brazil, Germany, France, South Africa and Japan voted in favour.
- Coverage: fundamental principles and rights at work, OSH, violence and harassment, remuneration, social security, **algorithmic/automated decision-making transparency and review**, data protection, suspension/deactivation/termination, migrants and refugees, access to justice.
- **ILO:** founded **1919** under the **Treaty of Versailles**; first specialised agency of the UN (**1946**); **187 members**; only **tripartite** UN agency; HQ **Geneva**. India a **founding member (1919)**.
- **India's gig economy (NITI Aayog):** **7.7 million** workers (2020) → projected **23.5 million (4.1% of the workforce) by 2029–30**; only about **15%** have any social security. Domestic instruments: **Code on Social Security, 2020** (aggregator contribution of **1–2% of annual turnover, capped at 5% of worker payouts**) — *not yet in force*; **e-Shram portal (2021)**.

**Notebook:** GS1/GS2 — Society & Governance (labour, social security); cross-tag GS3 Economy.

**Static anchor:** *Your Governance notes → "Labour Codes & Unorganised Sector"* and the **International Organisations** page. **Nudge:** on the ILO page, add a line listing India's ratification posture (India has ratified 6 of the 8 ILO core conventions — **not** C-87 Freedom of Association and **not** C-98 Right to Organise & Collective Bargaining) and cross-link it to the Code on Social Security entry so "India abstained on C-193" has a domestic explanation attached.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Environment, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *"Retrospective environmental clearance converts the precautionary principle into a transaction."* **Examine** the Supreme Court's 2026 verdict quashing the 2021 Office Memorandum on ex post facto environmental clearances, and **assess** whether a narrow statutory window for regularisation can be reconciled with the mandate of prior clearance under the EIA Notification, 2006.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the legal architecture (40–50 words).** Prior EC is mandatory under the EIA Notification, 2006, issued under Section 3 of the EP Act, 1986; its purpose is anticipatory — to assess impacts, hear public objections and prescribe safeguards *before* irreversible commitments are made. Ex post facto EC inverts this sequence.

2. **What the Court held (60–70 words).** The 2021 OM was quashed as an administrative instrument creating a perpetual, parallel amnesty regime; it embodied a "pollute and then pay" approach and failed the tests of proportionality and reasonable classification, offending Articles 14 and 21. Crucially, the Court located the *only* legitimate route for regularisation in a statutory notification under Section 3, EP Act, 1986 — a delegated-legislation route with parliamentary laying and public consultation, not an executive memo.

3. **The reconciliation argument (60–70 words).** The Court did not impose an absolute bar. Demolishing large, integrated public infrastructure imposes its own environmental cost — debris, embodied carbon, wasted resources — and hurts third-party public interest. Hence a "narrow, time-bound, reasoned and supervised window" limited to projects of immense public utility, with the precautionary principle balanced against polluter-pays and sustainable development. Existing clearances under the 2017 Notification and 2021 OM survive unless individually challenged, preserving legal certainty.

4. **Critical assessment and way forward (50–60 words).** Risk of moral hazard: any window, however narrow, is a signal that non-compliance is survivable — so the design must be self-liquidating (sunset clause), penalty-loaded (remediation cost + natural-resource damages), and independently audited. Strengthen prior compliance instead — credible EIA accreditation, real-time monitoring, and a functioning post-clearance compliance mechanism — so that regularisation stays the rare exception it was meant to be.

IRNSS has three satellites in geostationary and four satellites in geosynchronous orbits. 2. IRNSS covers entire India and about 5500 sq. km beyond its borders. 3.
