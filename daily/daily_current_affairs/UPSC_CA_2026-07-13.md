## PART A — BRIEFING (5 items)

### 1. China imposes a temporary ban on helium exports — a critical-gas squeeze that touches ISRO and India's chip push — International Relations
**Headline:** On **10 July 2026**, China's **Ministry of Commerce and the General Administration of Customs** banned helium exports with immediate effect, citing the renewed military conflict in West Asia (the Iran war) and the threat of fresh global helium shortages.
**Exam angle:** A textbook "weaponisation of supply chains / critical resource geopolitics" hook, sitting alongside China's earlier curbs on gallium, germanium and rare earths. India is a **net importer with virtually no domestic production**, so this is a clean India-relevance + strategic-autonomy line for both a Prelims fact and a Mains "critical resources & Atmanirbharta" argument.
**Key facts to memorize:** Ban announced **10 July 2026**, described as **temporary**. India imports almost all its helium; NE-Asia spot prices had already spiked to **$150–205 per thousand cubic feet in June 2026** (~double late-2025 levels). **Uses:** ISRO uses helium to **pressurise cryogenic/liquid fuel tanks** in PSLV/GSLV/LVM3; **semiconductor fabs** need it for cooling and inert atmospheres; also **MRI cryogenics** and welding. China itself **imports >80%** of its helium and produces only **~1.6%** of world supply — so the ban is defensive, to secure domestic stock. **Static:** helium is a **noble gas**, second-lightest element, **non-renewable** (accumulates from radioactive decay in natural-gas fields); **USA** (Federal Helium Reserve, Texas) and **Qatar** are the dominant suppliers.
**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (economic statecraft / export controls); GS3 — Sci-Tech & Economy (critical/strategic resources).
**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS / Vision "Critical Minerals & Strategic Resources" note.* Add a **"Helium"** card next to your gallium–germanium–rare-earths cards; cross-link to *China's export-control toolkit* and India's *Critical Minerals Mission / KABIL*.

### 2. World Bank approves a $890-million package for PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana — Environment
**Headline:** The **World Bank** approved a **US$890-million** financing package to scale up India's flagship residential rooftop-solar scheme, the **PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana**.
**Exam angle:** Marries a **scheme + a multilateral-finance instrument + India's climate targets** — high-yield for Prelims (who funds what, through which window) and a Mains "financing the energy transition" point. Note the **blended-finance structure** (loan + concessional loan + grant) and the private-capital multiplier.
**Key facts to memorize:** Package = **$820 m IBRD loan + $60 m concessional loan from the Clean Technology Fund + $10 m grant from the IBRD "Livable Planet" Fund** (= $890 m). Expected to **mobilise ~$4.2 billion** in private capital and support **~1.7 million jobs**. The scheme (launched **Feb 2024, Ministry of New & Renewable Energy**) targets **1 crore (10 million) households** with rooftop solar and up to **300 free units/month**. Ties to India's targets: **net-zero by 2070** and lifting non-fossil sources toward **~60% of electricity generation by 2035**.
**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment / Economy (renewable-energy transition & climate finance).
**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS "Renewable Energy / Solar" chapter.* Update your PM Surya Ghar card with the **World Bank $890 m** line; cross-link to **Panchamrit, National Solar Mission, International Solar Alliance (ISA)** and the IBRD-vs-IDA distinction.

### 3. Centre pulls the film *Satluj* off ZEE5 under Section 69A — an OTT free-speech flashpoint — Polity & Governance
**Headline:** On **5 July 2026**, two days after its premiere, the film ***Satluj*** (originally *Punjab '95*, directed by Honey Trehan, starring Diljit Dosanjh, on human-rights activist **Jaswant Singh Khalra**) was taken down from ZEE5's India catalogue after **MIB** invoked **Section 69A of the IT Act, 2000** citing "security concerns."
**Exam angle:** A live **Article 19(1)(a) vs 19(2) reasonable-restrictions** case plus the **CBFC-vs-OTT regulatory gap**. Excellent for a Prelims "grounds under Section 69A" factual and a Mains "creative freedom, censorship and the State" balance question.
**Key facts to memorize:** Takedown under **Section 69A**; matter referred to an **Inter-Departmental Committee under Rule 14 of the IT Rules, 2021**; film still streams via **ZEE5 Global** abroad. **Section 69A grounds:** sovereignty & integrity of India, defence, security of the State, friendly relations with foreign states, public order, or preventing incitement to a cognisable offence — upheld in ***Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015)*** because of procedural safeguards. **OTT content needs no CBFC certification** (CBFC works under the **Cinematograph Act, 1952**); OTT is instead governed by **Part III of the IT Rules, 2021** (three-tier self-regulation), whose *Code of Ethics* was **stayed by the Bombay HC in 2021**.
**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Fundamental Rights, Art 19) / Governance (IT Act & digital-media regulation).
**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth "Fundamental Rights" (Art 19) chapter.* Add a margin note: **Section 69A + Shreya Singhal (2015) + IT Rules 2021 three-tier structure**; cross-link CBFC/Cinematograph Act 1952 vs OTT jurisdiction.

### 4. IITM Pune produces India's first observational estimate of monsoon raindrop evaporation (~25%) — Geography
**Headline:** Scientists at the **Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM), Pune** published the **first observational estimate of sub-cloud raindrop evaporation over the Western Ghats** — on average **~25% of rain mass evaporates mid-air** before reaching the ground during the southwest monsoon.
**Exam angle:** A rare, concrete "geophysical phenomenon + Indian research institution" item that upgrades a static monsoon topic with a fresh data point — ideal for a Prelims statement-based question and a Mains monsoon-dynamics line.
**Key facts to memorize:** Study led by **Saikat Sengupta**, published in ***Atmospheric Chemistry and Physics***; average **~25% evaporation, daily range 4–61%** (June–Sept). Method = **stable-isotope technique** (surviving drops enrich in heavy **¹⁸O / ²H** as lighter molecules escape first), read by **laser spectrometer** and fed into a **Below Cloud Interaction Model (BCIM)**; data from the **2019 monsoon** in Pune. Global comparison: **Tropics ~20%, Zurich ~40%, Barbados ~60%**. IITM is under the **Ministry of Earth Sciences**; the SW monsoon supplies **~75–80% of India's annual rainfall**. Evaporative cooling drives **cold pools** that trigger fresh convective rain — so getting it wrong skews weather/climate models.
**Notebook:** GS1 — Geography (climatology / important geophysical phenomena); GS3 — Sci-Tech.
**Static anchor:** *NCERT (Class XI) "Climate" / GC Leong monsoon mechanism.* Cross-link to **ITCZ, orographic rainfall, Western Ghats windward-leeward, monsoon variability**; drop in the **~25% sub-cloud evaporation** figure as a modern factoid.

### 5. RBI launches its July 2026 round of household surveys ahead of the August MPC — Economy
**Headline:** The **RBI** launched the **July 2026 round of three household surveys** — the **Inflation Expectations Survey of Households (IESH)**, the **Rural Consumer Confidence Survey (RCCS)** and the **Urban Consumer Confidence Survey (UCCS)** — to feed inputs into the **August Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)** meeting.
**Exam angle:** UPSC repeatedly tests *which* surveys inform monetary policy and *who* conducts them. Clean Prelims "match the survey" fodder and a Mains "data inputs to inflation targeting" line.
**Key facts to memorize:** **IESH** — qualitative + quantitative household expectations on current, 3-month-ahead and 1-year-ahead inflation, across **19 cities**. **UCCS** — sentiment on the general economic situation, jobs, prices, income and spending, **19 cities** (Ahmedabad, Bengaluru, Chandigarh, Chennai, Delhi, Mumbai, Thiruvananthapuram, etc.). **RCCS** — rural/semi-urban perceptions & one-year expectations, across **31 states/UTs**. These are **bi-monthly** rounds. Anchor them to the **MPC** (6 members, 3 RBI + 3 government-nominated; **flexible inflation targeting at 4% ±2%**).
**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (monetary policy / RBI).
**Static anchor:** *Mrunal "Monetary Policy & RBI" pillar.* Cross-link the survey names to the **MPC framework** and RBI's other regular surveys (**OBICUS, Bank Lending Survey, Survey of Professional Forecasters**).

### Mains-style question

**Q8 (GS3, 250 words / 15 marks).** "Access to critical minerals and strategic gases has become a frontier of economic statecraft." In light of recent export restrictions on materials such as helium and rare earths, **examine** the vulnerabilities in India's supply chains and **suggest** measures to build strategic autonomy.

*Model-answer skeleton:*
- **Intro:** Define economic statecraft / weaponisation of supply chains; note China's 2023–26 curbs (gallium, germanium, rare earths, and now helium, July 2026).
- **Vulnerabilities:** Near-total import dependence for helium and heavy rare earths; concentration risk (single-supplier); downstream exposure of ISRO's launch programme, semiconductor fabs, EVs and defence; price volatility ($150–205/mcf helium spike).

- **Conclusion:** Strategic autonomy = diversification + domestic capacity + circular economy, aligned with Atmanirbhar Bharat.

## PART C — ANSWERS

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** China's ban was temporary and shortage-driven; India imports almost all its helium (statement 2 is wrong); ISRO and chip fabs are major users.

**Q2 — (b) PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana.** The $890 m ($820 m IBRD + $60 m Clean Technology Fund + $10 m grant) backs the residential rooftop-solar scheme.

**Q3 — (a) 1, 2 and 3 only.** Section 69A grounds are sovereignty/integrity, defence, security of State, friendly foreign relations, public order and preventing incitement — *not* protecting intermediaries' commercial interests.

**Q4 — (c) Both 1 and 2.** ~25% average evaporation; drops that evaporate more are enriched in heavy isotopes (lighter molecules escape first) — the principle behind the isotope method.

**Q5 — (c) Reserve Bank of India — to provide inputs for monetary policy.** IESH, RCCS and UCCS are RBI surveys feeding the MPC.

**Q6 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three statements about the SW monsoon are correct.

**Q7 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The MPC is statutory (RBI Act 1934, 2016 amendment), has six members (3 RBI + 3 govt-nominated), with a 4% ±2% CPI target.
