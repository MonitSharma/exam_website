## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. DRI busts a ₹2,500-crore SAFTA fraud in areca-nut imports — Economy

**Headline:** The Directorate of Revenue Intelligence has dismantled a syndicate that routed South-East Asian areca nuts through Bangladesh, obtained fraudulent Certificates of Origin, and claimed the full SAFTA duty exemption on an import line that otherwise attracts **100% Basic Customs Duty**.

**Exam angle:** This is the single cleanest real-world illustration of **trade deflection** — a non-member's goods being pushed through a member country purely to capture preferential tariffs — and therefore of why every trade agreement carries **Rules of Origin**. It also connects the enforcement machinery (DRI, CAROTAR 2020, Section 28DA of the Customs Act) to the policy debate on whether India's FTAs are being gamed. Note the Prelims trap that examiners love: **SAFTA is a free trade area, not a customs union** — members keep their own external tariffs, which is exactly why Rules of Origin become necessary.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Duty evaded: **over ₹2,500 crore** in recent years. Seizures: **~160 MT** of areca nuts and **₹75 lakh** cash; **9 arrests**; the customs broker's licence stands suspended.
- Origin countries actually involved: **Indonesia, Thailand, Malaysia**; consignments were routed through a **Bangladeshi export processing zone**, with containers and bags swapped before shipment to India.
- **BCD on areca nut = 100%**; SAFTA-eligible Bangladeshi-origin imports = **full exemption**.
- **SAFTA**: signed **2004** (12th SAARC Summit, Islamabad), in force **1 January 2006**; **8 members** (Afghanistan acceded 2011); covers **goods only** — services are handled separately under **SATIS (2010)**.
- **CAROTAR, 2020** — Customs (Administration of Rules of Origin under Trade Agreements) Rules — notified **21 August 2020**, in force **21 September 2020**, resting on **Section 28DA** of the Customs Act, 1962 (inserted by the Finance Act, 2020). It shifts the burden onto the *importer* to possess origin-related information, not merely a certificate.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (External Sector); secondary tag GS2 — IR (SAARC).

**Static anchor:** **Mrunal — External Sector pillar (BoP & Trade Agreements)**, plus Ramesh Singh's chapter on External Sector. *Update:* under your PTA → FTA → Customs Union → Common Market → Economic Union ladder, add a one-line rider — *"only a customs union has a common external tariff; every stage below it needs Rules of Origin to stop trade deflection"* — and cross-link a fresh CAROTAR 2020 / Section 28DA entry to your SAARC page in the IR notebook.

### 2. Supreme Court refuses to move the SIR base year for Sikkim — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** On **17 August 2026**, a bench of **CJI Surya Kant** with Justices **Joymalya Bagchi** and **V. Mohana** dismissed a PIL by the *Sikkimese Mulniwasi Surakacha Sangh* that sought **1993** instead of **2002** as the base year for the ongoing Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls in Sikkim.

**Exam angle:** Two threads worth separating. First, the **uniformity principle** — the Court's line, *"The goal post cannot be shifted in the middle of the exercise; 2002 has been kept as the base year for the entire country"* — is a statement about administrative consistency, not about the merits of 2002. Second, this is another data point on the **scope of judicial review over ECI's Article 324 powers**: courts have consistently declined to second-guess the ECI's *methodological* choices while remaining willing to police the *consequences* of those choices (compare the July 2026 ruling that SIR outcomes cannot travel outside electoral-roll revision to determine citizenship or welfare entitlements).

**Facts to memorise:**

- Petitioner's argument, rejected: Sikkim's demographic profile differs from other States and its population data does not correspond with its electoral data.
- **Article 324** — superintendence, direction and control of elections vests in the ECI. **Article 326** — adult suffrage. Electoral-roll preparation and revision sit in the **Representation of the People Act, 1950** (Sections 21–23), *not* the RPA 1951 (which governs the conduct of elections and disqualifications).
- Revision types: **intensive**, **summary**, and **partly intensive-partly summary** — an SIR is the house-to-house, document-backed variety.
- Sikkim-specific static: **Article 371F**, inserted by the **Constitution (Thirty-sixth Amendment) Act, 1975**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Elections / ECI).

**Static anchor:** **Laxmikanth — "Election Commission"** chapter, read alongside the "Elections" chapter. *Update:* add a three-row box distinguishing **RPA 1950 vs RPA 1951**, and a running "SIR litigation tracker" line linking the July 2026 ruling (SIR data cannot determine citizenship) with this one (SIR methodology is not for courts to redesign mid-exercise). Cross-link **Article 371F** in your special-provisions table.

### 3. 'Tigers Outside Tiger Reserves' moves from launch to money — Environment

**Headline:** The **₹88.70 crore** sanctioned outlay for the MoEFCC–NTCA **Tigers Outside Tiger Reserves (TOTR)** initiative has been fully released for conservation work in **40 forest divisions across nine States**, as disclosed at the recent meeting of the **Standing Committee of the National Board for Wildlife**.

**Exam angle:** *What is new here is the disbursal and the 40-division / 9-State footprint — the initiative itself was rolled out earlier.* TOTR is the policy answer to a structural fact: with **35–40% of India's tigers now living outside notified Tiger Reserves**, the protected-area model alone can no longer carry tiger conservation. This is a textbook shift from **site-based** to **landscape-level** conservation, and it is the best current hook for a Mains argument on human–wildlife conflict, corridor fragmentation, and coexistence economics in multiple-use forests.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **Nine States**: Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Assam. **40 forest divisions** in Phase 1.
- **All India Tiger Estimation 2022**: **3,682 tigers** (mean estimate) — India holds roughly **70%** of the world's wild tigers.
- Tech stack: **CaTRAT** (Camera Trap Data Repository and Analysis Tool — automated species classification) and **ExtractCompare** (individual identification from stripe patterns). India's camera-trap survey holds the **Guinness World Record** for the largest of its kind.
- Institutional static: **Project Tiger** launched **1 April 1973**. **NTCA** is a **statutory** body under **Section 38L** of the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972 (inserted by the 2006 Amendment), chaired by the **Environment Minister**. A Tiger Reserve is notified under **Section 38V**. The **NBWL** sits under **Section 5A**, chaired by the **Prime Minister**; its **Standing Committee** is chaired by the **Environment Minister**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Ecology (Conservation).

**Static anchor:** **Shankar IAS Environment — "Conservation Efforts / Project Tiger & NTCA"**. *Update:* add TOTR beside Project Tiger as the "outside-the-reserve" arm, and draw a cross-link to your **Eco-Sensitive Zones**, **wildlife corridors** and **human–wildlife conflict** pages. ### 4. Maharashtra's earthquake swarm — 29 tremors in 16 days, no mainshock — Geography

**Headline:** Between **30 July and 14 August 2026**, **29 tremors** were recorded in Maharashtra — **24 in and around Nashik**, **4 in Palghar** and **1 in Nagpur** — with magnitudes of **2.0 to 4.3** at shallow depths of about **5–8 km**, a pattern seismologists classify as an **earthquake swarm**.

**Exam angle:** The defining feature of a swarm is the **absence of a dominant mainshock** — it is not the mainshock–aftershock sequence of a conventional earthquake. Learn the causal menu (**pore-fluid pressure diffusion**, magma intrusion in volcanic settings, **slow-slip events**, stress on pre-existing faults) because Prelims tests mechanism, not location. The monsoon link is the interesting one: rainwater percolating into fractured Deccan basalt raises pore pressure, effectively "lubricating" faults into small slips. And note the standard trap — **a swarm does not imply a larger earthquake is coming**; swarm activity has no established predictive value.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Largest event: **M 4.3 at Nashik on 8 August 2026**, depth ~**5 km**.
- **Nashik and Palghar fall in Seismic Zone III** (moderate risk) under **IS 1893 (Part 1)**. India is divided into **four zones — II, III, IV and V** — with **Zone V** the most severe; there is no Zone I in the current classification.
- Maharashtra's swarm history: **Palghar/Dahanu** (2018–19) and **Nanded**.
- Peninsular precedents worth pairing: **Koyna, 11 December 1967 (M 6.5)** — the classic case of **reservoir-induced seismicity**; **Latur/Killari, 30 September 1993** — a damaging intraplate quake in what was then treated as a stable shield region.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Geography (Geomorphology / Physical Geography).

**Static anchor:** **NCERT Class XI *Fundamentals of Physical Geography*, Ch. 3 "Interior of the Earth"** (earthquakes, focus vs epicentre, P/S waves, shadow zones). *Update:* add a labelled **seismic zone map of India** with Nashik–Palghar marked, and a small table — *swarm vs foreshock–mainshock–aftershock vs induced seismicity* — cross-linked to **Koyna** in your dams-and-reservoirs note.

### 5. NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope heads for L2 on 30 August — Science & Technology

**Headline:** The **Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope** is set to launch on **30 August 2026** aboard a **SpaceX Falcon Heavy** from Kennedy Space Center, bound for a halo orbit around the **Sun–Earth L2 point**, about **1.5 million km** from Earth.

**Exam angle:** The mission's value for you is comparative. Roman carries **the same 2.4 m primary mirror as Hubble** but a field of view roughly **100 times larger** — a survey telescope rather than a pointing telescope, which is precisely what dark-energy cosmology and **gravitational microlensing** exoplanet searches require. Its **Coronagraph Instrument** is a technology demonstration for directly imaging exoplanets by suppressing starlight. For a Physics-optional candidate, the L2 geometry and the microlensing mechanism are free marks; for Prelims, the L1-vs-L2 mission mapping is the standard question format.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Named for **Nancy Grace Roman**, NASA's **first Chief Astronomer**, called the "Mother of Hubble". Primary mission ~**5 years**.
- **Wide Field Instrument (WFI)**: **300.8-megapixel** visible + near-infrared camera; FoV ~**100× Hubble's**. **Coronagraph Instrument (CGI)**: high-contrast starlight suppression.
- Science goals: **dark energy** and the expansion history of the universe, growth of cosmic structure/**dark matter**, **exoplanets via microlensing** (expected to find **1,000+**), galaxy evolution.
- **Lagrange points**: five per two-body system. **L1, L2, L3 are unstable** (need station-keeping); **L4 and L5 are stable** (Trojan asteroids sit there). **L2 residents**: JWST, Gaia, Euclid, and now Roman. **L1 residents**: SOHO and **India's Aditya-L1**.
- **Prelims trap:** L2 is **not** a zero-gravity point — it is a point where the combined Sun–Earth gravitational pull supplies exactly the centripetal force for a spacecraft to keep the same orbital period as Earth.
- India cross-reference: **Aditya-L1**, launched on **PSLV-C57** (September 2023), inserted into a halo orbit around **L1** on **6 January 2024**, carrying **7 payloads** with **VELC** as the lead instrument.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Space); doubles as Physics-optional revision.

**Static anchor:** Your **Sci-Tech "Space Missions" table** plus the Physics-optional **Classical Mechanics — restricted three-body problem** note. *Update:* add a two-column **L1 vs L2** table (what each is good for; who lives there) and a one-line derivation reminder of why L4/L5 are stable while L1–L3 are not. Cross-link **gravitational microlensing** to your **gravitational lensing / GR** page.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Economy, 250 words, 15 marks)

> India's preferential trade arrangements are only as strong as the origin regime that polices them. In the light of recent enforcement action against the misuse of SAFTA concessions, **examine** the vulnerabilities in India's rules-of-origin framework and suggest measures to protect the revenue and domestic industry without eroding the trade-facilitation objectives of its trade agreements.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the problem.** A free trade area, unlike a customs union, has no common external tariff — so goods enter through the member with the lowest duty and are re-exported inward. Rules of Origin (value addition, change in tariff heading, wholly-obtained criteria) are the only firewall. Anchor with the August 2026 DRI case: ₹2,500 crore-plus evaded, 100% BCD circumvented via a Bangladeshi EPZ and fraudulent Certificates of Origin.
2. **Diagnose the vulnerabilities.** Certificates issued by partner-country agencies over which Indian customs has no control; minimal-processing and EPZ trans-shipment loopholes; asymmetry between a high-tariff sensitive item (areca nut) and a zero-duty preferential channel; verification requests that go unanswered within timelines; capacity limits in post-clearance audit; and the political economy of not appearing to obstruct an FTA partner.
3. **Suggest calibrated remedies.** Deepen CAROTAR/Section 28DA enforcement through risk-based verification and data analytics rather than blanket suspicion; negotiate stricter product-specific rules for high-tariff sensitive lines; push for electronic origin data exchange and mutual-recognition of verification protocols; strengthen the sensitive list and use trade-remedy instruments (safeguards, anti-circumvention inquiries); build DRI–customs–DGFT intelligence integration; and keep legitimate importers whole via trusted-trader/AEO channels so that facilitation is not the casualty.
4. **Conclude on balance.** The objective is not fewer FTAs but credible ones: origin integrity is what makes preferential liberalisation politically sustainable at home and reciprocally valuable abroad.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (b).** Only statement 3 is correct. SAFTA came into force on 1 January 2006 but covers **goods only**; services are dealt with separately under SATIS (2010), so 1 is wrong. SAFTA is a **free trade area**, not a customs union — members retain independent external tariffs, so 2 is wrong. Precisely because there is no common external tariff, Rules of Origin are needed to stop **trade deflection**, so 3 is correct.

**Q2 — (c).** Statements 1 and 3 are correct. The PIL sought 1993 in place of 2002, and the bench held that the base year had been kept uniform nationally and the "goal post cannot be shifted in the middle of the exercise." Statement 2 is wrong: **preparation and revision of electoral rolls falls under the Representation of the People Act, 1950**; the RPA 1951 governs the actual conduct of elections, corrupt practices and disqualifications.

**Q3 — (b).** TOTR is an MoEFCC–NTCA initiative addressing the 35–40% of India's tigers that live **outside notified Tiger Reserves**, with ₹88.70 crore released for 40 forest divisions across nine States. It is neither a transboundary World Bank programme, nor a translocation scheme, nor a compensation mechanism.

**Q4 — (a).** Only statement 1 is correct. A swarm is defined by the **absence of a dominant mainshock**. Statement 2 is wrong — swarm occurrence has **no established predictive value** for a larger earthquake. Statement 3 is wrong — Nashik and Palghar are in **Zone III** (moderate), not Zone V.

**Q5 — (b).** Roman is a **wide-field survey telescope** for dark energy, cosmic structure and **microlensing** exoplanet detection, stationed at **Sun–Earth L2**. Option (a) describes Aditya-L1/SOHO-type solar missions at L1; (c) describes LISA; (d) describes a CMB mission such as Planck.

**Q6 — (a).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: CAROTAR 2020 flows from **Section 28DA of the Customs Act, 1962**, inserted by the Finance Act, 2020, and shifts the onus onto the **importer** to hold origin-related information rather than merely present a certificate. Statement 3 is wrong — the rules apply to **all trade agreements** under which a preferential rate is claimed, including PTAs and comprehensive economic agreements.

**Q7 — (b).** Pairs 1 and 2 are correct — **Aditya-L1 orbits Sun–Earth L1** and **JWST orbits Sun–Earth L2**. Pair 3 is wrong: **SOHO is stationed at L1**, not L2, since a solar observatory must sit between the Sun and the Earth.
