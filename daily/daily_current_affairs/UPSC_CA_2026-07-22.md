## BRIEFING

### 1. Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026 — sanctioned strength rises to 38 — Polity & Governance
**Exam angle:** Classic Prelims + GS2 judiciary hook. Parliament, not a constitutional amendment, decides the number of SC judges — the Bill replaces an ordinance and needs only a **simple majority** because Article 124(1) empowers Parliament to fix the number "by law." Ties into pendency/backlog and judicial reform Mains debates.
**Key facts to memorize:** Introduced in Lok Sabha by Law Minister Arjun Ram Meghwal (20 July 2026). Raises judges **excluding the CJI from 33 → 37**, i.e. total sanctioned strength **34 → 38 (including CJI)**. Constitutional basis: **Article 124(1)**. Previous hike (31→34) was in 2019.
**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity/Judiciary.
**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth,* "Supreme Court" chapter → update the sanctioned-strength figure to **38** and cross-link Article 124 (composition) with the 2019 amendment note.

### 2. India approves its first dengue vaccine — QDENGA (TAK-003) — Science & Technology
**Exam angle:** High-yield Sci-Tech/health item — India carries a huge dengue burden, and this is the *first* approved dengue vaccine in the country. Note "live-attenuated **tetravalent**" (covers all 4 serotypes) and that it needs **no pre-vaccination screening**, unlike the earlier global Dengvaxia.
**Key facts to memorize:** DCGI/CDSCO market authorisation granted **20 July 2026** (Form CT-20) to **Takeda's QDENGA**. For ages **4–60**, given as **two doses three months apart**, regardless of prior dengue exposure. Dengue is caused by a **flavivirus** spread by *Aedes aegypti*.
**Notebook:** GS3 — Sci-Tech (Biotech/Health).
**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment / Sci-Tech notes* → add QDENGA under "Vaccines & Diseases"; cross-link with **National Dengue Day (16 May)** and vector-borne disease control.

### 3. India–Spain to fast-track UPI–Bizum interoperability — International Relations
**Exam angle:** Digital Public Infrastructure as soft power / economic diplomacy — a recurring theme. **Bizum** is Spain's domestic instant-payment platform; linking it to **UPI** would let Indian travellers pay via QR in Spain. Good "India's DPI goes global" data point for GS2/GS3.
**Key facts to memorize:** Announced by Commerce Ministry **20 July 2026** during Minister Piyush Goyal's Europe visit (Spain, Belgium, Finland). Still **early-stage technical talks** — no go-live date. UPI is already live in ~**9–10 countries** (Singapore, UAE, Bhutan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, France, Mauritius, Qatar, Cambodia; **Greece** the latest). NPCI International (NIPL) drives these tie-ups.
**Notebook:** GS2 — IR (bilateral/economic diplomacy); overlaps GS3 Economy.
**Static anchor:** *Mrunal Economy — "Payment & Settlement / BoP" pillar* → maintain a running "UPI-abroad countries" list; cross-link with **Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007** and NPCI.

### 4. Madhya Pradesh Cabinet clears the Uniform Civil Code Bill, 2026 — Society
**Exam angle:** UCC is a perennial GS1 (society/secularism) + GS2 (DPSP, Article 44) favourite. MP moves to become the **second state** (after **Uttarakhand**) toward a UCC. Note the tribal exclusion — the friction point between UCC and Sixth Schedule / customary-law protections.
**Key facts to memorize:** Draft cleared **19 July 2026**, tabled in the Monsoon Session Assembly. Drafting committee headed by **Justice (Retd.) Ranjana Prakash Desai** (same jurist who led Uttarakhand's UCC panel). **Tribal communities excluded.** Provisions: bar on polygamy, criminalising triple talaq/nikah halala, mandatory marriage registration, marriage age 21 (men)/18 (women). Constitutional basis: **Article 44 (DPSP, Part IV)**.
**Notebook:** GS1 — Society (personal laws/secularism); GS2 — Polity (DPSP).
**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth,* "Directive Principles" chapter → tag **Article 44** with a live example note (Uttarakhand → MP); cross-link **Article 37** (DPSPs non-justiciable).

### 5. India's Bioeconomy Roadmap 2035 & ₹50,000-cr BioEconomy Growth Fund — Economy
**Exam angle:** Ties the **BioE3 policy** to real money and targets — good for GS3 (economy/sci-tech) "sunrise sector" answers. The fund is designed to cross the **"valley of death"** between lab research and commercial biomanufacturing.
**Key facts to memorize:** NITI Aayog roadmap targets growing the bioeconomy from **~$195 bn (2025) → $691 bn by 2035** (and $2.6 tn by 2047). **BioEconomy Growth Fund (2026–35) of ₹50,000 crore**, embedded within the ₹1-lakh-crore **RDI (Research, Development & Innovation) Scheme**. **BioE3 = Biotechnology for Economy, Environment & Employment** (approved 2024).
**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy / Sci-Tech (biotechnology).
**Static anchor:** *Mrunal Economy — "Sectors of Economy / Industrial policy" pillar* → add a BioE3 + Bioeconomy Roadmap card; cross-link with the **RDI Scheme** and "valley of death"/VGF concept.

### Mains-Style Question

**Q.** "The enactment of Uniform Civil Codes by individual states marks a shift from a national to a sub-national approach to a Directive Principle." Examine the constitutional and social implications of this trend. *(15 marks, 250 words)*

*Model-answer skeleton:*
- **Intro:** UCC under Article 44 (DPSP, Part IV, non-justiciable per Article 37); Uttarakhand followed by MP's 2026 Bill.
- **Constitutional dimension:** Personal laws sit in the **Concurrent List (Entry 5)**, so states can legislate; but risks a patchwork of "uniform" codes that are non-uniform across states, and possible conflict with central personal laws → repugnancy under Article 254.
- **Social dimension:** Advances gender justice (bar on polygamy, triple talaq) and equality; but tribal exclusion, minority apprehensions, and federalism-vs-secularism tensions.
- **Way forward:** Consultative, gradual reform (Law Commission's stance), codify commonalities first, protect Sixth Schedule/customary autonomy; balance Article 44 with Articles 25–26.

## ANSWERS

**Q1 — (b).** Strength rises to 38 including the CJI (37 excluding CJI), and the power flows from **Article 124** — so it needs only a simple-majority ordinary law, not a constitutional amendment. Statement 2 is wrong.

**Q2 — (b).** QDENGA (Takeda's TAK-003) is India's first approved dengue vaccine (DCGI, 20 July 2026); live-attenuated tetravalent, ages 4–60.

**Q3 — (c).** Bizum is Spain's domestic instant mobile-payment platform; India and Spain are advancing UPI–Bizum interoperability talks.

**Q4 — (b).** MP would be the **second** state (after Uttarakhand), so statement 1 is wrong. The draft excludes tribal communities, and UCC is a DPSP (Article 44).

**Q5 — (a).** The ₹50,000-crore BioEconomy Growth Fund (2026–35) sits within the ₹1-lakh-crore RDI Scheme to bridge the lab-to-market "valley of death."

**Q6 — (c).** All three pairs are correct — Article 44 (UCC), Article 48 (agriculture/animal husbandry), Article 50 (separation of judiciary from executive), all DPSPs.

**Q7 — (c).** Both correct — NPCI is a not-for-profit umbrella entity operating UPI; RBI regulates payment/settlement systems under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007.
