## Part A — Briefing

### 1. India backs a "fair PABS" at the UN pandemic meeting; the Pandemic Agreement still cannot open for signature — International Relations
**Exam angle:** At the UN High-Level Meeting on pandemic prevention, preparedness and response (New York, 25 Sep 2026), India (represented by the Union Health Secretary) said a fair Pathogen Access and Benefit-Sharing (PABS) system is central to the Agreement's credibility and that the political declaration should not prejudge the ongoing PABS talks. The fault line is North–South: high-income countries resist mandatory technology transfer, while developing countries want guaranteed access to vaccines and diagnostics in return for sharing pathogen data. India stresses national sovereignty, domestic financing and digital public infrastructure as a global public good.
**Key facts:** WHO Pandemic Agreement adopted by the WHA on 20 May 2025 (124 in favour, 0 against, 11 abstentions). Article 12 creates PABS via an annex; the Agreement opens for signature only once the annex is adopted (negotiated in an Intergovernmental Working Group). PABS is modelled on the Nagoya Protocol (prior informed consent, mutually agreed terms, benefit sharing). India's COVID-19 record: about 300 million vaccine doses to about 100 countries.
**Notebook:** GS2 – International Relations (global health governance).
**Static anchor:** Mrunal/Shankar IAS – International Organisations (WHO); Environment – CBD & Nagoya Protocol. *Nudge:* add a cross-link from the WHO page to the Nagoya Protocol/ABS note and to the Biological Diversity Act, 2002.

### 2. Wildlife Week recoveries: dugong reserve, snow leopard safari, vultures and lions — Environment
**Exam angle:** An editorial at the end of Wildlife Week (2–8 Oct) collects India's species-recovery data, a handy case-study bank for Mains. Key policy hook: the Integrated Development of Wildlife Habitats (IDWH) scheme funds recovery programmes for 24 critically endangered species. Bottlenecks: funding skewed to charismatic megafauna, power lines as a leading cause of Great Indian Bustard deaths, and poor assessment data for reptiles, amphibians and invertebrates.
**Key facts:** Asiatic lion 674 (2020) → 891 (2025). Madhya Pradesh vultures 8,397 (2019) → 12,981 (2025). Nilgiri tahr 1,303 (2025) → 1,364 (2026). Dugong: about 270 (158 Palk Bay, 112 Gulf of Mannar; 2026 WII assessment) in a 448.34 sq km Conservation Reserve in Palk Bay; Schedule I of WPA 1972, IUCN Vulnerable. Snow leopard: 718 (first national assessment, 2024), 477 of them in Ladakh. Ladakh has launched India's first high-altitude snow leopard safari (Rumbak, Shang, Uley). *(Figures per The Hindu / Insights digests, 8 Oct.)*
**Notebook:** GS3 – Environment & Biodiversity.
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS – Biodiversity: Conservation (in situ/ex situ, species recovery programmes); Wildlife Protection Act schedules. *Nudge:* update the species-population table (lion, vulture, tahr, dugong, snow leopard) with the new numbers.

### 3. Nobel Prize in Chemistry 2026: Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai for chirality and asymmetric catalysis — Science & Technology
**Exam angle:** The prize recognises how small imbalances between mirror-image (left/right-handed) molecules can be amplified, relevant to single-enantiomer drugs (one mirror form may be therapeutic, the other harmful) and to the long-standing puzzle of biological homochirality. Prelims-style questions usually ask "what is the discovery about", not the laureates alone.
**Key facts:** Henri B. Kagan (France; non-linear effects, mid-1980s) and Kenso Soai (Japan; Soai reaction, 1995, an asymmetric autocatalytic reaction). Prize money 12 million SEK, shared. Link to the Physics Nobel 2026 (Halzen, IceCube) covered on 7 Oct.
**Notebook:** GS3 – Science & Technology (basic science, pharma).
**Static anchor:** NCERT Class 11/12 Chemistry – Optical isomerism/stereochemistry. *Nudge:* add a "Nobel 2026" box listing Physics and Chemistry with one-line explainers.

### 4. Leprosy: 91,783 new cases in 2025-26, far above projections — Society (Health)
**Exam angle:** India eliminated leprosy as a public health problem in 2005 (national prevalence below 1 per 10,000), yet transmission persists in hotspots. The Hindu reports 91,783 new cases in 2025-26, about 41% above the projected 65,000, 114 districts still above the elimination threshold, and the highest prevalence in Chhattisgarh (1.66 per 10,000). The response toolkit: case-detection campaigns, contact tracing and single-dose rifampicin post-exposure prophylaxis (SDR-PEP; coverage 91.2% of about 16.94 lakh contacts), plus stigma reduction.
**Key facts:** 91,783 cases; prevalence 0.56 per 10,000 (Mar 2026); five priority states: Chhattisgarh, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Jharkhand, Odisha. Caused by *Mycobacterium leprae*; treated with multidrug therapy.
**Notebook:** GS2 – Social Justice (Health).
**Static anchor:** Spectrum/Ramesh Singh – Health: communicable diseases and national programmes (NLEP). *Nudge:* add "elimination ≠ eradication" with this year's numbers as a Mains example.

### 5. Supreme Court issues notice on accessibility in air travel for persons with disabilities — Polity & Governance
**Exam angle:** Per The Hindu (8 Oct), the SC issued notice to the Ministry of Civil Aviation, DGCA, AAI, CISF and the Digi Yatra Foundation on a plea citing gaps such as missing ambulifts, inaccessible in-flight systems and silent airports, and an alleged offloading of a wheelchair user. The petition estimates over 7 million disabled air passengers a year (using the 2011 Census figure of 2.21%). Constitutional hooks: Article 21 (dignity), Article 14, Article 41 (DPSP) and the RPwD Act, 2016 (accessibility provisions). *This is a notice, not a verdict; follow the next hearing.*
**Key facts:** RPwD Act 2016 recognises 21 disabilities; benchmark disability is at least 40%; 4% reservation in government posts. India's domestic air traffic grows about 10–12% a year (per petition).
**Notebook:** GS2 – Polity/Governance (vulnerable sections, judiciary).
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth – Ch. on Fundamental Rights (Art. 21) and DPSP; Social Justice notes on RPwD Act. *Nudge:* add a PIL case-study line under "judicial activism in welfare".

### C. Mains Question (GS2, 150 words, 10 marks)
**Critically examine India's position on the Pathogen Access and Benefit-Sharing (PABS) annex of the WHO Pandemic Agreement. How can it balance sovereignty over biological resources with the need for rapid global response?**

*Model-answer skeleton:*
1. **Context:** Agreement adopted May 2025; cannot open for signature until PABS annex is adopted; UN HLM of 25 Sep 2026 re-opened the debate.
2. **India's stance and its merits:** Access to pathogen data must be matched by timely access to vaccines/diagnostics; sovereignty and domestic financing; COVID-era record of vaccine supply gives credibility.
3. **Limits and tensions:** High-income countries resist mandatory technology transfer/IP waivers; India's dependence on imported APIs; risk that sovereignty claims slow data sharing.
4. **Way forward:** Align domestic law (Biological Diversity Act, 2002; Nagoya model) with PABS; PLI for key starting materials; regional bio-manufacturing hubs; push DPI/digital health as a public good; conclude with a balanced, equity-first multilateral line.

## Part C — Answers

1. **(c)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the WHA adopted the Agreement on 20 May 2025, and under Article 12 it opens for signature only after the PABS annex is adopted (still being negotiated). Statement 3 is wrong; it is not yet open for signature, so it cannot be in force.
2. **(a)** — Kagan (non-linear effects) and Soai (asymmetric autocatalysis, the Soai reaction) showed how a tiny imbalance between left- and right-handed molecules can be amplified, relevant to single-enantiomer drugs and the origin of biological homochirality.
3. **(a)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: the Dugong Conservation Reserve (about 448 sq km) is in Palk Bay, Tamil Nadu, not off Kerala. A 2026 WII assessment counted about 270 dugongs (158 Palk Bay, 112 Gulf of Mannar).
4. **(b)** — Leprosy is caused by the bacterium Mycobacterium leprae, not a virus, so statement 2 is wrong. Elimination as a public health problem (prevalence below 1 per 10,000) was declared in 2005, and single-dose rifampicin PEP (SDR-PEP) is used for contacts. In 2025-26 India reported 91,783 new cases and prevalence of 0.56 per 10,000.
5. **(b)** — The Act (which replaced the 1995 Act) recognises 21 disabilities and defines benchmark disability as at least 40 per cent. Government-post reservation is 4 per cent, not 5 per cent, so statement 3 is wrong.
6. **(a)** — The Nagoya Protocol on Access and Benefit-Sharing (adopted 2010, in force 12 October 2014; India ratified in 2012) supplements the CBD. India's Biological Diversity Act, 2002 and the National Biodiversity Authority implement ABS domestically.
7. **(a)** — Article 41 covers the right to work, to education and to public assistance in cases of unemployment, old age, sickness and disablement. Equal pay is Article 39(d), free legal aid is Article 39A and monuments is Article 49.
