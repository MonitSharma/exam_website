### 1. A nine-judge Bench reopens the oldest question in Indian labour law — what is an "industry"? — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** On **20 August 2026**, a nine-judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court headed by **CJI Surya Kant** delivered judgment in ***State of Uttar Pradesh v. Jai Bir Singh* (2026 INSC 897)**, holding by **5:4** that the "triple test" laid down by a seven-judge Bench in *Bangalore Water Supply & Sewerage Board v. A. Rajappa* (1978) will continue to govern **all pending disputes** under the now-repealed Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 — but that parts of the test require refinement, that the refinement operates **prospectively**, and that Section 2(p) of the **Industrial Relations Code, 2020** must be interpreted **independently** of *Bangalore Water Supply*.

**Exam angle:**
- This is the rare intersection of **judicial process** (Article 141, reference to larger Benches, prospective overruling) and **labour rights** — usable in GS2 Polity, GS2 Social Justice and GS3 Economy alike. A reference pending since 2005 finally answered by a nine-judge Bench is itself a comment on judicial delay.
- The substantive stake: "industry" is the gateway word. If an establishment is an "industry", its employees become "workmen" and get retrenchment protection, conciliation and adjudication machinery; if it is not, they fall outside the statute entirely. Hospitals, universities, research bodies, charitable institutions, clubs, social-forestry schemes and municipal departments have all been litigated on this line.
- **Justice B.V. Nagarathna**, with three others, dissented — holding the reference itself unnecessary and the 1978 test in need of no interference, since Section 2(p) of the IRC largely reproduces its essence. A live example for the "value of dissent" theme in GS4 and GS2.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Case: ***State of U.P. v. Jai Bir Singh*, 2026 INSC 897**; judgment **20 August 2026**; **nine judges**; majority **5:4**; presided over by **CJI Surya Kant**.
- Precedent under review: ***Bangalore Water Supply & Sewerage Board v. A. Rajappa* (1978)** — a **seven**-judge Bench interpreting **Section 2(j)** of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947.
- The **triple test**: (i) **systematic activity**, (ii) **cooperation between employer and employees**, (iii) **production or distribution of goods and services to satisfy human wants** (excluding purely spiritual or religious services). **Profit motive is irrelevant**; **sovereign functions** are the principal exclusion.
- Holding: 1978 test **undisturbed for pending ID Act disputes**; reformulated test **prospective**; **Section 2(p) of the Industrial Relations Code, 2020** (the ID Act's successor, brought into force in **November 2025**) to be read on its own terms.
- Dissent: **Nagarathna J.** and three colleagues.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Judiciary) with a cross-tag to GS2 Social Justice / GS3 Economy (labour reforms).

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth*, chapter on the **Supreme Court** — the sections on **Article 141 (law declared by the SC binding on all courts)** and **Article 145(3) (minimum five judges for substantial questions of constitutional interpretation)**. → Add a margin note: "Bench strength & overruling: a Bench can only be overruled by a **larger** Bench. 1978 BWSSB (7J) → reference (2005) → **9J, Jai Bir Singh, Aug 2026 (5:4)**. Triple test survives for pending ID Act cases; IRC 2020 s.2(p) to be read afresh." Cross-link to **Article 43 and 43A (DPSP — living wage, participation of workers)** and to *Mrunal* **Pillar 4 — the four Labour Codes**, where you should update the Industrial Relations Code entry with this ruling.

### 2. SEBI widens the online bond pipe — IFSCA products, 54EC bonds, and a new "Fixed Income Channel Partner" — Economy

**Headline:** SEBI has expanded the product universe of **Online Bond Platform Providers (OBPPs)** with effect from **14 August 2026** to include **IFSCA-regulated products** and specified **capital-gains tax-saving bonds under Section 54EC**, and followed it with a **consultation paper dated 21 August 2026** proposing a new intermediary class — **Fixed Income Channel Partners (FICPs)** — to push retail bond distribution into Tier-II, Tier-III and rural India.

**Exam angle:**
- The standard GS3 lament is that India's **corporate bond market is shallow and institution-dominated** — issuance is overwhelmingly private placement to a narrow set of buyers, and retail participation is negligible. OBPPs are the regulatory answer: bring bonds onto a regulated digital shopfront with disclosure, KYC and settlement discipline, instead of unregulated online sellers.
- The FICP proposal is explicitly modelled on the **mutual fund distributor** architecture — a low-cost, commission-based feet-on-street layer. This is a clean case study for "financial inclusion beyond bank accounts" and for the **regulatory sandbox → formal intermediary** progression.
- Note the **multi-regulator** point: an OBPP may now offer products regulated by **SEBI, RBI, IRDAI, PFRDA and IFSCA**. Prelims loves questions that test whether you can tell these five apart, and IFSCA (GIFT City) is the newest of them.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Legal basis: **Regulation 51A** of the **SEBI (Issue and Listing of Non-Convertible Securities) Regulations, 2021**.
- An OBPP must be a **company incorporated in India** and must register as a **stock broker in the debt segment** of a recognised stock exchange.
- Permitted instruments include **listed debt securities, municipal debt securities, securitised debt instruments, Government Securities, State Development Loans (SDLs) and Treasury Bills**.
- **14 August 2026 modification:** adds eligible **IFSCA-regulated products** and **Section 54EC** capital-gains bonds (and the corresponding provision of the Income-tax Act, 2025).
- **21 August 2026 consultation paper:** proposes **Fixed Income Channel Partners (FICPs)** to distribute permitted fixed-income securities through OBPPs, drawing on the **mutual fund distributor** model.
- **Section 54EC** bonds: capital-gains exemption bonds issued by bodies such as **NHAI, REC, PFC and IRFC** — the classic "long-term capital gain on land/building parked in a bond" route.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Financial markets / Financial inclusion).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* **Pillar 1C — Financial markets: SEBI, debt market, bonds**. → Add a line under the corporate-bond-market section: "Retail access ladder: RBI **Retail Direct** (G-Secs, 2021) → SEBI **OBPP** (Reg 51A, 2022–) → **FICP** proposal (Aug 2026)." Cross-link to the **five financial regulators** table (RBI / SEBI / IRDAI / PFRDA / IFSCA) and to the **Section 54EC** entry in your direct-tax notes.

### 3. NADCP crosses 147 crore FMD vaccine doses — and India's livestock disease map starts to shift — Agriculture

**Headline:** A PIB release of **22 August 2026** reports that the **National Animal Disease Control Programme (NADCP)** has administered **147.16 crore FMD vaccine doses** since its launch in **September 2019**, covering **8.04 crore farmers**, with **Foot and Mouth Disease outbreaks falling from 132 in 2019 to 40 in 2025**.

**Exam angle:**
- Livestock contributes a rising share of gross value added in agriculture and is disproportionately important to **small, marginal and landless households** — so animal disease control is an income-security question, not merely a veterinary one. FMD alone imposes large annual losses through milk-yield drop, draught-power loss and export bans.
- The **trade angle** is examinable: FMD-free-zone status (recognised by the **World Organisation for Animal Health, WOAH**) is the gate to bovine-meat and dairy export markets. Disease control is thus an export-competitiveness policy.
- **Brucellosis** is a **zoonosis** — it links straight into the **One Health** framework, which UPSC has been circling since COVID. Pair this item with any human-health item for a Mains paragraph on integrated human–animal–environment surveillance.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **NADCP** launched **September 2019**; nodal department: **Department of Animal Husbandry & Dairying, Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying**; **100% central funding** for the vaccination component.
- Targets **two** diseases: **Foot and Mouth Disease (FMD)** and **Brucellosis**.
- **FMD** — **viral**, extremely contagious, affects **cloven-hoofed** animals (cattle, buffalo, sheep, goats, pigs); rarely fatal in adults but devastating to productivity; **vaccination twice a year**.
- **Brucellosis** — **bacterial** (*Brucella abortus* in bovines), attacks reproduction, causes abortion and infertility; **zoonotic**; vaccination given **once**, to **female bovine calves aged 4–8 months**.
- Progress figures: **147.16 crore** FMD doses; **8.04 crore** farmers; outbreaks **132 (2019) → 40 (2025)**.
- Digital spine: **Bharat Pashudhan** portal — a unique **12-digit Tag ID** per animal; over **39 crore** livestock registered; more than **4,019 Mobile Veterinary Units** for doorstep care.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (Animal husbandry and allied sectors).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* **Pillar 4 — Agriculture: allied sectors / animal husbandry**, and the **livestock** section of the Economic Survey notes. → Add a scheme box: "**NADCP (2019)** — FMD + Brucellosis, 100% central funding | **Rashtriya Gokul Mission (2014)** — indigenous breeds | **National Livestock Mission (2014-15)** — feed, fodder, entrepreneurship | **NLM/NADCP delivery layer: Bharat Pashudhan portal + 1962 Mobile Veterinary Units**." Cross-link to **One Health** in your GS3 Sci-Tech notes and to **WOAH** in the international-organisations list.

### 4. The Golconda "secret tunnel" turns out to be a granite chamber — ASI on the limits of legend — Art & Culture

**Headline:** A structure reported on **21 August 2026** during work by the Army's Artillery Centre behind **Golconda Fort, Hyderabad** — immediately described locally as the long-rumoured "secret tunnel" to the Charminar — was examined by the **ASI Hyderabad Circle**, which found it to be a **rectangular granite chamber**, roughly **1.2 m × 1.8 m and 2.2 m deep**, with rough stone masonry and **red potsherds of apparently medieval date** nearby. Purpose undetermined; the Circle will report to the **Director General, ASI** for a detailed investigation.

**Exam angle:**
- A neat "how archaeology actually works" item — the examinable contrast is between **oral tradition/popular belief** and **stratigraphic evidence**. The centuries-old belief in an underground passage linking Golconda to Charminar is exactly the sort of claim ASI verification is designed to test.
- Golconda is a high-frequency Prelims name: **Kakatiya** origin → **Qutb Shahi** capital → **diamond entrepôt** → Mughal annexation. Deccani Indo-Islamic architecture (Bijapur, Bidar, Golconda, Ahmadnagar) is under-prepared by most candidates relative to how often UPSC asks about it.
- **Critical distinction:** the **Qutb Shahi Monuments of Hyderabad** — Golconda Fort, the Qutb Shahi Tombs and the Charminar — are on **UNESCO's Tentative List**, **not** the World Heritage List. UPSC has repeatedly exploited the Tentative-List/WHS confusion.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Chamber dimensions: **~1.2 m × 1.8 m × 2.2 m deep**; **granite**, rough stone masonry; **red potsherds**, medieval period; verified by **ASI Hyderabad Circle**, August 2026.
- **Golconda** — from Telugu ***Golla Konda***, "shepherd's hill"; began as a **Kakatiya** mud fort; rebuilt in granite by the **Qutb Shahis**.
- **Qutb Shahi dynasty: 1518–1687**, founded by **Quli Qutb Shah** after the break-up of the **Bahmani Sultanate**; capital at Golconda until **Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah** founded **Hyderabad in 1591** (and built the **Charminar**).
- Golconda annexed by **Aurangzeb in 1687** (Bijapur had fallen in 1686).
- Famous for the **diamond trade** — the Kollur mines of the Krishna valley were marketed through Golconda.
- **UNESCO status: Tentative List** (Qutb Shahi Monuments of Hyderabad), not inscribed.
- Nodal body: **Archaeological Survey of India**, under the Ministry of Culture, operating under the **Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains (AMASR) Act, 1958**.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Art & Culture (Medieval Indo-Islamic architecture) with a cross-tag to GS1 Medieval History (Deccan Sultanates).

**Static anchor:** *Nitin Singhania*, **Indo-Islamic architecture → Provincial / Deccan style**. → Add a comparison line: "**Bahmani** (Gulbarga, then **Bidar** from 1425) → five successor sultanates: **Adil Shahi–Bijapur, Nizam Shahi–Ahmadnagar, Qutb Shahi–Golconda, Barid Shahi–Bidar, Imad Shahi–Berar**; combined at **Talikota (1565)** against Vijayanagara." Cross-link to the **UNESCO World Heritage vs Tentative List** page in your Culture notes and re-check which Indian sites are actually inscribed.

### 5. A six-fold jump in Delhi's H1N1 count — and what India's flu surveillance actually looks like — Society

**Headline:** **Delhi has recorded 1,777 H1N1 cases in 2026** (114 of them on **20 August** alone) with **no deaths** — more than **six times** the 229 cases in the corresponding period of 2025 — prompting a Union Health Ministry review of influenza surveillance and hospital preparedness. **Karnataka**, which reported **4,212 laboratory-confirmed influenza-positive cases across 32 districts up to 22 August 2026**, issued an advisory on **24 August** describing the rise as a **normal cyclical monsoon surge**. **ICMR has said no new strain has been identified.**

**Exam angle:**
- The exam-worthy insight is the **denominator problem**: a "six-fold rise" in reported cases can reflect a genuine surge, wider testing, or better reporting through the digital surveillance platform. Karnataka's numbers — **4,212 in 2026 vs 4,583 in 2025** for the comparable window — actually show a **flat-to-lower** trend, which is why the state called it seasonal. Excellent material for a GS4/GS2 point on **data literacy in risk communication**.
- Understand India's **two influenza peaks**: **January–March** (winter) and **July–September** (monsoon). Seasonality is a Geography–Health crossover UPSC likes.
- The surveillance architecture is the memorisable part, and it recurs every outbreak season.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **Delhi:** 1,777 H1N1 cases in 2026; 114 reported on 20 August; **zero deaths**; against **229** in the same period of 2025.
- **Karnataka:** **4,212** lab-confirmed influenza-positive cases across **32 districts** to 22 August 2026; **4,583 (2025)** and **3,903 (2024)** for comparable periods; advisory issued **24 August 2026**.
- **Two seasonal peaks** in India: **January–March** and **July–September**.
- **H1N1pdm09** is the strain that caused the **2009 pandemic** and has since circulated as a **seasonal** influenza A subtype. **ICMR: no new strain detected (2026).**
- Surveillance chain: **Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (IDSP), launched 2004** → reporting now through the **Integrated Health Information Platform (IHIP)**, launched **5 April 2021** → outbreak identification coordinated by the **National Centre for Disease Control (NCDC)** → laboratory confirmation through **ICMR's VRDL network**, with **ICMR–NIV, Pune (est. 1952)** as the reference laboratory for viral outbreaks.
- Case definitions used: **ILI (Influenza-Like Illness)** and **SARI (Severe Acute Respiratory Infection)**.
- Antiviral of choice: **oseltamivir**. Influenza vaccines need annual reformulation because of **antigenic drift**; **antigenic shift** (reassortment) is what produces pandemic strains.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Society / GS2 — Governance (Health).

**Static anchor:** GS2 **Health governance** notes (the same file where you keep NHP 2017, Ayushman Bharat and the Epidemic Diseases Act). → Add a surveillance box: "**IDSP (2004) → IHIP (2021)** | **NCDC** = outbreak coordination | **ICMR-NIV Pune** = reference lab | **VRDL network** = testing | **ILI/SARI** = case definitions." Cross-link to **One Health** (and therefore to today's NADCP item — brucellosis is the animal-side twin of this human-side story) and to the **Epidemic Diseases Act, 1897** / **Disaster Management Act, 2005** legal toolkit.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Polity & Governance / Social Justice, 250 words, 15 marks)

**"The meaning of the word 'industry' has decided, for five decades, which Indian workers enjoy statutory protection and which do not. Examine the implications of the Supreme Court's 2026 nine-judge ruling for the coverage of workers under the new labour codes."**

*Model-answer skeleton:*

1. **Why the word matters.** "Industry" is the jurisdictional gateway of the Industrial Disputes Act, 1947 — it determines who is a "workman", and therefore who gets conciliation, adjudication, notice and compensation on retrenchment, and protection against unfair labour practice. Establishments held outside it — and litigation has repeatedly tested hospitals, universities, research institutions, clubs, charities and government departments — leave their employees to ordinary contract and service law.
2. **What the 1978 test did, and what 2026 changes.** *Bangalore Water Supply* (7J) read Section 2(j) expansively through the triple test — systematic activity, employer–employee cooperation, production or distribution of goods and services — with profit motive irrelevant and sovereign functions the main carve-out. The nine-judge Bench (5:4, 20 August 2026) preserves that test for all **pending** ID Act disputes, refines it **prospectively**, and — critically — detaches it from **Section 2(p) of the Industrial Relations Code, 2020**, which must now be construed on its own language.
3. **The implications — two readings.** The majority's reading is one of **statutory fidelity**: a new Code deserves a fresh interpretive start, unencumbered by five decades of accretion around a repealed provision. The dissent's reading is one of **continuity of legislative intent**: because Section 2(p) largely reproduces the triple test, removing the interpretive anchor invites years of fresh litigation and a narrower scope by default — precisely when the growth of private-sector, contractual and platform-mediated work makes an expansive definition more necessary, not less.
4. **Balance and way forward.** Note that the expansive definition is not purely pro-labour — it also imports discipline (regulated retrenchment, restrictions on strikes) and has historically served **industrial peace**. Way ahead: Parliament could remove the ambiguity by clarifying Section 2(p); tribunals should be guided by the demonstrable continuity of language between the two provisions; and the wider fix lies in expanding social security beyond the "workman" category altogether — through the Code on Social Security, 2020's provisions for gig and platform workers and the e-Shram registry — so that protection no longer hangs on a single contested noun.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The nine-judge Bench was examining the correctness of *Bangalore Water Supply & Sewerage Board v. A. Rajappa* (1978), a seven-judge decision; it held the triple test undisturbed for pending disputes under the repealed ID Act, 1947, and directed that Section 2(p) of the Industrial Relations Code, 2020 be interpreted independently.

**Q2 — (c) The presence of a profit motive.** The 1978 triple test comprises systematic activity, employer–employee cooperation, and production/distribution of goods and services to satisfy human wants; the Court expressly held that the **absence of a profit motive is immaterial**, which is how non-commercial bodies were brought within "industry".

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct: the incorporation-and-broker-registration requirement flows from Regulation 51A of the SEBI (Issue and Listing of Non-Convertible Securities) Regulations, 2021; the 14 August 2026 modification added IFSCA-regulated products and Section 54EC bonds; and the 21 August 2026 consultation paper proposed Fixed Income Channel Partners.

**Q4 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** Statement 2 is wrong on two counts — FMD is **viral**, not bacterial, and it affects **all cloven-hoofed** animals including sheep, goats and pigs, not only cattle and buffalo. Brucellosis vaccination is indeed a one-time dose for female bovine calves aged 4–8 months.

**Q5 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The Qutb Shahi Monuments of Hyderabad — Golconda Fort, the Qutb Shahi Tombs and the Charminar — are on UNESCO's **Tentative List**, not the World Heritage List, so statement 3 is incorrect.

**Q6 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** IDSP (2004) now reports through the IHIP portal (launched April 2021); NCDC coordinates outbreak identification; ICMR–NIV, Pune (established 1952) is the reference laboratory for viral outbreaks, supported by the VRDL network.

**Q7 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Article 145(3) prescribes a minimum of five judges for a substantial question of constitutional interpretation; Article 141 makes the law declared by the Supreme Court binding on all courts in India; and by settled practice a Bench can be overruled only by a numerically larger Bench — the very reason a nine-judge Bench was constituted to revisit a seven-judge decision.

**Q8 — (b) Only three.** Pairs 1, 3 and 4 are correct. Pair 2 is wrong: the **Nizam Shahi** dynasty ruled from **Ahmadnagar**; **Golconda** was the seat of the **Qutb Shahi** dynasty.
