## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Ladakh will be enumerated in September 2026 — carrying independent India's first caste count — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** The Centre has notified the Census schedule for the snow-bound non-synchronous areas of Ladakh, Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand — population enumeration runs 1–30 September 2026, making Ladakh the first Union Territory to complete its count, and therefore the first place in independent India where a full caste enumeration is actually carried out on the ground.

**Exam angle.** Census 2027 is the 16th Census, the first since 2011, the first conducted digitally, and the first to enumerate caste beyond SC/ST since 1931. The examiner's favourite hook here is not the politics but the *machinery*: two phases, two reference dates, and the fact that "Census" is a **Union** subject, not a Concurrent one — a point that decides half the statement-based questions on this theme. The Ladakh exception exists for a purely physical reason (winter inaccessibility), which is exactly the kind of geography–administration link UPSC likes.

**Key facts to memorise.**

- Gazette notification for the 16th Census issued **16 June 2025**.
- Two phases: **Houselisting Operation (HLO)** — housing condition, assets, amenities; **Population Enumeration (PE)** — demographic, socio-economic and cultural particulars, including caste.
- Reference date: **00:00 hrs, 1 March 2027** for most of India; **00:00 hrs, 1 October 2026** for the snow-bound non-synchronous areas of **Ladakh, J&K, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand**.
- Ladakh (Leh + Kargil): enumeration **1–30 September 2026**, revisional round **1–5 October 2026**. Self-enumeration window opens **17 August 2026**.
- Legal basis: **Census Act, 1948**. Constitutional basis: **Entry 69, Union List (List I), Seventh Schedule**. Conducting authority: **Registrar General and Census Commissioner of India**, under the **Ministry of Home Affairs**.
- Last full caste enumeration: **1931**. The SECC 2011 was *not* a Census — it was a separate survey whose caste data was never fully released.

**Notebook:** GS2 (Polity & Governance) → Constitutional & statutory bodies / Union–State legislative relations. Cross-tag to GS1 (Society) → caste.

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth* — chapter on **Centre–State Legislative Relations (Seventh Schedule)** and the chapter on **Scheduled Castes / Backward Classes (Arts. 340, 338, 338A, 338B; NCBC)**. **Update nudge:** in your Seventh Schedule notes, write "Entry 69 — Census" in the margin next to Entry 45 (Bank­ing) and Entry 97 (residuary), and cross-link to your SECC 2011 note with a one-line contrast: *Census Act 1948 → legally binding, confidential; SECC → executive survey, no statutory confidentiality.*

### 2. VB-G RAM G's first month: rural person-days down ~50% year-on-year — Economy

**Headline:** In July 2026, the first month of the **Viksit Bharat – Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin), or VB-G RAM G**, which replaced MGNREGS from 1 July 2026, person-days generated fell **49.94% to 7.67 crore** — a five-year low — with the Rural Development Ministry contesting the dashboard figures.

**Exam angle.** This is the first hard data point on India's flagship scheme transition and it hands you both sides of an answer. The scheme *expands* the statutory guarantee from 100 to **125 days**, yet output halved in month one — a textbook case of **transition friction in a demand-driven entitlement**: new job cards, new muster rolls, new software, and a monsoon-deficit sowing pause all landing at once. The deeper conceptual point UPSC probes: a rights-based scheme's performance is measured by *unmet demand*, not by expenditure, so any drop must be decomposed into "less demand" versus "demand not registered".

**Key facts to memorise.**

- VB-G RAM G replaced MGNREGS w.e.f. **1 July 2026**; the enabling Bill received Presidential assent earlier in 2026.
- Statutory guarantee: up to **125 days** of unskilled manual wage employment per rural household per financial year (MGNREGA: 100 days).
- July 2026: **7.67 crore person-days**, down **49.94%** y-o-y. Households that got work: **68.94 lakh** vs **1.42 crore** a year earlier (**−51.45%**).
- Ministry of Rural Development's counter: roughly **9 crore person-days** actually generated, and **over 98%** of households that demanded work received an offer.
- Constitutional hook: **Article 41 (DPSP)** — right to work, education and public assistance.

**Notebook:** GS3 (Economy) → Inclusive growth, employment, government budgeting. Cross-tag GS2 → welfare schemes and vulnerable sections.

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* — **Pillar 6 (Human Development / Poverty & Employment schemes)**, MGNREGA section. **Update nudge:** rewrite the MGNREGA heading as "MGNREGA → VB-G RAM G (1 July 2026)" and add a two-column comparison — days guaranteed, wage-fixation route, unemployment allowance, social audit — so you never mix the two in a Prelims statement question.

### 3. India crosses 300 GW of non-fossil installed capacity — 60% of the 2030 goal — Environment

**Headline:** As on **31 July 2026**, India's non-fossil-fuel-based installed electricity generation capacity stands at **300.50 GW** out of a total of roughly **552 GW** — over **54%** of the installed base and more than **60%** of the 500 GW-by-2030 target.

**Exam angle.** Two traps live inside this number. First, "non-fossil" is **not** "renewable": it includes **large hydro and nuclear**. Second, the **50%-of-installed-capacity-from-non-fossil-sources** commitment in India's updated NDC has already been *exceeded* (54%), whereas the **500 GW** figure comes from the **Panchamrit** announcement at COP26, not from the formal NDC text. Getting that distinction right is worth a mark in Prelims and a paragraph in Mains. Also note capacity ≠ generation: solar's capacity utilisation factor is far below coal's, so a 54% capacity share still translates into a much smaller share of actual electricity generated.

**Key facts to memorise.**

- **300.50 GW** non-fossil (31 July 2026) of ~**552 GW** total → **>54%** of installed capacity.
- Break-up: **Solar 164.59 GW · Wind 58.14 GW · Hydro 57.24 GW · Bio-power 11.75 GW · Nuclear 8.78 GW**. (Wind is *second* largest — narrowly ahead of hydro.)
- Record FY 2025-26 addition: **55.29 GW** non-fossil, of which **44.6 GW solar** and **6 GW wind**.
- Solar trajectory: **2.8 GW (2014) → ~165 GW (2026)**. Wind: **21 GW (2014) → 58+ GW**.
- Panchamrit (COP26, Glasgow 2021): 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030; 50% of energy requirements from renewables; 1 billion tonne cut in projected emissions; 45% cut in emissions intensity of GDP; **net zero by 2070**.

**Notebook:** GS3 (Environment / Energy) → climate change mitigation, energy security.

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment* — **Climate Change chapter (UNFCCC → Paris Agreement → NDCs)**. **Update nudge:** put the 300.50 GW break-up in a five-row table right under your NDC page, and draw an arrow from "50% non-fossil installed capacity" to "achieved 2026 — target was 2030", with a margin note distinguishing *NDC commitment* from *Panchamrit announcement*.

### 4. BESIII confirms X(2370) as the lightest pseudoscalar glueball — a particle made only of force — Science & Technology

**Headline:** On **5 August 2026**, at a plenary session of **ICHEP 2026 in Natal, Brazil**, the **BESIII collaboration** presented a complete experimental evidence chain identifying **X(2370)** as the **lightest pseudoscalar glueball** — closing a search that Quantum Chromodynamics opened nearly fifty years ago.

**Exam angle.** A glueball is a bound state made **purely of gluons**, with no valence quarks at all. It can exist because, unlike the photon in electromagnetism, the gluon itself **carries colour charge** and therefore couples to other gluons — self-interaction is the structural difference between QCD and QED. For Monit specifically this is double-value: a Prelims Sci-Tech factual *and* a clean illustration for the **Physics optional** (colour confinement, non-Abelian gauge theory, lattice QCD as a predictive tool). Frame the Mains/interview line as "prediction-first physics": lattice QCD computed the mass and spin-parity before the detector confirmed them.

**Key facts to memorise.**

- **Glueball** — a hadron composed entirely of gluons; permitted because gluons carry colour charge and self-interact (photons, being electrically neutral, do not).
- Candidate: **X(2370)**, mass ≈ **2370 MeV**, spin-parity measured as **0⁻⁺** (pseudoscalar) — matching the **lattice QCD** prediction.
- Evidence chain: (i) spin-parity 0⁻⁺ determined from a sample of **10 billion J/ψ events** (2024); (ii) **flavour-singlet** behaviour — no evidence of the decay X(2370) → K*(892)⁰K̄⁰; (iii) new decay modes **K⁰ₛK⁰ₛπ⁰, π⁰π⁰η and a₀π⁰**.
- Facility: **BESIII detector at BEPCII**, Institute of High Energy Physics, Beijing.
- Context for the four fundamental interactions: strong (**gluon**), electromagnetic (**photon**), weak (**W and Z bosons**), gravitational (graviton — hypothetical).

**Notebook:** GS3 (Science & Technology) → developments in science; also **Physics optional** — QCD / particle physics.

**Static anchor:** NCERT **Class XII Physics, Ch. 13 (Nuclei)** and your optional's **particle-physics / standard-model** notes. **Update nudge:** add one line to your "fundamental forces and their mediators" table — *gluons self-interact ⇒ bound states of pure force (glueballs) are possible; photons do not ⇒ no "photonballs"* — and cross-link it to your confinement/asymptotic-freedom page.

### 5. First sea-route export of GI-tagged Mithila Makhana — Bihar to Australia — Agriculture

**Headline:** **APEDA** facilitated the first-ever commercial **sea shipment of 18 metric tonnes of GI-tagged Mithila Makhana**, from the BIADA Industrial Area at **Bihta, Bihar**, to **Australia** — sourced from growers in **Darbhanga** district, who realised roughly **18% higher returns** than prevailing local market rates.

**Exam angle.** Small consignment, large idea: this is the GI-tag-to-export-value-chain story in miniature. Air freight is what usually caps the volume of high-value perishables; moving to **sea freight** cuts logistics cost per kilo and is what turns a niche GI product into a scalable export. Pair it with the **Makhana Board for Bihar** announced in the Union Budget 2025-26 and you have a ready Mains example of "one district, one product → GI → market linkage → farmer price realisation".

**Key facts to memorise.**

- **Makhana / foxnut / gorgon nut** = seed of ***Euryale ferox***, an aquatic plant grown in **ponds and wetlands**; India supplies the overwhelming bulk of world output, with **Bihar (Mithila region — Darbhanga, Madhubani, Purnia, Katihar, Saharsa)** dominant.
- **"Mithila Makhana"** received its **GI tag in 2022**.
- Consignment: **18 MT**, Bihta (Bihar) → Australia, by **sea**; flagged off by Bihar's Agriculture Minister **Vijay Kumar Sinha**; farmer price realisation **~18%** above prevailing rates.
- FY 2025-26: India exported **over 7,000 MT** of makhana and value-added makhana products to **20+ destinations**, including the USA, West Asia and Africa.
- **APEDA** — Agricultural and Processed Food Products Export Development Authority, statutory body under the **APEDA Act, 1985**, **Ministry of Commerce and Industry**. (Marine and fishery products are **MPEDA**'s remit, not APEDA's — a standard Prelims trap.)
- GI framework: **Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999**; GI Registry, Chennai; flows from **TRIPS**.

**Notebook:** GS3 (Agriculture) → food processing, agricultural marketing and exports. Cross-tag GS1 (Geography) → wetland agriculture.

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* — **Agriculture pillar: food processing and agri-export policy**; and your GI page. **Update nudge:** on your GI-products-of-India list, mark "Mithila Makhana (Bihar, 2022)" and add a one-line note beside APEDA: *"APEDA = agri & processed food; MPEDA = marine. Both under Min. of Commerce."*

### C. Mains-Style Question

**GS1 — Indian Society | 250 words | 15 marks**

> The Census of 2027 will enumerate caste for the first time since 1931. **Examine** the utility of such enumeration for public policy, and the risks it carries for social cohesion.

**Model-answer skeleton**

1. **Frame the gap.** India has designed reservation, welfare targeting and backward-class policy for seven decades on caste data that is either 95 years old (1931), partial (SC/ST-only Census counts), or methodologically contested (SECC 2011, whose caste returns were never fully released). Quantification has lagged entitlement.

2. **The policy case.** Evidence-based sub-categorisation of OBCs; a defensible empirical basis for the "adequacy of representation" tests the Supreme Court demands; better-calibrated welfare targeting; and a factual anchor for the *Indra Sawhney* ceiling debate. Cite that the NCBC became a constitutional body under Article 338B (102nd Amendment) but has had to advise without a current caste denominator.

3. **The risks.** Enumeration can harden fluid or overlapping identities; caste names vary across states and even within districts, creating classification disputes; the count risks converting a statistical exercise into a claim-making contest and intensifying competitive mobilisation. Administrative risk too — enumerator discretion at the doorstep becomes a policy input.

4. **The balanced close.** Data is a necessary but not sufficient condition for equity: publish disaggregated *outcome* indicators (education, occupation, assets) alongside caste counts so the numbers drive capability-building rather than headcount politics, and insulate the enumeration from political framing through the statutory confidentiality of the Census Act, 1948.
