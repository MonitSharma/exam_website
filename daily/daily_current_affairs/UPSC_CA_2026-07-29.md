## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. India–China border trade resumes through Nathu La and Lipulekh from 1 August 2026 — International Relations

**Headline.** After a six-year suspension (since 2020, on COVID grounds), the Centre confirmed on 28 July that border trade with China will restart on **1 August 2026** through **Nathu La (Sikkim)** and **Lipulekh (Uttarakhand)**, with a **trial run on 30 July** to test documentation, logistics and crossing procedures.

**Exam angle.** This is the clearest "normalisation" marker in India–China ties since the 2020 standoff, and it sits at the intersection of GS2 (bilateral relations, confidence-building measures) and GS1 Geography (Himalayan passes). Note the sequencing UPSC loves: political decision at the **Special Representatives (SR) mechanism** → operational restoration on the ground. The reopening flows from the **SR-level talks co-chaired by NSA Ajit Doval and Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi (2025)**, where both sides agreed to restore trade through **three** designated passes — Nathu La, Shipki La and Lipulekh — of which two are being activated first. Foreign Secretary **Vikram Misri** visited China on 27 July, meeting Vice Minister Sun Haiyan, keeping the diplomatic track warm.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Three designated India–China border trade points: **Lipulekh (Uttarakhand, since 1992)**, **Shipki La (Himachal Pradesh, since 1994)**, **Nathu La (Sikkim, since 2006)**.
- Nathu La: ~**4,310 m**, links Sikkim to the **Chumbi Valley / Yatung** in Tibet; a branch of the historic Silk Road; closed after the **1962 war**, reopened **6 July 2006**, suspended **2020**.
- Lipulekh sits at the **India–Nepal–China trijunction** near Kalapani (Nepal contests the alignment) and is a **Kailash Mansarovar Yatra** route.
- Shipki La is where the **Sutlej (Langqen Zangbo)** enters India, in Kinnaur district.
- Roughly **600 registered Indian traders** work the Nathu La route.

**Notebook.** GS2 — International Relations (India–China). Cross-tag to GS1 Geography (Passes / Places in News).

### 2. Ministry of Earth Sciences marks its 20th Foundation Day with an AI-heavy suite of launches — Science & Technology

**Headline.** At **Prithvi Bhawan, New Delhi (27–28 July)**, MoES used its **20th Foundation Day** to launch 13 IMD Centres of Excellence, upgrade a cyclone warning centre, roll out AI/ML forecasting platforms and expand Deep Ocean Mission outreach.

**Exam angle.** A single, high-density item that lets you refresh the whole MoES architecture — IMD, NCMRWF, NCCR, NIOT, NCPOR, INCOIS — plus **Mission Mausam** and the **Deep Ocean Mission**. The livestock-disease forecasting tie-up is the freshest hook: it converts weather data into an agriculture/animal-husbandry early-warning service, a ready-made Mains example of "science for last-mile resilience".

**Facts to memorise.**

- **13 Centres of Excellence** launched by IMD — thematic focus on **desert meteorology, mountain weather, thunderstorm forecasting and agrometeorology**.
- **Thiruvananthapuram Cyclone Warning Centre upgraded to an Area Cyclone Warning Centre (ACWC)**, now covering **Kerala, Karnataka and Lakshadweep**.
- **IMD NOVA** — web platform to monitor and validate **thunderstorm nowcasts** using lightning networks and automatic weather stations.
- **WINGS** — NCMRWF mobile app blending **numerical weather prediction with AI/ML** for real-time forecasts; plus a **National Doppler Weather Radar Reflectivity Mosaic**.
- **WAADRU** — Weather Driven Animal Disease Risk Forecasting Unit, an **IMD–ICAR-NIVEDI** first-of-its-kind unit giving **district-level livestock disease risk forecasts up to two months ahead**.
- **Sagar Prahari App** (NCCR) — AI-enabled citizen geo-tagging of beach clean-ups with automatic **marine-litter identification**; **SAHAV 2.0** — GIS decision-support for **marine spatial planning**.
- **NIOT** industry outreach to explore **75,000 sq km of the Central Indian Ocean Basin** for **polymetallic nodules** (Deep Ocean Mission).
- MoES Secretary: **Dr. N. Kalaiselvi**. MoES was created in **2006**.

### 3. Supreme Court issues notice on the challenge to the Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Amendment Act, 2026 — Polity & Governance

**Headline.** On **28 July**, a bench of **CJI Surya Kant, Justice Joymalya Bagchi and Justice V. Mohana** issued notice to the Union on a writ petition challenging the **proviso to Section 2(k)** of the 2026 Amendment Act, which excludes persons with **self-perceived gender identity** from the statutory definition of "transgender person"; the Court has also **stayed parallel High Court proceedings** on the Centre's transfer plea, signalling it will decide the constitutional question itself.

**Exam angle.** A textbook clash between a **statute** and a **judgment**: the Amendment deletes **Section 4(2)** of the 2019 Act (right to self-perceived gender identity) and makes a **District Medical Board recommendation** a precondition for the District Magistrate to issue a Certificate of Identity — squarely against the **"psychological test" over "biological test"** reasoning of **NALSA v. Union of India (2014)**. Petitioners invoke **Articles 14, 15, 19(1)(a) and 21** (dignity, autonomy, identity). The transfer-and-stay order is itself worth a line on **Article 139A** and avoidance of conflicting High Court rulings.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Parent law: **Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019**; the amending law is the **Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Amendment Act, 2026** (Bill taken up in Lok Sabha in March 2026).
- Amendment: mandatory **District Medical Board** (headed by CMO/Deputy CMO) recommendation before the **DM** issues the Certificate of Identity; hospitals must report gender-affirming surgery to the DM and Board; post-surgery, a fresh certificate with a 'male'/'female' marker must be applied for.
- **NALSA (2014)** recognised transgender persons as a "third gender", grounded identity in **self-perception**, and directed **socially and educationally backward class** treatment for reservations/welfare.
- Statutory body under the 2019 Act: **National Council for Transgender Persons (NCTP)**, chaired by the **Union Minister for Social Justice and Empowerment**.

**Notebook.** GS2 — Polity (Judicial Review, Fundamental Rights) and Social Justice; usable in GS1 Society (identity and marginalisation).

**Static anchor.** Laxmikanth — **Ch. 7 "Fundamental Rights" (Arts. 14, 15, 19, 21)** and **Ch. 26/27 on the Supreme Court (writ jurisdiction, Art. 139A transfer of cases)**. **Nudge:** on your NALSA (2014) card, add a "2026 statutory reversal → sub judice" line so you never quote NALSA as settled law without the caveat.

### 4. MoSPI to switch the GDP deflator from WPI to the output Producer Price Index — Economy

**Headline.** MoSPI has confirmed it will use the **output Producer Price Index (PPI)** as the deflator for **quarterly and annual real GDP**, starting with the **June (Q1 FY27) quarter**; **revised real GDP estimates for earlier years** on the new deflator are slated for release on **31 August 2026**, alongside the Q1 FY27 national accounts.

**Exam angle.** Deflators are a perennial Prelims trap and a live RBI Grade B topic. The point to internalise: **WPI measures transaction prices including taxes and trade margins; output PPI measures prices received by producers at the factory gate** — so PPI is conceptually the right deflator for value added. This is one leg of a broader statistical overhaul: the national accounts base year has moved to **2022-23**, and the new series replaces **single deflation** with **double deflation** in sectors such as manufacturing and agriculture. Until the **WPI base-year revision** is complete, WPI continues in the roles PPI has not yet taken over.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **GDP deflator = (Nominal GDP ÷ Real GDP) × 100**; it is *derived*, not directly observed, and covers **all** goods and services in GDP — broader than CPI or WPI.
- **PPI**: prices **received by producers**, excludes indirect taxes and trade/transport margins. **WPI**: transaction prices at the wholesale stage, **no services**, includes taxes.
- **Single deflation** deflates only output; **double deflation** deflates output and inputs separately — the internationally recommended method (SNA).
- New national accounts series base year: **2022-23** (from 2011-12). MoSPI has also been building an **Index of Services Production (ISP)**, using **CPI (non-food)** as a proxy deflator pending a services PPI.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Economy (National Income Accounting, Inflation & Indices).

**Static anchor.** Mrunal **Pillar 1 (BoP/National Income)** and **Pillar 2A (Inflation & Indices)**; NCERT Class XII *Macroeconomics* Ch. 2. **Nudge:** on your CPI–WPI comparison table, add a third column for **PPI** and a footnote row for the **GDP deflator**, then link it to the 21 July note on the WPI–CPI divergence.

### 5. Maharashtra becomes the first state to qualify for the second PM-RKVY instalment of FY27 — Agriculture

**Headline.** At a virtual review chaired by Union Agriculture Minister **Shivraj Singh Chouhan (27 July)**, **Maharashtra** became the **first state** to clear the **75% utilisation benchmark** on its first instalment under the **Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana**, spending about **₹260 crore of ₹335 crore released**; the second instalment of **₹335 crore** is now being processed.

**Exam angle.** Small news, big concept: this is the **performance-linked release of Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS) funds** in action — a standard GS2 governance point about conditional transfers, absorptive capacity and monitoring. Pair it with the **cafeteria design** of PM-RKVY, which is exactly the kind of "flexibility to states" feature UPSC likes to test in statement-based questions. The review also flagged Maharashtra's **Farmer ID (Agristack)** generation and flood compensation of **₹14,000 crore disbursed within five days**.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Cabinet (**October 2024**) rationalised all agriculture CSSs into two umbrella schemes: **PM Rashtriya Krishi Vikas Yojana (PM-RKVY)** — a **cafeteria scheme** for **sustainable agriculture** — and **Krishonnati Yojana (KY)** — for **food security / self-sufficiency**.
- Combined outlay **₹1,01,321.61 crore** (Centre **₹69,088.98 cr**, States **₹32,232.63 cr**); of this **RKVY ₹57,074.72 cr** and **KY ₹44,246.89 cr**.
- Funding pattern: **60:40 Centre:State** for general states; **90:10** for **North-Eastern and Himalayan** states; **100%** for **UTs**.
- Key design feature: states may **re-allocate funds across components** to suit agro-climatic needs.
- Benchmark in the news: **75% utilisation** of instalment-1 to unlock instalment-2.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Agriculture (schemes, subsidies, agri-finance); cross-tag GS2 Governance (Centre–State fiscal transfers).

**Static anchor.** Mrunal **Pillar 4 (Agriculture)** scheme table **+** Laxmikanth **Ch. 14 "Centre–State Relations" (financial relations)**. **Nudge:** on your PM-RKVY card, add the funding-pattern triplet (60:40 / 90:10 / 100%) and a cross-link to "Cafeteria vs Rigid CSS design" in your governance notes.

### C. Mains-Style Question

**GS2 — International Relations (250 words, 15 marks)**

*"The resumption of border trade through Himalayan passes is more a barometer of political trust than an instrument of economic gain."* **Examine** this statement in the context of India–China relations.

**Model-answer skeleton.**

1. **Frame the barometer claim with the timeline.** Trade through Nathu La resumed in 2006 as a post-1962 confidence-building measure, was suspended in 2020 after the standoff, and restarts on 1 August 2026 following SR-level understanding — each switch tracked the political temperature, not commercial demand.
2. **Concede the limited economics.** Volumes are small, the tradable list is short and administratively fixed, the passes close seasonally, roads and customs infrastructure are thin, and the trade is asymmetric against a bilateral deficit measured in tens of billions of dollars. Around 600 registered traders at Nathu La cannot move the needle on national trade.
3. **Argue the real value is political and local.** Border trade builds people-to-people stakes in stability, sustains border-area livelihoods (supporting the *Vibrant Villages Programme* logic), normalises military-diplomatic contact, and is a reversible, low-cost signal — exactly why it is the first thing suspended and the first thing restored.
4. **Balance with the caveats and conclude.** Trust must still be verified — de-escalation, patrolling protocols, transparency on the Brahmaputra/Yarlung Tsangpo, and the unresolved Lipulekh sensitivity with Nepal. Conclude that trade resumption should be read as a *signal to be tested*, valuable as a CBM but not a substitute for boundary settlement or de-risking of supply chains.

### D. PYQ Mirror

**Theme: Himalayan passes / Nathu La — *verified*.**
**UPSC CSE Prelims 2007:** "Which one of the following Himalayan passes was reopened around in the middle of the year 2006 to facilitate trade between India and China?" Options included Chang La, Jara La, Nathu La, Shipki La. **Answer: Nathu La.** UPSC has repeatedly used passes as a "location + event" hook — the 2026 reopening is a natural re-run, so lock down the pass-to-State mapping.

**Theme: price indices and the arithmetic of inflation — *verified*.**
**UPSC CSE Prelims 2011:** "A rapid increase in the rate of inflation is sometimes attributed to the 'base effect'. What is 'base effect'?" **Answer: It is the impact of the price levels of the previous year on the calculation of the inflation rate.** The examiner's habit here is conceptual, not data-based — expect the PPI/deflator shift to be asked as *"what does this index measure"* rather than *"what is the current value"*.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (d).** All three are correct. Lipulekh (1992), Shipki La (1994) and Nathu La (2006) are the three designated India–China border trade points; Nathu La in Sikkim opens onto the Chumbi Valley; Lipulekh sits at the India–Nepal–China trijunction.

**Q2 — (a).** WAADRU is the Weather Driven Animal Disease Risk Forecasting Unit, set up by IMD with ICAR-NIVEDI, issuing district-level livestock disease risk forecasts up to two months in advance.

**Q3 — (a).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. The 2026 Amendment routes certification through a District Medical Board and drops the 2019 Act's self-perceived gender identity provision. The National Council for Transgender Persons has not been abolished, so statement 3 is wrong.

**Q4 — (b).** Output PPI captures prices received by producers at the factory gate, net of indirect taxes and trade/transport margins, and is therefore conceptually closer to value added than WPI, which is a transaction-price index including taxes and margins.

**Q5 — (a).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: the October 2024 rationalisation created **two** umbrella schemes — PM-RKVY and Krishonnati Yojana — not one.

**Q6 — (b).** Only two pairs are correct. Shipki La is in Himachal Pradesh (Kinnaur) and Lipulekh in Uttarakhand (Pithoragarh), but Jelep La lies in Sikkim, not Arunachal Pradesh.

**Q7 — (d).** All three are correct. The ISA, established under UNCLOS, grants exploration contracts in the Area; India's polymetallic nodule block lies in the Central Indian Ocean Basin (currently about 75,000 sq km); the nodules are worked mainly for manganese, nickel, copper and cobalt.
