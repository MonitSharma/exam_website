## Part A — Briefing

### 1. A *dua* is not a religious appeal: Supreme Court leaves the Malegaon Central election standing — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** On **25 September 2026** a Bench of **Justices Vikram Nath, Sandeep Mehta and Augustine George Masih** dismissed the appeal of SP candidate Shaan E Hind Nihal Ahmed and upheld the **Bombay High Court's 8 June 2026** rejection of his election petition against AIMIM's **Mufti Mohammad Ismail Abdul Khalique** (Malegaon Central, Maharashtra, Nov 2024 election).

**Exam angle:** The cleanest live case on **Section 123(3), Representation of the People Act, 1951** — appeal to vote on grounds of religion as a "corrupt practice". The courts separated a religious *ritual* at a campaign gathering (the audience saying "Ameen") from an actual *appeal to vote* on religious grounds, and found the petition lacked the "material facts" a corrupt-practice charge needs. Read it against *Abhiram Singh* (2017), which read the section very broadly.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Margin in the original result: **162 votes** (1,09,653 vs 1,09,491); the petitioner himself polled 9,624.
- HC held: performing *dua* ≠ soliciting votes on religion; surrogates' speeches, support from religious organisations and general religious references in pamphlets did not make out **s.123(3) or s.123(4)**; petition dismissed **without trial** for want of material facts.
- Procedure: election petitions are tried by the **High Court** (s.80A), filed within **45 days** of the result (s.81); appeal lies to the **Supreme Court** (s.116A). A proven corrupt practice voids the election (s.100(1)(b)) and can lead to disqualification (s.8A).
- *Abhiram Singh v. C.D. Commachen* (2017), 7-judge Bench, **4:3** — "his religion" in s.123(3) covers the religion of the candidate, the agent **and the voter**.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Elections / Representation of the People Act).

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth*, "Elections" and "Electoral Reforms" chapters (RPA 1951 — corrupt practices and election disputes). **Nudge:** add a two-column box, "What *Abhiram Singh* bars / what *Malegaon* (2026) permits", and cross-link to Art. 329(b) (election disputes only by election petition).

### 2. India–New Zealand FTA gets its start date: 20 October 2026 — International Relations

**Headline:** Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal announced (22 Sept) that the **India–New Zealand FTA** — concluded 22 Dec 2025, signed in New Delhi on **27 April 2026** — enters into force on **20 October 2026**.

**Exam angle:** A textbook of India's new-generation FTA design: **100% duty-free access** for Indian exports, a **carve-out for dairy** (the Fonterra question that sank RCEP for India), a **binding investment pledge**, and a mobility window. Good for GS2 (Indo-Pacific / Pacific partners) and GS3 (trade policy, protecting smallholders).

**Facts to memorise:**
- India's exports get **zero duty on 100% of tariff lines** in NZ; India liberalises about **70% of its tariff lines**, covering about **95% of bilateral trade value**.
- **Excluded by India:** dairy (milk, cream, whey, yoghurt, cheese, butterfat), plus onions, chickpeas, peas, maize, almonds, sugar. NZ gains on kiwifruit, apples, wool, sheep meat, forestry.
- **Investment:** NZ commits to facilitating **US$20 billion over 15 years** — India's **second** FTA with an FDI pledge after the **EFTA TEPA** (US$100 bn/15 yrs).
- **Mobility:** a quota of **5,000** temporary employment visas for skilled Indians (stays of up to 3 years), including yoga instructors, chefs and AYUSH practitioners; post-study work visas for Indian graduates. Also a bilateral **safeguard mechanism** and strict Rules of Origin.
- Merchandise trade is small (≈ US$1.15 bn in 2025-26); the target is to double goods-and-services trade in about five years.
- *Companion IR lines (defence diplomacy, same week):* **Ex VARUNA** (India–France navies; 24th edition) — **INS Trishul** reached **Toulon** on 22 Sept; **Ex NOMADIC ELEPHANT** (India–Mongolia army; **18th** edition) under way at **Pithoragarh, Uttarakhand**.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India & Oceania) + GS3 Economy (External sector / trade agreements).

### 3. The sixth report card for India's tiger reserves — and waste is now graded — Environment

**Headline:** Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav launched the **6th cycle of the Management Effectiveness Evaluation of Tiger Reserves (MEE-TR 2026)** at an inception workshop in New Delhi on **25 September 2026**. It will cover **all 58 tiger reserves** and, for the first time, grade **solid waste management** as a separate criterion.

**Exam angle:** Tiger *numbers* are the headline Prelims fact, but MEE is UPSC's "governance of Protected Areas" hook. It shows how India audits conservation outcomes (IUCN-WCPA framework) rather than just counting animals. Previous cycles flagged climate action as the weakest area, which works well in a Mains answer.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Conducted by **NTCA with the Wildlife Institute of India (WII)** through independent multidisciplinary teams.
- Framework: **IUCN World Commission on Protected Areas (WCPA)** — six elements: **Context, Planning, Inputs, Process, Outputs, Outcomes**.
- Past cycles: **2006, 2010, 2014, 2018, 2022**, rising from 28 to **51** reserves. In 2022, **Periyar (Kerala)** topped (~94%), followed by Satpura, Bandipur and Nagarhole; 12 reserves were rated "Excellent", none "Poor"; "carbon capture and climate change" scored lowest.
- New criterion covers waste from **forest chowkis, tourism and wildlife rescue centres**.
- Context: 3,682 tigers (AITE 2022), about 75% of the world's wild tigers; an estimated 35–40% live **outside** reserves (hence the "Tigers Outside Tiger Reserves" pilot in 40 forest divisions).

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Biodiversity conservation / Protected Areas).

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS*, "Protected Area Network" and "Project Tiger" chapters. **Nudge:** next to your tiger-reserve list, add a line on "how reserves are evaluated (MEE)" versus "how tigers are counted (AITE, every 4 years)". The two are often confused.

### 4. ₹810.79 crore for 160 SAT-SAAW — DRDO's airfield-killer glide bomb goes into series order — Science & Technology

**Headline:** On **23 September 2026** the Ministry of Defence signed a **₹810.79-crore** contract with **Bharat Dynamics Limited (BDL)** for **160 Satellite-guided Smart Anti-Airfield Weapons (SAT-SAAW)** for the IAF, under the **Buy (Indian-IDDM)** category.

**Exam angle:** A clean example of a DRDO design going into series production with a DPSU, meaning lab-to-line indigenisation under DAP-2020. It also shows the doctrinal shift to **stand-off precision strike**, where a pilot can hit runways, radars and bunkers without flying into the enemy's air defences.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **Developer:** DRDO — **Research Centre Imarat (RCI), Hyderabad**. **Production:** BDL.
- **125-kg class** precision-guided **glide** weapon; stand-off range **up to ~100 km** (DRDO, 2021 test); targets runways, taxiways, radars, bunkers.
- Platforms: **Jaguar, Hawk and Su-30MKI**. It was flight-tested from HAL's **Hawk-i** in Jan 2021 off Odisha (9th successful trial).
- About **60% indigenous content**, with plans to indigenise the IMU, long-impact-delay fuse and twin store carrier. Deliveries **2027-28 and 2028-29**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Defence technology) / Internal Security (defence indigenisation).

### 5. "Paneer" must come from milk — FSSAI moves to strip the name from analogue products — Society

**Headline:** FSSAI published a **draft amendment on 22 September 2026** to the **Food Safety and Standards (Prohibition and Restrictions on Sales) Regulations, 2011**. It inserts in Regulation 2.1.1 a bar on "**paneer made of constituents not derived from milk**". Comments are open for **60 days** from 23 September.

**Exam angle:** A consumer-protection and food-regulation case study. **Analogue paneer** (vegetable oil and starch, replacing milk fat and solids) has been sold, legally licensed under the "Analogue in Dairy Context" category, in ways that mislead buyers and restaurant diners. This is useful for GS2 (health governance, regulatory bodies) and the Society/nutrition angle, and a good Prelims hook on FSSAI's legal basis.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Legal basis: **Section 92, Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006** (FSSAI's power to make regulations).
- Existing licensees under "Analogue in Dairy Context" must **drop the word "paneer"** from nomenclature, labels and marketing. It is a **naming and labelling** restriction; the product itself is not banned.
- Precedent: in **April 2026** FSSAI's Western Region directed accurate naming and **menu disclosure** of cheese analogues by food businesses.
- **FSSAI**: a statutory body under the FSS Act 2006, set up in 2008, under the **Ministry of Health & Family Welfare**, headquartered in New Delhi.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Governance (Health / regulatory bodies) + GS1 Society (consumer issues).

**Static anchor:** Your "Statutory and regulatory bodies" sheet (FSSAI, CDSCO, BIS). **Nudge:** add a row "FSSAI — FSS Act 2006 — MoHFW — s.92 regulations" and cross-link to the Consumer Protection Act 2019 (CCPA on misleading advertisements).

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Polity, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *"The law against appeals to religion in elections must police the appeal, not the faith of the candidate or the voter."* In light of recent judicial decisions, **critically examine** the scope and limits of Section 123(3) of the Representation of the People Act, 1951.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Intro:** s.123(3) makes an appeal to vote (or refrain) on grounds of religion, race, caste, community or language a corrupt practice. It voids the election (s.100) and can disqualify the candidate (s.8A). It is the statutory form of secularism in elections.
2. **The broad reading:** *Abhiram Singh* (2017, 4:3) held that "his religion" includes the voter's religion, so an election is a secular exercise and any religious appeal is barred. The dissent warned that this would silence legitimate talk of caste and religious *disadvantage*. Earlier Hindutva cases (*Manohar Joshi*, 1995) had held that general references to religion are not appeals.
3. **The limit now drawn (Malegaon, 2026):** a religious *ritual* at a rally (a *dua*), support from religious organisations and general religious references are not an *appeal to vote on religion*. Corrupt practice is quasi-criminal and needs specific "material facts" and proof to a high standard; otherwise the petition fails at the threshold.
4. **Critical evaluation:** *For* — protects free speech (Art. 19(1)(a)) and religious practice (Art. 25) and keeps courts out of policing piety. *Against* — "dog-whistle" appeals slip through; delays in election petitions (MCC enforcement is weak, and the ECI cannot de-register parties) blunt deterrence; hate speech now often comes from surrogates and social media, which are hard to attribute to the candidate or their agent.
5. **Way forward / conclusion:** time-bound trial of election petitions (six months, s.86(7) in letter and spirit); statutory backing for the MCC; include content spread through social media/influencers in "agent" attribution. The test should stay *appeal-centred*, but enforcement must be faster.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** The SC (Nath, Mehta, Masih JJ., 25 Sep 2026) upheld the Bombay HC's dismissal: a *dua* is not a religious appeal under s.123(3). Statement 2 is wrong because election petitions are tried by the **High Court** (s.80A), and the SC hears the appeal (s.116A). Statement 3 is right because a proven corrupt practice is a ground under s.100(1)(b).

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** It enters into force on 20 Oct 2026 with 100% duty-free access for Indian exports, and dairy is fully excluded. Statement 3 is wrong: NZ's US$20 bn/15-year pledge is India's **second** such commitment, after the **EFTA TEPA** (US$100 bn).

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** This is the sixth cycle (after 2006, 2010, 2014, 2018, 2022). It covers all 58 reserves, adds solid waste management as a new criterion, and uses the IUCN-WCPA framework. It is run by NTCA with WII.

**Q4 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** SAT-SAAW was developed by DRDO's RCI Hyderabad and is being built by BDL. It is a 125-kg-class stand-off glide weapon against airfield targets. Statement 3 is wrong because it was procured under **Buy (Indian-IDDM)**, the top indigenous category.

**Q5 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** FSSAI comes under the **Ministry of Health & Family Welfare**, not Consumer Affairs. The draft (22 Sep 2026; 60-day comments) amends the 2011 Prohibition and Restrictions on Sales Regulations under s.92 of the FSS Act, 2006.

**Q6 — (b) 1 and 2 only.** The High Court is the election tribunal (s.80A), and *Abhiram Singh* (2017, 4:3) extended "his religion" to include the voter's religion. Statement 3 is wrong because the limit is **45 days** from the declaration of the result (s.81).

**Q7 — (c) 1 and 3 only.** NTCA was constituted in 2006 by amending the WPA 1972 (s.38L), and the State notifies a tiger reserve on NTCA's recommendation (s.38V). Statement 2 is wrong because NTCA is chaired by the **Union Minister of Environment, Forest and Climate Change**.
