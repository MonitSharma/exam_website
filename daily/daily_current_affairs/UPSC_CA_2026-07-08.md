### 2. Supreme Court: propounder of a Will bears an additional burden where "suspicious circumstances" exist — Polity & Governance (Judiciary)

**Headline:** In ***Sardari Lal v. Bishan Dass & Ors.* (2026 INSC 669, decided 6 July 2026)**, the Supreme Court held that where a Will is surrounded by suspicious circumstances, merely examining an attesting witness does **not** discharge the propounder's burden — the propounder must additionally satisfy the court's "judicial conscience" that the document reflects the testator's free and informed wishes.

**Exam angle:** A recurring UPSC static-meets-current theme — the **"suspicious circumstances" doctrine** in probate law (rooted in *H. Venkatachala Iyengar v. B.N. Thimmajamma*, 1959) intersecting with the **Indian Evidence Act, 1872, Section 68** (proof of execution of a document required by law to be attested).

**Key facts:** Complete **disinheritance of a dependent, cordial spouse** in favour of a stranger/distant relative, without explanation, can itself be a suspicious circumstance; **uninitialed alterations/cuttings** on a registered Will **deprive the propounder** of the legal presumption that its contents were read over and understood by the executant; the propounder's onus is to **dispel** — not merely address — such doubts.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity/Governance (Judiciary) / Law of Evidence.

**Static anchor:** Vision IAS/Laxmikanth "Landmark SC Judgments 2026" running list → add this case; cross-link to **Indian Evidence Act, 1872, S.68 & S.71**, and to the *H. Venkatachala Iyengar* doctrine note.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Polity/Governance & Judiciary, 250 words, 15 marks)

**"Discuss the doctrine of 'suspicious circumstances' in the probate of Wills under Indian law. In this light, examine the significance of the Supreme Court's 2026 ruling in *Sardari Lal v. Bishan Dass & Ors.*"**

*Model-answer skeleton:*
1. **Legal framework:** Indian Succession Act, 1925 (S.63 — mode of execution) + Indian Evidence Act, 1872 (S.68 — proof of execution by attesting witness); the propounder always bears the initial onus of proving due and valid execution.
2. **The doctrine:** Originating in *H. Venkatachala Iyengar v. B.N. Thimmajamma* (1959) — unnatural disposition, exclusion of natural heirs, an enfeebled testator, or unexplained alterations are "suspicious circumstances" that shift an *additional* burden onto the propounder to affirmatively dispel doubt, not just produce an attesting witness.
3. **2026 ruling's contribution:** Extends the doctrine to (i) complete disinheritance of a dependent, cordial spouse in favour of a stranger, and (ii) uninitialed alterations on a registered Will, holding both can defeat the ordinary presumption of due execution.
4. **Significance:** Strengthens protection for vulnerable dependents (notably spouses) against fraudulent or coerced testamentary dispositions; reinforces judicial scrutiny as a safeguard distinct from mere procedural compliance; reiterates courts' role in weighing "judicial conscience" over technical proof.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1. (c) All three.** All three statements are correct per the June 2026 MoEFCC notification: S.39 BDA 2002, NBA recommendation, 21st repository, and its specimen-custodian role.

**Q2. (b) 2 only.** Statement 1 is wrong — the Court held mere examination of an attesting witness is **not** enough where suspicion exists; the propounder must additionally dispel the doubt. Statement 2 is correct. Statement 3 is wrong — the Court held uninitialed alterations **do** deprive the propounder of the presumption of due execution.

**Q3. (c) 2014.** The pact builds on the India–Australia civil nuclear cooperation framework agreement signed in 2014, now being operationalised via a commercial uranium supply arrangement.

**Q4. (c) All three.** All three statements accurately reflect the notification: Rule 43A amendment, total of 42 notified ports, and DTAB consultation under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940.

**Q5. (c) All three.** All three are correct: C25 is being replaced by the higher-capacity C32; the upgrade raises GTO payload capacity (~4,000 kg → ~5,200 kg); CE20 has flown on Chandrayaan-2 and Chandrayaan-3 among 8 successive LVM3 missions.

**Q6. (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong — the NBA's headquarters is in **Chennai**, not New Delhi.

**Q7. (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct static principles: S.68 Evidence Act attestation-proof requirement, the propounder's initial onus, and the suspicious-circumstances burden-shift — all directly illustrated by today's Sardari Lal ruling.
