## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Census 2027: Ladakh becomes the first place in independent India to enumerate caste in a statutory Census — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** Ladakh and the designated snow-bound tracts begin house-to-house Population Enumeration on 1 September 2026 — five months ahead of the rest of India — and will be the first territory ever to operationalise the caste column of Census 2027.

**Exam angle:** This is the single highest-probability Polity/Governance story of the cycle. Prelims will test *process* — which Act, which phase, which reference date, what is different from SECC 2011. Mains will test *consequences* — reservation jurisprudence (the 50% ceiling, the Indra Sawhney "adequate data" requirement, *Davinder Singh* 2024 on sub-classification), federal demands for state caste surveys, and the risk of enumeration hardening identity. The Ladakh angle adds a second layer: a border UT where the first digital Census has to make a deliberate exception for national security.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **Sequence in Ladakh:** self-enumeration (digital, web portal) **17–31 August 2026** → house-to-house Population Enumeration **1–30 September 2026** → revisional round **1–5 October 2026**. Reference date for these snow-bound areas is **1 October 2026**; for the rest of India, **1 March 2027** (PE in **February 2027**).
- Also covered early: snow-bound areas of **Jammu & Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh and Uttarakhand**.
- **Caste column is open-ended** for respondents *other than* SC and ST — i.e. the respondent states the caste, it is not picked from a pre-coded RGI list.
- Methodology was validated in the **Population Enumeration pre-test, 1–20 July 2026, across 16 States/UTs**. The PE schedule carries roughly **40 questions**.
- **Census 2027 is India's first digital Census.** Exception: sensitive defence establishments along the **LAC in Ladakh** are recorded on **paper schedules as "special charges"** to avoid geo-tagging.
- **Contrast with SECC 2011:** SECC was run *outside* the Census Act, 1948 (by MoRD/MoHUA), used open response, and threw up **over 46 lakh caste names** — unusable for policy. Census 2027 caste enumeration is **inside the statutory Census**, with the confidentiality and legal-penalty architecture of the 1948 Act.
- Last full caste enumeration in a regular Census: **1931**. Centre has released **₹4,102 crore** for Phase 2.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (also GS1 Indian Society).

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth* — chapter on **Constitutional/Statutory bodies & the Union executive** (Registrar General and Census Commissioner of India, under MHA); cross-read with the **Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes** chapter and **Article 340 / Articles 15(4), 16(4)**.
> *Update nudge:* On your Laxmikanth "Reservation" page, add a three-line box — **1931 → SECC 2011 (extra-statutory, 46 lakh names) → Census 2027 (statutory, open-ended column)** — and cross-link it to *Indra Sawhney* (1992) "adequate data" and *State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh* (2024).

### 2. UNSC holds the second straw poll for the next UN Secretary-General — International Relations

**Headline:** The Security Council has completed a second round of straw polls to pick a successor to António Guterres, whose second term ends on **31 December 2026**.

**Exam angle:** UPSC loves the *mechanics* of international institutions far more than the personalities. The straw-poll system — informal, anonymous, and with coloured P5 ballots in later rounds — is exactly the kind of "process detail" that becomes a three-statement Prelims question. For Mains, it is a live illustration of the argument for UNSC reform: the world's top diplomat is effectively chosen by five States.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **Article 97 of the UN Charter:** the Secretary-General is **appointed by the General Assembly upon the recommendation of the Security Council**.
- To be recommended a candidate needs **at least 9 of 15 affirmative votes** and **no veto** from any of the **P5 (US, UK, France, Russia, China)**.
- **Straw polls are informal and anonymous.** Members mark each candidate **"Encourage" / "Discourage" / "No Opinion"**. In later rounds the **P5 use differently coloured ballots**, so a **"Discourage" from a P5 member signals a veto**.
- Process begins with a **joint letter from the Presidents of the UNGA and UNSC** inviting all **193** Member States to nominate; candidates file CVs and vision statements and face **publicly broadcast informal dialogues**.
- The **Charter does not fix a term**; the **five-year term, renewable once**, is convention — as is **regional rotation** among the five UN regional groups (Africa, Asia-Pacific, Eastern Europe, Latin America & Caribbean, Western Europe & Others).
- UNGA appointment needs only a **simple majority**, but is traditionally by **consensus/acclamation**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (Important International Institutions).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal / IR notes* — **UN organs pillar**; and your **UNSC reform** page (G4, Uniting for Consensus/Coffee Club, Ezulwini Consensus, IGN process).
> *Update nudge:* Add a small table to the UNSC page: **Charter articles at a glance — Art. 23 (composition), Art. 27 (voting/veto), Art. 97 (SG appointment), Art. 108–109 (amendment)**. Most reform questions are answerable from those four.

### 3. Sugar goes from expected surplus to a 40% retail spike — the policy toolkit comes out — Agriculture

**Headline:** Retail sugar prices in major cities rose roughly **40% within a fortnight** in August 2026, and the Centre has responded with duty-free import quotas and stockholding limits.

**Exam angle:** A textbook case study for GS3 — food inflation, the **FRP–SAP dual pricing** distortion, the **ethanol–food security trade-off**, and the cost of ad-hoc trade policy. Note the counter-intuitive finding: ethanol diversion was *not* the main trigger. That nuance is exactly what a well-set Mains answer needs.

**Facts to memorise:**

- India is the world's **second-largest producer** of sugar (after Brazil) and the **largest consumer**. Normal output **320–340 LMT** against consumption of **280–290 LMT**.
- **2025-26:** initial estimate **343 LMT**, revised down to **306 LMT**. AISTA's net estimate fell to about **28.3 MT**. Around **8 LMT** had already been exported.
- **Triggers:** excess rainfall/waterlogging in **Maharashtra** plus **red rot and top borer**; festive demand (Onam, Raksha Bandhan); low carry-over stock; hoarding/speculative buying.
- **Global squeeze:** a **33 LMT deficit** projected for 2026-27; world prices up **over 16%**, from **USD 474/tonne (June)** to **USD 552/tonne (August 2026)**.
- **Ethanol diversion in 2025-26 was ~3 MT** — lower than expected, so *not* the primary cause.
- **Government response:** duty-free raw-sugar import under a **Tariff-Rate Quota of up to 10 LMT till 31 October** (normal basic customs duty on sugar is **100%**); under the **Essential Commodities Act, 1955**, a **400-tonne stock limit on dealers from 1 August to 30 November 2026**, and from **1 September 2026** bulk consumers capped at **15 days'** consumption; early start to crushing; joint Centre–State physical verification of mill stocks.
- **Structural:** Centre fixes **FRP** (on CACP advice); States announce higher **SAP**. Cane is **65–75% of mill costs**. **Rangarajan Committee (2012)** — revenue-sharing formula; **NITI Aayog Task Force (2020)** — crop diversification, dynamic sugar MSP linked to FRP, mills as **integrated bio-refineries**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture / Economy.

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* — **Agriculture pillar (marketing, MSP/FRP, buffer stocks)**; *Shankar IAS* — groundwater/water-stress section for the sugarcane-in-semi-arid-Maharashtra argument.
> *Update nudge:* On your MSP page, draw the **FRP vs SAP** two-column box (who fixes, statutory basis, why SAP > FRP creates cane arrears) and cross-link it to the **Ethanol Blended Petrol / E20** page under Energy.

### 4. India and Russia hold a snow-leopard roundtable in Moscow — the IBCA starts doing bilateral work — Environment

**Headline:** India took part in the **Russian–Indian Roundtable Meeting on Snow Leopard Study and Conservation on 26 August 2026** at the **A.N. Severtsov Institute of Ecology and Evolution, Russian Academy of Sciences, Moscow**.

**Exam angle:** The significant bit is institutional, not zoological: this is the **International Big Cat Alliance (IBCA)** — an India-headquartered body — being used as the *legal framework* for a bilateral science partnership. That is a clean example of India converting a conservation initiative into diplomatic infrastructure. Pair it with the standard snow-leopard static set, which UPSC has repeatedly mined for Prelims.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Held under the **Partnership Agreement between the Interregional Association 'Irbis' (Russia) and the International Big Cat Alliance (IBCA), signed December 2025**.
- Indian side: **MoEFCC** delegation; **Wildlife Institute of India (WII)** presented advances in **population estimation, radio telemetry and genetic analysis**.
- Russian side: Ministry of Natural Resources and Environment, Severtsov Institute, Moscow Zoo, **Rosprirodnadzor**.
- **Static to attach:** snow leopard (*Panthera uncia*) — **IUCN Vulnerable** (down-listed from Endangered in 2017), **CITES Appendix I**, **Schedule I** of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972.
- **Project Snow Leopard, 2009** (MoEFCC). **GSLEP** — Global Snow Leopard and Ecosystem Protection Programme, launched at **Bishkek, 2013**, with **12 range countries**.
- **SPAI (Status of Snow Leopard in India), released 2024:** **718** snow leopards — Ladakh 477, Uttarakhand 124, Himachal Pradesh 51, Arunachal Pradesh 36, Sikkim 21, J&K 9.
- Three Indian snow-leopard landscapes: **Hemis–Spiti**, **Nanda Devi–Gangotri**, **Khangchendzonga–Tawang**. **Hemis National Park (Ladakh)** is India's largest national park by area.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Biodiversity (with a GS2/IR cross-tag for the IBCA angle).

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment* — **Biodiversity Conservation / Protected Areas** and the **species-in-news** appendix.
> *Update nudge:* Add IBCA to your "India-led plurilaterals" list alongside **ISA, CDRI, Global Biofuels Alliance, IPOI** — with the one-line differentiator: **IBCA (2023, HQ India, treaty-based from 2024) covers seven big cats; GSLEP (2013, Bishkek) covers only the snow leopard across 12 range states.**

### 5. An 1,800-year-old Saraswati holding an arched harp reopens a lost chapter of Indian music — Art & Culture

**Headline:** An iconographic survey of a **2nd-century CE black sandstone Saraswati idol** from **Manasa (Neemuch district, Madhya Pradesh)**, now in the **Ujjain Museum**, has identified it as the oldest Saraswati image in MP holding a musical instrument — an **arched, harp-like veena**.

**Exam angle:** Art & Culture Prelims questions increasingly hang on *chronology of an object type* rather than on a single monument. The arched harp gives you a clean thread — Bronze Age rock art → Rigveda → Natyashastra → Gupta coinage → survival abroad. Also a rare "Places in News" hook for the **Malava** people, who appear in Alexander-era Greek/Roman accounts and again in Mandsaur epigraphy.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Excavated in the **1980s** at **Manasa, Neemuch district, MP**; housed at the **Ujjain Museum**; dated to the **2nd century CE**, the **Malava era**.
- Depicts a sweeping **arched (harp-like) veena** — an ancient branch of the Indian string tradition, distinct from the later stick-zither veena.
- **Chronology of the Indian arched harp:** Bronze Age rock paintings at **Nimbu Bhoj cave near Pachmarhi** → references in the **Rigveda** → systematic treatment in **Bharata's Natyashastra** → depicted on the **gold coinage of Samudragupta (c. 330–376 CE)**.
- The tradition largely died out in India but **survives in Myanmar as the *saung-gauk***.
- **The Malavas:** their resistance to **Alexander the Great** is recorded by the Roman historian **Quintus Curtius Rufus** in *Historiae Alexandri Magni*; the community later moved south toward **Mandsaur**.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Art & Culture.

**Static anchor:** *Nitin Singhania* — **Indian Music** chapter (classification of instruments: *tata/ghana/sushira/avanaddha*); *NCERT — An Introduction to Indian Art (Class XI)* on **Gupta coinage**.
> *Update nudge:* On the Samudragupta page, next to the **Ashvamedha, Archer, Battle-axe, Tiger-slayer** coin types, write in the **Lyrist (Veenavadini) type** and link it back to this arched-harp entry — the "musician emperor" coin is the standard Prelims hook.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS1 Indian Society / GS2 Governance — 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** The inclusion of caste in Census 2027 ends a seventy-year statistical silence in India's decennial enumeration. **Examine** the administrative and social implications of counting caste through a statutory Census rather than through a survey of the SECC 2011 type.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Set the baseline.** Caste-wise data for the whole population was last published in **1931**. SECC 2011 attempted it outside the Census Act, 1948, generating **over 46 lakh caste names** — statistically unusable. Census 2027 places caste inside the statutory instrument for the first time since Independence, beginning with Ladakh in September 2026.

2. **Administrative gains.** Statutory backing brings a trained enumerator cadre, a standard schedule (~40 questions), legal confidentiality and penalties, and a single reference date — which is what makes data comparable across States. It also supplies the "adequate quantifiable data" that *Indra Sawhney* (1992) requires for backwardness claims, and the empirical basis for sub-classification permitted by *State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh* (2024). Ends the current patchwork of unilateral State caste surveys.

3. **Administrative risks.** The **open-ended caste column** for non-SC/ST respondents reproduces SECC's core problem — synonyms, sub-castes, gotra and surname entries — unless post-enumeration classification is rigorous. Risk of strategic misreporting where benefits are tied to category; and, uniquely in a border UT like Ladakh, the security carve-out of **paper "special charges"** shows digital enumeration is not uniformly applicable.

4. **Social implications — both directions.** Counting can make invisible deprivation legible and shift welfare from proxy to evidence; equally, enumeration can harden identity, revive mobilisation around numbers, and fuel demands that collide with the **50% ceiling**. Conclude on the distinction that matters: **caste data is a diagnostic instrument, not a policy in itself** — its value depends entirely on whether the classification and the subsequent policy design are disciplined.

Sugarcane can be cultivated using settlings prepared from tissue culture." — **Answer: (c) 1 and 4 only.**

**Caste (verified):**

- *Mains 2018 (GS1):* "'Caste system is assuming new identities and associational forms. Hence, caste system cannot be eradicated in India.' Comment."

**Takeaway:** UPSC asks caste as a **sociological transformation** question (vertical hierarchy → horizontal association), not as a data question. A 2027-cycle answer should now use the Census 2027 enumeration as *evidence* of that associational turn, not as the subject of the answer.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (b).** Statements 1 and 3 are correct: PE in Ladakh and snow-bound tracts runs 1–30 September 2026 against February 2027 for the rest of India, and Census 2027 is India's first digital Census. Statement 2 is wrong — caste is to be captured in an **open-ended column** for respondents other than SC and ST, not from a pre-coded RGI list.

**Q2 — (a).** Only statement 1 is correct (Article 97, UN Charter). Statement 2 is wrong: straw polls are **informal and anonymous**, not formal recorded votes. Statement 3 is wrong: the Charter is **silent** on the term — the five-year, once-renewable term is established practice.

**Q3 — (b).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: a 400-tonne dealer stock limit (1 Aug–30 Nov 2026) under the Essential Commodities Act, 1955, and duty-free raw-sugar imports of up to 10 LMT under a TRQ till 31 October. Statement 3 is wrong — sugarcane pricing continues under the **FRP** (Centre) and **SAP** (States); no MSP replacement was announced.

**Q4 — (b).** The roundtable of 26 August 2026 in Moscow was held under the December 2025 Partnership Agreement between the Interregional Association 'Irbis' and the **International Big Cat Alliance**, the India-headquartered body covering seven big cats.

**Q5 — (d).** All three are correct: the idol shows an arched harp-like veena; it dates to the 2nd century CE Malava era and was excavated at Manasa in Neemuch district; and the arched harp appears on Samudragupta's gold **Lyrist** coinage.

**Q6 — (a).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: **SECC 2011 was conducted outside the Census Act, 1948**, by the Ministries of Rural Development and Housing & Urban Affairs — which is precisely why its data carried no statutory confidentiality and produced unusable caste counts.

**Q7 — (d).** All three are correct. The snow leopard is a Schedule I species under the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972 (also IUCN **Vulnerable** and CITES **Appendix I**); Project Snow Leopard dates to 2009; and GSLEP, launched at Bishkek in 2013, spans 12 range countries.
