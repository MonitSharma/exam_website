### 2. India reported to mandate 1% SAF blending for international flights from January 2027 — Environment
- **Exam angle:** Industry sources cited by The Hindu say the blending path is 1% (2027), 2% (2028) and 5% (2030), tied to ICAO's CORSIA, whose mandatory phase runs 2027–2035. It is a climate-meets-trade story: Indian carriers face offsetting liability if they do not blend. **Status: reported, official notification awaited.**
- **Memorize:** CORSIA adopted by ICAO in 2016; SAF costs roughly 2–5x conventional jet fuel; feedstocks include used cooking oil and agricultural residues; Indian Oil's Panipat refinery (about 30,000 t/yr) is expected to cover the first-year 1%; an ICAO study puts India's potential at 14–33 million tonnes of SAF.
- **Notebook:** GS3 Environment / Economy (Energy, Aviation).

### 3. UDAN completes ten years (21 Oct 2016 – 21 Oct 2026); Viksit UDAN takes over — Economy (Schemes/Infrastructure)
- **Exam angle:** UDAN is the market-based Regional Connectivity Scheme under NCAP 2016. A decade on, the next phase shifts from route subsidy towards airport and helipad creation. Good for Mains questions on regional development and inclusive growth.
- **Memorize:** Operational airports 74 (2014) to 166 (2026); 687 routes, 95 aerodromes (17 heliports, 2 water aerodromes), about 1.72 crore passengers; VGF disbursed about ₹5,091 crore (15 Oct 2026). **Viksit UDAN:** FY2026-27 to FY2035-36, about ₹28,840 crore; 100 airports from unserved airstrips (₹12,159 crore); 200 helipads (₹3,661 crore); O&M support for about 441 aerodromes (₹2,577 crore); VGF about ₹10,043 crore over ten years, up to five years per route.

### 4. SC: conversion alone does not carry over backward-class benefits (Secretary to Government v. Sameer Ahamed, 15 Oct) — Polity & Governance
- **Exam angle:** A bench of Justices Prashant Kumar Mishra and Shree Chandrashekhar dismissed Tamil Nadu's challenge and upheld the Madras High Court, which had invalidated a 9 March 2024 Government Order that let converts to Islam from certain backgrounds obtain BC (Muslim) certificates. The Court said historical disadvantage in one religion cannot be treated as proof of membership of a backward class in another; converts to religions not listed in the Constitution (Scheduled Castes) Order lose SC status. The ruling has reopened the debate on the religion-neutrality of OBC classification in Indra Sawhney (1992). The related Tamil Nadu certificates matter remains linked.
- **Memorize:** Article 16(4); SC Order 1950 (Hindus), Sikhs added 1956, Buddhists 1990; Indra Sawhney (1992) upheld 27% OBC quota, creamy layer, 50% ceiling.
- **Notebook:** GS2 Polity (Reservation); GS1 Society (caste).
- **Static anchor:** Laxmikanth "Fundamental Rights: Art 14–18" and "Social empowerment". Add the 2026 ruling under Art 16(4) and cross-link to the Andhra BC quota (Oct 12) note on the 50% ceiling.

### 5. eMudhra certificate-authority keys cracked; Chrome, Mozilla, Apple and Microsoft block the certificates — Science & Technology (Cybersecurity)
- **Exam angle:** Eight intermediate CA keys issued by Bengaluru-based eMudhra (a CA used in Indian digital-identity and tax infrastructure) were recoverable because a faulty key-generation step left them with too little randomness. Six were TLS certificates and two were code-signing. A compromised intermediate lets an attacker forge certificates for websites. eMudhra revoked the certificates; no malicious use had been seen as of the advisory. Links to the CERT-In AI-agents advisory (18 Oct) as a pattern of digital-trust failures.
- **Memorize:** Root CA → intermediate CA → end-entity certificate chain; Controller of Certifying Authorities (CCA) licenses CAs under the IT Act, 2000; Go team assigned CVE-2026-111943; the flawed key ceremony took place on 30 Apr 2025. (The Hindu reported Government platforms such as UIDAI and the Income Tax portal use eMudhra technology; the IT Ministry said it saw no sign the root certificates were compromised.)
- **Notebook:** GS3 Science & Technology / Cybersecurity.
- **Static anchor:** Mrunal/Shankar S&T "Cybersecurity"; add a PKI box (root, intermediate, revocation) and the 2014 NIC rogue-certificate precedent.

### B3. Mains question (150 words / 10 marks)
**Critically examine** the Supreme Court's recent reasoning on conversion and backward-class benefits in light of the religion-neutral approach to OBC identification adopted in *Indra Sawhney* (1992).
- **Point 1 (context):** Art 16(4) allows reservation for backward classes inadequately represented; OBC identification is on social and educational backwardness, not religion (Indra Sawhney); SC status, by contrast, is religion-linked under the 1950 Order.
- **Point 2 (the ruling):** Conversion alone does not transfer pre-conversion backwardness; a State order lumping converts into notified classes lacked a common basis.
- **Point 3 (critique):** Supports principled, evidence-based classification and prevents blanket claims; but it risks ignoring the lived reality that caste-like stratification persists among non-Hindu communities (Sachar Committee, Ranganath Misra Commission), and the "source of backwardness" test may create a hierarchy between religions.
- **Point 4 (way forward):** Evidence-based identification through NCBC/State commissions with caste-wise data (a caste census), case-by-case determination, and a Parliamentary approach to the SC Order question.
