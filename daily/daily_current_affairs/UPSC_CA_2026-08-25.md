### 1. Doval in Beijing for the 25th round of Special Representatives talks — three weeks before India hosts BRICS — International Relations

**Headline:** National Security Adviser **Ajit Doval** arrived in Beijing for the **25th round of India–China Special Representatives (SR) talks** on the boundary question with **Wang Yi**, China's Foreign Minister and its designated Special Representative, held on **25 August 2026** — the last high-level exchange before India hosts the **BRICS Summit in New Delhi on 12–13 September 2026**, which Chinese President Xi Jinping is expected to attend.

**Exam angle:**
- The SR mechanism is the **only dedicated bilateral channel** for the boundary question, and UPSC has repeatedly rewarded candidates who can name the instrument rather than narrate the news. Note the **alternating venue** convention: 23rd round Beijing (December 2024), 24th round New Delhi (August 2025, during Wang Yi's visit), 25th round Beijing (August 2026).
- The reported agenda is the examinable part: review of **disengagement** along the LAC, **confidence-building measures**, **border-management protocols**, and exploration of **"early harvest"** steps on delimitation in the **less contentious sectors** — i.e. sequencing the easier stretches (the Sikkim sector is the only one where the alignment is broadly agreed) ahead of the Western and Eastern sectors. Alongside this runs the normalisation basket: **direct flights, visas, trade and multilateral coordination**.
- The **timing** is the Mains line: India is chairing BRICS and hosting the summit, so border stabilisation and summit diplomacy are being run as a single sequence. Useful for the standard "can India compartmentalise the boundary dispute from the wider relationship?" question — and for the counter-argument that New Delhi's stated position since 2020 has been that **peace on the border is the precondition for normal ties**, not a parallel track.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **25th round of SR talks**, Beijing, **25 August 2026**. Indian SR: **NSA Ajit Doval**. Chinese SR: **Wang Yi** (Politburo member and Foreign Minister).
- SR mechanism **created in 2003**, during Prime Minister Vajpayee's visit to China. First pair of SRs: **Brajesh Mishra** and **Dai Bingguo**.
- Its principal output remains the **2005 Agreement on Political Parameters and Guiding Principles for the Settlement of the India–China Boundary Question** — Stage 1 of a **three-stage** design: political parameters → a framework → delineation and demarcation. **Stage 2 has never been completed.**
- The surrounding treaty architecture: **1993** Agreement on the Maintenance of Peace and Tranquillity along the LAC; **1996** Agreement on CBMs in the Military Field along the LAC; **2012** Working Mechanism for Consultation and Coordination (**WMCC**) — the official-level channel; **2013** Border Defence Cooperation Agreement (**BDCA**).
- Context: **BRICS Summit, New Delhi, 12–13 September 2026** under India's chairship.

### 2. Rajnath Singh clears transfer of *all* DRDO conventional missile technologies to Indian industry — Internal Security

**Headline:** On **25 August 2026**, Defence Minister **Rajnath Singh** approved the **transfer of technology (ToT) of all DRDO-developed conventional missile systems** to qualified Indian industry for domestic production — the first blanket, category-wide missile ToT clearance, as against the earlier practice of case-by-case licensing to a nominated production agency.

**Exam angle:**
- Read the word **"conventional"** carefully: in military usage it means missiles that destroy targets by **high explosive, fragmentation or kinetic impact**, and that carry **no nuclear, biological or chemical warhead**. Strategic systems (the **Agni** family, hypersonics) are therefore *outside* this decision — a classic Prelims trap.
- The structural point for GS3: India's missile programme has historically moved from **DRDO lab → DPSU (BDL, BEL) production**, with private firms as sub-vendors. A category-wide ToT is an attempt to move private primes and **MSMEs** into the missile supply chain itself, converting design capability into **industrial-scale capacity** and domestic **value addition**. Use it as the "capability vs capacity" case study.
- Distinguish it from the **17 August 2026** DRDO move, which is a separate track: **Research Centre Imarat (RCI), Hyderabad**, invited Indian companies to become **long-term manufacturing partners** for missiles and guided bombs on behalf of DRDO's **Missile and Strategic Systems (MSS) Cluster**.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Decision date **25 August 2026**; approving authority **Raksha Mantri**; covers **all conventional missile systems** developed by DRDO; transfers are subject to prescribed **qualifications, certifications and regulatory requirements**.
- The **MoD has not published a list** of covered systems. Commentary points to systems such as **Akash** (surface-to-air), **Astra** (air-to-air) and **Nag** (anti-tank guided missile) — treat this as *indicative, not official*.
- **MSS Cluster** (DRDO): design and development of missile and strategic systems; works on **ramjet and scramjet propulsion, guidance and homing, and warhead technologies**; the **Agni** series and hypersonic systems fall in its domain. **RCI, Hyderabad** issued the 17 August 2026 manufacturing-partner bid on its behalf.
- Stated objectives: reduce **import dependence**, raise **domestic value addition**, widen MSME participation, and shorten the **development-to-production** transition.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security (defence indigenisation, security challenges and their management in border areas) with a cross-tag to GS3 Economy (industrial policy).

**Static anchor:** Your **GS3 Internal Security — Defence Production / Aatmanirbharta** notes. → Add this under a single "indigenisation instruments" list so it does not float loose: *Defence Acquisition Procedure 2020 (Buy Indian–IDDM at the top of the priority chain) → **DPEPP 2020** → **Positive Indigenisation Lists** (the 6th, of 405 items worth ₹3,070 crore, was notified in August 2026 — see the 20 August briefing) → **SRIJAN** portal → **iDEX/DIO** → **Defence Industrial Corridors** in UP and Tamil Nadu → **DRDO ToT / Development-cum-Production Partner** route*. ### 3. Kerala High Court takes up the price of a patented cancer drug — Ribociclib, CDK4/6 inhibitors and the compulsory-licence question — Science & Technology

**Headline:** The **Kerala High Court** has allowed a **breast-cancer patient** undergoing treatment with **Ribociclib** to be impleaded in ongoing proceedings on the **exorbitant pricing of patented life-saving medicines**, and is examining whether the cheaper, locally manufactured, **off-patent Palbociclib** can be therapeutically substituted for **Ribociclib** and **Abemaciclib** — a question on which the Court has sought expert opinion.

**Exam angle:**
- This is the cleanest available hook for the **patents vs access-to-medicines** debate, which UPSC has asked in both Prelims (TRIPS flexibilities) and Mains (GS3 S&T / IPR). The specific novelty is that a **patient** — not a generic manufacturer or the State — is being heard on affordability.
- The science is testable: **CDK4/6 inhibitors** are **targeted oral therapies**, not cytotoxic chemotherapy. They inhibit **cyclin-dependent kinases 4 and 6**, blocking phosphorylation of the retinoblastoma protein and arresting the cell cycle at the **G1→S** transition. The class — Ribociclib, Palbociclib, Abemaciclib — is used in **hormone receptor-positive (HR+), HER2-negative** advanced or metastatic breast cancer.
- The trap the Court itself flagged: **patent expiry does not establish therapeutic equivalence**. Palbociclib being off-patent and cheap does not, by itself, make it a substitute for Ribociclib — the three CDK4/6 inhibitors differ in dosing, toxicity profile and trial-demonstrated survival benefit. Good material for a GS4/GS3 line on evidence-based policy.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **Ribociclib**: oral **CDK4/6 inhibitor**; blocks **cyclin-dependent kinases 4 and 6**; indication — **HR+/HER2− advanced or metastatic breast cancer**. Same class: **Palbociclib** (patent expired) and **Abemaciclib**.
- The statutory toolkit under the **Patents Act, 1970**: **Section 83** (general principles — patents must be worked in India, not for mere import monopoly, and must not impede public health); **Section 84** (**compulsory licence** obtainable after **three years** from grant, on any of three grounds — reasonable requirements of the public not satisfied, the invention not available at a **reasonably affordable price**, or the patent **not worked in India**); **Section 92** (CL by Central Government notification in a **national emergency, extreme urgency or for public non-commercial use**); **Section 100** (use of a patented invention **for the purposes of government**); **Section 66** (revocation in the public interest).
- India has granted exactly **one** compulsory licence to date: **Natco Pharma v. Bayer (2012)** for **Nexavar (sorafenib)**, a kidney and liver cancer drug — granted by the **Controller of Patents** at a **6% royalty**.
- Related static: **Section 3(d)** bars patents on new forms of known substances without enhanced efficacy — upheld in ***Novartis AG v. Union of India* (2013)** on imatinib mesylate (Glivec). Term of patent: **20 years** from filing.
- **Price control** operates on a separate track: the **DPCO 2013** and the **National List of Essential Medicines**, administered by the **NPPA**, with **Paragraph 19** allowing extraordinary price fixation in the public interest for non-scheduled drugs.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (IPR, indigenisation of technology, health technology) with a cross-tag to GS2 (Health / Judiciary).

**Static anchor:** Your **GS3 S&T — Intellectual Property Rights** chapter and *Mrunal* **Pillar 4 (industry/IPR)**. → Add a one-page "**access-to-medicines toolkit**" card: *Section 3(d) → Section 84/92/100 CL → Bolar exemption (s.107A) → parallel imports → DPCO 2013 + NLEM + NPPA Para 19 → TRIPS Article 31 and the **Doha Declaration on TRIPS and Public Health (2001)***. Cross-link the **National IPR Policy, 2016** and the single Indian CL precedent (**Natco–Bayer, 2012**) so you never have to guess whether India has "used" compulsory licensing.

### 4. 216 trafficked Star Tortoises go back to the wild at Tadoba under 'Project STRIPES' — Environment

**Headline:** **216 rescued Indian Star Tortoises** seized from wildlife-trafficking and illegal-possession cases in **Mumbai and Thane** have been repatriated to the **Tadoba–Andhari Tiger Reserve (TATR)** in Maharashtra under **Project STRIPES** — the *Star Tortoise Repatriation Initiative Protecting Endangered Species* — a joint effort of the **Maharashtra Forest Department (Mumbai Range)** and **RAWW (Resqink Association for Wildlife Welfare)**.

**Exam angle:**
- Most conservation news is about *seizure*; the examinable novelty here is **structured post-seizure disposal**. Under the Wild Life (Protection) Act the State becomes custodian of confiscated live animals, and the standing problem is what to do with them — captivity is expensive and release is ecologically risky. Project STRIPES builds a repeatable pipeline: **rescue → veterinary and health assessment → species and provenance verification → habitat-suitability matching → periodic soft release**.
- The trap: a rescued animal **cannot be released anywhere**. Star Tortoises have **genetically distinct regional populations** (broadly a north-western Indian and a southern Indian/Sri Lankan lineage); releasing trafficked animals of unknown origin risks **genetic swamping** and **pathogen introduction**. This is exactly the nuance that separates a 2-mark answer from a 5-mark one.
- The **demand-side** point for Mains: the Indian Star Tortoise is trafficked overwhelmingly for the **exotic-pet trade**, not for meat or medicine — which is why enforcement at **airports and courier hubs** matters more than forest patrolling for this species.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **216** tortoises; source **Mumbai and Thane**; destination **Tadoba–Andhari Tiger Reserve, Chandrapur district, Maharashtra**; executed by a **10-member** team of the Maharashtra Forest Department and **RAWW**.
- **Indian Star Tortoise — *Geochelone elegans***: terrestrial tortoise with radiating star patterns on the carapace; range **India, Pakistan and Sri Lanka**; habitat **dry scrub forest, thorn forest and grassland**.
- **IUCN Red List: Vulnerable.** **CITES: Appendix I** — uplisted from Appendix II at **CoP18 (Geneva, 2019)**, an India-backed proposal. Protected under the **Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972** (Schedule I after the 2022 Amendment restructured the schedules to four).
- **TATR**: Maharashtra's **oldest and largest** national park (Tadoba National Park, 1955) combined with the **Andhari Wildlife Sanctuary (1986)**; declared a **tiger reserve in 1993–95** under Project Tiger; lies in the **Chandrapur** district of Vidarbha.
- Distinguish **tortoise** (terrestrial, columnar limbs, high-domed shell) from **freshwater turtle** and **marine turtle** (flippers, streamlined shell) — a recurring Prelims discriminator.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (biodiversity conservation, wildlife trade, protected-area management).

**Static anchor:** ***Shankar IAS Environment*, chapter on Biodiversity Conservation / Conservation Efforts.** → Add a species card for *Geochelone elegans* alongside the other trade-driven species you already track (pangolin, tokay gecko, red sanders, Tibetan antelope) with columns for **IUCN status, CITES Appendix, WPA Schedule**. Cross-link the **CITES Appendix I vs II** distinction (Appendix I = commercial international trade prohibited; Appendix II = regulated by permit) and add a margin note: "**Repatriation ≠ release**: provenance, genetics, disease screening and habitat suitability first." Cross-link TATR to your Maharashtra protected-areas map.

### 5. A cloud-and-AI skilling push aimed at Tier-2 and Tier-3 towns — NSDC, MSDE and AWS — Economy

**Headline:** A PIB release of **24 August 2026** announces a nationwide **Cloud and AI Skilling Initiative** by the **National Skill Development Corporation (NSDC)** and **Cultus Education** in collaboration with **Amazon Web Services (AWS)**, supported by the **Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE)**, targeting youth in **Tier-2 and Tier-3 cities**.

**Exam angle:**
- The Economy angle is **human capital and the employability gap**, not the technology. India's recurring problem is that the workforce grows faster than *formal-sector-ready* skills; the fully **online** delivery model here is explicitly designed to remove the **relocation cost** that has historically excluded small-town and female candidates from metro-based training.
- The examinable design feature is the **funnel**: a wide, shallow tier (**1.5 lakh learners**, foundational literacy) narrowing to a deep, job-linked tier (**10,000 learners**, intensive cohort training with an **industry-recognised certification**). Contrast this with volume-only skilling schemes that report enrolment but not placement — the standard CAG/Standing Committee criticism of PMKVY.
- **Do not confuse** this with the **IndiaAI Mission** (MeitY) or with **Skill India Digital Hub**. The distinguishing marker here is **MSDE → NSDC → private training partner + cloud provider**, i.e. a PPP skilling model, not a government compute/AI mission.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **1.5 lakh learners** to receive foundational **AI and cloud literacy** through AWS digital training content; **10,000 participants** to receive intensive, job-oriented training through **AWS re/Start** (a free, cohort-based workforce-development programme).
- Curriculum spans **cloud infrastructure, networking, cybersecurity, programming, databases and AI fundamentals**; delivery **100% online** via Cultus Education; culminates in a **National Hackathon**; successful candidates earn an **AWS Certification**.
- Institutional chain: **MSDE → NSDC → Cultus Education + AWS**.
- **NSDC static**: incorporated **31 July 2008** as a **not-for-profit public limited company** (Section 25 of the Companies Act, 1956 / now Section 8 of the 2013 Act); **Government of India holds 49%** and the **private sector 51%** — the standard "PPP in skilling" example; originally under the Ministry of Finance, now under **MSDE**, which was created in **2014**. The **Skill India Mission** was launched on **15 July 2015** (World Youth Skills Day).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (human resource development, employment, inclusive growth) with a cross-tag to GS2 (Government policies and interventions).

**Static anchor:** ***Mrunal* Pillar 6 — Human Resource Development / Skill Development**, and the **Economic Survey** chapter on employment. → Update the skilling-architecture page with a clean institutional line: *MSDE (2014) → **NSDC** (2008, 49:51 GoI–private) → Sector Skill Councils → **NSQF** → **PMKVY 4.0** → Skill India Digital Hub*, and add this initiative as the "**private-cloud-provider PPP**" example. ### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Science & Technology, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *"India's patent law was written to hold innovation incentives and access to medicines in the same frame."* **Examine** the statutory instruments available to the State when a patented life-saving drug is priced beyond the reach of patients, and the reasons why these instruments have been used so sparingly.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the tension in one line.** A patent is a time-limited exclusionary right (20 years) intended to reward disclosure and investment; for a life-saving drug that exclusivity translates directly into a price that can exceed household capacity. India's 1970 Act, and the post-2005 TRIPS-compliant version, both retain public-interest levers — the question is not whether they exist but why they lie idle.

2. **Map the instruments — patentability, licensing, price.** *Grant stage*: Section 3(d) filters ever-greening (*Novartis*, 2013); pre- and post-grant opposition. *Post-grant*: Section 84 compulsory licence after three years on three grounds, including unaffordable price; Section 92 CL by government notification in national emergency, extreme urgency or public non-commercial use; Section 100 government use; Section 66 revocation in the public interest; Section 107A Bolar exemption and parallel imports. *Outside patent law*: DPCO 2013, NLEM and NPPA — including Paragraph 19 extraordinary price fixation — plus government procurement and public-sector production.

3. **Explain the sparing use.** Exactly one CL granted (Natco–Bayer, *Nexavar*, 2012, at 6% royalty) and none since; the reasons include sustained trade pressure (US Special 301 listings, the "innovation-hostile jurisdiction" narrative), fear of chilling FDI and of retaliation in ongoing trade negotiations, litigation cost and delay before the Controller and appellate forums, the burden on the applicant to establish grounds, the absence of a domestic manufacturer willing to invest in complex biologics and targeted small molecules, and the fact that voluntary licences and price-tiering by originators reduce the political pressure to act.

4. **Add the constraint the Kerala High Court has surfaced.** Substitution is a clinical question, not merely a price one — an off-patent analogue in the same class is not automatically therapeutically equivalent. Any access remedy must rest on expert clinical evidence, which argues for institutional capacity (a standing expert body advising on therapeutic equivalence and priority drugs) rather than case-by-case judicial improvisation.

5. **Close with a calibrated position.** The gap is one of institutional readiness, not statutory design: a transparent, pre-published framework for when Section 84/92 will be invoked; NLEM expansion to cover high-burden oncology; strengthened public manufacturing; and reliance on the Doha Declaration's affirmation that TRIPS "can and should be interpreted and implemented in a manner supportive of public health".

## PART C — ANSWERS

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statement 3 is incorrect: India is represented in the SR mechanism by the **National Security Adviser** (currently Ajit Doval), while China's SR is its **Foreign Minister**, Wang Yi. The asymmetry of designation is itself a favourite detail. The mechanism dates to 2003 and produced the 2005 Political Parameters agreement, which remains Stage 1 of the agreed three-stage process.

**Q2 — (b).** In military usage a "conventional" missile is defined by its **warhead**, not its launch platform, speed or guidance. It destroys targets through high explosive, fragmentation or kinetic impact and carries no nuclear, biological or chemical warhead — which is why strategic systems such as the Agni family fall outside the August 2026 ToT approval.

**Q3 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** Statement 2 is wrong: Ribociclib is an **oral targeted therapy** (a CDK4/6 inhibitor acting at the G1→S checkpoint), not an intravenous cytotoxic chemotherapy agent. Statement 3 correctly states the Section 84 threshold — a compulsory-licence application may ordinarily be made after **three years** from grant.

**Q4 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statement 3 is wrong: *Geochelone elegans* is **Vulnerable** on the IUCN Red List, not Critically Endangered. It occurs in India, Pakistan and Sri Lanka, and was uplisted to **CITES Appendix I** at CoP18 (Geneva, 2019), which prohibits commercial international trade in wild-taken specimens.

**Q5 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statement 3 is wrong: NSDC is **not a statutory body**. It is a not-for-profit public limited company incorporated in 2008, with the Government of India holding **49%** (a minority stake) and the private sector **51%**, functioning under the administrative control of MSDE. Distinguish it from the **NSDA** and from Sector Skill Councils.

**Q6 — (a) 1 – 2 – 3 – 4.** Peace and Tranquillity Agreement **1993** → Military CBMs Agreement **1996** → **WMCC** established **2012** → **BDCA** signed **2013**. A useful mnemonic: the two 1990s agreements set the *norms*; the two 2010s instruments built the *machinery*.

**Q7 — (b) 1 and 2 only.** Statement 3 is wrong: India granted a compulsory licence to **Natco Pharma** in **2012** for Bayer's **Nexavar (sorafenib)** at a 6% royalty — the only such grant to date, which is precisely why it is examinable. Statements 1 and 2 correctly restate **Section 84** and **Section 92** respectively.
