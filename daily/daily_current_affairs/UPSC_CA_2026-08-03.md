# UPSC Daily CA Briefing — 3 August 2026

## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Lok Sabha passes the Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Bill, 2026 — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** On 31 July 2026 the Lok Sabha passed, by voice vote amid Opposition uproar, the Registration of Births and Deaths (Amendment) Bill, 2026, introduced by MoS Home Nityanand Rai; it now goes to the Rajya Sabha.

**Exam angle:** This is the second amendment to the Registration of Births and Deaths (RBD) Act, 1969 in three years, and it targets the single most litigated provision — *delayed registration* under Section 13. Since the 2023 amendment made the birth certificate a **single, universal proof-of-date-of-birth document** (school admission, driving licence, voter roll, Aadhaar, passport, marriage registration, government employment), the integrity of late-entry registration became a document-fraud question, not a clerical one. Expect this as a "consider the following statements" Prelims item and as a GS2 governance/e-governance illustration.

**Key facts to memorise:**
- Amends the **RBD Act, 1969**; parent Act administered by the **Registrar General & Census Commissioner of India (RGI)**, Ministry of Home Affairs; actual registration is done by State-appointed Registrars.
- Existing rule: registration within **21 days** is free/normal; 21–30 days and 30 days–1 year attract escalating permissions; **beyond 1 year** required an order of a **magistrate of the first class**.
- New two-tier structure: delay of **more than 1 year but within 2 years** → registrable on approval of the **District Magistrate / Sub-Divisional Magistrate / authorised Executive Magistrate** after verification of authenticity; delay of **more than 2 years** → only on the order of a **Judicial Magistrate of the First Class**.
- Effect: an **executive** route for the 1–2 year window, a **judicial** route beyond it.
- Recall from the **RBD (Amendment) Act, 2023** (in force 1 October 2023): national and State-level databases of registered births and deaths; Aadhaar of parents/informant may be collected; electronic certificates.
- Constitutional hook: *"Vital statistics including registration of births and deaths"* is **Entry 30 of the Concurrent List** (List III).

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Union–State legislative relations; e-governance; statutory bodies).

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth* — Chapter on **Centre–State Legislative Relations (Seventh Schedule)** and the chapter on **Union Executive/administrative machinery**. **Update:** add a margin note under Concurrent List Entry 30 — "RBD Act 1969 → 2023 amendment (single document + national database) → 2026 amendment (two-tier delayed registration: Executive Magistrate 1–2 yrs, Judicial Magistrate >2 yrs)". Cross-link to your Census/CRS-CRVS page.

### 2. India launches its first sovereign-backed marine P&I insurance product under the Bharat Maritime Insurance Pool — Economy

**Headline:** On 30 July 2026 the Department of Financial Services (Ministry of Finance) launched India's first **sovereign-backed Protection & Indemnity (P&I) insurance product** under the **Bharat Maritime Insurance Pool (BMIP)**, with the first policy issued by **The New India Assurance Company** to the **Shipping Corporation of India**.

**Exam angle:** A textbook case of **strategic autonomy in financial infrastructure**. Global P&I cover is dominated by the London-centred **International Group of P&I Clubs**; sanctions or war-risk withdrawal there can strand Indian-flagged tonnage overnight (recall the LNG-tanker delays during the West Asia conflict). A sovereign guarantee converts a private-market chokepoint into a domestic one. This is the maritime twin of the "de-risking payments" story — pair it with UPI-internationalisation and rupee trade settlement in your Mains notes.

**Key facts to memorise:**
- **BMIP**: Cabinet-approved with a **sovereign guarantee of ₹12,980 crore (~USD 1.5 billion)**; **operational since 12 May 2026**.
- Original mandate: **war-risk** cover (hull war-risk, cargo war-risk). The 30 July launch **expands BMIP beyond war risk into P&I**.
- **P&I = third-party liability**: crew liability, cargo liability, **pollution liability**, **wreck removal**, plus a 24×7 port-correspondent network. (Distinct from **Hull & Machinery**, which covers damage to the ship itself.)
- Combined indemnity limit: **up to USD 1.5 billion**.
- Outcome data: war-risk premiums down ~**35–40%** from peak; **1,608 policies** issued as of 29 July 2026.
- Regulator for insurance: **IRDAI**; the governing commercial statute is the **Marine Insurance Act, 1963**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (infrastructure, shipping, financial services); GS2 — IR (economic statecraft).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* — **Pillar 4B/4C: Financial Markets & Insurance Sector** (and the Infrastructure pillar for shipping/ports). **Update:** open a sub-head "Sovereign risk-pooling instruments" and list BMIP alongside the government-backed export-credit and crop-insurance architecture; cross-link to your Maritime India Vision / Sagarmala / Merchant Shipping Act, 2025 notes.

### 3. Ministry of Ayush signs an MoU with the IndiaAI Mission; Ayush datasets to be onboarded onto AIKosh — Science & Technology

**Headline:** On 31 July 2026 the **Ministry of Ayush** and **IndiaAI** (MeitY) signed a two-year MoU to drive AI-led research, digital health and capacity building across Ayurveda, Yoga, Naturopathy, Unani, Siddha and Homoeopathy.

**Exam angle:** The examinable substance is not "AI + Ayurveda" but the **public-AI-commons architecture** it plugs into: **AIKosh** as the national datasets/models repository and the **IndiaAI Compute** pool offering subsidised GPU access. Ayush becomes a test case for whether India's AI stack can serve a domain with weak digitised evidence bases. It also revives the standing question of **evidence standards and regulation in traditional medicine**.

**Key facts to memorise:**
- Signed **31 July 2026**; initial validity **two years**, renewable.
- Ayush will onboard **AIKosh** — contributing anonymised datasets, metadata, AI models, toolkits and use cases.
- Ayush stakeholders get access to **IndiaAI Compute** (GPU-based HPC) at subsidised rates; an **IndiaAI Innovation Challenge 2026** track for Ayush-linked public-health solutions, with pilot support and possible government contracts.
- **IndiaAI Mission**: approved **2024** under **MeitY**, outlay **₹10,371.92 crore**; seven pillars including IndiaAI Compute Capacity, IndiaAI Datasets Platform (AIKosh), IndiaAI Innovation Centre, Application Development Initiative, FutureSkills, Startup Financing and Safe & Trusted AI.
- Ayush institutional map: **Ministry of Ayush** (created 2014, upgraded from the Department of AYUSH under Health), **CCRAS/CCRUM/CCRYN/CCRH** research councils, **NCISM** and **NCH** as regulators, **AIIA** New Delhi, and the **WHO Global Traditional Medicine Centre at Jamnagar** (2022).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (AI, digital health, IPR/traditional knowledge); GS2 — Health governance.

**Static anchor:** *Shankar/standard S&T notes* — **Artificial Intelligence & Digital Public Infrastructure** topic. **Update:** add AIKosh to your DPI stack diagram (Aadhaar → UPI → ONDC → Account Aggregator → **AIKosh/IndiaAI Compute**) and cross-link to the **Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL)** note — the anti-biopiracy angle is the natural Mains bridge.

### 4. Favara–UPI cross-border payment corridor goes live between the Maldives and India — International Relations

**Headline:** The **Maldives Monetary Authority (MMA)** operationalised the linkage of **Favara**, the Maldives' instant payment system, with India's **UPI**, enabling real-time person-to-person transfers from Maldivian bank apps into UPI-enabled Indian accounts.

**Exam angle:** *(New development, distinct from the India–Spain UPI–Bizum negotiation noted on 22 July — that was an in-principle fast-track; this is an operational go-live with a Neighbourhood First partner.)* The corridor matters as **strategic-connectivity diplomacy**: the Maldives hosts a large Indian expatriate workforce whose remittances previously moved through costly correspondent-banking rails, and the go-live consolidates the post-2024 normalisation of ties under **Neighbourhood First / MAHASAGAR**. Note the design asymmetry — transfers are **initiated in Maldivian Rufiyaa and credited in Indian Rupees**, i.e. one-directional in the first phase.

**Key facts to memorise:**
- **Favara** = the Maldives' national instant payment system; **MMA** = the Maldives' central bank.
- Indian counterpart: **NPCI International Payments Limited (NIPL)**, the international arm of NPCI; RBI as the enabling regulator. Underlying agreement signed **July 2025**.
- Phase 1 covers **Bank of Maldives Plc** and **Maldives Islamic Bank Plc**; permitted categories are **family-maintenance** and **gift** remittances. **QR-code merchant payments** are deferred to a later phase.
- NPCI operates under the **Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007**; NPCI is an RBI/IBA-promoted **not-for-profit company under Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013**.
- Wider frame: UPI acceptance now spans a group of ~10 countries (incl. Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Mauritius, UAE, Singapore, France, Qatar).

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India and its neighbourhood); GS3 — Economy (payments, remittances).

**Static anchor:** *Standard IR notes* — **India–Maldives bilateral brief**, plus *Mrunal* **Pillar 1D: Payments & Financial Inclusion**. **Update:** in the Maldives brief, append a "connectivity deliverables" line — Greater Malé Connectivity Project, Hanimaadhoo airport, RuPay/UPI, currency-swap support — and cross-link to your "UPI internationalisation" tracker so the Spain, Maldives and earlier corridors sit on one page.

### 5. Varsha Ashok Aglawe becomes the first woman Director General of the Geological Survey of India — Geography

**Headline:** On **31 July 2026**, Varsha Ashok Aglawe assumed charge as the **54th Director General of the Geological Survey of India (GSI)** — the first woman to head the organisation in its **176-year** history — succeeding Asit Saha.

**Exam angle:** Treat the appointment as a peg, and the **institution + its current mandate** as the examinable content. GSI is the executing agency for **critical-mineral exploration** under the **National Critical Mineral Mission**, and the stated priorities — critical minerals, mineral security, **offshore exploration**, geoscientific infrastructure — map directly onto the highest-frequency Prelims cluster in mineral geography right now.

**Key facts to memorise:**
- **GSI**: established **1851**; headquarters **Kolkata**; an **attached office of the Ministry of Mines**; six regional offices. It is the national repository of geoscience information and does *not* itself grant mineral concessions.
- Aglawe joined GSI in **1992** (earlier with the **Indian Bureau of Mines**); specialises in **palaeontology and palynology** — Late Cretaceous–Palaeocene sediments, the **K–T boundary**, and the **Satpura–Gondwana basins**.
- **National Critical Mineral Mission (NCMM)**: approved **January 2025**, outlay **₹34,300 crore over seven years**, Ministry of Mines; GSI tasked with **1,200 critical-mineral exploration projects between 2024-25 and 2030-31**.
- GSI classifies resources using the **United Nations Framework Classification (UNFC)** and reports under the **Minerals (Evidence of Mineral Contents) Rules, 2015**.
- Distinguish from sister agencies: **Indian Bureau of Mines** (regulation/conservation of mines), **Mineral Exploration & Consultancy Ltd (MECL)**, **National Geoscience Data Repository (NGDR)**.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Geography (mineral and energy resources); GS3 — Economy (mineral policy, critical minerals).

**Static anchor:** *NCERT Class XII, India: People and Economy — "Mineral and Energy Resources"*; and the MMDR chapter of your Economy notes. **Update:** add an institutions box — GSI (exploration/data) vs IBM (regulation) vs MECL (detailed exploration) vs NGDR (data portal) — and cross-link to **MMDR Amendment Act, 2023** (which allowed private exploration licences and moved 24 critical/deep-seated minerals to Central auction) and to the **Offshore Areas Mineral (Development and Regulation) Act, 2002 / 2023 amendment**.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Economy, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **"Sovereign risk-pooling has emerged as an instrument of economic statecraft rather than merely a financial-sector reform." Examine this proposition with reference to India's recent initiatives in maritime insurance.**

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points):**

1. **Frame the vulnerability.** India carries ~95% of its trade by volume over sea, yet specialised marine covers — war risk and P&I — are underwritten largely by the London-centred International Group of P&I Clubs and allied reinsurers. Sanctions regimes, conflict-zone exclusions and abrupt premium spikes therefore transmit *foreign policy* shocks into Indian trade logistics. Cite the West Asia conflict episode and the resulting premium peak.
2. **Describe the instrument.** BMIP as a sovereign-guaranteed pool (₹12,980 crore, operational May 2026), initially for war risk, now extended to P&I with a USD 1.5 billion indemnity limit, with domestic public-sector underwriting capacity (New India Assurance) and the first policy to the Shipping Corporation of India. Note the measurable outcome — a 35–40% fall in war-risk premiums from peak — and the 1,608 policies issued.
3. **Argue the statecraft dimension.** (i) Removes a third-country veto over Indian shipping; (ii) retains insurance value-added — and the associated data on Indian trade flows — within the domestic economy; (iii) complements payment-rail de-risking (UPI corridors, rupee vostro settlement) as part of a wider architecture of transactional autonomy; (iv) supports the flag-carrier and shipbuilding ambitions of Maritime India Vision 2030 / Amrit Kaal Vision 2047.
4. **Balance and conclude.** Counterpoints: contingent-liability risk on the exchequer; moral hazard and adverse selection in a state-guaranteed pool; limited domestic reinsurance depth and claims-handling experience for pollution and wreck-removal liabilities; global port acceptance of a non-IG certificate is not automatic. Conclude that the instrument is sound but its credibility depends on actuarial discipline, IRDAI supervision and a graduated exit of the sovereign guarantee as private capacity matures.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The Bill creates a two-tier route: an Executive Magistrate approval for delays of 1–2 years and a Judicial Magistrate (First Class) order beyond 2 years. Statement 3 is wrong — registration of births and deaths is **Entry 30 of the Concurrent List**, not the Union List.

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** BMIP carries a ₹12,980 crore sovereign guarantee and became operational on 12 May 2026; the 30 July 2026 launch extended it from war risk into P&I. Statement 3 is wrong — **P&I covers third-party liabilities** (crew, cargo, pollution, wreck removal); damage to the vessel itself is covered by **Hull & Machinery** insurance.

**Q3 — (b).** **AIKosh** is the IndiaAI Mission's national datasets/models/toolkits platform under MeitY; the Ayush MoU of 31 July 2026 commits the Ministry to onboarding anonymised health-research artefacts onto it.

**Q4 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct: Favara is the Maldives' instant payment system under the MMA; phase-one flows are initiated in Rufiyaa and credited in Rupees; and NIPL, NPCI's international arm, is the Indian counterpart.

**Q5 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** GSI was set up in 1851 with headquarters at Kolkata and is an attached office of the Ministry of Mines. Statement 3 is wrong — **grant of mineral concessions rests with State Governments** (with Central approval/auction rules under the MMDR Act); GSI only explores and generates baseline geoscience data.

**Q6 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The RGI works under the Ministry of Home Affairs; the 2023 amendment mandates national and State databases; and registration on the ground is performed by Registrars appointed by State Governments — a classic Concurrent-List division of labour.

**Q7 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Minor minerals are regulated through State rules under Section 15 of the MMDR Act while major minerals fall under Central rules; the 2023 amendment introduced **exploration licences** auctionable to the private sector. Statement 3 is wrong — **bentonite is a minor mineral** (UPSC Prelims 2020).
