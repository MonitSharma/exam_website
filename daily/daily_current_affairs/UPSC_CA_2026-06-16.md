### 1. PM at the G7 Summit, Evian (France) — India as an outreach partner
**Headline:** India joins the 52nd G7 Summit (15–17 June, Evian, France) as an invited "partner/outreach" country, alongside Brazil, Egypt, Kenya, South Korea, the UAE and others; agenda centred on renewing international partnerships, development solidarity and the responsible rollout of Artificial Intelligence.

**Exam angle:** Classic IR "groupings" item — the G7 is the highest-PYQ-yield format (members vs invitees, no charter/secretariat, G7 vs G20 vs former G8). India is *not* a member but has now participated 13 times, reflecting its rising global profile and "bridge to the Global South" positioning. Strong Prelims (statement-type) and Mains (plurilateralism / reformed multilateralism) potential.

**Memorize:** G7 members = US, UK, France, Germany, Italy, Canada, Japan + EU (non-enumerated). 2026 host = **France** (venue **Evian**), the 52nd summit. India = invited partner, *not* a member. Russia was expelled in 2014 (ending the G8). The G7 has **no permanent secretariat or founding charter**.
**Notebook:** GS2 IR.
**Static anchor:** Your "International Groupings" table (Vision/Mrunal IR) — add a 2026 row under G7 with "India: 13th participation, partner country." Cross-link to "Reformed Multilateralism / UNSC reform" and contrast G7 (rich-democracies club) vs G20 (where India *is* a full member).

### 2. India–Slovakia ties elevated to a "Comprehensive Partnership"
**Headline:** During the PM's first-ever standalone visit to Slovakia (14–16 June), the two sides announced **14 outcomes** and upgraded ties to a **Comprehensive Partnership**, including a Joint Working Group on Counter-Terrorism, an MoU on labour migration/mobility, and a science MoU between INSA (India) and the Slovak Academy of Sciences.

**Exam angle:** Bilateral + "Places in News" map value (Central Europe, Visegrad-4 region) and the partnership-taxonomy ladder UPSC loves (Strategic → Comprehensive Strategic → Comprehensive Partnership). Focus areas — automobiles, railways, advanced manufacturing, green tech — link to India's supply-chain diversification in Europe.

**Memorize:** Slovakia = landlocked Central European country, capital **Bratislava**; EU member (2004) and Eurozone (euro since 2009); **NATO** member; borders Ukraine to the east; the Danube flows through Bratislava. Outcome of the visit = **Comprehensive Partnership** + 14 deliverables.
**Notebook:** GS2 IR.
**Static anchor:** India–Europe bilateral notes — add a Slovakia card and mark it on your Europe political map (note the Visegrad-4: Slovakia, Czechia, Poland, Hungary). Cross-link to your "levels of partnership" ladder so you can rank-order India's bilateral upgrades.

### 3. DRDO validates Phase-II Ballistic Missile Defence; maiden NASM-MR test
**Headline:** DRDO conducted three flight tests on **10–11 June** validating India's **Phase-II Ballistic Missile Defence (BMD)** programme via the **AD-1 and AD-2** interceptors (engaging targets in both endo- and exo-atmospheric regions), and carried out the **maiden flight test of the NASM-MR** (Naval Anti-Ship Missile–Medium Range). The MoD said India now sits in the "elite group" of nations with BMD capability scalable toward ICBM-class threats.

**Exam angle:** Top S&T + Internal Security item. High Prelims potential on interceptor types, BMD phases, and endo- vs exo-atmospheric interception; feeds GS3 Mains on deterrence and indigenisation (Atmanirbhar in defence).

**Memorize:** **AD-1 / AD-2** = Phase-II BMD interceptors; **two-layered** defence (endo + exo-atmospheric); tests targeted intermediate-range class (~2,000–5,000 km); **NASM-MR** = naval anti-ship missile (medium range), indigenous. BMD Phase-I used the PAD/AAD (PDV) interceptors.
**Notebook:** GS3 Sci-Tech (cross-tag GS3 Internal Security).
**Static anchor:** Defence/Missiles chapter — update the BMD entry (Phase-I PAD/AAD/PDV vs Phase-II AD-1/AD-2) and refresh your master missile table (Agni, Prithvi, BrahMos, NASM). Add the endo/exo-atmospheric distinction beside it.

### 4. Retail inflation (CPI) for May 2026 eases to ~3.93%
**Headline:** CPI-based retail inflation for **May 2026** came in around **3.93%** (provisional), released by MoSPI on 12 June; food inflation ~4.78%, with a rise from April driven partly by seasonal vegetables and firmer fuel (Brent near $97/bbl). This is the new **base year 2024 = 100** series.

**Exam angle:** A data point tied squarely to the syllabus — inflation indices and RBI's flexible inflation-targeting framework. Reliable Prelims fodder (who releases CPI vs WPI, the 4% ±2% band, base-year change) and a usable GS3 Mains data line on price stability.

**Memorize:** CPI released by **MoSPI/NSO** (not RBI); WPI by **DPIIT**. RBI's medium-term **CPI target = 4%, band 2–6%**, set by the **Central Government in consultation with the RBI** (RBI Act, amended 2016). New CPI base year = **2024=100**. May 2026 CPI ≈ **3.93%**, food ≈ 4.78%.
**Notebook:** GS3 Economy.
**Static anchor:** Mrunal Pillar-1 (Money & Banking → Inflation) — update the latest CPI figure and the base-year change to 2024=100; cross-link to the MPC / flexible inflation-targeting page (Section 45ZA, RBI Act).

### Mains-style Question (GS2 — IR)

**Q.** India has now participated in the G7 Summit thirteen times as a partner country, even as it remains outside the formal grouping. *Critically analyse* India's strategy of engaging with plurilateral and minilateral groupings in an increasingly multipolar world order. **(250 words / 15 marks)**

*Model-answer skeleton:*
- **Intro / context:** India's 2026 G7 participation (Evian) as a partner, not member; frame around "strategic autonomy" and multi-alignment in a multipolar order.
- **Why India engages (the case for):** agenda-setting on the Global South, AI governance, climate-energy and supply chains; access to influence without binding obligations; complements its G20 presidency legacy; issue-based coalitions — QUAD, I2U2, IBSA — alongside BRICS and SCO let it balance partners.
- **Critique / limits:** risk of remaining a permanent "invitee" rather than a rule-maker; tension in simultaneously sitting in West-led (G7/QUAD) and non-West (BRICS/SCO) platforms; non-binding outcomes; great-power rivalry (US–China, Russia) constrains room for manoeuvre.
- **Way forward / conclusion:** push for reformed multilateralism and UNSC permanent membership; leverage the "Voice of the Global South"; keep engagement selective and interest-driven rather than bloc-bound.

## ANSWERS

**Q1 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** India is *not* a G7 member (St.1 wrong); the G7 has no permanent secretariat/charter (St.2 correct); India attended the 2026 summit as an invited partner (St.3 correct).

**Q2 — (c) 1 and 3 only.** Slovakia is an EU + NATO member (St.1 correct) but is **landlocked** — no Baltic coast (St.2 wrong); ties were raised to a Comprehensive Partnership in June 2026 (St.3 correct).

**Q3 — (a) India's Phase-II Ballistic Missile Defence programme.** AD-1 and AD-2 are the Phase-II BMD interceptors capable of endo- and exo-atmospheric interception.

**Q4 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** CPI is released by MoSPI/NSO, not the RBI (St.1 wrong); the target is set by the Centre in consultation with the RBI (St.2 correct); target is 4% with a 2–6% band (St.3 correct).

**Q5 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** GPI is published by the IEP (St.1 correct); Iceland is the most peaceful country (St.2 correct); India is ranked ~127th, not in the top 50 (St.3 wrong).

**Q6 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** BrahMos = supersonic cruise missile (correct); Agni-V = ICBM (correct); Prithvi is a surface-to-**surface** short-range ballistic missile, not surface-to-air (St.3 wrong).

**Q7 — (c) 1 and 3 only.** Higher CRR cuts banks' lending capacity (St.1 correct); SLR can be held as cash, gold or approved securities **by the bank itself** — not solely as cash with the RBI (St.2 wrong); MSF is an overnight RBI borrowing window against securities (St.3 correct).
