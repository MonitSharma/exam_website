## PART A — BRIEFING (5 items)

### 1. PM Modi's State Visit to Seychelles — Golden Jubilee guest, Western Indian Ocean anchor
**Subject / Notebook:** International Relations (India & Indian Ocean island states) — GS2.

**Headline:** PM Modi is on a **three-day State Visit to Seychelles (27–29 June 2026)** as **Guest of Honour at Seychelles' 50th National Day** — the island nation became independent from Britain on **29 June 1976**, the same year India and Seychelles established diplomatic ties (so it is also **50 years of the bilateral relationship**). It is Modi's **second visit** to Seychelles after 2015.

**Exam angle:** A clean "India in the Indian Ocean Region (IOR)" item. Seychelles sits in the **Western Indian Ocean**, astride sea lanes near the Mozambique Channel — central to India's role as **"first responder" / net security provider** and to the **SAGAR → MAHASAGAR** evolution. Note the dual track: deep maritime-security cooperation *and* a sensitive **Assumption (Assomption) Island** base question that draws domestic protest in Seychelles — a ready example of "host-nation sensitivities" in basing diplomacy.

**Key facts to memorise:**
- Capital **Victoria**; President **Dr Patrick Herminie**; archipelago of ~115 islands; ~5% of citizens are **Persons of Indian Origin**.
- **SAGAR** = *Security and Growth for All in the Region*, articulated by PM Modi in **Mauritius, March 2015**; its 2025 expansion is **MAHASAGAR** = *Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions* (articulated in Mauritius, March 2025).
- Focus areas of the visit: **maritime domain awareness, blue economy, defence/Coast Guard cooperation, capacity building**, plus new frontiers — **AI, cyber, marine science & conservation**.
- India's other Western IOR island partners: **Mauritius, Maldives, Madagascar, Comoros**; relevant grouping = **IORA** (Indian Ocean Rim Association, est. 1997, 23 members).

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS / Vision IR notes — "India and the Indian Ocean Region" + island neighbours*. **Nudge:** add a Western-IOR map (Mozambique Channel, Agalega, Assumption Is., Duqm) and a one-box **SAGAR (2015) → MAHASAGAR (2025)** timeline; cross-link to GS1 Geography (Indian Ocean chokepoints) and GS3 (blue economy).

### 2. 'Project Hawk Eye' — AI-and-drone security grid for the Amarnath Yatra 2026
**Subject / Notebook:** Internal Security (technology in security management; J&K) — GS3.

**Headline:** On **25 June 2026**, **Anantnag Police (J&K)** launched **'Project Hawk Eye'**, an integrated, technology-linked surveillance framework to secure the **Shri Amarnath Ji Yatra 2026** route from "sky to ground."

**Exam angle:** A compact GS3 internal-security case study on **technology as a force-multiplier for crowd and route security**, and a hook for the **facial-recognition-vs-privacy** debate (links to *K.S. Puttaswamy* and the DPDP Act, 2023). Useful for Mains lines on securing mass religious gatherings and on AI-enabled policing.

**Key facts to memorise:**
- Layers: **5 drones** (aerial real-time monitoring) + **28 "Machan Morchas"** (elevated observation posts) + **416 high-resolution CCTV cameras with a Facial Recognition System (FRS)** + **22 sniper teams**.
- AI analyses live feeds to flag suspicious activity, track crowd movement and alert personnel.
- Sits within the broader Amarnath Yatra security architecture (multi-agency: J&K Police, CAPFs, SANJY board logistics).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal / Vision Internal Security — "Role of technology & media in internal security; security challenges in J&K."* **Nudge:** add a "surveillance tech ↔ right to privacy" two-column note (FRS, DPDP Act 2023, Puttaswamy 2017) and cross-link to GS2 (data protection) and Disaster-Management (crowd safety).

### 3. AIR SUVIDHA 2.0 relaunched as India tightens points-of-entry screening amid the Ebola PHEIC
**Subject / Notebook:** Science & Technology / Health governance (epidemics & surveillance) — GS3 (S&T) + GS2 (health).

**Headline:** On **25–26 June 2026**, the **Ministry of Civil Aviation** and **Delhi International Airport Ltd (DIAL)** relaunched the **AIR SUVIDHA 2.0** portal — a fully contactless, paperless **passenger health self-declaration** system — as India steps up airport screening against the ongoing **Ebola** outbreak abroad.

**Exam angle:** Ties a **technology-governance** story to a **global-health-security** story. The trigger is the **Ebola outbreak in the DR Congo and Uganda** (caused by the **Bundibugyo ebolavirus**), which the **WHO declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) on 16 May 2026**, with **Africa CDC** declaring a continental emergency. Good for "**International Health Regulations (IHR), 2005 / points of entry / one-health surveillance**" Prelims-Mains material — and a reminder that *no Ebola case has been reported in India*.

**Key facts to memorise:**
- AIR SUVIDHA 2.0: online **Self-Declaration Form** with **21-day travel history**, fillable up to **24 hours before arrival**, shown as a **digital PDF / QR** at health desks — no paper.
- Real-time data shared with the **Airport Health Officer, Bureau of Immigration, Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (IDSP)** and State Surveillance Officers.
- Concept chain: **PHEIC** is declared under the **IHR (2005)** (WHO); India's nodal disease-surveillance bodies = **NCDC** and **IDSP**; original AIR SUVIDHA dates to the COVID-19 era.

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment/Health + Vision GS2 (Health) — "IHR 2005, PHEIC, NCDC/IDSP."* **Nudge:** add an "Ebola one-pager" (zoonotic, Filoviridae, fruit-bat reservoir, species incl. Zaire/Bundibugyo) and a PHEIC-declarations list; cross-link to Disaster Management (biological disasters).

### 4. Supreme Court: Article 329(b) bars a writ against rejection of nomination papers mid-election
**Subject / Notebook:** Polity & Governance (elections, Article 329, writ jurisdiction) — GS2.

**Headline:** In **Meenakshi Natarajan v. ECI (June 2026)**, the Supreme Court **dismissed** a Congress leader's **Article 32 writ** challenging the rejection of her **Rajya Sabha by-election nomination (Madhya Pradesh)**, holding that **Article 329(b)** bars judicial interference in an election once the process has begun — the only remedy is an **election petition after the poll**.

**Exam angle:** Textbook GS2 polity on the **"non-interference during elections"** principle. The Bench (Justices **P.K. Mishra and A.S. Chandurkar**) clarified there is **no exception even for "glaring" illegality** — reinforcing the classic *N.P. Ponnuswami* line that electoral disputes follow a **self-contained statutory code**, not Article 32/226.

**Key facts to memorise:**
- **Article 329(b):** no election to Parliament/State legislature shall be questioned **except by an election petition** to the authority and in the manner provided by law (the **Representation of the People Act, 1951**).
- Writ under **Article 32 / 226** is **not maintainable** to halt an ongoing election; remedy = **election petition** (filed in the **High Court** under RPA 1951) after results.
- Anchoring precedent: **N.P. Ponnuswami v. Returning Officer (1952)**.

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth — "Elections" (Articles 324–329; election petitions) + "Writs/Article 32."* **Nudge:** add a flow-box "Election dispute → only via election petition (HC, RPA 1951) → SC appeal," and cross-link Article 329(a)/(b) with the *Ponnuswami* and *Mohinder Singh Gill* cases.

### 5. NCRB launches four digital-policing apps to build a "fingerprint-to-conviction" chain
**Subject / Notebook:** Internal Security / Criminal-justice governance (ICJS, forensics) — GS3 + GS2.

**Headline:** At the **26th All India Fingerprint Conference 2026** (NCRB HQ, New Delhi, 19–20 June 2026), Home Minister Amit Shah launched **four NCRB applications — NCRB-Abhigyan, CrPI, e-Forensics 2.0 and e-Prosecution 2.0** — to knit criminal **identification → investigation → forensics → prosecution** into one digital chain under the **Integrated Criminal Justice System (ICJS)**.

**Exam angle:** A high-yield governance/internal-security item linking the **new criminal laws (BNS/BNSS/BSA, 2023)** and the **Criminal Procedure (Identification) Act, 2022** to real digital tooling. Good Mains material on **tech-enabled criminal justice** and on the **privacy/data-security caveats** of biometric databases.

**Key facts to memorise:**
- **NCRB-Abhigyan:** field officers match thumb impressions via smartphone-linked scanners against a database of **~1.3 crore** suspects/convicts — criminal history in **~35 seconds**.
- **CrPI:** integrates **facial recognition + iris + DNA** matching (operationalises the **Criminal Procedure (Identification) Act, 2022**).
- **e-Forensics 2.0** digitally links forensic labs and investigators; **e-Prosecution 2.0** links police–prosecution–judiciary.
- Parent body: **NCRB** (under **MHA**, set up **1986**), which also runs **CCTNS** and **ICJS**, and publishes *Crime in India*.

**Static anchor:** *Vision/Mrunal GS3 IS + GS2 Governance — "NCRB, CCTNS/ICJS, Criminal Procedure (Identification) Act 2022, new criminal laws 2023."* **Nudge:** add a "ICJS 5 pillars (police–prisons–courts–prosecution–forensics)" box and a privacy caveat line (DPDP Act 2023, *Puttaswamy*).

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (c) All three.** Seychelles' independence: 29 June 1976 (50 years in 2026); it lies in the Western Indian Ocean; MAHASAGAR (2025) expands SAGAR (2015).

**Q2 — (c).** Project Hawk Eye is Anantnag Police's drone + AI-CCTV/FRS + observation-post grid for the Amarnath Yatra 2026.

**Q3 — (d).** AIR SUVIDHA 2.0 is a contactless, paperless passenger health self-declaration portal used for screening at points of entry (relaunched amid the Ebola alert).

**Q4 — (b).** A PHEIC is declared by the WHO Director-General under the International Health Regulations (2005).

**Q5 — (a).** NCRB-Abhigyan (field fingerprint matching) and CrPI (face/iris/DNA, under the Criminal Procedure (Identification) Act, 2022) are NCRB tools within the Integrated Criminal Justice System.

**Q6 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** IORA was set up in 1997; it spans Asian, African, Middle-Eastern and Australasian littorals; India and Seychelles are both members.

**Q7 — (b) Only two.** Statement 1 is correct (Article 329(b): an election is questionable only by an election petition). Statement 2 is correct (the petition is presented to the High Court under the RPA, 1951). Statement 3 is wrong — the Supreme Court in June 2026 (Meenakshi Natarajan) held there is **no** Article 32 exception even for a "glaring" illegality during an ongoing election. Hence only 1 and 2 are correct.
