## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Environmental Performance Index 2026: India ranks 176th of 177 — Reports & Indices

**Headline.** Yale University's biennial Environmental Performance Index (EPI) 2026 places India 176th out of 177 countries with a score of **22.46/100** — second-lowest globally, ahead only of Laos.

**Exam angle.** This is the single most quotable "India's environmental governance deficit" data point available for Mains 2027, and indices are a permanent Prelims staple (publisher + parameters + India's rank). Equally important is the *critique*: Indian experts and MoEFCC have contested EPI's methodology before (weighting, data vintage, per-capita vs absolute framing), which gives you a ready-made "critically examine" second half. Note the paradox worth a Mains line — India ranks poorly overall but has *improved most* on Climate Change, the pillar where its per-capita emissions are genuinely low.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Publisher: **Yale Center for Environmental Law & Policy (YCELP)** with **CIESIN, Columbia University**; biennial.
- India: **176/177**, score **22.46**; regional average 31.81; improvement of **+7.47 points** over 10 years.
- Top 5 (all European): **Estonia (74.79), Luxembourg (74.24), UK (71.51), Finland, Netherlands**.
- Bottom 5: **Laos (177th, 21.78)**, India (176th), **Bangladesh (175th, 23.46)**, Mali (174th), Vietnam (173rd).
- India's weakest pillars: **air quality and biodiversity — 174th on both**; air quality actually *declined* (−0.15 points in a decade).
- India's strongest: **Climate Change (130th, +13.98 points)**; Waste Management & Sanitation (141st).
- India's rank history: 141st (2016) → 177th (2018) → 168th (2020) → 180th (2022) → 176th (2024) → 176th (2026).

**Notebook.** GS3 — Environment (Pollution & Environmental Governance).

**Static anchor.** *Shankar IAS Environment* — chapter on **Environmental Organisations & International Reports/Indices**, plus the **Air Pollution** chapter. **Update:** add an "Indices" table row for EPI (Yale + CIESIN, biennial, 2 policy objectives — Environmental Health & Ecosystem Vitality) and cross-link it to NCAP targets, CPCB's NAMP network and India's updated NDCs so one revision covers rank + cause + policy response.

### 2. ZSI completes India's first national Catalogue of Lepidoptera — 13,703 species — Environment

**Headline.** The Zoological Survey of India completed (30 July 2026) India's first comprehensive taxonomic **Catalogue of Lepidoptera** — butterflies and moths — documenting **13,703 species**, with the final volume published in the journal *Zootaxa*.

**Exam angle.** A rare "India's faunal wealth" item with a hard, memorable number, and it lands squarely on a favourite UPSC theme — **pollinator decline and nocturnal pollination**. The catalogue's dedicated volume on the moth superfamily Geometroidea is the hook: UPSC has repeatedly asked about pollinator biology, and the *moths as nocturnal pollinators* angle is under-taught. Also useful as a "baseline data for conservation" argument — you cannot conserve what you have not catalogued, which links directly to National Biodiversity Targets and the Kunming-Montreal GBF.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **13,703 Lepidoptera species** in India = **~8.25%** of the world's known Lepidoptera.
- Break-up: **1,417 butterflies** + **12,286 moths**.
- Taxonomic spread: **3,705 genera, 240 subfamilies, 102 families, 31 superfamilies**.
- Compiled in **22 volumes** over **12+ years**; final volume covered the remaining **1,689 species**, authored by **Dr. Navneet Singh and Dr. Rahul Joshi**, published in ***Zootaxa***.
- Superfamily **Geometroidea** (nocturnal pollinators): **2,205 Indian species**, 479 genera, 13 subfamilies, 4 families = **~8.80%** of world diversity.
- **ZSI**: est. **1916**, HQ **Kolkata**, under **MoEFCC**.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Environment (Biodiversity & Conservation).

**Static anchor.** *Shankar IAS Environment* — **Biodiversity** chapter and the **Institutions/Organisations** chapter (ZSI, BSI, WII, ICFRE, NBA); NCERT Class XII Biology, **Ch. 15 Biodiversity and Conservation**. **Update:** add ZSI (1916, Kolkata) next to Botanical Survey of India (1890, Kolkata) in your institutions table, and add a one-line cross-link from "pollinators" to Geometroidea moths so the pollinator page is not bee-only.

### 3. Places in News: Nauru is now the **Republic of Naoero** — Geography

**Headline.** The Pacific island nation of **Nauru** formally became the **Republic of Naoero** in early August 2026, restoring its indigenous name through a constitutional amendment rather than a referendum.

**Exam angle.** Classic "Places in News" bait — UPSC likes small island states because they force you to know Pacific sub-regions and climate-vulnerability politics. Naoero is a **Micronesian** island, the world's **third-smallest country by population** and the smallest island *republic*, and its history is the textbook case of **extractive phosphate mining wrecking an economy and an ecosystem** — roughly four-fifths of the island was strip-mined. That gives you a Mains example for both "resource curse" and "SIDS and climate vulnerability". It is also a **Taiwan-recognising / China-switching** flashpoint in Pacific diplomacy, relevant to India's own Indo-Pacific and FIPIC outreach.

**Facts to memorise.**

- New official name: **Republic of Naoero**; abbreviation **Naoero**; country code changes **NRU → NRO**; citizens are **dei-Naoero**.
- Effected by the **Constitution (Amendment) (No. 2) Bill 2026**, tabled 29 January, passed **May 2026**; no referendum — government argued the name already appears on the coat of arms and in the Constitution.
- President: **David Adeang**. Population **~12,000** — world's **3rd-smallest by population**. Currency: **Australian Dollar**. Independence: **1968**.
- Geography: **Micronesia**, Central Pacific, a raised **coral atoll** just south of the Equator; historically dependent on **phosphate (guano) mining**; **no official capital** — government offices at **Yaren** district.

**Notebook.** GS1 — Geography (World Geography / Places in News).

**Static anchor.** NCERT Class XI *Fundamentals of Physical Geography* + your **Oceania outline map**. **Update:** mark Naoero on the Pacific map alongside Tuvalu, Kiribati, Palau, Marshall Islands and Micronesia, and tag them collectively as **SIDS/AOSIS** members so a climate-negotiations question and a map question are served by the same page.

### 4. PM Viksit Bharat Rojgar Yojana completes one year — 72 lakh first-time formal workers — Economy

**Headline.** PM-VBRY completed its first year on **1 August 2026**, with over **72 lakh** first-time employees entering the EPFO-covered formal workforce since launch.

**Exam angle.** This is India's flagship **Employment-Linked Incentive (ELI)** scheme and the natural counterpart to the PLI scheme in any "jobs vs output" answer. The exam-relevant idea is **formalisation as the policy instrument** — the incentive is routed through EPFO enrolment, so the scheme simultaneously creates a payroll data trail and extends social security. Note the design nuance worth a Mains line: the first-timer incentive is paid **in two instalments, the first only after six months of service**, which is a deliberate retention-and-anti-churn device rather than a pure hiring subsidy. The **~30% women share** is your inclusion data point against a stubbornly low female LFPR in formal employment.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Implementation window: **1 August 2025 – 31 July 2027**; outlay **₹99,446 crore**; Ministry of **Labour & Employment**, through **EPFO**.
- Two parts: **Part A employee-focused** (first-time entrants) and **Part B employer-focused** (incentives for additional employment).
- Target: employment for **over 3.5 crore** persons, including **1.92 crore first-time employees**.
- Achievement in year 1: **72 lakh+** first-time employees; **~30% women**; **15 lakh+** have already received incentives.
- First-timer benefit: wage ceiling **₹1 lakh/month**; one-time incentive equal to **one month's EPF wage, capped at ₹15,000**, paid in **two instalments**, first after **6 months** of service.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Economy (Employment, Growth & Development).

**Static anchor.** *Mrunal* **Pillar 4B — Human Resource Development / Employment**, and the Economic Survey's employment chapter. **Update:** put PM-VBRY in your ELI-vs-PLI comparison box, and cross-link it to **PLFS** definitions (UPS vs CWS, worker-population ratio) and to the **Code on Social Security, 2020** so "formalisation" is one integrated page rather than three scattered ones.

### 5. IAF's Exercise ROTOR CLAP-III: helicopters as counter-drone hunters at Pokhran — Internal Security

**Headline.** The Indian Air Force concluded the third edition of **Exercise ROTOR CLAP-III**, a rotary-wing **Counter-Unmanned Aerial Systems (C-UAS)** exercise, at the **Pokhran Field Firing Range, Jaisalmer, Rajasthan** on **1–2 August 2026**, with live missile and rocket firing.

**Exam angle.** The doctrinal point is what makes this examinable: cheap drones are currently defeated by expensive surface-to-air missiles, an unfavourable **cost-exchange ratio**. Using armed helicopters — which can loiter low and slow, exactly where small UAS operate and where fast jets and most radars struggle — is an attempt to fix that asymmetry. Tie this to the **drone threat on the western border** (cross-border narcotics and weapons drops in Punjab and Jammu) and to India's layered counter-drone stack. Pokhran itself is a "place in news" you should be able to locate — **Thar Desert, Jaisalmer district, site of Pokhran-I (1974) and Pokhran-II (1998)**.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **ROTOR CLAP-III** — 3rd edition; **IAF**; theme **Counter-UAS**; venue **Pokhran Field Firing Range, Jaisalmer, Rajasthan**; **1–2 August 2026**.
- Platforms: attack — **AH-64 Apache, Mi-25/35, HAL Prachand (LCH)**; transport/utility — **CH-47 Chinook, Mi-17, HAL Dhruv (ALH)**.
- Tasks validated: live missile and rocket firing, precision strikes, reconnaissance, troop insertion, casualty evacuation, coordinated C-UAS missions.
- Aim: refine **TTPs** (tactics, techniques and procedures), interoperability and rapid response against low-slow-small aerial threats.
- Context to cross-link: **Drone Rules, 2021**; DRDO's **D-4 (Drone Detect, Deter and Destroy)** anti-drone system; the **Bhargavastra** counter-swarm micro-missile system.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Internal Security (Border Management & Security Challenges; Role of Non-State Actors).

**Static anchor.** Your **GS3 Internal Security** notes — *Border Management* and *Security Challenges in Border Areas*. **Update:** open a running "**Counter-drone / C-UAS**" page listing Drone Rules 2021 categories, D-4, Bhargavastra, Akashteer and this exercise, and cross-link it to the Punjab/J&K narco-drone entry in Border Management so one page answers both the technology and the border-security question.

### C. Mains-Style Question

**GS3 — Environment | 250 words | 15 marks**

> "India's placement near the bottom of the Environmental Performance Index reflects a genuine governance deficit, but the index itself is an incomplete measure of environmental justice." **Critically examine.**

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points):**

1. **Establish the fact, briefly.** EPI 2026 — India 176/177, score 22.46; worst on air quality and biodiversity (174th on both); air quality has *worsened* over the decade. State this in two lines; do not spend the answer describing the index.

2. **Concede the genuine deficit.** Air quality (NCAP's city-level PM targets repeatedly missed; continued coal dependence), habitat and biodiversity loss to linear infrastructure and land-use change, weak enforcement capacity of SPCBs, and a compliance-driven rather than outcome-driven regulatory culture. Use the ex post facto environmental clearance jurisprudence as evidence that process integrity itself has been contested.

3. **Now the critique — why the index is incomplete.** EPI is heavily weighted towards *current-state* outcomes, which structurally penalises densely populated, low-income, late-industrialising countries; it under-weights **per-capita emissions and historical responsibility**, where India performs far better; data vintage and coverage gaps distort ranks; and the framework does not capture **CBDR-RC**, the organising principle of India's climate diplomacy. India's *own* best pillar — Climate Change, +13.98 points — proves the point.

4. **Balanced way forward.** Treat the index as a diagnostic, not a verdict: strengthen real-time monitoring and third-party audit of pollution data, move NCAP from city-level to airshed-level management, fund SPCBs adequately, mainstream biodiversity into infrastructure appraisal, and simultaneously push at multilateral fora for indices that internalise per-capita and historical dimensions. Close with the LiFE/Mission LiFE and NDC framing — India's argument must be *both* "we will do better" and "measure us fairly".

### D. PYQ Mirror

**Theme: air quality parameters (connects to Item 1 — India's 174th rank on air quality).**
UPSC Prelims **2016** asked: *"In the cities of our country, which among the following atmospheric gases are normally considered in calculating the value of Air Quality Index? 1. Carbon dioxide 2. Carbon monoxide 3. Nitrogen dioxide 4. Sulphur dioxide 5. Methane"* — the answer being 2, 3 and 4 only. The trap was CO₂ and methane, which are greenhouse gases but **not** AQI pollutants. Expect the same discrimination to be tested again: know that India's National AQI (2015, CPCB) uses **eight** pollutants — PM10, PM2.5, NO₂, SO₂, CO, O₃, NH₃ and Pb.

**Theme: insect biology and pollinators (connects to Item 2 — Geometroidea as nocturnal pollinators).**
UPSC Prelims **2022** *(year approximate — verify against your PYQ compilation)* asked which organism performs the **waggle dance** to communicate the direction and distance of a food source, the answer being **honey bees**. The pattern to note: UPSC tests pollinators through *behaviour and ecological function*, not taxonomy. So from today's item, the examinable idea is not "13,703 species" alone but **moths as nocturnal pollinators** and the consequences of light pollution for them.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** EPI is a biennial Yale (YCELP) + Columbia (CIESIN) product; India stood 176/177 with 22.46, above only Laos; air quality and biodiversity were India's worst (174th each) while Climate Change gained +13.98 points in a decade.

**Q2 — (b) Zoological Survey of India; 13,703 species of butterflies and moths.** ZSI (est. 1916, Kolkata, under MoEFCC) completed the 22-volume catalogue on 30 July 2026 — 1,417 butterflies and 12,286 moths, about 8.25% of world Lepidoptera.

**Q3 — (c) Micronesia; it officially changed its name from Nauru through a constitutional amendment.** The Constitution (Amendment) (No. 2) Bill 2026 restored the indigenous name; the country code shifts NRU → NRO and citizens become dei-Naoero.

**Q4 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** Statement 2 is wrong: PM-VBRY has two parts — Part A for first-time employees and Part B incentivising employers for additional employment. The first-timer incentive equals one month's EPF wage capped at ₹15,000, paid in two instalments.

**Q5 — (b) An Indian Air Force rotary-wing exercise to validate counter-unmanned aerial systems capability.** Held at Pokhran Field Firing Range, Jaisalmer, on 1–2 August 2026 with Apache, Mi-35, Prachand, Chinook, Mi-17 and Dhruv helicopters.

**Q6 — (d) 1, 2, 3 and 4.** Articles 48A (DPSP) and 51A(g) (Fundamental Duty) were both inserted by the 42nd Amendment, 1976; Article 253 is the treaty-implementation power that underpins the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986 and the Air Act, 1981.

**Q7 — (b) Micro — greater than 250 grams and up to 2 kilograms.** Drone Rules, 2021 categories: Nano ≤250 g; Micro >250 g–2 kg; Small >2–25 kg; Medium >25–150 kg; Large >150 kg. Options (a), (c) and (d) misstate the thresholds.
