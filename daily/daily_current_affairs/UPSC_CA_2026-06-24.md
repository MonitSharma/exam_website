## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. SC: "Right to Walk" on safe footpaths is a Fundamental Right
**Headline:** In *Maniyar Iliyaz @ Shaik Riyaz v. P. Ayyappan* (2026 INSC 647), the Supreme Court held the right to walk on safe, demarcated footpaths is a fundamental right.

**Exam angle (GS2 — Polity/Governance):** A fresh entry in the long line of judgments *expanding the horizon of Article 21*. The Court rooted the right in **Article 19(1)(d)** (freedom of movement) read with **Article 21**, ruled pedestrians' rights are "primary" over motorised traffic, and made the state's duty to maintain footpaths judicially **enforceable** (citizens can claim a *restitutionary remedy*). It re-registered the matter suo motu under **Article 32** as *"Re: Fundamental Right to Walk and Footpath."*

**Memorise:** Bench — Justices P.S. Narasimha & Atul S. Chandurkar (19 June 2026). Trigger — death of a 5-year-old hit by a tanker en route to school; compensation enhanced to **₹11.44 lakh**. Pedestrian deaths rose **163%** (13,894 in 2015 → 36,526 in 2024); pedestrian share of road deaths ~**20.6%**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Fundamental Rights). **Static anchor:** Laxmikanth, *Fundamental Rights → Article 21* — add "right to safe footpaths/right to walk" to your running list of judicially-derived Article 21 rights (alongside clean environment, privacy, livelihood).

### 2. Western Ghats: Centre moves to notify Eco-Sensitive Areas
**Headline:** MoEFCC is finalising demarcation of **Ecologically Sensitive Areas (ESAs)** in the Western Ghats across (at least) three states.

**Exam angle (GS3 — Environment):** The perennial **conservation-vs-livelihood + Centre-vs-State** deadlock. The original **2014 draft notification** has gone through **five revisions** without resolving state objections. Tests your hold on the **Gadgil (2011) vs Kasturirangan (2013)** divide and the legal basis of ESAs.

**Memorise:** ESAs notified under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**. **Gadgil/WGEEP (2011):** entire Ghats as ESA (~1,29,037 sq km, 100%), graded ESZ-I/II/III. **Kasturirangan/HLWG (2013):** ~37% (~60,000 sq km) as ESA, splitting natural vs cultural landscapes. Western Ghats span **6 states** (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Goa, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu); a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of **4 biodiversity hotspots in India**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment. **Static anchor:** Shankar IAS *Environment* — Eco-Sensitive Zones + Biodiversity Hotspots chapters; refresh the Gadgil-vs-Kasturirangan comparison table.

### Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Environment)

**Q.** *"Despite being a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a global biodiversity hotspot, demarcation of Ecologically Sensitive Areas in the Western Ghats remains contested."* Examine the reasons for this impasse and suggest a balanced way forward. (250 words)

**Model-answer skeleton:**
- **Intro:** Western Ghats — 1,600 km, 6 states, "Water Tower of Peninsular India," 4 of India's biodiversity hotspots; ESAs under EPA 1986 to regulate mining/quarrying/red-category industry.
- **Why the impasse:** (i) Centre–State friction (cooperative federalism) over jurisdiction/area; (ii) conservation-vs-livelihood — Gadgil's 100% ESA vs Kasturirangan's ~37% balance; (iii) five revisions since 2014 draft → policy uncertainty; (iv) local protests (e.g., Kerala) and political economy of mining/plantations/quarrying; (v) weak ground-truthing of village boundaries.
- **Way forward:** participatory demarcation with district-level ground-truthing; graded ESZ with clear positive/negative lists; alternative-livelihood + eco-tourism support; cooperative-federal consultation; scientific monitoring (remote sensing) and time-bound final notification.
- **Conclusion:** Reconcile ecology and equity — protect the "water tower" while securing local livelihoods.

## PART C — ANSWERS

**Q1 — (c) All three.** UNCTAD 2025 put India first (35.4% share); Alang is the hub; the Recycling of Ships Act, 2019 implements the Hong Kong Convention.

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** ESAs are under EPA 1986; Gadgil (~1.29 lakh sq km, 100%) > Kasturirangan (~37%); the Ghats span Gujarat→Tamil Nadu (6 states).

**Q3 — (b) Article 19(1)(d) read with Article 21.** The Court located the right in freedom of movement + right to life; Article 32 was the route for suo motu re-registration, not the source of the right.

**Q4 — (b) Resistant to RF jamming and spoofing.** They use a fibre-optic cable, emit minimal RF; the trade-off is limited range and a breakable cable.

**Q5 — (b) Indus–Saraswati (Harappan) — Haryana.** Rakhigarhi is among the largest Harappan sites; ASI transferred remains to AnSI (Kolkata) under a Ministry of Culture MoU.

**Q6 — (c) All four.** India hosts four of the 36 global biodiversity hotspots: Himalaya, Indo-Burma, Sundaland (Nicobar Is.), and Western Ghats–Sri Lanka.

**Q7 — (a) 1, 2 and 3 only.** Godavari, Krishna and Cauvery rise in the Western Ghats; the Mahanadi rises in Chhattisgarh.
