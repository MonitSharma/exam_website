## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. DISHA 2.0 launched — ₹255 crore to take legal aid to 3 crore people by 2031 — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** The Department of Justice (Ministry of Law and Justice) has launched **DISHA 2.0** — *Designing Innovative Solutions for Holistic Access to Justice* — a restructured **Central Sector Scheme** with a ₹255-crore outlay for 2026–31.

**Exam angle:** This is the cleanest current-affairs hook available for **Article 39A (equal justice and free legal aid)** — a DPSP that UPSC likes to pair with Article 21. It is also a textbook "pre-litigation vs post-litigation" access-to-justice question, and a Central Sector (100% Centre-funded) vs Centrally Sponsored Scheme discriminator.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Outlay **₹255 crore**; period **1 April 2026 – 31 March 2031** (5 years); **100% central funding** → Central Sector Scheme.
- Target: **3 crore beneficiaries** by 2031; pan-India including Andhra Pradesh.
- **Four components:** (i) **Tele-Law: Reaching the Unreached** (free *pre-litigation* advice), (ii) **Nyaya Bandhu** (pro bono legal services), (iii) **Legal Literacy & Legal Awareness Programme**, (iv) **VIDHI–Sanjeevani** (Vision for Integrated Delivery of Harmonized Legal Initiatives).
- Nodal body: **Department of Justice**, Ministry of Law and Justice.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Judiciary / Welfare schemes for vulnerable sections).

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth* — Directive Principles chapter (**Article 39A**, inserted by the **42nd Amendment Act, 1976**) and the Judiciary chapter. → **Update:** add a cross-link from Article 39A to the **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987** (in force 9 Nov 1995) → **NALSA** (CJI as Patron-in-Chief; a sitting/retired SC judge nominated by the President in consultation with the CJI as Executive Chairman) → **Lok Adalats** → DISHA 2.0 as the delivery arm.

### 2. India orders GitHub to pull down BitChat repositories under Section 79(3)(b) — Science & Technology

**Headline:** The **Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C)**, MHA, directed **GitHub** on **23 July 2026** to disable three repositories hosting **BitChat**, Jack Dorsey's Bluetooth-mesh messaging app, giving a **three-hour** compliance window.

**Exam angle:** A rare item that sits exactly on the **Sci-Tech ↔ Polity ↔ Internal Security** junction. It tests the difference between **§69A (blocking of information)** and **§79(3)(b) (loss of intermediary safe harbour)**, and revives **Shreya Singhal v. Union of India (2015)**, where the Supreme Court *read down* §79(3)(b) to mean an intermediary must act only on a **court order or a government notification**. The technology angle — decentralised **Bluetooth mesh**, no internet, no central server — is itself a Prelims-worthy concept.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Order by **I4C (MHA)**, notice dated 23 July 2026, under **Section 79(3)(b), IT Act, 2000** read with **Rule 3(1)(d), IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Rules**.
- Stated ground: peer-to-peer **Bluetooth mesh** architecture works without mobile networks/internet/central servers, impeding **lawful interception and attribution**.
- Context: the app spread during **NEET 2026 protests** amid internet shutdowns; the **Internet Freedom Foundation** called the order unconstitutional for targeting *architecture* rather than specific unlawful content.
- **§79 = safe harbour** (conditional immunity); **§69A = blocking**; **§69 = interception/decryption**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Sci-Tech (Cyber/Emerging tech) with a GS2 Governance cross-entry.

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth* — Fundamental Rights, **Article 19(1)(a) and 19(2)** reasonable restrictions. → **Update:** add a one-line box in your IT Act notes distinguishing §69 / §69A / §79(3)(b), tagged with **Shreya Singhal (2015)** (struck down §66A, read down §79(3)(b) and upheld §69A with procedural safeguards).

### 3. Press Note 3 (2026 Series): FDI allowed in inventory-based e-commerce — but only for exports — Economy

**Headline:** DPIIT's **Press Note 3 (2026 Series)**, issued **23 July 2026**, permits FDI in **inventory-based e-commerce** entities that deal **exclusively in exports of goods manufactured or produced in India**.

**Exam angle:** A precise, high-probability Prelims fact because it changes a long-standing prohibition. Mains angle: reconciling **export promotion** with **protection of small domestic retailers** — the classic FDI-in-retail debate, now split by *destination of sale* rather than by *business model alone*.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **Before:** 100% FDI under the **automatic route** in the **marketplace model** only; FDI **prohibited** in inventory-based **B2C** e-commerce.
- **After:** the prohibition does **not** apply where the entity deals **exclusively in exports** of India-made goods. **Domestic B2C inventory-based FDI stays barred.**
- Takes effect only once the **FEM (Non-Debt Instruments) Rules, 2019** under **FEMA, 1999** are correspondingly amended.
- Stated goal: **$200–300 billion in e-commerce exports by 2030**.
- Distinguish from the famous **Press Note 3 (2020 Series)** — government approval for investors from land-bordering countries.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (External Sector / Investment).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* — Balance of Payments & FDI pillar. → **Update:** in your FDI table, split the e-commerce row into *marketplace / inventory-domestic / inventory-export-only*, and cross-link to **FEMA, 1999** and the **NDI Rules, 2019** as the operative instrument (FDI policy itself is not law until notified under FEMA).

### 4. Rajasthan growers object to the GI tag on Unjha (Gujarat) jeera and saunf — Agriculture

**Headline:** Farmer bodies in **Rajasthan** — India's largest cumin-producing state — have objected to the **Geographical Indication** granted for **Unjha jeera (cumin)** and **Unjha saunf/variyali (fennel)** of North Gujarat, arguing that Unjha is primarily a **trading mandi** rather than the growing region.

**Exam angle:** GI is a recurring Prelims theme and this dispute makes the underlying concept examinable: a GI protects goods whose quality/reputation is **essentially attributable to geographical origin**, and the registered proprietor is a **collective body**, never an individual. It also raises the *mandi-vs-farm* question — can a market yard hold the GI for produce grown largely elsewhere?

**Facts to memorise:**

- GI certificates issued **28 March 2026**; **registered proprietor: APMC, Unjha**; **Class 30** (spices).
- Governing law: **Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999**, in force **15 September 2003**; **GI Registry, Chennai**, under the **CGPDTM**, DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce & Industry.
- Registration valid **10 years, renewable indefinitely**; GI is **not assignable or transmissible**; aggrieved parties may seek **rectification/cancellation** of the register.
- India's first GI: **Darjeeling Tea**. India's obligation flows from **TRIPS Articles 22–24**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture / Economy (IPR & agri-marketing).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal/Economic Survey* — Agriculture Marketing & IPR. → **Update:** in your GI notes add the **"registered proprietor vs authorised user"** distinction and one line on why GI is a **community right**, cross-linked to APMC reform notes.

### 5. ICAR-CMFRI launches DeepDATA — a citizen-science app for India's deep-sea fishes — Environment

**Headline:** **ICAR-CMFRI, Kochi** has launched **DeepDATA**, a mobile app developed under the **Deep Ocean Mission** with **Ministry of Earth Sciences** funding, offering verified data on deep-sea fish species and letting users upload field observations into a **national digital repository**.

**Exam angle:** A "Places/Schemes in News" item that lets you revise the **Deep Ocean Mission** in full. The examinable idea is **citizen science with expert validation** — public submissions are verified by CMFRI scientists before entering the database. It also connects to **Blue Economy** and to biodiversity documentation beyond the EEZ.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **DeepDATA** — ICAR-**CMFRI** (headquartered **Kochi, Kerala**, under **ICAR**); funded under the **Deep Ocean Mission**, **Ministry of Earth Sciences**.
- Content: taxonomy, characteristics, **distribution and conservation status** of deep-sea fishes; supports biodiversity mapping, conservation planning and sustainable fisheries management.
- **Deep Ocean Mission**: approved **June 2021**, outlay **₹4,077 crore**, **six components**; includes **Samudrayaan**, whose crewed submersible **Matsya-6000** targets **6,000 m** depth; also covers **polymetallic nodule** exploration in the **Central Indian Ocean Basin** (India is an **ISA**-registered pioneer investor).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Biodiversity / Sci-Tech (Blue Economy).

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment* — Marine ecosystems & Biodiversity chapters. → **Update:** add DeepDATA as an example under "biodiversity documentation initiatives" next to **ZSI/BSI faunal & floral surveys**, and cross-link to the **Deep Ocean Mission** entry in your Sci-Tech schemes list.

### C. Mains-Style Question

**GS2 — Governance / Judiciary (250 words, 15 marks)**

> **Examine** the extent to which technology-driven initiatives can bridge India's access-to-justice deficit, and the institutional limits within which such initiatives must operate.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the constitutional mandate** — Article 39A read with Article 21 (*Hussainara Khatoon*: speedy trial as part of Article 21); the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 as the delivery statute; the scale of the deficit (pendency, undertrials, awareness gap).
2. **What technology genuinely solves** — reach and cost: Tele-Law delivers *pre-litigation* advice through CSCs into villages; Nyaya Bandhu mobilises pro bono lawyers; e-Courts digitises records and cause lists; DISHA 2.0's ₹255-crore, 3-crore-beneficiary target shows the marginal cost of outreach falling sharply.
3. **What it cannot solve** — judge strength and vacancies, investigation quality, legal-aid lawyer competence and remuneration, procedural delay; the **digital divide** (connectivity, device access, language, disability, gender) risks converting a technology gateway into a fresh exclusion; digitisation without process reform simply digitises delay.
4. **Way forward and balanced conclusion** — sequence technology *behind* institutional reform: fill judicial vacancies, strengthen legal-aid defence counsel systems, build multilingual and assisted-access modes, audit outcomes rather than logins. Conclude that technology is a **force multiplier for capacity, not a substitute for it**.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (b).** DISHA 2.0 is a **Central Sector Scheme with 100% central funding**, not a 60:40 Centrally Sponsored Scheme, so statement 1 is wrong. Tele-Law and Nyaya Bandhu are two of its four components, and the Department of Justice (Ministry of Law and Justice) implements it.

**Q2 — (b).** The order invoked **Section 79(3)(b) of the IT Act, 2000** — the provision under which an intermediary forfeits its **safe-harbour exemption from liability** if it fails to act on notice. Option (a) describes §69A (blocking), (c) describes §69 (interception/monitoring/decryption), and (d) describes §66A, which was struck down in *Shreya Singhal* (2015).

**Q3 — (c).** Press Note 3 (2026 Series) permits FDI in **inventory-based e-commerce dealing exclusively in exports** of India-made goods. Option (a) is Press Note 3 of the **2020** Series — a classic distractor. FDI in inventory-based domestic B2C remains prohibited.

**Q4 — (b).** The GI is registered in the name of **APMC, Unjha**, and GI registration is valid for **ten years and renewable**. Statement 2 is wrong: under the 1999 Act a GI is a community right and is **not assignable or transmissible** — this is the key structural difference from a trademark.

**Q5 — (b).** **DeepDATA** is ICAR-CMFRI's app for documenting **deep-sea fish species**, developed under the **Deep Ocean Mission** with Ministry of Earth Sciences support, combining verified scientific data with citizen-submitted field observations.

**Q6 — (b).** Article 39A was inserted by the **42nd Amendment Act, 1976**, and Lok Adalats received statutory backing under the **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987**. Statement 2 is wrong: the CJI is **Patron-in-Chief** of NALSA; the **Executive Chairman** is a serving or retired Supreme Court judge nominated by the President in consultation with the CJI.

**Q7 — (b).** The **GI Registry at Chennai** works under the **CGPDTM**, and the Act was enacted to meet India's **TRIPS** obligations. Statement 2 is wrong: the Act is administered by **DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce and Industry**, not the Ministry of Agriculture.
