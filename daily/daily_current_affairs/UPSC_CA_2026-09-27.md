## Part A — Briefing

### 1. Jaishankar's UNGA-81 statement: the Indus Waters Treaty goes on the UN record, and India co-founds a shipping-safety group — International Relations

**What's new since the 21 Sept preview:** India delivered its national statement on **26 September 2026**. Two things in it are new for your notes. India defended keeping the **Indus Waters Treaty (IWT) in abeyance** from the UNGA podium. India also joined **Liberia** and about two dozen partners to launch a **Group of Friends on the Safety and Security of Shipping and Seafarers**.

**Exam angle:** This is India's first statement at the General Assembly that ties a water treaty directly to cross-border terrorism. The line was that "friendship and goodwill cannot co-exist with terrorism", and that understandings premised on good faith "will naturally be called into question". For Mains, use it on how far treaty obligations stand when there is a security threat (IR / GS2). The shipping group adds to India's maritime-security material alongside the Red Sea attacks, SAGAR/MAHASAGAR and the IMO.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Speech: 26 Sept 2026, 81st UNGA General Debate. Lines to quote: "Our right to defend ourselves against terrorism will be exercised". He called Pakistan a "serial practitioner of terrorism".
- IWT stays **in abeyance** (in place since April 2025, after Pahalgam) "until Pakistan … credibly and irrevocably ends its support for cross-border … terrorism".
- **IWT basics:** signed **1960, Karachi**, by **Nehru and Ayub Khan**, with the **World Bank** as a signatory. **Eastern rivers** (Ravi, Beas, Sutlej) go to India. **Western rivers** (Indus, Jhelum, Chenab) go to Pakistan, and India has limited non-consumptive uses such as run-of-river hydro. Disputes pass through the **Permanent Indus Commission**, then a Neutral Expert, then a Court of Arbitration.
- Shipping: "the targeting of commercial shipping and seafarers … is simply unacceptable". **Liberia** is one of the world's largest flag-registries, so a flag state is co-leading the group.
- Other lines from the speech: the "**4F crisis**" in the Global South (food, fuel, fertiliser, finance); "endless war must give way to an end to war" (on Ukraine).

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — IR (India–Pakistan; Important international groupings). GS3 link: water security.

**Static anchor:** Your GS2 India–Neighbourhood file (India–Pakistan → IWT). Cross-link to *Spectrum / NCERT Geography — Indus river system*. **Nudge:** add a box showing "IWT: Eastern vs Western rivers + three-tier dispute ladder", and link it to the 21 Sept UNGA card.

### 2. Centre cuts FY27 gross borrowing by ₹1.2 lakh crore — H2 calendar set at ₹7.86 lakh crore — Economy

**Headline:** On 25 September 2026 the Finance Ministry, after consulting the RBI, announced that it will raise **₹7.86 lakh crore through dated securities in October–March (H2 FY27)**. FY27 gross market borrowing falls to about **₹15.99 lakh crore**, down from the Budget estimate of **₹17.2 lakh crore**.

**Exam angle:** The cut is mostly a **debt-management move, not a fiscal saving**. The government **switched ₹1,14,286 crore of G-secs maturing in FY27** into longer-dated papers, which lowers this year's redemption bill. The item tests G-sec vocabulary that Prelims likes: **dated securities vs T-bills, switch operations, Ways and Means Advances (WMA), Sovereign Green Bonds (SGrBs)**. In Mains, use it for fiscal consolidation and for how public debt management affects bond yields and crowding-out.

**Facts to memorise:**
- H2 borrowing is **₹7.86 lakh crore** over **23 weekly auctions**, with tenors of **3 to 50 years**. The 10-year tenor has the largest share (**26.3%**), then 15-year (**17.6%**) and 5-year (**12.1%**).
- Total reduction is **₹1,20,494 crore** against the BE, driven mainly by the **₹1,14,286 crore switch**. H1 borrowing works out to roughly ₹8.1 lakh crore (derived figure).
- **SGrBs: ₹15,000 crore** in H2. They are *part of* gross market borrowing, and the proceeds are earmarked for green projects.
- **T-bills (Q3):** about **₹23,000 crore a week** across 91-, 182- and 364-day bills.
- **WMA limit for H2: ₹50,000 crore.** WMA are temporary RBI advances to cover mismatches between receipts and payments. Using them beyond the limit counts as an overdraft.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Government Budgeting / Public Debt / Money Market).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal — Pillar #1B (Banking) and #2 (Budget, Fiscal Policy — public debt)*; *Spectrum/Ramesh Singh — "Money market & capital market" (G-secs, T-bills)*. **Nudge:** update your "G-sec toolkit" table with a row for **Switch = swap short-maturity paper for long; lowers current-year redemptions; gross borrowing falls, debt stock does not**.

### 3. Did the President return Bengal's "anti-goonda" Bill? The confusion is itself a lesson in Articles 200, 201 and 254 — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** On 25 September media reports said President Murmu had **returned** the *West Bengal Public Safety and Control of Anti-Social Activities Bill, 2026* (passed by the Assembly on **29 June 2026**). The reported reason was the Centre's concern about NDPS-related overlap. On 26 September the **PIB Fact Check Unit** called the reports "misleading" and said the Bill is **still under examination by the Union Home Ministry**. The CM confirmed that it had not yet reached the President.

**Exam angle:** Whatever the final outcome, this is a live case of a **state Bill reserved for the President's consideration**. The substantive issue is also a textbook one. The Bill counts NDPS offenders as "goondas" who can be held in preventive detention for up to **one year**. That overlaps with the **central PITNDPS Act, 1988**, which raises **Article 254 repugnancy** on a **Concurrent List** subject (preventive detention, Entry 3, List III). The Centre's suggested fix is to drop the NDPS clause (sec. 2(d)(iii)(B)).

**Facts to memorise:**
- **Art. 200:** the Governor may assent, withhold assent, return a non-Money Bill for reconsideration, or **reserve** the Bill for the President. Reservation is **mandatory** for a Bill that endangers the position of the High Court.
- **Art. 201:** the President may assent or withhold assent, or direct the Governor to **return a non-Money Bill** to the House. The House must reconsider it **within six months**. If it is re-passed, the **President is *not* bound to assent**. This differs from Art. 111, where the President must assent to a re-passed Bill.
- In practice the **MHA** processes reserved Bills and gathers the views of the ministries concerned. That is the stage this Bill is at.
- **Art. 254(2):** a state law on a Concurrent-List matter that is repugnant to a central law **prevails in that state** if it was reserved and received the President's assent. Parliament can still override it later.
- **Art. 22(4):** preventive detention beyond **3 months** needs an Advisory Board report. The 44th Amendment's reduction to 2 months has **never been brought into force**.
- **2025 SC reference** (*State of Tamil Nadu v. Governor*, April 2025, and the Presidential Reference opinion of Nov 2025): useful background on timelines for Governors and the President.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Governor; Centre–State legislative relations; Fundamental Rights — Art. 22).

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth — "Governor" (veto over state Bills) and "Centre–State Relations" (Art. 254), plus "Fundamental Rights" (Art. 22)*. **Nudge:** in the Governor chapter, add a flowchart of a state Bill's path: Art 200 → reserve → Art 201 → return, 6 months, re-pass → President free to decide. ### 4. TRAI launches the Digital Connectivity Rating (DCR) Platform — indoor signal quality becomes a certified property attribute — Science & Technology

**Headline:** On **25 September 2026** TRAI Chairman **Anil Kumar Lahoti** launched the **DCR Platform** (portal plus mobile app, at dcrp.trai.gov.in). It digitises the rating of buildings for indoor digital connectivity.

**Exam angle:** Around **70–80% of mobile data is used indoors**, and 5G's higher frequencies pass through walls poorly. Connectivity is now being treated like an **energy-efficiency label**. The idea is that market pressure will make builders plan **Digital Connectivity Infrastructure (DCI)** at the design stage, including fibre ducts and in-building solutions (IBS). Use it for GS3 infrastructure and digital-divide answers, and in Prelims to identify the regulator.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Legal basis: **TRAI (Rating of Properties for Digital Connectivity) Regulations, 2024** (Oct 2024), amended **13 May 2026**. **Rating Manual 2026** was issued on **9 June 2026**.
- Ratings are given by accredited **Digital Connectivity Rating Agencies (DCRAs)**. TRAI approved the first **eight** in Aug 2025.
- Parameters: **fibre readiness, 4G/5G mobile coverage, in-building solutions and Wi-Fi**, measured in common areas, lifts and basements.
- Features: ratings for **under-construction and completed** properties, **Aadhaar eSign** certificates with **QR verification**, and public search by property, certificate ID or location. **Participation is opt-in**, which critics call its main weakness.
- TRAI: statutory body under the **TRAI Act, 1997**, set up **20 Feb 1997**. Disputes go to **TDSAT**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Sci-Tech / Infrastructure (Telecom). GS2 link: regulatory bodies.

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal — Pillar #5 (Infrastructure: telecom)*; your GS2 "Statutory/Regulatory bodies" sheet (TRAI vs TDSAT). **Nudge:** add DCR next to BEE star labelling under "information-disclosure regulation", which works by shaping behaviour through labels.

### 5. Places in News: Tigray — TPLF seizes Mekelle, Axum and Shire airports, and the Pretoria peace deal is fraying — Geography

**Headline:** From **23 September 2026** forces of the **Tigray People's Liberation Front (TPLF)** seized the **Mekelle, Axum and Shire** airports and announced a "defensive war". **Ethiopian Airlines suspended flights** to all three. Fighting has spread into **Amhara and Afar**. A seven-group anti-government alliance announced on 21 September includes **Fano** militias and the **Oromo Liberation Army**, and there are reports of Eritrean support.

**Exam angle:** This is a Places-in-News map drill for the **Horn of Africa**, which sits next to the **Bab el-Mandeb** and the Red Sea shipping route. India has a direct stake: Ethiopia is a **BRICS member (since 1 Jan 2024)**, it hosts the **African Union HQ (Addis Ababa)**, and it is a major destination for Indian investment and lines of credit. The **Pretoria Agreement (Nov 2022)**, brokered by the AU, ended a war that the AU estimates killed about **6 lakh** people.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **Landlocked** since **Eritrea's independence (1993)**. Six neighbours: **Eritrea, Djibouti, Somalia, Kenya, South Sudan, Sudan**. Most trade goes through **Djibouti**.
- **Lake Tana** is the source of the **Blue Nile (Abay)**. **GERD** is on the Blue Nile. Ethiopia is called the "water tower of East Africa".
- **Danakil Depression (Afar)**, part of the **Great Rift Valley**, is one of the hottest and lowest places on Earth. The **Tekeze** river forms part of the Tigray–Amhara boundary zone.
- **Axum** (Aksum) was the seat of the ancient Aksumite kingdom and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. **Mekelle** is Tigray's capital.

**Destination notebook:** GS1 — Geography (Places in News: Africa). GS2 link: India–Africa.

### Mains question (GS2, 15 marks, 250 words)

**"Examine the constitutional scheme for Presidential consideration of State Bills under Articles 200 and 201. Is the reservation power being used as a tool of Centre–State friction?"**

*Model-answer skeleton:*
1. **Introduction:** Arts 200/201 give the Governor four options and the President three. Reservation is mandatory for Bills affecting the High Court's powers (Art 200 2nd proviso), and is used to secure Art 254(2) protection. Hook: the WB anti-goonda Bill (2026) sitting with the MHA.
2. **Legitimate uses:** curing repugnancy on Concurrent subjects (Art 254(2)); protecting national policy (e.g., NDPS/PITNDPS coherence); Art 31A/31C and Art 304(b) Bills.
3. **Friction points:** no constitutional time limit (the TN Governor case of 2025 set 1–3 month outer limits; the Nov 2025 Presidential Reference opinion said courts cannot impose fixed timelines); the MHA as a *de facto* gatekeeper; "pocket veto" by delay; the President not bound after re-passage.
4. **Way forward / conclusion:** Sarkaria (reserve only in rare cases, decide within set months) and Punchhi Commission recommendations; transparent inter-ministerial timelines; consultation at the drafting stage (pre-legislative scrutiny with the Centre on Concurrent-List Bills). Close on cooperative federalism.
