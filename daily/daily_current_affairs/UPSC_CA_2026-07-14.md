## PART A — BRIEFING (5 items)

### 1. Skyroot's Vikram-1 on the pad at Sriharikota — India's first *private* orbital rocket enters its launch window — Science & Technology
**Headline:** Hyderabad-based **Skyroot Aerospace** has fully stacked **Vikram-1**, India's first privately developed *orbital-class* launch vehicle, on the First Launch Complex at Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota; the maiden flight — mission **"Agaman"** — has a launch window of **12 July–4 August 2026**.
**Exam angle:** This is the flagship proof-point of India's 2020 space-sector opening (IN-SPACe, NSIL, Indian Space Policy 2023). Distinguish it clearly from Skyroot's **Vikram-S** (Mission *Prarambh*, Nov 2022) — that was India's first private rocket but **sub-orbital**; Vikram-1 aims for **orbit**. A classic Prelims trap: "first private rocket" (Vikram-S, 2022) vs "first private *orbital* rocket" (Vikram-1, 2026).
**Key facts to memorise:** Two-to-three-stage, all-**carbon-composite** vehicle; solid-fuel boosters + **3D-printed** engines; capacity **up to ~350 kg to Low Earth Orbit**; target orbit **~450 km, 60° inclination**. Founders are ex-ISRO engineers; regulated/enabled by **IN-SPACe**. Named after Vikram Sarabhai.
**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Space / Achievements of Indians in sci-tech; indigenisation).
**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS / Mrunal — Sci-Tech "Space" module.* Add a cross-link under "Space sector reforms": IN-SPACe (single-window authorisation) → NSIL (commercial arm) → Indian Space Policy 2023 → private launch vehicles (Skyroot Vikram-S 2022 sub-orbital, **Vikram-1 2026 orbital**, Agnikul Agnibaan SOrTeD 2024).

### 2. India launches the Index of Services Production (ISP) — a services-sector counterpart to the IIP — Reports & Indices
**Headline:** The **Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation (MoSPI)** released the first set of **trial monthly Index of Services Production (ISP)** figures on **14 July 2026**, giving India — for the first time — a high-frequency official gauge of services-sector output, mirroring what the **Index of Industrial Production (IIP)** does for factories/mining/electricity.
**Exam angle:** Services are **~55%+ of India's GVA** yet had no monthly volume index until now — a long-standing data gap. High-yield because it is a *new official statistical product* with a fresh base year, exactly the kind of "what does X measure / who releases it" Prelims item.
**Key facts to memorise:** Released by **MoSPI/NSO**; **base year 2024-25** (aligned with the rebased CPI series); **Laspeyres fixed-weight volume index** with **GVA-based weights**; covers trade, transport, finance/banking, insurance, telecom, real estate, professional & technical services, hotels & recreation; **health and education excluded for now** (to be added after ASISSE results); monthly release with a **~60-day lag**.
**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (National Income / economic indicators).
**Static anchor:** *Mrunal — Pillar 1A National Income Accounting (GDP/GVA) & indicators.* Add a one-line pairing: **IIP (industry, base 2011-12)** ⇄ **ISP (services, base 2024-25)**; note both are volume indices released by MoSPI.

### 3. India, China in the crosshairs of a US Senate bill authorising up to 500% "secondary tariffs" on Russian-oil buyers — International Relations
**Headline:** A US Senate sanctions bill — reported to have **White House backing** in July 2026 — would authorise the President to impose **secondary tariffs of up to 500%** on countries that continue buying Russian energy; **China and India**, which together take **80%+ of Russia's seaborne crude**, are the principal targets. The Senate returned from recess on **13 July**.
**Exam angle:** A live case study in **energy security vs strategic autonomy** and the reach of **US secondary sanctions** (recall the CAATSA/S-400 debate). Note the sequencing: the temporary **US Treasury general licence** shielding India's Russian-crude purchases **expired 17 June 2026**, and the Feb-2026 India–US trade understanding had cut US reciprocal tariffs on India to **18% (from 25%)** — all now under fresh pressure.
**Key facts to memorise:** Instrument = **secondary tariffs** (penalise third countries, not Russia directly); ceiling **500%**; India ≈ world's **3rd-largest crude importer**, with Russia a top supplier since 2022; discount-driven imports underpin India's energy-cost management.
**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India–US, India–Russia; effect of foreign-country policies on India's interests).
**Static anchor:** *Vision/Shankar IR notes — "India–US relations" & "India's energy diplomacy."* Add a cross-link: **secondary sanctions/tariffs → strategic autonomy → diversification of crude sourcing (US, Gulf, Russia).**

### 4. Department of Posts–TRAI sign an MoU to survey mobile-network quality across 5.68 lakh villages — Polity & Governance
**Headline:** The **Department of Posts (DoP)** and the **Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI)** signed an **MoU in New Delhi on 10 July 2026** for a nationwide, app-based survey of mobile-network performance (Airtel, Jio, Vi, BSNL) across **over 5.68 lakh villages** — leveraging the postal network's last-mile reach.
**Exam angle:** A neat governance case study in **inter-agency convergence + last-mile digital delivery** — India Post's field muscle plus TRAI's regulatory mandate to close the rural connectivity/"digital divide." Good "which body does what" material: TRAI is a **statutory regulator (TRAI Act, 1997)**; India Post is under the Ministry of Communications.
**Key facts to memorise:** **~1.4 lakh Gramin Dak Sevaks (GDS)** will collect data on their delivery rounds using a **TRAI-built Android app**; **one-year** partnership; DoP does fieldwork, TRAI provides app/training/central monitoring; feeds evidence-based rural-connectivity and QoS policy.
**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (statutory/regulatory bodies; e-governance; government schemes for vulnerable/rural sections).
**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth — "Regulatory bodies / Non-constitutional bodies" (place TRAI correctly as statutory, not constitutional).* Add a cross-link to Mrunal-governance "Digital India / bridging the digital divide."

### 5. Retail inflation climbs to 4.38% in June 2026 — the highest print since December 2024 — Economy
**Headline:** India's **CPI (Consumer Price Index) retail inflation** rose to **4.38%** in **June 2026** (provisional), up from **3.93%** in May — its **highest reading since December 2024** — driven mainly by food; **CFPI (food) inflation was 5.32%**. Data released by **MoSPI/NSO on 13 July 2026**.
**Exam angle:** Keep the sub-index literacy sharp: **rural 4.74% vs urban 3.92%** (rural higher, food-weighted); the print stays **within the RBI's 2–6% tolerance band** around the **4% target**, but the up-tick matters ahead of the **August MPC**. Distinguish **CPI (base 2024=100, MoSPI)** from **WPI (base 2011-12, DPIIT)**; the RBI targets **CPI**, not WPI.
**Key facts to memorise:** Headline **4.38%**; food **5.32%**; rural **4.74%**, urban **3.92%**; housing **2.10%**; new **CPI base year 2024=100**; flexible-inflation-target framework (RBI Act amendment, 2016) — **4% ±2%**.
**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (inflation; monetary policy).
**Static anchor:** *Mrunal — Pillar 1C "Inflation" & Pillar 1D "Monetary Policy / MPC."* Add a cross-link: CPI basket & weights (food ~ 45.9%), CPI vs WPI vs GDP-deflator, and the MPC's inflation-targeting mandate.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — International Relations, 250 words, 15 marks)

> "India's continued imports of discounted Russian crude illustrate the tension between **energy security** and the pressures of an interdependent, sanctions-driven global order." In light of recent moves to impose **secondary tariffs** on buyers of Russian energy, **examine** the challenges to India's strategic autonomy and suggest a way forward.

**Model-answer skeleton:**
- **Intro:** Frame strategic autonomy + India as 3rd-largest crude importer; Russia a leading supplier since 2022; the new secondary-tariff threat as the trigger.
- **Body — the tension:** (i) Energy security & inflation management vs Western pressure (secondary tariffs up to 500%, lapse of the US Treasury general licence, India–US trade truce at stake). (ii) Strategic-autonomy stakes: precedent of CAATSA/S-400; avoiding forced alignment. (iii) Economic exposure: current-account & import-bill risk if discounts vanish; GDP hit estimates.
- **Way forward:** Diversify crude sourcing (US, Gulf, Latin America, Africa); accelerate energy transition & strategic petroleum reserves; use plurilateral coalitions and quiet diplomacy; rupee-trade & payment resilience.
- **Conclusion:** Autonomy is managed, not absolute — hedging + diversification + credible diplomacy.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Vikram-1 is India's first *private orbital-class* vehicle by Skyroot, launched from Sriharikota. Statement 3 is wrong: the first privately built Indian rocket to fly was **Vikram-S** (sub-orbital, Nov 2022), not Vikram-1.

**Q2 — (b).** ISP is a **monthly volume index of services output** released by **MoSPI**, base year **2024-25** — the services counterpart to the IIP.

**Q3 — (b).** "Secondary tariffs/sanctions" penalise **third countries** that keep trading with a sanctioned nation (here, buyers of Russian energy such as India and China).

**Q4 — (a) 1 and 3 only.** GDS collect the data (1 ✓); the survey spans 5.68 lakh villages (3 ✓). Statement 2 is wrong: **TRAI is a statutory body** (TRAI Act, 1997), not a constitutional one — there is no "Article 324A."

**Q5 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** CPI (for the RBI target) is compiled by NSO/MoSPI; WPI by the Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT; the RBI's flexible target is **4% ±2%**.

**Q6 — (b) Only two.** TRAI (statutory ✓) and NSIL (DoS commercial arm ✓) are correct. **IN-SPACe is not a constitutional body** and is under the **Department of Space (not MHA)** — it is a single-window autonomous/regulatory agency created in 2020. Hence pair 3 is wrong.
