## Part A — Briefing

### 1. RBI lets banks break the weekly queue for FCNR(B) swaps above $100 million — Economy

**Headline.** On **27 August 2026** the Reserve Bank relaxed the access rule for its concessional dollar–rupee swap facility, allowing banks to transact **outside their designated weekly window for any swap above USD 100 million**.

**What is new since the 2 August briefing.** That briefing covered the facility itself at USD 40.8 billion of mobilisation. Two things have changed since: the RBI has **wound the window up early** (fresh FCNR(B) mobilisation now shuts on **31 August 2026** instead of 30 September, and banks may avail swaps with the RBI only till **11 September 2026** instead of 16 October), and it has now **relaxed the weekly-window rule** for large tickets. Do not re-explain the scheme in your notes — just append these two lines.

**Exam angle.**
- This is a live demonstration of the **liquidity-management vs. external-stability trade-off**. Banks sitting on undeployed dollars while waiting for a once-a-week window were bidding up **short-term dollar–rupee swap and hedging costs**; the relaxation is a plumbing fix, not a policy loosening.
- The deeper point examiners like: FCNR(B) money is a **debt-creating capital-account inflow**, not FDI. It props up reserves today and creates a **repayment/maturity wall** in three to five years. Contrast this with the 2013 FCNR(B) swap window, which India had to refinance in 2016.
- Ties into **rupee management, forward premia, and the impossible trinity**.

**Key facts / figures to memorise.**
- Relaxation effective **27 August 2026**; threshold **USD 100 million** per swap; smaller transactions stay on the weekly schedule.
- Total mobilisation under the facility, as on **21 August 2026: USD 72.85 billion**.
- Break-up: **FCNR(B) deposits USD 65.40 bn**, **overseas foreign-currency borrowings USD 4.86 bn**, **ECBs USD 2.59 bn**.
- Facility was announced 5 June 2026 and operationalised 8 June 2026; early closure was signalled around **14 August 2026** after inflows crossed USD 52.3 bn.
- **FCNR(B)** = Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) term deposit — denominated in foreign currency, so the **depositor bears no exchange risk**; the bank (and, through the swap, the RBI) does.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Economy (External Sector / Monetary Policy).
**Static anchor.** *Mrunal* **Balance of Payments / External Sector pillar** — the NRI-deposit sub-topic. → You already have the NRE/NRO/FCNR(B) comparison table from 2 August; add a two-line "2026 facility timeline" box (5 June announced → 8 June live → 14 Aug early-closure signal → 27 Aug weekly-window relaxation → 31 Aug deposit cut-off → 11 Sep swap cut-off) and cross-link to "forward premia and covered interest parity".

### 2. PM SVANidhi's restructured phase completes one year — Society

**Headline.** On **27 August 2026** the **Prime Minister Street Vendor's AtmaNirbhar Nidhi (PM SVANidhi)** completed one year of its restructured phase, approved by the Cabinet on 27 August 2025 and extended to **31 March 2030**.

**Exam angle.**
- A clean case study of a **COVID-era relief credit line evolving into a permanent urban-livelihoods architecture** — credit plus digital payments plus social security plus skilling. That evolution is the Mains argument, not the loan amounts.
- The scheme is the operational arm of the **Street Vendors (Protection of Livelihood and Regulation of Street Vending) Act, 2014**: identification through surveys and Certificates of Vending is what makes a vendor bankable. Vending regulation is a **Municipality function under the Twelfth Schedule**, so this is also a fiscal-federalism and urban-governance item.
- Watch the **coverage expansion beyond statutory towns to census towns and peri-urban areas** — an admission that India's "urban" definition under-counts urban informal work.

**Key facts / figures to memorise.**
- Original launch **1 June 2020**, a **central sector scheme** of the **Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs**.
- Restructured outlay **₹7,332 crore**; lending period to **31 March 2030**; target about **1.15 crore street vendors**, including **50 lakh new beneficiaries**.
- Loan tranches revised: **first ₹15,000** (from ₹10,000), **second ₹25,000** (from ₹20,000), **third ₹50,000** (unchanged).
- **7% per annum interest subsidy** on timely/early repayment, credited quarterly by **DBT**.
- **UPI-linked RuPay credit card** for vendors who repay the second tranche; **digital cashback up to ₹1,600** on retail/wholesale UPI transactions.
- Capacity building via financial literacy, digital skills, entrepreneurship and **FSSAI-linked hygiene and safety training**; **'SVANidhi se Samriddhi'** welfare saturation through monthly **Lok Kalyan Melas**.

**Notebook.** GS2 — Governance / Social Justice (welfare schemes for vulnerable sections); spills into GS3 Economy (informal sector, financial inclusion).
**Static anchor.** *Laxmikanth* — **Municipalities chapter (Part IXA, Articles 243P–243ZG, Twelfth Schedule)**. → Add a one-line cross-link: "Street Vendors Act 2014 → Town Vending Committee (40% vendor members, at least one-third women) → Certificate of Vending → PM SVANidhi credit eligibility", and note the Article 19(1)(g) line of cases (*Bombay Hawkers' Union*, 1985; *Sodan Singh v. NDMC*, 1989) that pushed Parliament toward the 2014 Act.

### 3. India's first dedicated Quantum and AI university campus cleared for Amaravati — Science & Technology

**Headline.** Andhra Pradesh CM **N. Chandrababu Naidu** approved, at a **CRDA Authority meeting on 27 August 2026**, India's **first dedicated Quantum and Artificial Intelligence university campus**, to be set up in **Amaravati** by **NIELIT**.

**Exam angle.**
- The interesting institutional fact is *who* is building it: **NIELIT (National Institute of Electronics and Information Technology)**, an **autonomous scientific society under MeitY** — not a UGC university, not an IIT. A central grant-in-aid campus executed by a State authority is a useful federal-cooperation example.
- Sits at the junction of three national missions — **National Quantum Mission (DST)**, **IndiaAI Mission (MeitY)** and the **Semicon India Programme** — and the "deep-tech talent pipeline" argument: India's constraint is skilled people, not fabs alone.
- Pairs naturally with the **Amaravati Quantum Valley** build-out, giving you a "cluster / innovation-ecosystem" example for GS3 Sci-Tech and Economy answers.

**Key facts / figures to memorise.**
- **8.5 acres**; investment **₹730.70 crore over five years**, **fully funded by GoI grant-in-aid from MeitY**.
- Programmes across **UG, PG, PhD, Diploma and Executive** streams; focus on **quantum computing, AI, VLSI and semiconductor fabrication**.
- Expected to train about **8,250 learners over five years**; programmes targeted to begin **September 2026**.
- Proposed academic partners: **IIT Tirupati, IIIT Sri City, Andhra University and JNTU** institutions.
- The associated **Amaravati Quantum Valley** facility, housing the **Quantum Research, Innovation & Incubation Block (QRIIB)**, is reported for completion by **December 2026** and operations from **January 2027**.
- Static peg: **National Quantum Mission**, approved April 2023, outlay **₹6,003.65 crore** for 2023-24 to 2030-31, run by **DST**, with **four Thematic Hubs** — quantum computing (IISc Bengaluru), quantum communication (IIT Madras), quantum sensing & metrology (IIT Bombay), quantum materials & devices (IIT Delhi). Headline target: intermediate-scale quantum computers of **50–1,000 physical qubits in eight years**.

### 4. A new Leopard's bane from Tawang — *Doronicum sanctum* is the genus's first record from Northeast India — Environment

**Headline.** Researchers at **CSIR–IHBT (Institute of Himalayan Bioresource Technology), Palampur** described **_Doronicum sanctum_**, a new species of the sunflower family (**Asteraceae**), from **Tawang district, Arunachal Pradesh**, at roughly **4,086 metres**.

**Exam angle.**
- The taxonomic point that makes it examinable: this is the **first documented record of the genus _Doronicum_ from Northeast India** — all earlier Indian records were confined to the **Western Himalaya**. That is a **disjunct-distribution / biogeography** fact, exactly the kind UPSC likes to hang a "places and species in news" question on.
- Reinforces the **Eastern Himalaya biodiversity hotspot** and the argument that India's high-altitude alpine flora remains under-surveyed — useful for Mains lines on **taxonomic capacity and access-and-benefit-sharing under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002**.
- The epithet ***sanctum*** comes from the type locality near the **Chumi Gyatse ("Holy") Waterfalls** in Tawang — a site of religious significance to local communities, which quietly links biodiversity documentation to a sensitive border landscape.

**Key facts / figures to memorise.**
- Genus **_Doronicum_**, common name **Leopard's bane**; family **Asteraceae** (the sunflower/daisy family, among the largest families of flowering plants).
- Collected on slopes en route to **Damteng**, Tawang district; altitude about **4,086 m**; described in a **Springer Nature** peer-reviewed journal.
- Diagnostic characters: **pubescent (hairy) ray cypselae**, a **caducous/short-lived feathery pappus**, **dilated peduncle apex**, and **flat, lance-shaped green bracts**; bright yellow ray florets with heart-shaped leaves.
- Distinguished from its nearest Chinese congeners **_D. conaense_** and **_D. stenoglossum_**.
- Institutional peg: **CSIR–IHBT** is at Palampur, Himachal Pradesh; annual national species descriptions are compiled by the **Botanical Survey of India** in its *Plant Discoveries* series.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Environment & Biodiversity; GS1 Geography for the Eastern Himalaya/alpine-zone angle.
**Static anchor.** *Shankar IAS Environment* — **Biodiversity chapter (hotspots and India's biogeographic zones)**. → Add *Doronicum sanctum* to your running "new species 2026" list, and cross-link the Eastern Himalaya hotspot entry to the Trans-Himalayan/Himalayan biogeographic zones and to Tawang as a place-in-news.

### 5. Banas Bio-CNG model plant fires up in Banaskantha — and dung becomes a rural taxi fleet — Agriculture

**Headline.** The **Banas Bio-CNG Model Plant** — a joint venture of **Banas Dairy, Suzuki Motor Corporation and the NDDB** — was inaugurated at **Vinchhivadi, Dhanera taluka, Banaskantha district, Gujarat**, alongside a commercial Bio-CNG dispensing station and the flag-off of a **"Gramin Bharat Taxi"** fleet.

**Exam angle.**
- This is the cleanest available **circular-economy case study** for GS3: cattle dung → **anaerobic digestion** → **compressed biogas (CBG)** for transport fuel **plus** fermented organic manure back to the field, with the **dairy cooperative** as the aggregator. Dung stops being a disposal problem and becomes a second income stream for the livestock farmer.
- The **cooperative-ownership taxi model** (drivers own their vehicles) is a small but quotable example of **rural entrepreneurship and asset ownership** — better than a generic "employment generation" line.
- Connects the two policy stacks you must be able to name in one breath: **SATAT** (supply-side, MoPNG) and **GOBARdhan** (waste-to-wealth, Ministry of Jal Shakti), with the **CBG Blending Obligation** now creating demand-side pull.

**Key facts / figures to memorise.**
- Plant capacity **100 metric tonnes per day (MTPD)** of cattle dung via **anaerobic digestion**; outputs are CBG and organic fertiliser.
- Network now **four plants with a combined 340 MTPD**; this is Suzuki's **third operational biogas plant in India**, after **Agthala (December 2025)** and **Bhukhala (January 2026)**; **nine plants planned in Gujarat**.
- **Gramin Bharat Taxi** — described as India's first rural-level cooperative-model taxi service; **nine vehicles** flagged off, with drivers owning their vehicles.
- **NDDB**: established **1965**, headquartered at **Anand, Gujarat**.
- Static pegs: **SATAT** (Sustainable Alternative Towards Affordable Transportation), launched **October 2018** by MoPNG, target of **5,000 CBG plants** producing **15 MMT/year**; **GOBARdhan** launched 2018 under the Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation; **CBG** is purified biogas with methane above ~90% (raw biogas is roughly 50–70% methane); a phased **CBG Blending Obligation** in CNG/PNG began at 1% in FY2025-26 and steps up in later years.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Agriculture / Animal Husbandry, and Environment (renewable energy, waste-to-wealth).
**Static anchor.** *Mrunal* **Agriculture pillar — allied sectors / animal husbandry**, and *Shankar IAS* **renewable energy** topic. → Add a three-line CBG box (feedstock → SATAT offtake → CBO demand mandate) and cross-link it to the National Dairy Development Board, Operation Flood and the Rashtriya Gokul Mission entries.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Economy, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **Special swap windows for non-resident foreign-currency deposits can stabilise the exchange rate in the short run, but they add to India's debt-creating capital inflows. Examine this trade-off with reference to the 2026 FCNR(B) swap facility.**

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points):**

1. **Set up the instrument, not the anecdote.** Define the facility: a concessional dollar–rupee swap in which banks mobilise fresh FCNR(B) deposits and swap the dollars with the RBI at a favourable rate, transferring the exchange risk from the bank to the central bank. Anchor with the 2026 numbers (USD 72.85 bn mobilised, of which USD 65.40 bn FCNR(B), as on 21 August 2026) and the precedent of the 2013 window.

2. **The short-run case in favour.** Immediate accretion to forex reserves; a cushion for the rupee during a period of capital-flow volatility; import-cover and reserve-adequacy improvement; a signalling effect that calms markets faster than intervention in the spot market; and a lower-cost dollar funding line for banks than commercial borrowing.

3. **The costs and risks.** These are **debt-creating** inflows, not FDI — they enter the capital account as liabilities and swell the NRI-deposit component of external debt. Risks: a **three-to-five-year maturity wall** requiring refinancing; the RBI absorbs the **exchange-rate risk** on its own balance sheet; **quasi-fiscal cost** of the concessional swap rate; distortion of **forward premia** and the domestic swap market; and moral hazard if such windows become an expected backstop. Note that the 2026 facility was itself **closed early** once the target was exceeded, and the weekly-window rule had to be relaxed because the plumbing could not handle the volume.

4. **Balanced way forward.** Position such windows as **exceptional, time-bound, and pre-announced with an exit**, not a standing instrument; pair them with structural fixes — deepening the corporate bond and currency-derivative markets, improving the **quality** of the capital account by tilting toward FDI, rupee internationalisation and rupee-denominated borrowing, and reserve adequacy assessed against short-term debt on residual maturity. Conclude that the instrument buys time; it does not substitute for reducing the underlying external financing requirement.

### D. PYQ Mirror — how UPSC has asked these themes before

**Theme: India's external debt and NRI deposits (Item 1, Q6).**
**UPSC Prelims 2019** asked: *"Consider the following statements: 1. Most of India's external debt is owed by governmental entities. 2. All of India's external debt is denominated in US dollars."* — the answer is **Neither 1 nor 2**. Commercial borrowings are the largest component, followed by **NRI deposits**, and the debt is denominated across the US dollar, Indian rupee, SDR, yen and euro. **Verified.** The lesson: UPSC tests the *composition* of external debt, not the headline number — which is exactly the slot an FCNR(B) question would occupy.

**Theme: the informal/unorganised sector and urban livelihoods (Item 2, Q7).**
**UPSC Mains GS3 (2016)** asked: *"How has globalization led to the reduction of employment in the formal sector of the Indian economy? Is increased informalization detrimental to the development of the country?"* — **Year approximate; verify against your PYQ compilation before quoting.** A closely related Mains question on street vending and PM SVANidhi has also appeared in the post-2020 cycle. Either way, the takeaway is that UPSC asks about **informalisation as a structural condition**, so PM SVANidhi should be deployed as evidence inside that argument rather than described as a scheme.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the RBI relaxed the weekly-window rule on 27 August 2026 for swaps above USD 100 million, and FCNR(B) deposits (USD 65.40 bn) were the dominant share of the USD 72.85 bn mobilised. Statement 3 is wrong on both counts — FCNR(B) deposits are held in **foreign currency**, and it is precisely because of this that the **depositor bears no exchange risk**.

**Q2 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** PM SVANidhi is a **central sector** scheme of the **Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs**, not a centrally sponsored scheme of the Finance Ministry, so 1 is wrong. The restructured scheme runs to **31 March 2030** and raised the first tranche from ₹10,000 to **₹15,000**.

**Q3 — (b).** *Doronicum sanctum* is a newly described species of **Asteraceae** (Leopard's bane) found by CSIR–IHBT at about 4,086 m in **Tawang district, Arunachal Pradesh** — the first record of the genus from Northeast India.

**Q4 — (c) Both 1 and 2.** The campus is to be developed by **NIELIT**, an autonomous scientific society under **MeitY**, on 8.5 acres at a cost of **₹730.70 crore**, proposed to be met fully through **GoI grant-in-aid from MeitY**.

**Q5 — (b).** "Gramin Bharat Taxi" was flagged off alongside the **Banas Bio-CNG Model Plant** at Vinchhivadi, Banaskantha, Gujarat — a Banas Dairy–Suzuki–NDDB initiative — and is described as a rural cooperative-model taxi service in which drivers own their vehicles.

**Q6 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** NRE accounts are **rupee-denominated** (so the depositor carries the exchange risk); FCNR(B) accounts are maintained in **foreign currency** (the bank carries it); and **NRI deposits are counted within India's external debt**, historically the second-largest component after commercial borrowings.

**Q7 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The 2014 Act requires each local authority to constitute a **Town Vending Committee** and to conduct a **survey of all existing street vendors**, with vending certificates issued on that basis. **Urban planning including regulation of land use** is the first entry in the **Twelfth Schedule** (Article 243W).
