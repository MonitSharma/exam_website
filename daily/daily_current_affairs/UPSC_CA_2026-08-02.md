## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Centre front-loads ₹1,09,019 crore of tax devolution to States — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** On 1 August 2026 the Union Finance Ministry released an *additional* instalment of ₹1,09,019 crore in tax devolution to States, over and above the regular monthly transfer due on 10 August 2026.

**Exam angle:**
- This is a **cash-flow/timing** decision, not a change in entitlement. The Centre is front-loading the States' constitutional share so that States can push capital expenditure earlier in the fiscal year — a classic "quality of expenditure" and counter-cyclical fiscal argument.
- It lands in the **first year of the Sixteenth Finance Commission award period (2026–31)**, which makes it a live illustration for any fiscal-federalism answer.
- The politics of the horizontal formula is visible in the numbers: the largest shares go to the most populous / lowest-income States, which is exactly the income-distance criterion at work.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Amount: **₹1,09,019 crore**, released **1 August 2026**; in addition to the normal devolution due 10 August 2026.
- Top recipients: **Uttar Pradesh ₹17,908 cr; West Bengal ₹7,986 cr; Maharashtra ₹7,022 cr**.
- **16th Finance Commission**, chaired by **Arvind Panagariya**, award period **2026–31**; vertical devolution **retained at 41%** of the net divisible pool (18 States had demanded 50%).
- Devolution is **untied** money — States decide the use; unlike CSS grants.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Centre–State fiscal relations); cross-tag GS3 Economy.

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth*, Ch. on **Centre–State Financial Relations** (Articles 268–281) + Ch. on **Finance Commission**. → Add a margin note under Article 280 listing the **16th FC: 41% vertical share, Panagariya, 2026–31**, and cross-link to the *Mrunal* fiscal-federalism pillar so the 41%-vs-50% debate sits in one place.

### 2. RBI's concessional swap facility mobilises USD 40.8 billion — Economy

**Headline:** The RBI's special concessional swap window had drawn **USD 40.816 billion** in forex inflows as of 31 July 2026 — nearly double the USD 20.718 billion recorded a fortnight earlier on 17 July.

**Exam angle:**
- The RBI is using a **price incentive on the forward leg** (a concessional swap rate) rather than an interest-rate move to pull in dollars — an instrument-choice question waiting to be asked.
- Almost the entire haul is **FCNR(B) deposits**, i.e. **debt-creating, foreign-currency-denominated** inflows. This is the key analytical distinction from FDI: it shores up the BoP and reserves today but creates a repayment obligation and a maturity wall later.
- Read it alongside the rupee/BoP stress narrative: reserves accretion without a matching current-account improvement is a *financing*, not a *correction*.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **USD 40.816 bn** as on **31 July 2026**; up ~97% from **USD 20.718 bn** on 17 July.
- Break-up: **FCNR(B) deposits USD 36.725 bn** | **Overseas Foreign Currency Borrowings (OFCBs) USD 2.575 bn** | **External Commercial Borrowings (ECBs) USD 1.516 bn**.
- Facility **announced 5 June 2026, operationalised 8 June 2026**. Window: fresh **FCNR(B) till 30 September 2026**; **OFCB/ECB concessional swaps till 31 December 2026**.
- FCNR(B) = **Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank)** term deposits, held in foreign currency, so the **depositor bears no exchange risk** — the bank/RBI swap does.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (External Sector, BoP, monetary management).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* **BoP / External Sector pillar** — the NRI-deposit sub-topic (NRE vs NRO vs FCNR(B)). → Add a one-line comparison table (currency of denomination, who bears exchange risk, repatriability, debt-creating or not) and cross-link to the "capital account convertibility" note.

### 3. Cabinet approves Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana — 5,000 MW of floating solar with storage — Environment

**Headline:** The Union Cabinet on 31 July 2026 approved the **Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana (PM-SSY)** with a ₹5,070 crore outlay to build ~**5,000 MW of Floating Solar Photovoltaic (FSPV)** capacity co-located with **Energy Storage Systems**.

**Exam angle:**
- The design point is **land-neutrality**: FSPV sits on reservoirs and industrial ponds, sidestepping the land-acquisition and land-use-change friction that ground-mounted solar now faces. Co-benefit claimed: reduced evaporation and panel cooling (higher efficiency).
- The **mandatory storage** condition is the real policy signal — India's problem is no longer solar capacity but the **evening peak and grid firming**; this is an RE-plus-storage scheme, not a plain capacity scheme.
- Counter-argument for Mains balance: FSPV can alter reservoir **thermal stratification, dissolved oxygen and aquatic productivity**; siting on Ramsar or ecologically sensitive water bodies is contested.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Outlay **₹5,070 crore**; target **~5,000 MW FSPV**; **minimum 2 hours of storage (≈10,000 MWh)**.
- Central Financial Assistance: **₹1 crore per MW post-commissioning**, plus up to **₹50 lakh for feasibility studies**.
- Sanction window **FY2026-27 to FY2030-31**; disbursement up to **FY2032-33**. Nodal ministry: **MNRE**.
- **NISE (National Institute of Solar Energy)** assessed India's FSPV potential at about **102.18 GWp**.
- Claimed impact: ~**10 million tonnes CO₂ avoided annually**; ~16,000–17,000 jobs.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Energy (renewables, climate mitigation).

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment*, **Renewable Energy / Climate Change mitigation** chapter. → Insert PM-SSY into the solar-scheme ladder you already have (National Solar Mission → PM-KUSUM → PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli → PM-SSY) and tag each by *where* the panel sits: farm, roof, water body.

### 4. India and Bhutan sign a ₹4,000 crore concessional Line of Credit at the 5th Development Cooperation Talks — International Relations

**Headline:** At the **Fifth India–Bhutan Development Cooperation Talks in Thimphu on 31 July 2026**, India Exim Bank and Bhutan's Ministry of Finance signed a **₹4,000 crore (Nu 40 billion)** concessional Line of Credit, principally for energy projects under Bhutan's 13th Five Year Plan.

**Exam angle:**
- Bhutan is the standing test case for **"Neighbourhood First" done well**: India is Bhutan's largest development and trade partner, and the relationship is structured around **hydropower** — India finances and builds, Bhutan exports surplus power back to India.
- The shift visible here is from **grant-heavy** assistance towards **concessional credit plus institutional MoUs** — a maturing, less paternalistic aid model that India is also trying in Africa and the Indian Ocean.
- Strategic subtext (say it carefully in Mains): Bhutan is the only immediate neighbour without diplomatic relations with China, and the **Doklam tri-junction** sits on its territory.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **₹4,000 crore / Nu 40 billion** concessional LoC; **Exim Bank of India ↔ Bhutan Ministry of Finance**; signed **31 July 2026, Thimphu**.
- Tied to **Bhutan's 13th Five Year Plan (2024–29)**; energy projects the stated focus.
- Also announced: **12 new projects worth ₹332 crore**; an **AIIMS – Khesar Gyalpo University of Medical Sciences** MoU on medical research and education; **45 electric vehicles** handed over.
- Static pegs: **India–Bhutan Friendship Treaty 1949, revised 2007**; **Ngultrum pegged at par with the Indian Rupee**; India–Bhutan border ≈ **699 km** (Sikkim, West Bengal, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh).

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India and its neighbourhood).

**Static anchor:** Your **India–Bhutan country note** (IR section) and *NCERT/Mrunal* on **hydropower cooperation**. → Update the hydro-project ladder (Chukha, Kurichhu, Tala, Mangdechhu, Punatsangchhu-I & II) with a line on the 2026 LoC, and cross-link to the "line of credit vs grant vs buyer's credit" distinction in the Economy notes.

### 5. PM launches 'Nasha Mukt Yuva for Viksit Bharat Sankalp Abhiyaan' — a 100-week youth campaign — Society

**Headline:** On **2 August 2026** the Prime Minister launched the **Nasha Mukt Yuva for Viksit Bharat Sankalp Abhiyaan**, a 100-week, youth-led *Jan Bhagidari* campaign against substance abuse, driven by **MY Bharat** under the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports.

**Exam angle:**
- Note the **institutional shift**: the older **Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan (2020)** is a Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment programme built on demand reduction and de-addiction infrastructure. This new campaign is **Youth Affairs-led and mobilisational**, using MY Bharat volunteers, NSS units and faith organisations. Two ministries, two logics — a good "convergence vs duplication" point.
- The design choice — **weekly Sunday activity for 100 weeks** rather than a one-day pledge — is an explicit answer to the standard criticism that awareness campaigns are event-driven. Whether sustained mobilisation changes behaviour is the honest counterpoint.
- Links cleanly to the **demographic dividend** argument and to the NDPS enforcement-vs-treatment debate (addiction as a public-health problem, not only a criminal one).

**Facts to memorise:**
- Launched **2 August 2026** by the PM via video conferencing; **100-week** campaign, activities **every Sunday**.
- Implemented through **MY Bharat (Mera Yuva Bharat)** under the **Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports**.
- Launch scale: pledge at nearly **10,000 locations**, **over 1 crore youth**; partners include **NSS units, youth clubs, NGOs and 125+ spiritual organisations**.
- Distinguish from **Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan (NMBA)** — launched **15 August 2020**, Ministry of **Social Justice & Empowerment**, originally in 272 most-affected districts.
- Statutory backdrop: **NDPS Act, 1985**; treatment infrastructure via **De-addiction Centres (IRCAs)**.

**Notebook:** GS1/GS2 — Society (youth, social issues) and Governance (campaign-mode implementation).

**Static anchor:** *Social Justice / Vulnerable Sections* notes — the **substance abuse** sub-head. → Create a two-column entry: *NMBA (2020, MoSJE, demand reduction)* vs *Nasha Mukt Yuva Sankalp Abhiyaan (2026, MoYAS, mobilisation)*, and cross-link to the **NDPS Act, 1985** note.

### C. Mains-Style Question

**GS2 — Polity & Governance (250 words, 15 marks)**

*"Front-loading the States' share of central taxes eases their cash flow but does not enlarge their fiscal space."* **Examine** this statement in the context of the Sixteenth Finance Commission award period.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the distinction.** Devolution timing (liquidity) vs devolution quantum and composition (fiscal space). Anchor in Article 270 and the 16th FC's decision to retain the vertical share at 41% for 2026–31, against an 18-State demand for 50%. Cite the ₹1,09,019 crore advance release of 1 August 2026 as the illustrative event.

2. **What front-loading genuinely achieves.** Reduces States' reliance on high-cost market borrowing and ways-and-means advances early in the year; enables earlier capex tendering and a smoother, less March-loaded expenditure profile; untied character preserves State autonomy over allocation.

3. **Why it does not enlarge fiscal space.** The divisible pool is shrunk at source by cesses and surcharges outside Article 270; the CSS matching-share burden pre-commits State budgets; committed expenditure (salaries, pensions, interest, subsidies) crowds out capex; FRBM borrowing ceilings and conditional net-borrowing headroom bind irrespective of transfer timing.

4. **Way forward and balanced close.** Rationalise cesses and surcharges into the divisible pool; simplify and reduce the number of CSS; stabilise the horizontal formula so States can plan multi-year; strengthen State Finance Commissions so devolution reaches the third tier. Conclude that timing reform is welcome and cheap, but structural reform of the pool itself is the binding constraint.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** The release was explicitly additional to the instalment due on 10 August 2026, and devolution is untied. Statement 2 is wrong: the share itself is unchanged at 41%; only the timing was advanced.

**Q2 — (b) Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) deposits.** FCNR(B) accounted for USD 36.725 bn of the USD 40.816 bn total; OFCBs contributed USD 2.575 bn and ECBs USD 1.516 bn.

**Q3 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** PM-SSY targets ~5,000 MW of FSPV with a mandatory co-located storage system of at least two hours. The nodal ministry is the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy, not Jal Shakti.

**Q4 — (c) Export-Import Bank of India; Bhutan's 13th Five Year Plan.** The LoC was signed between India Exim Bank and Bhutan's Ministry of Finance and is linked to the 13th FYP (2024–29), with energy as the stated focus.

**Q5 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The campaign runs through MY Bharat under the Ministry of Youth Affairs & Sports for 100 weeks. It does not replace the Nasha Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan (2020), which continues under the Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment.

**Q6 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Article 280 provides for the Finance Commission's constitution by the President; cesses and surcharges under Article 271 sit outside the net proceeds shared with States. Its recommendations are **advisory**, not binding, though conventionally accepted.

**Q7 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** FCNR(B) deposits and ECBs create a repayment obligation and are debt-creating. FDI is a non-debt-creating inflow, as is portfolio equity investment.
