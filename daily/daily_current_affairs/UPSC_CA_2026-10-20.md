## Part A — Briefing

### 1. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio in India (17–22 Oct); talks with EAM Jaishankar in Mumbai today — International Relations
**Exam angle:** The visit is the first high-level US engagement while the India–US trade deal is unfinished and the Russian-oil question is open. It is a good case for Mains questions on "strategic autonomy vs. economic pressure" and on how India handles tariff and sanctions leverage. The MEA calls the visit a chance to advance cooperation across the pillars of the "Comprehensive Global Strategic Partnership".
**Key facts:**
- Rubio is in India 17–22 Oct. He holds talks with EAM S. Jaishankar in **Mumbai on 20 Oct**, then goes to **Jamnagar (Gujarat)** for energy talks.
- Agenda: energy, trade, investment, defence, critical and emerging technologies.
- Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal says the **first phase of the trade deal is finalised and in legal drafting**; it will be operationalised only when India gets a "competitive advantage". An **interim framework** was announced by the White House in February.
- US Ambassador Sergio Gor says President Trump is expected to decide **within about 10 days** whether to invoke sanctions powers that could hit buyers of Russian oil and gas. India says its energy choices follow national interest and market conditions.
- The MEA rejected claims that Rubio "bypassed Delhi", noting that hosting dignitaries outside the capital has been routine for about a decade.

**Notebook:** GS2 – International Relations (India–USA).
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS / Mrunal "India and its neighbourhood & major powers" – India–USA. Add a one-line timeline: interim framework (Feb 2026) → phase-1 legal drafting (Oct 2026) → sanctions decision (~end Oct). Cross-link to energy security (Russian crude).

### 2. CWC reservoir bulletin: storage at ~68.6% of live capacity across 178 major reservoirs; the South is the weak spot — Geography
**Exam angle:** An El Niño year plus a deficient monsoon is stressing water storage. Useful for GS1 (monsoon, water resources), GS3 (irrigation, food security) and GS2 (inter-state water disputes, e.g., Cauvery, Krishna).
**Key facts:**
- The CWC bulletin of **15 Oct 2026** (as reported by a UPSC digest; the full bulletin was not accessed) puts storage at **126.717 BCM, or 68.58%** of the usable (live) capacity of **178 reservoirs**.
- Earlier, a 24 Sept CWC update showed about **70% storage against a 10-year average of 76.62%**. **South India was at about 50%**; cumulative monsoon rainfall was about 12% below normal, and parts of Maharashtra and Karnataka were declared drought-hit.
- Reported weak spots: Aliyar, Idukki, Somasila and Mettur reservoirs; Pennar, Cauvery and Krishna basins show the largest deficits. Narmada and Mahanadi basins are above normal.
- **CWC:** under the Ministry of Jal Shakti; formed 1945 (renamed 1951); HQ New Delhi; monitors reservoir storage, issues flood forecasts, handles dam design and safety.

**Notebook:** GS1 Geography (Indian monsoon, water resources) / GS3 Agriculture (irrigation).
**Static anchor:** NCERT Class XI *India: Physical Environment* – Climate; Spectrum "Water Resources". Add today's figure to a "storage vs 10-year average" tracker and cross-link to the 15 Oct WMO El Niño note.

### 3. Draft lab-animal rules, 2026: 3Rs, registration and Institutional Animal Ethics Committees — Environment (Animal Welfare)
**Exam angle:** A draft rule-making story that links the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act, 1960 with Article 51A(g) (compassion for living creatures). Animal-rights groups such as PETA India have asked for withdrawal, arguing it weakens oversight. Good for "Statements" MCQs on regulators and committees.
**Key facts:**
- Issued by the **Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying** on **6 Oct 2026** as **G.S.R. 864(E)**; comments invited until **6 Nov 2026**.
- Built on the **3Rs: Replacement, Reduction, Refinement**.
- Breeders, traders and establishments register with the **CCSEA** (generally valid 3 years); experiments need prior approval of the CCSEA or the **Institutional Animal Ethics Committee (IAEC)**, which has **nine members** including a scientist, a veterinarian, an external member and a socially aware member.
- Use of protected wildlife, wild/stray animals and **non-human primates is restricted**, subject to scientific justification and permission.
- Which earlier rules it supersedes was not stated in the source I read; check the notification text before writing it into notes.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment (animal welfare) / GS2 Governance (delegated legislation).
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS – Environment: "Animal welfare laws"; Laxmikanth – Fundamental Duties (Art. 51A(g)). Nudge: add a table of Animal Welfare Board of India vs. the experiments committee, with legal basis for each.

### 4. National Strategic Trade Command Centre (NSTCC) launched by Piyush Goyal — Economy
**Exam angle:** A data-governance reform for foreign trade: one dashboard for policymakers across DGFT functions. Prelims angle is what it covers; Mains angle is "data-driven policy and ease of doing trade".
**Key facts:**
- Launched in New Delhi on **15–16 Oct 2026** (reports differ on the exact day) by the **Commerce & Industry Minister**.
- Gives a **consolidated view** of trade performance, export-promotion schemes, regulatory approvals, application processing and operational indicators.
- Covers DGFT areas such as **Importer-Exporter Code (IEC), Advance Authorisation, EPCG, Certificate of Origin, SCOMET**, and restricted imports/exports.
- The minister asked for **regular Inter-Ministerial Committee meetings** to clear pending applications.

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (external sector, trade facilitation).
**Static anchor:** Mrunal – "Foreign Trade Policy and DGFT"; Ramesh Singh – Chapter on Foreign Trade. Nudge: add a one-line glossary of IEC, EPCG, Advance Authorisation, SCOMET.

### 5. TDB–DST backs QNu Labs with ₹150 crore from the RDI Fund for quantum-secure and sensing technologies — Science & Technology
**Exam angle:** Distinct from the 16 Oct DRDO QKD field trial: this is about funding and the wider "quantum-secure and sensing" stack, including navigation without GPS. Pairs with the National Quantum Mission in notes.
**Key facts:**
- **₹150 crore** from the **Technology Development Board (TDB, under DST)** through the **Research, Development and Innovation (RDI) Fund**, as **optionally convertible debt**; total project cost **₹300 crore**. Recipient: **QNu Labs, Bengaluru**.
- Four pillars: identity security (quantum-secure PUFs, quantum-safe TPMs), location integrity (**quantum magnetometers**, navigation without GPS), network security (chip- and satellite-based **QKD**), time integrity (indigenous high-precision **atomic clocks**).
- Goal: take technologies from **TRL 4–5 to TRL 9** under "Quantum Secure and Sensing Networks".

**Notebook:** GS3 Science & Technology (quantum, indigenisation).
**Static anchor:** Spectrum / Shankar IAS – "Quantum technologies". Nudge: add a TRL ladder note and a cross-link to the 16 Oct DRDO QKD entry.

### B3. Mains question
**GS3 · 150 words · 10 marks:** *Examine* how a deficient monsoon in an El Niño year affects India's reservoir storage, agriculture and water governance. Suggest measures to improve preparedness.

**Model-answer skeleton**
- Intro: link El Niño to weaker monsoon; cite storage figures (about 68–70% of live capacity vs a 10-year average near 77%; the South at roughly half).
- Impacts: rabi sowing and irrigation; drinking water and hydropower; inflation pass-through; inter-state tension in the Cauvery and Krishna basins.
- Governance gaps: fragmented storage data, weak groundwater regulation, thin crop insurance in rain-fed areas.
- Way forward: dynamic reservoir operation rules, micro-irrigation (PMKSY), groundwater recharge, crop-diversification advisories, early drought declaration under the Manual for Drought Management.
