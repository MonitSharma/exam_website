## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Supreme Court cuts the Bar-practice requirement for civil judges from three years to one — Polity & Governance

**Headline.** On 21 August 2026 a three-judge Bench of the Supreme Court, deciding review petitions against its May 2025 ruling in the *All India Judges Association* line of cases, reduced the mandatory minimum Bar practice for entry-level recruitment as Civil Judge (Junior Division) from **three years to one year**, while adding **one year of judicial-academy training and one year of structured clerkship** in its place.

**Exam angle.**
- This is the clearest recent illustration of the Supreme Court exercising a *quasi-legislative* function over service conditions of the district judiciary — a standing GS2 theme (judicial overreach vs. judicial supervision of the judiciary itself).
- It is also a fresh data point for the "district judiciary as the base of the pyramid" argument: vacancy, pendency and the pipeline of young law graduates.
- The composition of the relief matters more than the headline: the Court did not abandon the three-year *equivalence*, it re-engineered it into a hybrid.

**Key facts to memorise.**
- Bench: **CJI Surya Kant, Justice Augustine George Masih, Justice K. Vinod Chandran**; decision **2:1**, with **Justice K. Vinod Chandran dissenting**. The review petition was formally dismissed even as the earlier direction was modified.
- New hybrid model = **1 year actual Bar practice + 1 year intensive judicial academy training + 1 year structured clerkship** (reported as 6 months under a District Judge / Higher Judicial Service officer + 6 months under a High Court judge) — i.e. three years of *equivalent* practical exposure.
- **Transitional window**: candidates appearing in judicial-service examinations notified between **25 May 2025 and 31 March 2027** are eligible irrespective of prior practice.
- Backstory worth one line: the practice requirement had been *removed* by the Supreme Court in **All India Judges Association (2002)** on the **Shetty Commission** (First National Judicial Pay Commission) recommendation, and was **restored in May 2025** — this order is the third turn of the same wheel.
- **Same 24 hours, same subject:** the Union Cabinet on **20 August 2026** approved a **bench of the High Court of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh at Ladakh** — the Court's **third seat** after Srinagar and Jammu. Note the distinction: this is a *bench of the existing common High Court*, **not** a separate High Court for Ladakh.

**Destination notebook.** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Judiciary; structure and appointment of subordinate courts).

**Static anchor.** *Laxmikanth*, chapter on **Subordinate Courts (Articles 233–237)** — and the chapter on **High Courts (Articles 214–231)** for the Ladakh bench.
> **Update nudge:** In the Subordinate Courts chapter, add a margin timeline against Art. 233 — *pre-2002 practice rule → All India Judges Association (2002) removes it on Shetty Commission advice → May 2025 restores 3 years → 21 Aug 2026 reduces to 1 year + academy + clerkship.* Cross-link Art. 231 (common High Court for two or more States/UTs) to the new Ladakh bench, and to your earlier note on the reorganisation of J&K.

### 2. India and Japan sign a Memorandum of Arrangement on Maritime Security Cooperation — International Relations

**Headline.** At the India–Japan Defence Ministers' Meeting in New Delhi on **20 August 2026**, Raksha Mantri **Rajnath Singh** and Japan's Defence Minister **Shinjirō Koizumi** signed a **Memorandum of Arrangement (MoA) on Maritime Security Cooperation** between the two Defence Ministries — the first dedicated maritime-security instrument in the India–Japan defence stack.

**Exam angle.**
- The MoA converts a lot of loose Indo-Pacific language into a named framework: **Maritime Domain Awareness (MDA) information sharing**, **Search and Rescue (SAR)** and **HADR** between the **Japan Maritime Self-Defence Force (JMSDF)** and the **Indian Navy**.
- Watch the *industrial* half of the outcome — mine countermeasures and possible joint naval shipbuilding/design is Japan's first serious offer of hull-and-yard technology to India, which is a Make-in-India + defence-exports argument, not just a diplomacy one.
- Excellent GS2 material on "minilateral hedging": India–Japan bilateral defence architecture running in parallel with the Quad rather than inside it.

**Key facts to memorise.**
- Koizumi's visit: **19–20 August 2026**, his **first ever visit to India** as Defence Minister; in Mumbai he inspected a Guard of Honour at **Western Naval Command**, laid a wreath at the **Gaurav Stambh**, and visited the indigenous stealth destroyer **INS Chennai**.
- Other outcomes: a **Working Group at DG / Joint Secretary level**; **DRDO** and Japan's **ATLA (Acquisition, Technology & Logistics Agency)** to deepen advanced-technology cooperation; a **Defence Industry Forum**; expansion of **Dharma Guardian** (Army) and **JIMEX** (Navy) exercises; **mine countermeasures** capability synergy.
- Context reported: Japanese PM **Sanae Takaichi** visited India in **July 2026** for the **16th India–Japan Annual Summit**, with agreements reported at over **USD 10 billion** *(figure as reported in the press — treat as approximate)*.
- The existing scaffolding you must be able to name: **Special Strategic and Global Partnership (2014)**, **2+2 Foreign & Defence Ministerial Dialogue (since 2019)**, **Acquisition and Cross-Servicing Agreement (ACSA, 2020)**, exercises **Dharma Guardian (Army)**, **JIMEX (Navy)**, **Veer Guardian (Air Force)**, and **Malabar** (with the US and Australia).

**Destination notebook.** GS2 — International Relations (India and its bilateral relations; Indo-Pacific).

**Static anchor.** Your **India–Japan bilateral one-pager** (IR notes) and the **Indo-Pacific / Quad** page.
> **Update nudge:** Add a two-column table to the Japan page — *Agreement | Year* (SSGP 2014 · Civil Nuclear 2016 · ACSA 2020 · Maritime Security MoA 2026) — and cross-link to the MAHASAGAR/Mauritius note from 11 August so the Indian Ocean MDA thread reads as one story.

### 3. CMLRE releases the first National Report on Deep-Sea and Distant-Water Fishery Resources of India's EEZ — Environment

**Headline.** The **Centre for Marine Living Resources & Ecology (CMLRE)**, an attached office of the **Ministry of Earth Sciences**, released the **'National Report on Deep-Sea and Distant-Water Fishery Resources of the Indian Exclusive Economic Zone'** at Kochi during its **Silver Jubilee** — the first consolidated national assessment of what lives, and can be sustainably harvested, below 200 m in Indian waters.

**Exam angle.**
- This is the missing evidence base for the **Blue Economy**: India's marine fisheries are almost entirely a *shelf* (0–200 m) activity, and the report maps the unexploited **mesopelagic** layer beyond it.
- Sustainability framing for Mains: the report is simultaneously a resource inventory *and* a conservation caution — mesopelagic fish are the engine of the **biological carbon pump**, so "harvest the twilight zone" is not a free lunch.
- Prelims will most likely test the *institution* (CMLRE ↔ MoES, not DoF/Ministry of Fisheries) and the *vessel*.

**Key facts to memorise.**
- India's **Exclusive Economic Zone: ~2.37 million sq km**.
- Evidence base: nearly **40 years** of oceanographic research, **30 years** of systematic surveys, **400+ research cruises** since the early 1990s under the **Marine Living Resources (MLR) Programme**.
- Flagship research vessel: **FORV Sagar Sampada** (Fishery and Oceanographic Research Vessel).
- The **mesopelagic or "twilight" zone** = roughly **200–1,000 m** depth; key resources flagged are **myctophids (lanternfishes)**, **deep-sea shrimps and lobsters**, **squids/cephalopods** — described as largely **unexploited / non-conventional** resources.
- Released in the presence of **Dr M. V. Ramana Murthy**, Director, **Deep Ocean Mission**; CMLRE head **Dr R. S. Maheshkumar**.
- Adjacent facts you should be able to attach: **Deep Ocean Mission** (2021, MoES; **Samudrayaan** with the **Matsya-6000** submersible), **PM Matsya Sampada Yojana** (Department of Fisheries), and the **Maritime Zones Act, 1976** as the domestic law behind the EEZ.

**Destination notebook.** GS3 — Environment & Ecology (marine ecosystems, Blue Economy); spillover into GS3 Economy (fisheries) and GS1 Geography (oceanography).

**Static anchor.** *Shankar IAS Environment*, topic on **Marine Ecosystems / Marine Biodiversity**; and the **Blue Economy** page in your Economy notes.
> **Update nudge:** On the marine-ecosystems page add a small depth-zone ladder — *epipelagic 0–200 m · mesopelagic 200–1,000 m · bathypelagic 1,000–4,000 m* — and pin CMLRE, FORV Sagar Sampada and Deep Ocean Mission beside it, so the institution and the depth band are learned together.

### 4. Former ISRO chief S. Somanath joins the RBI's Central Board — Economy

**Headline.** The Government of India has appointed **Somanath Sreedhara Panicker (S. Somanath)**, former Chairman of ISRO, as a **part-time non-official Director on the Central Board of the Reserve Bank of India**, and **re-appointed Anand Gopal Mahindra**, both for **four years with effect from 20 August 2026**.

**Exam angle.**
- The appointment itself is minor; the **statutory architecture it hangs on is high-yield** and UPSC has repeatedly asked about who sits on the RBI's Board and who appoints them.
- Useful Mains line on central-bank governance: bringing a space technologist onto the Board reflects the RBI's shift from a purely monetary institution to a regulator of payments, cyber resilience and digital public infrastructure.

**Key facts to memorise.**
- **Section 8, RBI Act, 1934** — the **Central Board of Directors** has a maximum of **21 members**: the **Governor** + up to **4 Deputy Governors** (appointed by the Central Government); **4 Directors nominated by the Central Government, one from each of the four Local Boards**; **10 Directors nominated by the Central Government** (the "part-time non-official" category to which Somanath and Mahindra belong); and **2 Government officials** nominated by the Centre.
- Term of the nominated non-official directors: **four years**; the Governor and Deputy Governors hold office for up to five years.
- **Section 9** — **four Local Boards** at **Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai and New Delhi** (Western, Eastern, Southern and Northern areas), five members each, four-year terms.
- Also worth carrying: **Section 7** — the Central Government may, after consultation with the Governor, give directions to the RBI in the public interest (never yet formally invoked).
- Somanath was ISRO's **10th Chairman** and Secretary, Department of Space, **15 January 2022 – 14 January 2025**; **Chandrayaan-3** soft-landed near the lunar south pole on **23 August 2023** during his tenure. *(Related: 23 August is observed as **National Space Day**.)*

**Destination notebook.** GS3 — Economy (Indian financial system; RBI's structure and functions).

**Static anchor.** *Mrunal* **Pillar 1 — Banking / Money & Banking**, sub-topic "RBI: organisation and structure"; or the RBI chapter of your Ramesh Singh notes.

### 5. A Bengaluru start-up unveils 'FWD Supreme' — India's first AI-piloted combat aircraft programme — Science & Technology

**Headline.** **Flying Wedge Defence & Aerospace Technologies (FWDA)**, Bengaluru, has unveiled **FWD Supreme**, billed as India's first **AI-piloted fighter aircraft programme**, with a roughly **250 kg technology demonstrator, 'FWD Supreme Lite'**, now in airframe development, system integration and ground testing.

**Exam angle.**
- The relevant *category* is what Prelims will test: this is a **Collaborative Combat Aircraft (CCA) / "loyal wingman"** — an autonomous platform that fights alongside crewed fighters, distinct from a remotely piloted **MALE/HALE drone** like the MQ-9B (which you noted on 19 August) and from a conventional UCAV.
- The declared **"Mobbing Doctrine"** — deploying a large number of cheap AI-piloted aircraft to create **numerical, tactical and economic asymmetry** — is a quotable Mains line on cost-imposition strategy and attritable airpower.
- Ethics/GS4 and internal-security overlap: lethal autonomy and the meaningful-human-control debate under the **CCW** framework.

**Key facts to memorise.**
- **FWD Supreme Lite**: all-up weight **~250 kg**, the initial technology demonstrator; **first flight targeted for Q3 2026**.
- Company-stated performance goals *(treat as manufacturer claims, not validated)*: endurance up to **30 hours**, range up to **3,000 km**; **Phase-I** max **Mach 0.9**, cruise ~**Mach 0.5**, operational range **700–1,000 km**; **Phase-II** supersonic (up to **Mach 2**) with **multi-aircraft autonomous teaming**.
- Where it sits in the Indian stack: **DRDO/HAL CATS Warrior** (loyal wingman), **Ghatak** UCAV, and the **AMCA** (see 21 August note on the Reliance–Rolls-Royce engine intent) — FWD Supreme is a **private, startup-led** entrant, not a DRDO programme.
- Global peer set cited: the US, Germany and Türkiye among countries actively fielding AI-piloted combat aircraft programmes.

**Destination notebook.** GS3 — Science & Technology (indigenisation of technology; developing new technology) with a GS3 Internal Security cross-tag.

**Static anchor.** Your **Defence Technology / Indigenisation** page in Sci-Tech notes.
> **Update nudge:** Add a one-line taxonomy row at the top of that page — *RPAS (human-in-the-loop) → UCAV → CCA / loyal wingman (human-on-the-loop) → fully autonomous* — and slot MQ-9B, Ghatak, CATS Warrior and FWD Supreme into it. This single row will answer most drone MCQs.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Polity & Governance, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **"Prescribing a minimum period of practice at the Bar for entry into the subordinate judiciary is a remedy whose costs fall unevenly. Critically examine."**

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points):**

1. **Frame the problem, not the rule.** Open with the diagnosed defect — fresh law graduates entering the Bench with no courtroom exposure, contributing to poor-quality orders, high appellate reversal and low judicial productivity in the district judiciary, which handles the overwhelming bulk of India's pendency. Note that the requirement's history is itself unsettled: removed in 2002 on Shetty Commission advice, restored in 2025, recalibrated in August 2026.
2. **The case for the requirement.** Practical familiarity with procedure, evidence and client realities; maturity in a post that exercises liberty-affecting powers from day one; alignment with the Bar-Bench continuum; comparative practice in several common-law jurisdictions.
3. **The uneven costs — the "critically" half.** A practice bar functions as an economic filter: junior-Bar incomes are low and unevenly distributed, so the burden falls hardest on first-generation lawyers, women, and candidates from rural and marginalised backgrounds — precisely the diversity the district judiciary lacks. Add the verification problem (practice certificates are weakly audited) and the opportunity cost of leaving posts vacant amid a large sanctioned-strength shortfall.
4. **Evaluate the 2026 hybrid and conclude.** The 1 + 1 + 1 design (practice + academy + clerkship) substitutes *structured, supervised* exposure for *unstructured* Bar time, lowering the entry cost while preserving competence — but its success turns on judicial-academy capacity, funded clerkship stipends and uniform State adoption. Conclude that competence should be built by the system rather than priced into the candidate, and link to All India Judges Association's larger project of an All India Judicial Service.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (a).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: practice was cut to one year, supplemented by one year of judicial-academy training and one year of clerkship. Statement 3 is wrong — the decision was **2:1**, with Justice K. Vinod Chandran dissenting.

**Q2 — (d).** All three are correct. The MoA on Maritime Security Cooperation was signed on 20 August 2026 between the two Defence Ministries; it covers MDA information sharing, SAR and HADR between the JMSDF and the Indian Navy; and it came during Koizumi's maiden India visit (19–20 August 2026).

**Q3 — (b).** CMLRE is an attached office of the **Ministry of Earth Sciences**, not of the fisheries or environment ministries — a classic institution-mapping trap, since the subject matter is fisheries.

**Q4 — (d).** All three are correct. Under **Section 8 of the RBI Act, 1934**, the ten part-time non-official Directors are nominated by the Central Government for a four-year term, and the Central Board's maximum strength is 21 (Governor + 4 Deputy Governors + 4 Local Board Directors + 10 nominated Directors + 2 Government officials).

**Q5 — (b).** FWD Supreme Lite is the ~250 kg technology demonstrator of Flying Wedge Defence & Aerospace's privately developed AI-piloted combat aircraft programme. Option (a) describes the MQ-9B Sea Guardian, and (c) describes the AMCA engine effort — both distinct items from earlier this month.

**Q6 — (a).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct — **Article 233** requires the Governor to appoint district judges in consultation with the High Court, and a person not already in Union or State service needs **seven years** as an advocate or pleader. Statement 3 is wrong: under **Article 235**, control over the district courts and subordinate courts vests in the **High Court**, not the State Public Service Commission.

**Q7 — (b).** Statements 1, 2 and 4 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: in the EEZ a coastal State has **sovereign rights** for exploring, exploiting, conserving and managing resources — not full territorial sovereignty; other States retain freedoms of navigation and overflight there.
