### 1. SC: "Right to Walk" on safe footpaths is a Fundamental Right
**Headline:** A two-judge Supreme Court bench (Justices P.S. Narasimha & Atul S. Chandurkar) held on **19 June 2026** that the right to walk safely on demarcated footpaths is a fundamental right, with pedestrians taking priority over motor vehicles.

**Exam angle:** Classic example of judicial *expansion* of Part III by reading Articles together — the court anchored "right to walk" in **Art 19(1)(d)** (freedom of movement) and **Art 21** (life, liberty, dignity), supported by 19(1)(a)/(b)/(c). It imposes a positive duty on the State and names the duty-bearers, and calls for a dedicated pedestrian law + regulator. Excellent Prelims fact-set and a Mains "rights-as-State-obligations" hook.

**Memorize:**
- Bench: **Narasimha & Chandurkar**; date **19 June 2026**; trigger case — death of a 5-year-old hit by a tanker on a road with no footpath.
- Anchored in **Art 19(1)(d) + Art 21**; pedestrians' right is **primary over motor vehicles**.
- Duty-bearers named: **urban development authorities, municipal corporations, municipalities, panchayats**; remedies independent of Motor Vehicles Act, 1988.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Fundamental Rights / judicial activism).
**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth* → "Fundamental Rights" (Art 19 & Art 21). Cross-link nudge: add this beside **Olga Tellis v. BMC (1985)** (pavement dwellers, right to livelihood under Art 21) and **Maneka Gandhi (1978)** to build a one-page "expanding scope of Art 21" timeline.

### 3. Western Ghats ESA: Centre moves to phased, state-wise notification
**What's NEW (don't re-explain the whole saga):** The Centre is finalising the **6th draft ESA notification (issued July 2024, valid till 27 July 2026)** through a **phased, state-by-state** route — notifying states that have reached consensus (Gujarat, Goa, Maharashtra largely on board) rather than waiting for all six states.

**Exam angle:** Perennial Gadgil-vs-Kasturirangan Prelims/Mains theme refreshed by a governance twist (cooperative federalism vs ecology-vs-development). Map + committees + figures are all testable.

**Memorize:**

- Current 6th draft proposes **~56,825.7 sq km**; bans new **mining/quarrying, thermal power, red-category industries**, and large (>**20,000 sq m**) construction.

### Mains-style Question

**Q (GS2/GS1, ~150 words).** "Recognising the *right to walk* as a fundamental right converts a civic amenity into a State obligation." **Examine** this statement in the light of the expanding scope of Article 21 and the challenges of implementing pedestrian-friendly urban infrastructure in India.

*Model-answer skeleton:*
- **Intro:** Frame the June 2026 SC verdict (Art 19(1)(d) + Art 21); note shift from amenity → enforceable right with named duty-bearers.
- **Why significant:** Continuity of Art 21 jurisprudence (Maneka Gandhi → Olga Tellis → Puttaswamy → clean environment); positive State duty; remedies independent of the MV Act.
- **Implementation challenges:** Fragmented urban governance (74th CAA, weak ULBs), encroachment/parking on footpaths, fund/capacity gaps, vehicle-centric planning, absence of a pedestrian law.
- **Way forward:** Dedicated pedestrian-safety statute + regulator (as the Court urged), non-motorised transport (NMT) in city mobility plans, accountability of municipal bodies, "complete streets" design.

## PART C — ANSWERS

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The remedy is *independent* of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, so statement 3 is wrong.
**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Alang is in **Gujarat**, not Tamil Nadu; share 35.4% and the HKC alignment are correct.
**Q3 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** Kasturirangan recommended only ~37% (~60,000 sq km) as ESA; it was the **Gadgil** panel that proposed the entire range — so statement 2 is wrong.
**Q4 — (c) All three.** IN-SPACe (single window), NSIL (commercial PSU arm of DoS), and the Indian Space Policy 2023 are all correctly matched.
**Q5 — (b)** Ras Laffan is the world's largest LNG export complex, in **Qatar**.
**Q6 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three have been read into Article 21 — Olga Tellis (livelihood), K.S. Puttaswamy (privacy), and the right to a clean/healthy environment.
**Q7 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All correct — Rakhigarhi (largest Indian Harappan site, Ghaggar-Hakra, Haryana), Dholavira (water management), and the Bronze/Chalcolithic dating.
