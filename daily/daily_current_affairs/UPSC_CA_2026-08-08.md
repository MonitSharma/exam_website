### 1. Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Türkiye sign the Mecca joint defence pact — International Relations

**Headline:** On **7 August 2026**, at a summit in **Mecca**, Saudi Crown Prince and PM **Mohammed bin Salman**, Turkish President **Recep Tayyip Erdoğan** and Pakistan PM **Shehbaz Sharif** signed a trilateral collective-defence agreement — reported variously as the **Mecca Joint Deterrence Agreement / Mecca Joint Defence Agreement** — under which **an armed attack on any one of the three is to be treated as an attack on all three**. India's MEA said it is "closely following" the development.

**Exam angle:** This is the single most consequential IR item of the week for India, and the exam angle is *not* the pact's text but its structural consequence. It formalises a **Riyadh–Islamabad–Ankara axis** that gives Pakistan strategic depth on two fronts India cares about: Saudi financial and diplomatic weight, and Turkish defence-industrial capacity (drones, naval platforms) that Pakistan already uses. It also complicates India's carefully built **"Link West" / de-hyphenated Gulf policy**, in which Saudi Arabia and the UAE were partners insulated from the Pakistan file. Note the analysts' caveat that is examiner-bait: this is a **framework for strategic, military and defence-industrial coordination, not a NATO-style integrated command** — Article 5 of the North Atlantic Treaty comes with a standing military structure; this does not. Balance it against India's counterweights: the **I2U2** grouping, **IMEC**, the **India–UAE CEPA**, and the fact that the Gulf hosts ~9 million Indians and supplies the bulk of India's crude and LPG.

**Memorise:**

- Signed **7 August 2026, Mecca**; parties **Saudi Arabia, Pakistan, Türkiye**; core clause — **attack on one = attack on all**; described as **defensive, not directed at a third country, and open to other regional states**.
- It **builds on the bilateral Saudi–Pakistan Strategic Mutual Defence Agreement of September 2025**, extending a comparable commitment to Türkiye.
- Context: the **US–Israel war on Iran** that began February 2026, Iranian and Houthi missile/drone strikes on Gulf states, and the **Strait of Hormuz** disruption.
- Complementary strengths cited: Türkiye has **NATO's second-largest military**; Saudi Arabia is the **world's largest crude oil exporter**; Pakistan is the **only Muslim-majority state with nuclear weapons**.
- India's stake: West Asia supplies roughly **two-thirds of India's crude imports**; the **Gulf Cooperation Council** is among India's largest trading blocs; **~9 million** Indians live in the Gulf.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India and its neighbourhood; groupings and agreements involving India / affecting India's interests).

**Static anchor:** Your **India–West Asia** page (Mrunal/Vision IR notes) and the **collective security** section of your IR theory notes. Add a three-column comparison — **NATO Article 5 vs Saudi–Pak 2025 bilateral vs Mecca 2026 trilateral** — on treaty text, command structure and automaticity. Cross-link to your **Pakistan** page (external balancing) and your **energy security** page.

### 2. Astrobase unveils 'Everest' — India's first full-flow staged-combustion rocket engine, from the private sector — Science & Technology

**Headline:** Bengaluru-based **Astrobase Space Technologies** unveiled **'Everest'** on **7 August 2026** — India's first fully integrated **full-flow staged combustion (FFSC)** rocket engine, rated at **800 kilonewtons (~80 tonne-force) of vacuum thrust** and burning **methalox** (liquid oxygen + liquid methane).

**Exam angle:** Two separate syllabus hooks live here. First, **propulsion physics** — a full-flow staged combustion cycle sends *both* propellants through preburners so that the entire propellant mass passes through the main chamber, giving very high efficiency and, critically, low turbine temperatures and no soot, which is what makes an engine **reusable**. Only a handful of engines worldwide have flown this cycle (SpaceX's Raptor is the obvious comparator). Second, **space-sector reform** — this is a private Indian firm building high-thrust propulsion and operating India's **first privately owned high-thrust test stand**, which is the direct downstream effect of the **Indian Space Policy 2023**, **IN-SPACe** and **NSIL**, and of 100% FDI liberalisation in space. Methalox also matters for **in-situ resource utilisation**: methane can in principle be synthesised on Mars, which is why it is the propellant of choice for deep-space reusables.

**Memorise:**

- **'Everest'** — India's first fully integrated **FFSC** engine; **800 kN (~80 tf) vacuum thrust**; **LOX–methane (methalox)**; **throttleable between 50% and 110%** — the enabling feature for propulsive landing and reuse.
- Development milestones: **sub-scale FFSC hot-fire, September 2025**; **high-speed turbopump trials, January 2026**.
- Test infrastructure: a **21.5-acre propulsion test facility near Anantapur, Andhra Pradesh** — India's **first privately owned high-thrust test stand**, rated to about **200 tf (2,000 kN)**.
- Roadmap: **cluster of seven engines** on a partially reusable launcher, targeting orbit by **2029**, with an aspiration of **~USD 300/kg** launch cost in the 2030s.
- For contrast, ISRO's engine family: **Vikas** (liquid, UDMH/N₂O₄), **CE-20** (cryogenic upper stage, LOX–LH₂, ~200 kN class) and the in-development **SCE-200** (semi-cryogenic, LOX–kerosene).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Space).

**Static anchor:** The **Space Technology** chapter of your Sci-Tech notes. Add a half-page box on **rocket engine cycles** — gas generator vs staged combustion vs full-flow staged combustion vs expander — and a propellant table (solid / hypergolic / kerolox / methalox / cryogenic LH₂). Cross-link to **Indian Space Policy 2023, IN-SPACe, NSIL and the Space Activities regulatory framework** — the examiner is far likelier to test the *institutional* reform than the thermodynamics.

### 3. India releases captive-bred slender-billed vultures into the wild for the first time — Environment

**Headline:** On **8 August 2026**, Assam's Environment and Forest Minister released **10 captive-bred vultures — five slender-billed (*Gyps tenuirostris*) and five white-rumped (*Gyps bengalensis*)** — from a purpose-built release aviary near **Tewaripal**, in the **6th Addition to Kaziranga National Park and Tiger Reserve, Biswanath Wildlife Division**. This is the **first-ever release of captive-bred slender-billed vultures anywhere in India**.

**Exam angle:** This is the payoff of a twenty-year conservation arc that UPSC has repeatedly circled: the **diclofenac crash** of the 1990s–2000s wiped out over 97% of India's *Gyps* populations, the veterinary use of diclofenac was banned in **2006**, **meloxicam** was validated as the vulture-safe substitute, and conservation breeding centres were built to hold a founder population until the landscape was safe again. The release is the *reintroduction* stage. Two examinable nuances: the birds carry **satellite tags** so survival and dispersal can be measured (adaptive management), and releases are targeted at **Vulture Safe Zones** — landscapes where the toxic-NSAID load in cattle carcasses has been surveyed and found acceptable. The ecological services argument is the Mains line: vultures are obligate scavengers whose collapse raised feral dog populations and rabies risk, and disrupted carcass disposal.

**Memorise:**

- Released **8 August 2026**: **10 birds — 5 slender-billed + 5 white-rumped**; bred at the **Vulture Conservation and Breeding Centre, Rani (Guwahati, Kamrup)**, run jointly by the **Assam Forest Department and the Bombay Natural History Society (BNHS)**.
- The **Central Zoo Authority** has approved a wider release of **35 birds (30 white-rumped + 5 slender-billed)** into the 6th Addition to Kaziranga.
- All nine Indian vulture species are protected; **white-rumped, slender-billed, long-billed (*Gyps indicus*) and red-headed vultures are IUCN Critically Endangered** and listed in **Schedule I** of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972.
- **Diclofenac banned for veterinary use in India in 2006**; safe alternative **meloxicam**; between 1992 and 2007 white-rumped vultures declined **~99%**, slender-billed and long-billed **~97%**.
- Institutional frame: **Vulture Action Plan 2020–2025**, the **SAVE** (Saving Asia's Vultures from Extinction) consortium, **Vulture Safe Zones**, and the **Jatayu Conservation Breeding Centre, Pinjore (Haryana)** — the flagship centre.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Ecology (Conservation, species recovery).

**Static anchor:** **Shankar IAS, "Conservation Efforts" / "Animal Diversity of India"**. Add a one-page vulture card: nine Indian species, IUCN status, the diclofenac mechanism (renal failure/visceral gout), the 2006 ban, breeding centres, Vulture Safe Zones. Cross-link to **Kaziranga** on your protected-areas map and to your **ecosystem services** page — vultures are the standard example of a scavenging service with quantifiable public-health value.

### 4. India's revised Model Bilateral Investment Treaty heads to the Cabinet — Economy

**Headline:** On **7 August 2026**, Department of Economic Affairs Secretary **Anuradha Thakur** said the review of India's **Model Bilateral Investment Treaty** is nearing completion and the revised template will go to the **Union Cabinet** shortly. Two signals stand out: the mandatory domestic-litigation period before arbitration may be **cut from five years to about two**, and — for the first time — the model will be written with **outward Indian investment (ODI)** in mind, not just inbound FDI.

**Exam angle:** The **Model BIT 2016** was drafted in the shadow of the **White Industries** award and the **Vodafone** and **Cairn** retrospective-tax arbitrations, so it was deliberately sovereignty-protective: an **enterprise-based definition of investment**, **no Most-Favoured-Nation clause**, **no fair-and-equitable-treatment clause** (replaced by a narrower customary-international-law standard), taxation carved out, and a **five-year exhaustion of local remedies** before Investor-State Dispute Settlement. The result was that most partners refused to sign it — India terminated some 70-odd older BITs and concluded very few replacements. The pivot now is the classic **policy-space versus capital-attraction trade-off**, and the new twist is that **India is now a significant capital exporter**, so treaty protection cuts both ways. That reframing — "India as home state, not only host state" — is the quotable Mains line.

**Memorise:**

- **Model BIT 2016** features: **enterprise-based** definition of investment; **MFN clause omitted**; **no FET clause** (a narrower customary-international-law minimum standard instead); **taxation measures excluded**; **5-year exhaustion of local remedies** before ISDS.
- Proposed direction of change: **local-remedies period possibly reduced to ~2 years**; dispute-settlement provisions reworked; **express protection for Indian outward investors**.
- India is **not a party to the ICSID Convention**; Indian BIT arbitrations therefore run under **UNCITRAL rules**, with enforcement via the **New York Convention, 1958** (acceded 1960) as implemented in **Part II of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996**.
- Trigger cases: **White Industries v India (2011)** — the first BIT award against India, won on an MFN-imported "effective means" clause; **Vodafone (2020)** and **Cairn Energy (2020)** — retrospective-tax awards; the retrospective tax was repealed by the **Taxation Laws (Amendment) Act, 2021**.
- Recent practice: **India–UAE BIT (2024)** and **India–Uzbekistan BIT (2024)** are the model's first significant takers; the **India–Israel Bilateral Investment Agreement** was signed in 2025.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Investment, external sector) with a GS2 spillover (India and international agreements).

**Static anchor:** **Mrunal Pillar 3 (External Sector / BoP & FDI)** and your **International Economic Institutions** page. Add a compact BIT box: 2016 model's five defining features, why partners balked, the ISDS debate, and the ICSID-non-membership point. Cross-link to your **FDI policy** page and to **UNCTAD's World Investment Report**, which is where the examiner usually sources FDI data.

### 5. Places in News: India standardises 27 place names in Arunachal Pradesh on the official map — Geography

**Headline:** On **7 August 2026**, the **Ministry of Home Affairs**, in consultation with the Arunachal Pradesh government, formally identified **27 places and features in Arunachal Pradesh by their standard names on the official Survey of India map** — a direct, cartographic response to China's repeated "standardisation" of Chinese and Tibetan names for locations in the state.

**Exam angle:** Toponymy as statecraft. China refers to Arunachal Pradesh as **"Zangnan" (South Tibet)** and has issued successive batches of renamed locations — **6 in April 2017, 15 in December 2021, 11 in April 2023, 30 in March 2024 and 27 in May 2025**, roughly 89 in all. India's answer is not merely a protest note but an **official map entry**, which matters because maps are evidence in territorial argument and because the **Survey of India** is the only authority whose depiction of India's external boundary is legally authentic. For Prelims, the value is the **place-in-news list**: several of the 27 are strategically loaded — **Long Ju**, **Thag La** and **Dzo La** are 1962-war and LAC-friction names, and **Jaswant Garh** houses the memorial to Rifleman **Jaswant Singh Rawat** of the Battle of Nuranang. Others include the high-altitude lake **Sambho Sarovar**, **Bara Kundun** and **Chhota Kundun**, **Kamlang Nagar** and **Sunpura**.

**Memorise:**

- **27 places and features**, notified **7 August 2026** by the **MHA** with the **Arunachal Pradesh** government, entered on the **Survey of India** official map; stated purpose — accurate recognition and public awareness.
- China's renaming batches: **2017 (6), 2021 (15), 2023 (11), 2024 (30), 2025 (27)** — about **89** cumulatively; India's standing position is that such renaming is "vain and preposterous" and does not alter the fact that Arunachal Pradesh **is and always will be an integral part of India**.
- Strategic names on the list: **Long Ju** (1962 flashpoint), **Thag La** ridge (the 1962 trigger area, north-west of Tawang), **Dzo La**, **Riza La**, **Jaswant Garh** (Battle of Nuranang, 1962), **Sambho Sarovar**.
- Static geography of Arunachal: passes **Bum La, Se La, Diphu La, Kibithu/Walong sector**; rivers **Siang (Brahmaputra), Subansiri, Lohit, Dibang, Kameng**; the **McMahon Line** (Simla Convention, 1914) is the boundary India recognises and China rejects.
- The **Survey of India (est. 1767)**, under the **Department of Science and Technology**, is the national mapping agency and the sole authority for the authentic depiction of India's external boundaries.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Geography (Places in News) with a GS2 spillover (India–China border question).

**Static anchor:** Your **India–China border** page and the **Northeast India** map in your atlas. Physically mark Tawang, Thag La, Long Ju, Kibithu/Walong and Sela on the Arunachal outline, and cross-link to the **McMahon Line**, the **1962 war**, the **Sela Tunnel**, and the **Border Area Development Programme / Vibrant Villages Programme**. Keep a running list of Chinese renaming batches with dates — it is a two-line answer that is easy to lose marks on.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — International Relations, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **"The consolidation of a Riyadh–Islamabad–Ankara security arrangement tests the assumption on which India built its de-hyphenated Gulf policy." Examine the implications of the 2026 Mecca joint defence agreement for India, and suggest the elements of an appropriate Indian response.**

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the shift in one paragraph.** India's West Asia policy since 2014 rested on de-hyphenation — treating Gulf partnerships (energy, remittances, investment, counter-terror intelligence) as insulated from Pakistan. A treaty-level, attack-on-one-is-attack-on-all commitment linking Riyadh, Islamabad and Ankara reintroduces the Pakistan variable into the Gulf equation. Anchor with data: ~two-thirds of crude imports from West Asia, ~9 million Indians in the Gulf, GCC among India's top trading blocs.

2. **Set out the implications, but calibrate.** *Risks:* Turkish defence-industrial transfer to Pakistan; possible Saudi reticence in multilateral fora on Pakistan-linked terror listings; a Sunni security bloc that squeezes India's room on Iran (Chabahar, INSTC) while it courts the Gulf. *Limits of the risk:* the pact is a coordination framework, not an integrated command; it is declared defensive and open-ended; Saudi and UAE economic stakes in India (CEPA, IMEC, sovereign investment) are large and rising; the agreement is directed at the Iran–Israel theatre, not South Asia.

3. **Suggest a layered response.** Deepen bilateral defence and intelligence ties with Saudi Arabia and the UAE so that India remains a stakeholder, not a bystander; accelerate **IMEC** and **I2U2** as economic anchors; sustain the **Iran** channel (Chabahar, INSTC) as hedging without antagonising the Gulf; expand defence-industrial self-reliance and counter-drone capability; use the diaspora and energy interdependence as structural ballast; keep the Türkiye file separate and pragmatic while strengthening ties with Greece, Cyprus and Armenia.

4. **Conclude on the principle.** India's advantage in the Gulf is economic indispensability rather than alliance membership; the response should be to raise the cost of ignoring India, not to seek a counter-bloc — consistent with strategic autonomy and multi-alignment.

### D. PYQ Mirror

**Theme: India and West Asia (Item 1).** UPSC has tested this as an energy-security question rather than a security-alliance one.

> *"The question of India's Energy Security constitutes the most important part of India's economic progress. Analyze India's energy policy cooperation with West Asian countries."* — **UPSC Mains 2017, GS Paper 2 (15 marks).** *(Verified.)*

The lesson: the examiner's default entry point into West Asia is **dependence** — crude, LPG, remittances, diaspora — not bloc politics. Write the Mecca pact into your West Asia notes as a *risk to the energy-and-diaspora relationship*, and the 2017 framing will carry most of the answer.

**Theme: India's space programme and the private sector (Item 2).**

> *"India has achieved remarkable successes in unmanned space missions including Chandrayaan and Mars Orbiter Mission, but has not ventured into manned space missions. What are the main obstacles to launching a manned space mission, both in terms of technology and logistics?"* — **UPSC Mains 2017, GS Paper 3 (10 marks).** *(Verified.)*

The lesson: UPSC asks about **capability gaps and enablers**, not hardware specifications. Astrobase's FFSC engine belongs in your answer as evidence that the **reusability and heavy-lift gap** is now being addressed by private industry under the Indian Space Policy 2023 — that is the sentence worth a mark, not the thrust figure.
