### 5. Reform of UAPA, 1967: long pre-trial detention and the bail bar under Section 43D(5) — Internal Security / Polity
**Exam angle:** An analytical Mains theme: how far a strict bail threshold can be reconciled with Article 21 and speedy trial. Pairs the statute with landmark rulings and a quotable constitutional-morality line.
**Key facts:** UAPA bars bail where the court, on the case diary, finds the accusation prima facie true (s.43D(5)). *NIA v. Zahoor Ahmad Shah Watali* (2019) read this strictly; *Union of India v. K.A. Najeeb* (2021) held constitutional courts can still grant bail where trial is unduly delayed. 2019 amendment allowed designation of individuals as terrorists. Commentary this week cites NCRB data of conviction rates below ~3% (secondary source; verify before quoting).
**Notebook:** GS3 Internal Security / GS2 Polity (Judiciary and rights).

### C. Mains question
**Critically analyse** whether the bail regime under the Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967 strikes a fair balance between national security and the right to life and personal liberty under Article 21. (15 marks, 250 words)

*Model-answer skeleton:*
1. Intro: UAPA's purpose (terror/unlawful associations) and s.43D(5) bail bar; 2019 amendment widened its reach.
2. Case for the regime: gravity of offences, evidence-gathering challenges, the *Watali* approach of limited scrutiny at bail stage.
3. Case against: prolonged pre-trial incarceration, low conviction rates (NCRB), process as punishment; *Najeeb* (2021) — constitutional courts retain Art. 21 power after long delay.
4. Way forward: fast-track/special courts, time-bound trials and charge-sheets, periodic review, narrower definitions, safeguards against misuse; conclude with proportionality and rule of law.

## Answers
1. **(c)** — CEPA conclusion is targeted by end-2026 and Maninder Sidhu leads a ~300-delegate mission. Two-way trade was about USD 30.4 billion in 2025 (target USD 70 billion by 2030), so 3 is wrong.
2. **(b)** — El Niño = warm central/eastern Pacific; a positive IOD (as in 1997–98) can offset its drying effect. El Niño weakens, not strengthens, the Walker circulation and trade winds, so 3 is wrong. WMO (8 Oct 2026) forecasts the Oct–Dec Niño 3.4 anomaly near +3.7°C versus the previous record of +2.6°C.
3. **(a)** — COP17 is on 19–30 Oct 2026 in Yerevan with the first global review of the KMGBF. The Cartagena Protocol concerns biosafety (living modified organisms); access and benefit-sharing is the Nagoya Protocol (NP-MOP 6 also meets alongside).
4. **(a)** — Launched 13 Oct 2021; the NPG appraises projects above ₹500 crore (396 projects worth ₹18.66 lakh crore evaluated by Aug 2026). The National Master Plan links 58 central ministries/departments and all 36 States/UTs, so 3 is wrong.
5. **(b)** — Statements 1 and 2 reflect s.43D(5) and Najeeb (2021). The UAPA is a punitive, not preventive-detention, statute — preventive detention laws are those under Article 22(3)(b) such as NSA — so 3 is wrong.
6. **(c)** — The NBA (Chennai) is statutory and SBBs are set up by State Governments. BMCs are constituted by local bodies at the local level (to prepare People's Biodiversity Registers), not by SBBs.
7. **(d)** — Art. 22(4): no detention beyond three months without an Advisory Board's report (unless Parliament provides otherwise under 22(7)). Rights in 22(1)–(2) (lawyer, 24-hour production) do not apply to preventive detention, and both Parliament and States legislate on it (Entry 9 List I, Entry 3 List III).
