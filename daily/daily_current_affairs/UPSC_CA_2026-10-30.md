## Part A — Briefing

### 1. SC (29 Oct) proposes strict liability on banks for "digital arrest" and cyber-fraud losses — Internal Security / Polity
- **Exam angle:** In the suo motu case on digital arrests, the bench moved from asking RBI for an SOP (4 Aug directions) to proposing that banks compensate victims where the fraud followed from the bank's failure of due diligence. Mains angle: shifting the liability from the victim to the regulated intermediary, and the role of courts in filling a regulatory gap (judicial activism vs. executive domain).
- **Key facts:** Bench of CJI Surya Kant, Justice Joymalya Bagchi and Justice V. Mohana; case *In Re: Victims of Digital Arrest Related to Forged Documents*, SMW (Crl.) 3/2025. RBI's proposed rules should require compensation at least equal to the amount lost where due diligence failed. The Attorney General said RBI measures are likely operational by December. The bench floated risk-grading of accounts, holding unusual transactions and verifying with the customer, and bringing intermediaries such as WhatsApp into scope. Background from 4 Aug order: RBI SOP on temporary debit holds, State Cyber Crime Coordination Centres, e-Zero FIR, and an inter-departmental committee on shared liability. *Verification: the strict-liability points are from court-hearing reports; the written order's final wording may differ.*
- **Notebook:** GS3 Internal Security (cyber crime); GS2 Polity (judiciary).
- **Static anchor:** Laxmikanth, "Supreme Court" chapter (suo motu, Art. 32/142) and Mrunal/Vision "Cyber Security" notes (I4C, IT Act s.66C/66D). Add a "digital arrest: SC timeline" box and cross-link to the RBI "limited liability of customers" circular.

### 2. 13th ADMM-Plus: Rajnath Singh in Cebu, Philippines (28–29 Oct); India offers counter-terrorism expertise — International Relations
- **Exam angle:** ADMM-Plus is ASEAN's main defence-dialogue platform with eight dialogue partners, and India's Act East policy runs through it. India's role in the Experts' Working Group on Counter-Terrorism links defence diplomacy to the Indo-Pacific security architecture.
- **Key facts:** Philippines chairs ASEAN in 2026. Rajnath addressed the session on regional and international security and said India is ready to share battle-tested counter-terrorism expertise. India co-chairs the ADMM-Plus EWG on Counter-Terrorism with Malaysia for 2024–27. ADMM-Plus first met in Hanoi on 12 Oct 2010 and has met annually since 2017. India became an ASEAN dialogue partner in 1992 (sectoral 1992, full 1996). *Dates and venue from one secondary source; confirm with MoD/PIB.*

### 3. India's cloud ecosystem: MeghRaj 2.0, data-centre capacity and the Cloud Selection Framework — Science & Technology
- **Exam angle:** Government cloud adoption underpins DPI, AI compute and data sovereignty. UPSC ties this to IndiaAI Mission, DPDP Act 2023 and data localisation; a Mains line is the trade-off between hyperscaler efficiency and sovereign control.

### 4. Hanging glaciers of the Alaknanda basin: exposure study flags risk around Badrinath — Geography
- **Exam angle:** Hanging glaciers are ice perched on steep slopes that can detach as ice avalanches and trigger cascading hazards such as GLOFs. After the Ghepan Ghat GLOF early-warning story (18 Oct) this is the *source* side of the Himalayan hazard chain. Disaster-management angle: risk-informed land-use planning around Char Dham shrines.
- **Key facts:** Study by IIT Bhubaneswar and IISc's Divecha Centre for Climate Change covers 219 hanging glaciers in the Alaknanda basin (Garhwal Himalaya), ~71.7 sq km of glacier area. Projections: exposed land area ~120% higher in 2030 than in 2000; exposed population +17%. Press headline: 398 buildings, a helipad and two bridges in Badrinath fall in the hazard zone (headline only; I could not open the full text). Pilgrim season (May–Oct) coincides with peak slope instability.
- **Notebook:** GS1 Geography (Himalayan glaciology); GS3 Disaster Management.
- **Static anchor:** Spectrum / NCERT XI *Physical Geography* "Glaciers" and the Panch Prayag map (Vishnuprayag, Nandprayag, Karnaprayag, Rudraprayag, Devprayag). Add "hanging glacier vs. valley glacier" to the glacier-types table and cross-link to GLOF/NDMA guidelines.

### 5. Clean air: NCAP progress and the Swachh Vayu Sarvekshan scoring gap — Environment
- **Exam angle:** Lucknow topped the latest Swachh Vayu Sarvekshan among million-plus cities, yet its PM10 (~135 µg/m³) is more than double the annual standard, and actual PM reduction carries only 2.5% of the score. This is a clean case study on process-based versus outcome-based indicators.
- **Key facts:** NCAP launched 2019, covers 130 cities. In 2025–26, 108 cities showed lower PM10 than 2017–18 but only 14 met the annual PM10 standard; 70.5% of NCAP grants used by July 2026. NAAQS annual limits: PM2.5 40 µg/m³, PM10 60 µg/m³. Monitoring network of 1,646 stations in 603 cities (Mar 2026). SPCBs have a 47.59% vacancy rate (Aug 2025 data). *Figures from a PIB-sourced daily summary; single aggregator, flag for verification.*
- **Notebook:** GS3 Environment (pollution).
- **Static anchor:** Shankar IAS "Environmental Pollution" (Air Act 1981, NAAQS, NCAP, GRAP). Update the NCAP box with the 130-city / 14-city outcome and cross-link to the CAQM Act 2021.

### B3. Mains question

**Critically examine** whether fixing strict liability on banks is an adequate response to the rise of "digital arrest" and other cyber-enabled financial frauds in India. (15 marks, 250 words)

*Model-answer skeleton:*
1. **Intro:** the scale of "digital arrest" losses (SC: thousands of crores) and the 4 Aug and 29 Oct hearings; the shift from victim vigilance to institutional responsibility.
2. **For:** banks control the rails (KYC, mule-account detection, transaction monitoring), so liability aligns incentives and gives victims quick relief; builds on the RBI limited-liability circular.
3. **Limits:** fraud begins on telecom and messaging platforms, so banks alone cannot fix it; risk of friction and over-blocking for genuine users; courts legislating by order; risk of moral hazard.
4. **Way forward:** RBI risk-graded SOP, telecom and intermediary accountability (DoT, MeitY), State Cyber Crime Coordination Centres and e-Zero FIR, I4C coordination, public awareness, a shared-liability compensation framework.

## Part C — Answers

1. **(b)** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the bench proposed strict liability for due-diligence failures in a suo motu case. Statement 3 is wrong: the AG said RBI measures are likely operational by December, and nothing was notified.
2. **(c)** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: ADMM-Plus has met annually since 2017.
3. **(b)** MeghRaj is MeitY's cloud initiative, and IndiaAI's outlay is ₹10,371.92 crore. The Cloud Selection Framework prioritises NIC and State Data Centres, not foreign hyperscalers.
4. **(c)** Hanging glaciers detach as avalanches and can trigger secondary floods, so statement 2 is wrong. Alaknanda and Bhagirathi meet at Devprayag to form the Ganga.
5. **(d)** NCAP covers 130 cities and the PM10 annual standard is 60 µg/m³. NCAP targets are non-binding (it has no statutory backing).
6. **(c)** Section 66D punishes cheating by personation using a computer resource. 66B is dishonestly receiving a stolen computer resource, 66C is identity theft, 66F is cyber terrorism.
7. **(b)** Pairs 1 and 2 are correct. Nandprayag is the confluence of the Alaknanda and the Nandakini (Mandakini meets at Rudraprayag).

*Verification notes: Item 1 relies on hearing reports (LiveLaw and Business Standard headlines) and the 4 Aug order summary. Items 2, 3, 4 and 5 each rest on a single secondary source; the "398 buildings" Badrinath figure is from a newspaper headline only. PYQs are flagged as approximate.*
