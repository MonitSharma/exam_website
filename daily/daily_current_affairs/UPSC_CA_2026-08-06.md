## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Cabinet approves 'GOBARdhan', the National Circular Bioenergy Scheme — ₹23,731 crore — Environment

**Headline:** The Union Cabinet on 6 August 2026 cleared GOBARdhan, a unified National Circular Bioenergy Scheme with a ₹23,731 crore outlay, to scale Compressed Biogas (CBG) production roughly ten-fold by FY2035-36.

**Exam angle:** This is the first time India has bolted the full policy toolkit — administered price + assured offtake + capital subsidy + credit guarantee — onto biogas, the way ethanol was scaled after 2018. It converts GOBARdhan from a sanitation-side waste-management brand into an energy-security instrument, which is exactly the kind of re-framing UPSC likes to test. It also sits at the junction of GS3 Energy, GS3 Environment (waste management, methane abatement) and GS3 Agriculture (farm-residue monetisation, bio-slurry as organic manure).

**Facts / figures to memorise:**

- Outlay **₹23,731 crore**; scheme period **FY2026-27 to FY2035-36** (10 years).
- Administered CBG price of **₹2,110 per MMBTU**, guaranteed under a **minimum 10-year pricing framework**.
- Capital assistance up to **₹2 crore per TPD** of installed capacity for eligible greenfield CBG projects; dedicated credit-guarantee window for MSME projects.
- **CBG Blending Obligation (CBO)** trajectory in CNG (Transport) and PNG (Domestic): **3% in FY2026-27 → 4% in FY2027-28 → 5% from FY2028-29**. Obligated buyers are **City Gas Distribution (CGD)** entities.
- Context: the predecessor **SATAT** initiative (2018) targeted 5,000 CBG plants by 2023; only ~132 were commissioned by June 2026 — the offtake-and-price failure GOBARdhan is designed to fix.
- Chemistry to keep handy: raw biogas is ~55–60% methane, ~40–45% CO₂, traces of H₂S; CBG is biogas purified (CO₂/H₂S/moisture removed) and compressed, functionally interchangeable with CNG.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (waste-to-energy, circular economy) and GS3 — Economy (energy security).

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment* — "Renewable Energy / Waste Management" chapter. Add GOBARdhan alongside SATAT, National Bioenergy Programme and the Biomass Aggregation Machinery (BAM) scheme, and cross-link to the **Mrunal Economy pillar on Energy & Infrastructure** where you keep the ethanol-blending (E20) story — the two now share an identical policy architecture and make a ready-made comparative answer.

### 2. Parliament clears the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026 — strength rises to 38 — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** The Rajya Sabha passed the Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Bill, 2026 on 5 August 2026 (Lok Sabha had passed it on 3 August), raising the sanctioned strength of the Supreme Court from **34 to 38 judges including the Chief Justice of India**.

**Exam angle:** A textbook illustration that the Supreme Court's strength is *not* fixed by the Constitution — Article 124(1) leaves it to Parliament "by law". The Bill amends the **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act, 1956**, not the Constitution, which is precisely the distinction a Prelims statement-question would exploit. It also carries a live governance argument: pendency versus Constitution-Bench capacity.

**Facts / figures to memorise:**

- Sanctioned strength: **33 → 37 judges *other than* the CJI**, i.e. **34 → 38 including the CJI**.
- Amends the **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act, 1956**; replaces the **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Ordinance, 2026, promulgated on 16 May 2026**.
- Introduced in Lok Sabha **20 July 2026**; passed LS **3 August 2026**, RS **5 August 2026**.
- Trigger: a letter from **CJI Surya Kant to the Prime Minister dated 11 May 2026**, flagging rising litigation volume and the difficulty of constituting Constitution Benches while sustaining regular disposal.
- First increase in strength since **2019** (when it went from 31 to 34).
- Constitutional hook: **Article 124(1)** — SC to consist of a CJI and, until Parliament by law prescribes a larger number, not more than seven other judges. Original strength: **CJI + 7**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Judiciary).

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth*, Chapter on **"Supreme Court"** (composition, Articles 124–147). Update the strength table (8 → 11 → 14 → 18 → 26 → 31 → 34 → **38**) and add a one-line cross-link to the **"Judicial Reforms / Pendency"** chapter noting that strength increases are a legislative, not constitutional, act.

### 3. Supreme Court extends the domestic-cruelty offence to women in live-in relationships — Society

**Headline:** The Supreme Court (Justices Sanjay Karol and N. Kotiswar Singh; judgment by Justice Karol) held on 5 August 2026 that the offence of domestic cruelty under **Section 498A IPC — now Sections 85 and 86 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023** — extends to women in live-in relationships that are "in the nature of marriage".

**Exam angle:** The provision literally says "husband or his relatives", so this is purposive interpretation stretching a *penal* statute — which is why the judgment is contested as judicial overreach as much as it is welcomed as substantive equality. For Mains, it is the cleanest available example of the judiciary reading changing social institutions into old criminal law, and it converts a familiar civil-law point (live-in partners are already covered by the PWDVA, 2005) into a criminal-law one.

**Facts / figures to memorise:**

- **Section 498A IPC ≡ Sections 85 & 86, BNS 2023** (85 = the offence; 86 = the definition of "cruelty"). Learn this mapping — BNS section-number swaps are prime Prelims material.
- Constitutional basis invoked: **Articles 14, 15(3) and 21**.
- Precedent chain: **D. Velusamy v. D. Patchaiammal (2010)** — criteria for a relationship "in the nature of marriage"; **Indra Sarma v. V.K.V. Sarma (2013)** — distinguishing marriage-like relationships from casual cohabitation; **Lalita Toppo v. State of Jharkhand (2018)** — live-in partners can seek relief under the **PWDVA, 2005**; **Navtej Singh Johar (2018)** — dignity, autonomy, privacy.
- Counter-argument to carry into any answer: **no statutory definition** of "relationship in the nature of marriage" exists in penal law, creating uncertainty; expanding criminal liability beyond statutory text is arguably Parliament's domain.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Society (family, changing social institutions), with a cross-entry in GS2 — Polity (judicial review, judicial activism).

**Static anchor:** *NCERT Sociology Class XII* — "Social Institutions: Family and Marriage" — and the *Laxmikanth* chapter on **"Judicial Activism"**. Add a cross-link to your **PWDVA 2005** notes marking the new civil-remedy vs criminal-liability distinction for live-in partners.

### 4. DST's Research & Development Statistics 2025-26: GERD crosses 0.8% of GDP for the first time since 2009-10 — Science & Technology

**Headline:** Department of Science and Technology data released on 5 August 2026 shows India's Gross Expenditure on R&D (GERD) rose to **0.83% of GDP in 2021-22** — the first crossing of the 0.8% mark since 2009-10 — with private industry now the single largest spender.

**Exam angle:** GERD-to-GDP is a perennial Prelims/Mains figure, and this release contains a genuine *structural* story, not just a number: for the first time private industry outspends all levels of government combined. That flips the standard critique of India's innovation ecosystem ("state-dominated, low commercialisation") into a more nuanced one, and gives you a defensible data point for any GS3 Sci-Tech or Economic Survey-style answer.

**Facts / figures to memorise:**

- **GERD = 0.83% of GDP (2021-22)**; expenditure rebounded **over 53%** in 2021-22 after the pandemic dip in 2020-21.
- **Private industry share: 51.8% of total R&D expenditure in 2023-24** — first time above all government tiers combined.
- Compilation body: **NSTMIS (National Science & Technology Management Information System)**, a DST programme established in **1982**; follows **UNESCO** and **OECD (Frascati Manual)** methodology; publishes *R&D Statistics at a Glance*.
- International comparison to quote: Israel ~5%, South Korea 4.8%, USA 3.5%, Japan 3.3%, China 2.4%, **India 0.83%**.
- Medium-term aspiration: raise GERD to **~2% of GDP**.
- India's **Global Innovation Index (WIPO)** rank moved from **46th (2021) to 38th (2025)** — the classic "high output, low input" paradox.
- Enabling architecture: **Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF)**, the **RDI Scheme** for private investment, Geospatial Policy 2022, Space Policy 2023, BioE3 Policy 2024, National Quantum Mission, India Semiconductor Mission, IndiaAI Mission.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (indigenisation, R&D ecosystem).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal Economy* pillar on **Science, Technology & Innovation** (or your Economic Survey S&T chapter). Update the GERD figure to 0.83% and add a cross-link to **ANRF / NEP 2020's research-funding provisions**, noting that the private-sector crossover changes what "under-investment in R&D" now means.

### 5. Foreign Secretary Misri in Colombo: two INR-denominated Lines of Credit to Sri Lanka — International Relations

**Headline:** Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri visited Sri Lanka on 5 August 2026 — his first stand-alone visit in that capacity — during which EXIM Bank of India and Sri Lanka's Ministry of Finance concluded **two rupee-denominated Lines of Credit**: **USD 250 million for railway projects** and **USD 100 million for livestock and other priority requirements**.

**Exam angle:** The operative novelty is *INR-denomination* — this is rupee internationalisation being executed through development finance in the neighbourhood, not just through trade settlement. For a debt-distressed partner it shifts currency risk; for India it extends the rupee's transactional footprint. Pair it with the Ditwah reconstruction package for a complete "India as first responder + long-term development partner" case study.

**Facts / figures to memorise:**

- Two **INR-denominated LoCs**: **USD 250 mn (railways)** + **USD 100 mn (livestock and priority needs)**; signed between **EXIM Bank of India** and Sri Lanka's Ministry of Finance.
- **Cyclone Ditwah reconstruction package: USD 450 million**, of which a **USD 100 million grant** covers damaged-house reconstruction and permanent bridges.
- Completed: **Northern Railway corridor rehabilitation** under a **USD 5 million grant**; train services resumed.
- In the pipeline: **Kankesanthurai harbour** rehabilitation under a **USD 61.5 million grant** (MoU to be initiated); electricity **grid interconnection**, **Sampur solar power project**, **Trincomalee** as an energy hub; Sri Lanka **Unique Digital Identity** project; island-wide **disaster warning system**; expansion of the **1990 Suwa Seriya** ambulance service.
- Economic track: negotiations to **update** the existing India–Sri Lanka FTA (ISFTA) to be taken forward; **India–Sri Lanka Social Security Agreement** negotiations concluded, signature pending.
- Policy framing: **Neighbourhood First** and **Vision MAHASAGAR**. Interlocutors: President **Anura Kumara Disanayaka**, PM **Harini Amarasuriya**, FM **Vijitha Herath**.
- Raised by India: early release of detained fishermen and boats; **Provincial Council elections** and full implementation of Sri Lanka's constitutional provisions for Tamil aspirations; the December 2024 Joint Statement assurance that Sri Lankan territory will not be used against India's security.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India and its neighbourhood).

**Static anchor:** Your **India–Sri Lanka** country file (bilateral relations notes). ### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Economy / Environment, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *"Assured offtake, an administered price and a statutory blending obligation have become the standard toolkit for scaling India's alternative fuels."* **Examine** this proposition with reference to the compressed biogas sector.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the diagnosis.** Open with the SATAT record — a 2018 target of 5,000 CBG plants by 2023 against roughly 132 commissioned by mid-2026. Establish that the binding constraint was never technology or feedstock but *revenue certainty*: no guaranteed buyer, no stable price, no bankable cash flow. This is the same failure that stalled ethanol before 2018.

2. **Set out the toolkit and how GOBARdhan applies it.** Administered price of ₹2,110/MMBTU with a minimum 10-year horizon (price risk); a CBO of 3%→4%→5% with CGD entities as obligated buyers (demand risk); capital assistance up to ₹2 crore/TPD plus an MSME credit guarantee (financing risk); pipeline connectivity (evacuation risk). Note that the ₹23,731 crore, ten-year design is deliberately mirrored on the ethanol-blending precedent.

3. **Examine the co-benefits — and be specific.** Import substitution in natural gas and energy security; methane abatement from open dumping of agri-residue, cattle dung, press mud and MSW (methane's high global-warming potential makes this disproportionately valuable for NDC accounting); farm-residue monetisation as an alternative to stubble burning; bio-slurry as organic manure feeding into natural-farming programmes; rural entrepreneurship and MSME employment.

4. **Examine the risks honestly — this is where the directive is earned.** Administered prices can become fiscal or consumer-price liabilities if input costs diverge; blending mandates are only as strong as their enforcement and can raise CNG/PNG costs for end users; feedstock aggregation logistics remain the unsolved bottleneck (the SATAT plants that failed mostly failed here); and a ten-fold scale-up requires digester technology and skilled O&M that do not yet exist at that scale. Conclude that the toolkit reliably solves *market* failure but not *supply-chain* failure — and that the decade-long horizon should be used to build feedstock aggregation institutions, not merely to disburse subsidy.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The outlay is ₹23,731 crore over FY2026-27 to FY2035-36, and the CBO trajectory is 3% (FY27) → 4% (FY28) → 5% (from FY29). Statement 3 is the inverse of the scheme: GOBARdhan provides capital assistance of up to ₹2 crore per TPD *and* an administered price of ₹2,110/MMBTU under a minimum 10-year framework — precisely because market-determined pricing failed under SATAT.

**Q2 — (b) 1 and 2 only.** The Bill raises judges other than the CJI from 33 to 37 (34 → 38 including the CJI) and replaces the Ordinance of 16 May 2026. Statement 3 is wrong: it amends the **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Act, 1956**, an ordinary law. Article 124(1) itself leaves the number to Parliament "by law", so no constitutional amendment is needed.

**Q3 — (b).** The Court held that the domestic-cruelty offence under Section 498A IPC (now Sections 85 and 86, BNS 2023) covers women in live-in relationships "in the nature of marriage", relying on Articles 14, 15(3) and 21. (a) is wrong — no equivalence for succession was declared. (c) is wrong — the provision was expanded, not struck down. (d) is the pre-existing position from *Lalita Toppo* (2018), which this judgment goes beyond.

**Q4 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct. GERD stood at 0.83% of GDP in 2021-22 (first crossing of 0.8% since 2009-10, after a >53% rebound from the pandemic dip); private industry's share reached 51.8% in 2023-24, exceeding all government tiers combined for the first time; and NSTMIS, a DST programme set up in 1982, is the nodal compiler using UNESCO/OECD Frascati Manual methodology.

**Q5 — (c) 1 and 2 only.** The two INR-denominated LoCs (USD 250 mn railways, USD 100 mn livestock and priority needs) were concluded between EXIM Bank and Sri Lanka's Finance Ministry, under the Neighbourhood First and Vision MAHASAGAR framing. Statement 3 misstates the outcome: the two sides agreed to take forward negotiations on **updating** the existing India–Sri Lanka FTA, not replacing it.

**Q6 — (c) 1 and 2 only.** The Constitution originally provided for a CJI and seven other judges, and Article 124(1) permits Parliament to prescribe a larger number by law — which is exactly what the 1956 Act and its amendments have done. Statement 3 is wrong: a Supreme Court judge holds office until the age of **65** (High Court judges retire at 62 — the classic trap).

**Q7 — (b) 1 and 2 only.** The Palk Strait connects the Bay of Bengal with the Gulf of Mannar, and Adam's Bridge is the shoal chain between Pamban (Rameswaram) Island and Mannar Island. Statement 3 is wrong: **Katchatheevu lies in the Palk Strait**, between Neduntheevu (Delft) and Rameswaram — not in the Gulf of Mannar.
