## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Union Cabinet clears a Ladakh bench of the Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh High Court — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** On 20 August 2026 the Union Cabinet approved the establishment of a bench at Ladakh for the High Court of Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh — the court's third bench, after Srinagar and Jammu.

**Exam angle:**
- This is a *bench*, not a new High Court. Ladakh remains under the common High Court it shares with J&K — a live illustration of **Article 231** (common High Court for two or more States / States and a UT) rather than **Article 214** (a High Court for each State).
- Feeds directly into the "access to justice" strand of GS2: geography as a barrier to constitutional remedies under Article 226. Litigants from Leh and Kargil previously had to travel outside the UT.
- Pairs neatly with the 21 August Supreme Court order on the Bar-practice requirement (covered on 22 Aug) as a "judicial infrastructure" cluster for Mains.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Decision date: **20 August 2026** (announced by Home Minister Amit Shah).
- Will be the **third bench** of the High Court of J&K and Ladakh; existing benches at **Srinagar** and **Jammu**.
- Ladakh became a separate UT (without legislature) on 31 October 2019 under the **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019**; the common High Court was retained by that Act.
- Establishing a *new* High Court for a UT requires Parliament (Article 241 read with Article 231); establishing a *bench* is an executive/Presidential-notification route on the Chief Justice's recommendation.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Judiciary).

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth*, chapter on **High Court** (Articles 214–231) and the chapter on **Union Territories** (Article 239A, 241). → Add a margin note under Article 231: "Punjab & Haryana + Chandigarh; Guwahati HC for Assam/Nagaland/Mizoram/Arunachal; Bombay HC benches at Nagpur, Aurangabad, Panaji; **J&K and Ladakh HC — Srinagar, Jammu, and now Ladakh (Cabinet, Aug 2026)**." Cross-link to Article 226 and to the Article 241 route for UT High Courts.

### 2. MeitY notifies the Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme — ₹62,500 crore, five years — Economy

**Headline:** The Ministry of Electronics and IT notified the **Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme (MPMS)** on 21 August 2026 with an outlay of **₹62,500 crore** for FY 2026–27 to FY 2030–31 — the successor architecture to the PLI for large-scale electronics.

**Exam angle:**
- The design shift is the story: the old PLI rewarded *volume*; MPMS explicitly rewards **domestic value addition, Indian-owned brands, and design/IP** — i.e. moving up the smile curve from assembly to design.
- Classic GS3 "changes in industrial policy and their effects on industrial growth". Also a ready case study for the *import-substitution vs export-orientation* debate and for the criticism that India's electronics exports have high import intensity.
- Note the two-segment structure — this is exactly the kind of detail Prelims turns into a statement.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Outlay **₹62,500 crore**; tenure **FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31** (5 years); nodal ministry **MeitY**.
- **Two segments:** (i) mobile phone manufacturing — incentive **2.25% to 5%**; (ii) Indian mobile phone brands — **5%**, plus an additional **3%** for domestic design and R&D.
- Additional incentive of up to **1.5%** for domestic sourcing of key components and sub-assemblies.
- Stated aim: raise domestic value addition from about **23% to 35–40%**.
- Expected cumulative production over the scheme period: about **₹39 lakh crore**; about **60,000 direct jobs**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Industry / Manufacturing).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* Pillar **4A — Industrial policy and manufacturing** (PLI section). → Update the PLI table with a new row for MPMS and write one line: "PLI (2020, 14 sectors, volume-linked) → **MPMS (2026, value-addition- and design-linked)**." Cross-link to **SPECS**, **Semicon India**, the **Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme**, and the National Manufacturing Mission.

### 3. Jaishankar in Moscow for the 27th IRIGC-TEC — International Relations

**Headline:** EAM S. Jaishankar is in Russia on **23–24 August 2026** to co-chair the **27th session of the India-Russia Inter-Governmental Commission on Trade, Economic, Scientific, Technological and Cultural Cooperation (IRIGC-TEC)** with First Deputy Prime Minister **Denis Manturov**, and to hold talks with Foreign Minister **Sergey Lavrov**.

**Exam angle:**
- The institutional detail is the examinable bit: India-Russia runs **two** IGCs. IRIGC-TEC is co-chaired by India's **External Affairs Minister** and a Russian **Deputy PM**; IRIGC-M&MTC (Military and Military-Technical Cooperation) is co-chaired by the two **Defence Ministers**. UPSC loves this kind of mapping.
- The context — US tariff pressure on India over Russian crude — makes this a textbook **strategic autonomy / multi-alignment** case study for GS2.
- Quotable Mains line: the relationship has shifted from a defence-heavy axis to an *energy-and-connectivity* axis, with the trade balance heavily skewed by discounted crude.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **27th** IRIGC-TEC session; Indian co-chair **EAM**, Russian co-chair **First Deputy PM Denis Manturov**; visit at Manturov's invitation.
- The **26th** session was held in Moscow in August 2025.
- Distinguish: **IRIGC-TEC** (trade/economic/S&T/cultural) vs **IRIGC-M&MTC** (defence). The **Annual Summit** is the apex mechanism.
- Standing anchors of the relationship: **S-400 Triumf**, **INSTC**, **Chennai–Vladivostok Eastern Maritime Corridor**, **RELOS** (Reciprocal Exchange of Logistics Agreement), Kudankulam, and the proposed India-EAEU FTA.

### 4. India and ADB sign a $230 million loan for Chennai's climate-resilient water and sewerage system — Environment

**Headline:** On **21 August 2026** the Government of India and the **Asian Development Bank** signed a **$230 million** loan for the **Chennai Climate-Resilient Water Security and Sewerage Project**, which will make Chennai the first Indian city to deploy a comprehensive **ring-main** water distribution system.

**Exam angle:**
- Chennai is *the* Indian case study for urban water stress — Day Zero in June 2019, then record floods. A project explicitly framed as **climate-resilient water security** is the cleanest available example of adaptation finance meeting urban infrastructure.
- The **ring main** is a small but very examinable technical idea: a closed-loop distribution network that equalises pressure across the service area, cuts non-revenue water, and keeps supply going if one segment fails — contrast with conventional branched/dead-end networks.
- Signed by the **Department of Economic Affairs, Ministry of Finance** — a reminder that all sovereign multilateral borrowing routes through DEA, not the line ministry.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **$230 million**; signed **21 August 2026**; signatories — Deputy Secretary, **DEA, Ministry of Finance** for India, and the **Country Director, India Resident Mission** for ADB.
- Scope: over **170 km** of water supply and sewer pipes; upgrade of **7 water pumping stations** and **38 sewer pumping stations**.
- Beneficiaries: about **4.5 million** residents. First Indian city to adopt a comprehensive **ring-main** solution.
- ADB basics: established **19 December 1966**, HQ **Mandaluyong, Metro Manila (Philippines)**, **69 members** (49 regional + 20 non-regional), President **Masato Kanda**. Japan and the US are the largest shareholders with equal shareholdings; India is a founding member and among the largest borrowers.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Urban environment / climate adaptation) with a GS3 Economy cross-tag.

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment*, **Sustainable Development / Environmental Management** chapter (urban water). → Add Chennai as a worked example under "urban water stress and adaptation" and note the ring-main concept. Cross-link to **AMRUT 2.0**, **Jal Jeevan Mission (Urban)**, and to Mrunal Pillar 3 for the **ADB vs World Bank vs AIIB vs NDB** comparison table.

### 5. Centre restricts chlorpheniramine–phenylephrine cold combinations for children under four — Society

**Headline:** The Ministry of Health and Family Welfare has notified **S.O. 4595(E) dated 18 August 2026** restricting **all** fixed-dose combinations containing **Chlorpheniramine Maleate and Phenylephrine Hydrochloride**, making a warning — "not to be used in children below four years of age" — mandatory on the label, package insert and promotional literature.

**Exam angle:**
- The legal hook is **Section 26A of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940**, which lets the Centre prohibit or restrict manufacture, sale and distribution of a drug in the public interest. This is the same provision used for the 2016 and 2018 FDC bans — and the one litigated in *Pharmaceutical Manufacturers vs Union of India*.
- Institutional chain worth memorising: **expert committee → DTAB (Drugs Technical Advisory Board, the highest statutory advisory body under the 1940 Act) → Central Government notification**.
- The wider theme is India's **irrational fixed-dose combination** problem — thousands of FDCs marketed without separate safety evaluation, flagged by the **Mashelkar Committee (2003)** and the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Health's 59th Report (2012).

**Facts to memorise:**
- Notification **S.O. 4595(E)**, dated **18 August 2026**, under **Section 26A**, Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940.
- Extends and widens an earlier notification of **15 April 2025** that covered only *certain* such FDCs; the new one covers **all formulations** containing the two molecules.
- Advisory body: **DTAB**; regulator: **CDSCO** headed by the **Drugs Controller General of India (DCGI)**.
- Chlorpheniramine maleate is a first-generation **antihistamine**; phenylephrine is a **decongestant** (alpha-adrenergic agonist) — the combination is the standard paediatric "cold syrup".

**Notebook:** GS2 — Governance / Health (with a GS1 Society cross-tag on child health).

**Static anchor:** GS2 **Health governance** notes. → Add a box: "Section 26A D&C Act 1940 — the ban power. Timeline: 344 FDCs banned 2016 → SC in *Pharma Manufacturers* (2018) → DTAB re-review → 2023 and 2026 restrictions." Cross-link to **CDSCO/DCGI**, the **Mashelkar Committee (2003)**, and the **Pharmacovigilance Programme of India**.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Economy, 250 words, 15 marks)

**"India has become one of the world's largest assemblers of mobile phones, but assembly is not manufacturing. Examine how the Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme, 2026 attempts to close India's domestic value-addition gap, and the constraints that could blunt it."**

*Model-answer skeleton:*

1. **Frame the gap.** India is now a top-two handset producer and a large exporter, but domestic value addition sits at roughly a quarter of output — most of the bill of materials (display, camera modules, chipsets, precision components) is imported. High gross exports, thin net gains; the smile-curve argument.
2. **What MPMS changes.** ₹62,500 crore over FY27–FY31; two tracks — manufacturers (2.25–5%) and Indian-owned brands (5%); an extra 3% for domestic design and R&D and up to 1.5% for local sourcing of key components. The incentive is therefore tilted toward **IP, design and backward linkages**, not just incremental sales — the structural break from PLI 1.0.
3. **Constraints.** Component ecosystem depth (a sub-assembly cannot be localised faster than the fab/substrate/display supply chain allows); tariff inversion and the cost of importing inputs; scale disadvantages for Indian-owned brands against entrenched global players; skilled design talent; fiscal cost per job; and WTO/subsidy-discipline exposure of output-linked incentives.
4. **Way ahead.** Sequence MPMS with the Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme and Semicon India so component capacity arrives before the sourcing incentive bites; simplify the inverted duty structure; deepen design-linked incentives and university-industry IP creation; and add sunset clauses with measurable value-addition milestones so the incentive does not become a permanent entitlement.

### D. PYQ Mirror

**Theme: High Courts and judicial review.**
UPSC **CSE Prelims 2019** asked: *"With reference to the Constitution of India, consider the following statements: 1. No High Court shall have the jurisdiction to declare any central law to be constitutionally invalid. 2. An amendment to the Constitution of India cannot be called into question by the Supreme Court of India."* — the answer was **(d) Neither 1 nor 2**. High Courts *can* strike down central laws; the 42nd Amendment (1976) had removed that power and the 43rd Amendment (1977) restored it. Today's Ladakh-bench item is the *institutional* face of the same chapter, so expect either the Article 231 angle or the judicial-review angle.

**Theme: Production-linked incentives.**
The PLI scheme has been examined on both sides of the exam — it appeared in a **Prelims 2023** statement-based question on companies taking advantage of India's Production-Linked Incentive scheme, and coaching compilations record a **GS3 Mains 2025** question along the lines of *"Discuss the rationale of the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) scheme. What are its achievements? In what way can the functioning and outcomes of the scheme be improved?"* — *(the 2025 Mains wording is reported by secondary sources; treat the exact phrasing as approximate.)* MPMS is the natural update to that answer.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** The Cabinet approved a *bench* of the existing High Court of J&K and Ladakh, not a separate High Court, so statement 1 is wrong. It will be the third bench after Srinagar and Jammu. Article 231 empowers Parliament to establish a common High Court for two or more States (or States and a UT).

**Q2 — (b).** MPMS is a MeitY scheme of ₹62,500 crore for FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31, with a manufacturing track (2.25–5%) and an Indian-brands track (5%), plus 3% for domestic design and R&D and up to 1.5% for domestic component sourcing.

**Q3 — (c) All three.** IRIGC-TEC is co-chaired by India's External Affairs Minister (with a Russian Deputy PM); IRIGC-M&MTC is the defence-side commission co-chaired by the Defence Ministers; the Annual Summit is the apex leaders' mechanism.

**Q4 — (b).** The project makes Chennai the first Indian city to implement a comprehensive ring-main solution — a closed-loop network that balances pressure and improves reliability and equity of supply. It also adds 170+ km of pipes and upgrades 7 water and 38 sewer pumping stations for about 4.5 million residents.

**Q5 — (b).** Section 26A of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 empowers the Central Government to prohibit or restrict the manufacture, sale and distribution of a drug in the public interest, acting on DTAB advice. Notification S.O. 4595(E) of 18 August 2026 used this power.

**Q6 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Article 214 provides a High Court for each State and Article 231 allows a common High Court for two or more States; Article 230 lets Parliament extend or exclude a High Court's jurisdiction over a Union Territory. Statement 3 is wrong — High Courts can and do test the constitutionality of central legislation (Prelims 2019 tested exactly this).

**Q7 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** ADB was established on 19 December 1966 with headquarters in Mandaluyong, Metro Manila. Statement 2 is wrong — of its 69 members, 20 are non-regional (including the US, Germany, France and the UK). India is a founding member.
