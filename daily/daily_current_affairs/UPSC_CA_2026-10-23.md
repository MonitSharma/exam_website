## Part A — Briefing

### 1. Three Indian sites added to UNESCO's World Heritage Tentative List (20 Oct) — Art & Culture / Geography
**Exam angle:** Tentative List entry is the mandatory first step before a World Heritage nomination. The additions pair two rare geological sites with an early-medieval archaeological landscape, a useful "Places in News" cluster.
**Key facts:**
- Karst–Mud-Volcano Complex of Baratang (Baratang Island, Andaman): limestone caves and mud volcanoes.
- Andaman Subduction Volcanoes: Narcondam and Barren Island (India's only active volcano).
- Sirpur Cultural and Archaeological Landscape, Chhattisgarh.
- Reported totals: 81 sites on the Tentative List; 45 World Heritage Sites after Sarnath's inscription in July 2026 (secondary sources; verify before quoting).
**Notebook:** GS1 Art & Culture / Geography.
**Static anchor:** Nitin Singhania, *Art & Culture* (UNESCO heritage chapter) and Spectrum/NCERT Geography (Andaman volcanoes, mud volcanoes). Nudge: add Baratang, Narcondam and Sirpur to your "Places in News" map and cross-link to Barren Island.

### 2. UNEP Emissions Gap Report 2026, "The Point of Return" (21 Oct) — Environment / Reports
**Exam angle:** UNEP's annual assessment now says 1.5°C with limited overshoot is no longer possible. That shifts the debate toward overshoot management and carbon removal ahead of COP31.
**Key facts:**
- Cuts needed versus 2025 levels: 18% by 2030, 45% by 2035, 69% by 2040.
- Current NDCs promise only 5–8% cuts by 2030 and 11–15% by 2035.
- Projected warming: about 2.8°C on current policies, 2.6°C if NDCs are fully delivered, 2.1°C if NDCs plus net-zero targets are delivered.
- Best remaining pathway peaks at 1.7–1.8°C around mid-century; it needs about 320 Gt CO₂ of removals this century.
- Solar PV has grown about 7 times faster than expected since Paris.
**Notebook:** GS3 Environment (Climate Change).
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS, *Environment* (Climate change and UNFCCC chapter). Nudge: add a "UNEP reports" table (Emissions Gap, Adaptation Gap, GEO) with the latest headline figure for each.

### 3. Core sector output grows 7.9% in September on the revised ICI — Economy
**Exam angle:** September's reading is the fastest since April 2024 and the first prominent print under the new 2022-23 base. Aggregate vs. component weights is a classic Prelims trap.
**Key facts:**
- Base year 2022-23 (old: 2011-12); nine industries, with Iron Ore newly added.
- Weights: Electricity 30.93%, Refinery Products 22.57%, Steel 17.58%, Crude Oil 7.43%, Coal 5.59%, Iron Ore 4.90%, Cement 4.41%, Natural Gas 3.84%, Fertilizers 2.73%.
- Combined weight in IIP fell from 40.27% to 32.88%.
- Compiled by the Office of Economic Adviser, DPIIT.
- September: Iron Ore 18.4%, Electricity 11.4%, Coal 10.7%, Refinery 10.3%. Fertilizers, Natural Gas and Crude Oil contracted.
**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (Industry, Indices).
**Static anchor:** Ramesh Singh, *Indian Economy* (Industry / Index numbers). Nudge: replace the old "eight core industries, 40.27%" note with the nine-industry table.

### 4. Badr MoU: Pakistan, Türkiye, Qatar and Indonesia sign a defence-industrial accord in Doha (21 Oct) — International Relations
**Exam angle:** It is a cooperation memorandum on joint manufacturing, technology sharing, training and R&D, not a mutual-defence treaty. Contrast it with the August Saudi–Türkiye–Pakistan Makkah pact, which has an "attack on one is an attack on all" clause.
**Key facts:**
- Signed by the defence ministers or deputies of the four states.
- India's concerns: Pakistan could diversify away from China-dependent supply, and tech access could aid its drone programme.
- Indonesia is also in talks with India on a reported ~$630 million BrahMos/Astra deal, which adds a diplomatic wrinkle.
**Notebook:** GS2 International Relations (India's neighbourhood, West Asia).
**Static anchor:** Mrunal IR pillar "India–Pakistan / West Asia". Nudge: add a comparison table of Badr vs Makkah pact vs NATO Article 5.

### 5. 7th Agrivoltaics World Conference, New Delhi (21–23 Oct) — Agriculture

### B3. Mains question (GS3 · 15 marks · 250 words)
**"UNEP's Emissions Gap Report 2026 says 1.5°C with limited overshoot is no longer attainable. Critically examine what this means for India's climate diplomacy and its development pathway."**
Model-answer skeleton:
1. Intro: state the report's headline (cuts of 18%/45%/69% vs 2025 by 2030/35/40; NDCs deliver 5–8% and 11–15%; 2.6–2.8°C trajectory).
2. Diplomacy: overshoot makes adaptation finance, loss and damage, and CBDR-RC central; India can press developed countries on historical responsibility and on carbon-removal burden-sharing.
3. Development: India's low per-capita emissions give moral space, but coal dependence and a rising absolute-emissions trend invite scrutiny; stress renewables scale-up, CCTS, green hydrogen.
4. Risks: CBAM-style trade measures, technology-transfer gaps, climate vulnerability (heat, monsoon shifts).
5. Conclusion: balanced path of rapid clean-energy deployment plus adaptation and a firm equity-based negotiating line at COP31.

## Part C — Answers
- **Q1 (b):** Tentative List entry is the first step before nomination; Narcondam and Barren Island are in the Andaman Subduction Volcanoes entry. Sirpur is in Chhattisgarh, so statement 3 is wrong.
- **Q2 (a):** Statements 1 and 2 match the report. Full NDC delivery projects about 2.6°C, so statement 3 is wrong.
- **Q3 (c):** The ICI is compiled by the Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT (not NSO). Iron Ore is the ninth industry; Electricity has the top weight (30.93%).
- **Q4 (a):** Badr is an industrial/technology cooperation MoU; it has no mutual-defence clause. The Makkah pact is the one with a collective-defence clause.
- **Q5 (c):** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. The conference was led by MNRE (with Agriculture), not Agriculture as sole host.
- **Q6 (d):** Khangchendzonga National Park (inscribed 2016) is India's only mixed site.
- **Q7 (b):** The IIP is compiled by the NSO under MoSPI; the ICI is compiled by the Office of Economic Adviser, DPIIT.
