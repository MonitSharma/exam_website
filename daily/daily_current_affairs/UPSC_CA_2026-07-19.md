### 1. Cabinet approves Semicon 2.0 — ₹1,27,500 crore for the full chip value chain — Economy

**Headline:** The Union Cabinet has cleared **Semicon 2.0 (India Semiconductor Mission 2.0)** with an outlay of **₹1,27,500 crore**, part of a ₹2,19,353 crore package of approvals that also included MPMS and NIPU-2026.

**Exam angle:** This is the *successor* generation of the India Semiconductor Mission — and the design shift is the examinable point. Semicon 1.0 was fab-and-ATMP heavy; Semicon 2.0 explicitly funds **equipment, materials, chemicals and gases**, i.e. the upstream layer where India is most import-dependent. Pair it with the MPMS "domestic value addition" logic (18 July) and with the China helium/rare-earth export controls (13 July) — together they form a single Mains argument on **technological sovereignty and supply-chain de-risking**.

**Key facts to memorize:**
- Outlay **₹1,27,500 crore**; Semicon 1.0 was **₹76,000 crore** (12 approved projects, ~₹1.64 lakh crore of investment mobilised).
- Targets: **₹4 lakh crore** of investment attracted and **₹2 lakh crore** of semiconductor production during the scheme period.
- **Six pillars:** (i) chip **design**, (ii) **equipment & materials** (machines, chemicals, gases), (iii) more **fabs** (silicon, compound-semiconductor, discrete, display), (iv) **ATMP/OSAT** (Assembly, Testing, Marking & Packaging / Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test), (v) **R&D** beyond current nodes, (vi) **talent**.
- Ecosystem numbers cited: ~**105 chip-design startups**; **315 universities** using advanced **EDA** tools; ~**68,000 students** trained.
- Same Cabinet package: **MPMS ₹62,500 crore**, **NIPU-2026**, Paradeep–Haridaspur rail doubling (₹2,542 cr), Dangoaposi–Rajkharsawan 4th line (₹1,365 cr). Total **₹2,19,353 crore**.

### 2. Supreme Court: SIR data cannot be used to strip welfare entitlements or decide citizenship — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** A three-judge bench headed by **CJI Surya Kant** (with Justice **Joymalya Bagchi**) heard a challenge to West Bengal orders that used **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** electoral-roll data to cancel caste certificates and withdraw PDS and cash-transfer benefits; the Court held that SIR outcomes are tied **exclusively to electoral-roll revision** and cannot determine citizenship or entitlements.

**Exam angle:** A near-perfect GS2 question — it sits at the junction of **Article 324** (limits of ECI's mandate), **Articles 5–11 + Citizenship Act, 1955** (who actually adjudicates citizenship), and **Article 21** (right to food read into right to life). The transferable principle: *an administrative process has a defined scope, and its output cannot be transplanted into an unrelated statutory regime without due process.*

**Key facts to memorize:**
- **SIR** = ECI's door-to-door comprehensive electoral-roll revision, under the **Representation of the People Act, 1950** and **Registration of Electors Rules, 1960**.
- Precedent relied on: the **Bihar SIR case (SC, 27 May)** — deletion from a roll is *not* a conclusive finding on citizenship.
- **ECI is constituted under Article 324**; it has *no* power to adjudicate citizenship. It must refer suspected non-citizens to the competent government authority under the **Citizenship Act, 1955**. In Assam, **Foreigners Tribunals** (under the **Foreigners Act, 1946**) do this.
- Articles invoked: **Art. 9** (voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship), **Art. 10** (continuance of citizenship rights), **Art. 11** (Parliament's power to regulate citizenship).
- WB orders challenged: **14 May** (re-verification/cancellation of Backward Caste certificates), **19 May** (Annapurna cash-transfer withdrawal), **4 June** (PDS deletion).
- PDS entitlements flow from the **National Food Security Act, 2013**; right to food read into **Article 21**.
- Petitioner: Prasenjit Bose.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Election Commission; Citizenship; Fundamental Rights).
**STATIC ANCHOR:** *Laxmikanth* Ch. on **Election Commission** (Art. 324) and Ch. on **Citizenship** (Arts. 5–11). *Add a boxed note: "Electoral roll ≠ citizenship register" with the Bihar SIR (May 2026) and this WB case as the two anchors; cross-link to the NFSA/Art. 21 right-to-food line in your Fundamental Rights chapter.*

### 3. *Gitchak nakana* — India's first subterranean fish recorded from Assam — Environment

**Headline:** A doctoral researcher at **Assam Don Bosco University, Guwahati** has confirmed *Gitchak nakana*, the **first subterranean (aquifer-dwelling) fish species recorded from Assam**, collected from a dug well in **Goalpara district**; it is **eyeless and lacks a skull roof**.

**Exam angle:** UPSC loves "Species in News" with a *concept* attached — here the concept is **troglomorphism** and, more importantly, the **governance gap**: subterranean fauna live in aquifers, which sit outside the protected-area system and outside the Wildlife (Protection) Act's schedules for most freshwater fish. Good Mains line on **30x30 and the invisibility of groundwater ecosystems in protected-area accounting**.

**Key facts to memorize:**
- **Troglobites** = obligate cave-dwellers; **stygobites** = obligate groundwater-dwellers.
- **Troglomorphism** — *regressive* traits: **anophthalmia** (eye loss), depigmentation, skull-bone reduction; *constructive* traits: enlarged **lateral line** system, elongated sensory appendages.
- Location context: Goalpara adjoins Meghalaya's **Garo Hills** karst limestone — the geological precondition for cave-aquifer systems. Other Indian subterranean-fish areas: Meghalaya caves (**Krem Umthloo, Liat Prah**), **Borra Caves** (Andhra Pradesh), Kerala.
- The Northeast lies in the **Indo-Burma Biodiversity Hotspot** — one of **36** global hotspots; hotspot criteria = **≥1,500 endemic vascular plant species** AND **≥70% original habitat lost**.
- Threats: groundwater extraction, agricultural runoff, **limestone/karst quarrying**, water-table decline.
- Governance gap: **WPA, 1972** generally does not schedule freshwater fish; groundwater is regulated by the **Central Ground Water Authority (CGWA)** on a resource, not ecological, logic. **Kunming–Montreal GBF (2022)** — **30x30** target for land and inland waters.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Biodiversity.
**STATIC ANCHOR:** *Shankar IAS* — **Biodiversity Hotspots** and **Conservation Efforts** chapters. *Add a "Subterranean / cave fauna" sub-head under species conservation and cross-link it to your Groundwater (CGWA) note in the Water Resources chapter — the exam-worthy link is habitat-outside-PA.*

### 4. Mansbal Lake shows measurable ecological recovery after WMDA restoration — Geography

**Headline:** **Mansbal (Manasbal) Lake** in **Ganderbal district, central Kashmir** — India's **deepest natural freshwater lake (~43 feet)** — is recovering, with a rise in migratory-bird arrivals in winter 2025–26 following weed removal and dredging by the **Wular–Manasbal Development Authority (WMDA)**.

**Exam angle:** A "Places in News" item that doubles as a wetland-governance case study. The examinable nuance: **Manasbal is NOT a Ramsar site** even though it is ecologically significant — J&K's Ramsar sites are **Wular Lake** and **Hokersar**. Also note the shift from *reactive* management (dredging, deweeding) to *proactive* **water budgeting** — a quotable Mains phrase.

**Key facts to memorize:**
- **Mansbal Lake:** deepest natural freshwater lake in India, **~43 ft**; length **3.5 km**, breadth **1.5 km**; **Ganderbal district**, J&K.
- Supports **46+ bird species from 23 families** (Mallard, White-headed Duck, Eurasian Hoopoe, Golden Eagle, Tickell's Thrush); a node on the **Central Asian Flyway** (Siberia/Central Asia → South & SE Asia). India is a signatory to the **Central Asian Flyway Action Plan**.
- Restoration: ~**7,202.5 cubic metres** of weeds removed; **>70,000 sq ft** dredged; planned sewerage treatment plant + **1-acre** integrated solid-waste facility.
- **Water budgeting** by the **J&K Water Resources Regulatory Authority** — quantifies inflows/outflows to fix an ecologically safe minimum water level.
- **Eutrophication** (N and P from sewage + agri runoff) is the primary driver of weed proliferation across Kashmir's lakes.
- Legal frame: **Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules, 2017** under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**; **National Wetland Conservation Programme** (MoEFCC).
- Kashmir lake cluster to revise: **Dal, Wular, Nagin, Anchar, Manasbal**.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Geography (Lakes & drainage) / GS3 — Environment (Wetlands).
**STATIC ANCHOR:** **NCERT Class XI *India: Physical Environment*** — Drainage/Lakes chapter, plus *Shankar IAS* **Wetlands** chapter. *Update your "Lakes of India" table with a Deepest = Manasbal row, and mark Ramsar/non-Ramsar status against each Kashmir lake — that distinction is exactly how a statement-based question gets set.*

### 5. US CDC flags a large cyclosporiasis surge — a food-safety warning with direct Indian export relevance — Science & Technology

**Headline:** The **US CDC** issued a health advisory on **14 July 2026** reporting **1,645 confirmed** and ~**5,100 probable** cyclosporiasis cases across **34 states** since 1 May — against **249** by the same point last year. No food source has been confirmed.

**Exam angle:** Parasitology + food safety, both PYQ-favoured. The two facts UPSC would build a statement question on: cyclosporiasis is **strictly faeco-oral and does NOT spread person-to-person** (oocysts need environmental **sporulation** first), and the oocysts are **resistant to chlorine-based sanitisers** — so washing/chemical sanitation of produce is not a reliable control. India is endemic and a growing fresh-produce exporter, which is the India-relevance hook.

**Key facts to memorize:**
- Agent: ***Cyclospora cayetanensis*** — a unicellular **coccidian protozoan** of phylum **Apicomplexa**, the same phylum as ***Plasmodium*** (malaria) and *Cryptosporidium*.
- **No person-to-person transmission**; oocysts must **sporulate** in the environment (days to weeks) before becoming infectious.
- **Chlorine-resistant** oocysts → standard produce sanitising is insufficient; **cooking** kills the parasite.
- Diagnosis: **stool PCR** or **modified acid-fast staining** — missed by routine stool panels (irregular shedding → false negatives).
- Treatment: **trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole (TMP-SMX)**, 7–10 days, plus hydration.
- Incubation ~1 week; hallmark symptom is **prolonged watery diarrhoea**; complications include malabsorption, cholecystitis, reactive arthritis.
- High-risk foods: raw leafy greens (lettuce, spinach, basil, cilantro) and berries. CDC's cyclosporiasis season: **1 May – 31 August**.
- India link: endemic; **FSSAI** is the food-safety regulator — parasitological surveillance is a market-access issue for produce exports to the EU/US/Gulf.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Health) / Food Security.
**STATIC ANCHOR:** Your Sci-Tech **Biology/Diseases** note → add *Cyclospora* to the protozoan table alongside *Plasmodium*, *Entamoeba*, *Giardia*, *Leishmania*, with a "mode of transmission" column. *Cross-link to the FSSAI/food-standards entry in your Governance–Regulatory Bodies list.*

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Polity & Governance, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **"An administrative process carries only the authority of its own statute."** In the light of recent judicial observations on the use of Special Intensive Revision data for non-electoral purposes, **examine** the constitutional limits on the Election Commission of India's mandate and the due-process safeguards that must precede any withdrawal of welfare entitlements.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the two distinct legal tracks.** Electoral-roll revision under the RPA, 1950 and Registration of Electors Rules, 1960 determines *voting eligibility only*. Welfare eligibility flows from separate statutes — NFSA, 2013 for PDS, state rules for cash transfers, and the SC/ST/OBC certification machinery. Conflating them is the constitutional error.
2. **Locate the ECI's outer limit.** Article 324 confers superintendence, direction and control *of elections* — not adjudication of civil status. Citizenship is governed by Articles 5–11 read with the Citizenship Act, 1955; adjudication rests with the competent government authority (Foreigners Tribunals under the Foreigners Act, 1946, in Assam). Deletion from a roll may reflect death or change of residence — administrative imperfection, not a legal finding.
3. **Bring in the rights dimension.** Right to food has been read into Article 21; arbitrary PDS removal without a hearing engages both Article 21 and Article 14 (arbitrariness). Procedural due process — notice, hearing, reasoned order, appeal — is the minimum before any deprivation, and the burden cannot be inverted onto the beneficiary.
4. **Way forward.** Statutory firewalling of electoral data from welfare databases; mandatory referral protocol when the ECI suspects non-citizenship; independent grievance redressal with suspensive effect (benefits continue pending appeal); and a data-protection-consistent purpose-limitation rule for electoral data.

### D. PYQ Mirror

**Theme 1 — Election Commission / electoral machinery (Polity).**
**UPSC Prelims 2017** asked a statement question on the ECI: that it "is a five-member body" (incorrect — it is a three-member body, CEC + 2 ECs), that the **Union Ministry of Home Affairs** decides the election schedule (incorrect — the ECI does), and that the **ECI resolves disputes relating to splits/mergers of recognised political parties** (correct). *Takeaway:* UPSC tests the **boundary of the ECI's powers**, which is precisely what today's SIR item sharpens — the ECI decides party disputes and schedules, but not citizenship.

**Theme 2 — Wetlands & migratory birds (Environment/Geography).**
UPSC has repeatedly used wetlands as a statement-question vehicle — including a **2014 Prelims** question built around the **Montreux Record** under the Ramsar Convention, and a **2022 Prelims** question on the ecological functions of wetlands ("kidneys of the landscape" — filtration, groundwater recharge, flood moderation). *Flag: these two years are cited from recall of standard PYQ compilations and should be re-verified against the original papers before you commit them to notes.* *Takeaway:* the reliable pattern is **function + status (Ramsar / Montreux) + flyway**, which is exactly the Mansbal fact-set to hold.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (a).** Semicon 2.0's outlay (₹1,27,500 cr) exceeds Semicon 1.0's ₹76,000 cr, so 1 is correct. Incentivising equipment, materials, chemicals and gases is one of its six pillars, so 2 is correct. Statement 3 is wrong — **ATMP/OSAT is explicitly one of the six pillars** of Semicon 2.0.

**Q2 — (b).** The Court held SIR is tied exclusively to electoral-roll revision; its outcomes cannot determine citizenship or strip entitlements such as PDS, cash transfers or caste certificates. (a) and (d) invert the ECI's role; (c) is the very state action that was challenged.

**Q3 — (b).** *Gitchak nakana* is an eyeless, skull-roof-lacking subterranean fish found in a dug well in Goalpara district — the first subterranean fish recorded from Assam.

**Q4 — (a).** Mansbal is India's deepest natural freshwater lake (~43 ft) and lies in Ganderbal district, so 1 and 2 are correct. It is **not** a Ramsar site — J&K's Ramsar sites include **Wular Lake** and **Hokersar** — so 3 is incorrect.

**Q5 — (b).** *Cyclospora* is a coccidian protozoan of phylum **Apicomplexa**, like *Plasmodium* (1 correct). It does **not** spread person-to-person because oocysts must sporulate in the environment first (2 incorrect). Oocysts resist chlorine-based sanitisers (3 correct).

**Q6 — (a).** The ECI is created by **Article 324** (1 correct) and decides splits/mergers of recognised parties (2 correct, as tested in Prelims 2017). It has **no** power to adjudicate citizenship — that must be referred to the competent authority under the Citizenship Act, 1955 (3 incorrect).

**Q7 — (b).** The two hotspot criteria are ≥1,500 endemic vascular plant species and ≥70% loss of original primary vegetation (1 and 2 correct). Statement 3 is incorrect — North-East India falls within the **Indo-Burma** hotspot, not the Western Ghats–Sri Lanka hotspot.
