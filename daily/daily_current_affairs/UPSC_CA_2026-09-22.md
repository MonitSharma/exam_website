### 1. India extends a ₹4,000-crore concessional Line of Credit to Bhutan — the first LoC aimed squarely at energy — International Relations

**Headline:** EAM S. Jaishankar's 16–18 September visit to Thimphu produced a ₹4,000-crore concessional Line of Credit dedicated to Bhutan's energy sector, alongside the launch of five development projects.

**Exam angle:**
- Bhutan is the standing test case for India's "Neighbourhood First" — and the one relationship where development assistance, hydropower commerce and strategic buffer all sit in the same file. An LoC ring-fenced for *energy* signals a shift from the older project-by-project hydro model (India builds, India buys) toward financing Bhutan's own generation portfolio.
- The subtext is the northern border. Bhutan is the only neighbour with which China has no diplomatic relations but an active boundary negotiation (Doklam sits on the Bhutan–China–India trijunction). Every rupee of Indian concessional finance is also a hedge.
- Note the instrument: a **Line of Credit**, not a grant. India's LoCs are routed through **EXIM Bank** under the Indian Development and Economic Assistance Scheme (**IDEAS**); grant assistance is a separate MEA budget head.

**Facts to memorise:**
- ₹4,000 crore concessional LoC, energy sector; visit 16–18 September 2026; counterpart: Foreign Minister D.N. Dhungyel.
- Launched: 65-bed Gyaltsuen Jetsun Pema Mother & Child Hospital (Mongar); Jaganathan Bridge (Samdrup Jongkhar–Trashigang highway); **500 kW Lunana** hydropower project.
- Hydro status: **Punatsangchhu-II (1,020 MW)** recently inaugurated with a tariff protocol signed; **Punatsangchhu-I (1,200 MW)** dam construction resumed.
- Support reaffirmed for Bhutan's **13th Five Year Plan (2024–29)**. Also: 54 EVs and 43 Jersey cows transferred; scholarships doubled.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India & Neighbourhood).

**Static anchor:** *India's Neighbourhood Policy* — your India–Bhutan note. Add a cross-link to the **1949 Treaty of Perpetual Peace and Friendship, revised 2007** (Article 2 of the 1949 text — "guided by" India on external relations — dropped in 2007), and to Bhutan's river map in the Geography notebook (see the static MCQ below). Cross-link to **EXIM Bank / IDEAS** in the Mrunal BoP–external-sector pillar.

### 2. 'KAL' — India's first indigenous 1,000-km one-way attack drone completes Pokhran trials, and a private firm built it — Science & Technology

**Headline:** A long-range one-way attack (OWA) drone named **KAL**, developed by Noida-based **IG Defence**, completed trials at the Pokhran range on 20 September 2026 — 1,000 km range, 50 kg payload, 7–8 hours endurance.

**Exam angle:**
- Learn the taxonomy, because UPSC tests the distinction, not the brand. A **one-way attack drone / loitering munition** is expendable: the airframe *is* the weapon. That separates it from a reusable **armed UAV** (Predator/MQ-9 class) that releases a munition and returns, and from a **cruise missile**, which flies a pre-planned profile at high speed without loitering. OWA drones trade speed for cost — they are attritable mass, not precision exquisite.
- The economics is the story: a 1,000-km strike option at a fraction of a cruise missile's unit cost changes the arithmetic of standoff attack, which is exactly what Shahed-class systems demonstrated in recent conflicts.
- Second story: this is a **private-sector** platform, not a DRDO lab product — the visible output of the defence-industrial opening (iDEX, DAP 2020's *Buy Indian-IDDM* category, the positive indigenisation lists).

**Facts to memorise:**
- KAL: range **1,000 km**; payload **up to 50 kg**; endurance **7–8 hours**; ceiling **~5,000 m**; **multi-band GNSS + INS** autonomous navigation; vehicle-mounted launcher; trials at **Pokhran, Rajasthan**, 20 September 2026; developer **IG Defence** (Noida).
- Contrast pair for revision: **Nagastra-1** (short-range loitering munition, Solar Industries) vs KAL (long-range OWA).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology / Internal Security (indigenisation of defence).

**Static anchor:** Mrunal/Vision *Defence Technology* module — the UAV classification table. Add a row separating loitering munition / OWA drone / armed UAV / cruise missile by reusability, speed and cost. Cross-link to **counter-drone** (DRDO's D4, and the SHIELD HPM rig from your 10 September note) — attack and defence belong on facing pages.

### 3. Legacy dumpsites: 3.66 crore tonnes remediated in one year, and the new SWM Rules make it a legal obligation — Environment

**Headline:** The Centre's stocktake of legacy dumpsite remediation under SBM-Urban 2.0 reports **3.66 crore metric tonnes** of legacy waste remediated in Jan–Dec 2025, with **459 ULBs across 29 States** fully remediated — the first full-year read since the **Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026** came into force on 1 April 2026.

**Exam angle:**
- This is the rare environment story that is simultaneously Polity (74th Amendment, Twelfth Schedule entry on solid waste), Judiciary (*Almitra H. Patel v. Union of India*, which produced the first MSW Rules in 2000), and Urbanisation (land value — Bhalswa's ~70 acres in Delhi is the real prize, not the tonnage).
- Know the **biomining** process chain as a diagram: excavate → stabilise → screen through a **trommel** → four fractions: **RDF** (refuse-derived fuel, to cement kilns), **bio-soil**, **recyclables**, **inerts**.
- The honest critique — which is what earns Mains marks — is that tonnage remediated is self-reported and unverified by third parties, RDF quality limits cement-kiln uptake, bio-soil can carry heavy metals, and fresh waste keeps rebuilding the mound unless segregation at source works.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **SWM Rules, 2026**: notified January 2026, in force **1 April 2026**, superseding the 2016 Rules; mandate mapping of all legacy dumpsites with time-bound biomining, a centralised monitoring portal, landfills restricted to inert/non-recoverable waste, and **'polluter pays'** compensation.
- **SBM-U 2.0**: launched 1 October 2021, outlay **₹1.41 lakh crore**, goal "Garbage Free Cities". **DRAP** (Dumpsite Remediation Accelerator Programme) launched November 2025; **Lakshya Zero Dumpsite** is the parallel campaign.
- Case sites: **Bhalswa** (Delhi, ~70 acres, biomining since July 2022, 99.07 lakh MT remediated by 31 July 2026); **Gorakhpur** (40 acres → "Kachre Se Kanchan" Rapti Eco Park); **Bhandewadi** (Nagpur, 10 lakh+ MT); **Yakalaspura** (Raichur, ~22 acres reclaimed, **Miyawaki** forest from 5 June 2026).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (pollution & waste management); GS2 cross-entry for urban local bodies.

**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS *Environmental Pollution → Solid Waste Management*. Update the rules table from SWM Rules 2016 to **2026**, and add the biomining flow-chart. Cross-link to Laxmikanth **Ch. 38 (Municipalities) → Twelfth Schedule**, and to the **EPR** framework you have under Plastic Waste Rules.

### 4. For the first time, alternative-fuel cars outsell petrol cars in India — Economy

**Headline:** In **August 2026**, alternative-fuel vehicles took **41.95%** of passenger-vehicle retail registrations against **40.85%** for petrol/ethanol — the first month the cleaner set collectively crossed petrol (FADA data).

**Exam angle:**
- The composition matters more than the crossover. **CNG/LPG 25.28%**, **hybrids 9.04%**, **EVs 7.63%** — i.e. the transition India is actually having is a *gas* transition with an electric tail, not the pure-EV story the headlines imply. That reframes three policy debates at once: city-gas-distribution infrastructure, the GST/tax treatment of hybrids, and whether charging subsidies are chasing the smaller segment.
- Link to the external account: every percentage point off petrol demand eases the **crude oil import bill** (~85% import dependence) and the CAD — the standard Mains hook for energy security.
- Diesel is the silent variable; the alternative-fuel share is measured against petrol, so quote the comparison precisely.

**Facts to memorise:**
- August 2026 PV retails: alternative fuels **41.95%** vs petrol/ethanol **40.85%**; up from **35.26%** in August 2025. Source: **FADA** (Federation of Automobile Dealers Associations), which reports *retail* registrations off the **VAHAN** portal — distinct from SIAM's *wholesale despatch* numbers.
- Split: CNG/LPG **25.28%**, hybrid **9.04%**, EV **7.63%**. Total PV retails **4.02 lakh units**, up **16.14%** y-o-y — the first August above four lakh.
- Policy peg: **PM E-DRIVE** — ₹2,000 crore earmarked for public charging infrastructure.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (infrastructure/energy); cross-entry GS3 Environment (mitigation).

**Static anchor:** Mrunal **Pillar 5 (Infrastructure → Energy)** and the *Economic Survey* energy chapter. Add the FADA-vs-SIAM definitional note (retail vs wholesale) — that distinction alone has decided Prelims statements before. Cross-link to **ethanol blending (E20)** and the **National Green Hydrogen Mission** so the whole fuel-transition page sits together.

### 5. "Rearranging the deck chairs on the Titanic": SC strikes down Maharashtra's methanol denaturing rules — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** In ***M/s Balaji Formalin Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India*** (**2026 INSC 1009**, decided 19 September 2026), a Bench of Justices **J.B. Pardiwala and K. Vinod Chandran** struck down Rules **18A and 18B** of the **Maharashtra Poisons Rules, 1972** as violative of **Articles 14 and 19(1)(g)**.

**Exam angle:**
- This is a clean, quotable application of the **proportionality** doctrine to economic regulation — the four-prong test (legitimate aim → rational connection → necessity/least-restrictive means → balancing) that Indian courts adopted through *Modern Dental College* (2016) and *Puttaswamy* (2017), now used against a State's subordinate legislation.
- The reasoning is the takeaway: mandating a bitterant and colourant in *industrial* methanol burdened lawful non-drug manufacturers while doing nothing about the actual mischief — **pilferage and diversion** into the unregulated hooch market. Less restrictive alternatives existed (licence cancellation, tighter record-keeping). Regulation aimed at the symptom fails necessity.
- Mains line worth carrying: *a restriction on a fundamental right must be shown to be the least intrusive means available, not merely a well-intentioned one.*

**Facts to memorise:**
- Rules struck: **18A(1)** (restriction on methanol purchase via Form A licences), **18A(2)** (mandatory bitterant + colourant), **18B** (confiscation for unlicensed possession).
- Grounds: **manifest arbitrariness** under Art. 14 + disproportionate restriction on the Art. **19(1)(g)** right to practise any profession/trade, not saved by Art. **19(6)**.
- Backdrop: the 1991 Maharashtra hooch tragedy (~93 deaths) that prompted the rules.
- Quote: the Court called the additive requirement *"rearranging the deck chairs on the Titanic."*

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Fundamental Rights, judicial review of subordinate legislation).

**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth **Ch. 7 — Fundamental Rights**, the Article 19 section. Add a running "proportionality ladder" box: *Modern Dental College* (2016) → *Puttaswamy* (2017) → *Anuradha Bhasin* (2020) → *Balaji Formalin* (2026), each with the one-line ratio. Cross-link to Art. **47** (DPSP — State duty to prohibit intoxicating drinks) so you can argue both sides in a Mains answer.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — International Relations, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *"Hydropower has been both the anchor and the constraint of the India–Bhutan relationship."* **Examine** this statement in the light of recent developments in India's development assistance to Bhutan.

**Model-answer skeleton:**
1. **Anchor** — Establish the mutual dependence: Indian-financed projects (Chhukha, Tala, Mangdechhu, Punatsangchhu-I & II) built Bhutan's generation base; electricity exports to India are a dominant source of Bhutanese government revenue and the settlement anchor of the Ngultrum–Rupee peg. Cite Punatsangchhu-II (1,020 MW) inauguration with tariff protocol.
2. **Constraint** — Cost and time overruns (the Punatsangchhu-I geology problem), Bhutanese debt exposure concentrated in one sector, tariff renegotiation frictions, and criticism that the loan-heavy model transfers construction risk to the smaller partner. Hydrology risk under a warming Himalaya (GLOF exposure) compounds this.
3. **Diversification now under way** — The ₹4,000-crore concessional LoC for the energy sector broadens beyond single mega-projects; support to Bhutan's 13th Five Year Plan (2024–29); the widening basket — health infrastructure, cross-border connectivity, digital payments, EVs, education — plus Bhutan's own pivot toward the Gelephu Mindfulness City.
4. **Strategic frame and way forward** — Bhutan as the only neighbour without Sino-diplomatic ties but with an active boundary negotiation touching the trijunction; argue for outcome-based development partnership: transparent tariff formulae, green-finance instruments, grid interconnection enabling third-country trade, and a shift in the assistance mix from debt to grant-plus-capacity where Bhutan's fiscal space is thin.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**Q1 — (d).** All three are correct. The ₹4,000-crore concessional LoC for Bhutan's energy sector was welcomed during EAM Jaishankar's 16–18 September 2026 visit; India's sovereign LoCs are routed through EXIM Bank under IDEAS.

**Q2 — (a).** KAL is a long-range one-way attack (kamikaze) drone by Noida-based IG Defence — 1,000 km range, up to 50 kg payload, 7–8 hours endurance — trialled at Pokhran on 20 September 2026.

**Q3 — (a).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: DRAP (launched November 2025) sits under the Swachh Bharat Mission–Urban 2.0 framework of the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, not the National Clean Air Programme (which addresses ambient air quality).

**Q4 — (b).** In August 2026 alternative fuels took 41.95% of PV retails against petrol/ethanol's 40.85% — a first. CNG/LPG at 25.28% was the largest component, ahead of hybrids (9.04%) and EVs (7.63%), so (a) and (d) are wrong.

**Q5 — (a).** 2026 INSC 1009 struck down Rules 18A and 18B of the Maharashtra Poisons Rules, 1972 for manifest arbitrariness (Art. 14) and a disproportionate restriction on trade (Art. 19(1)(g)); the measures failed the necessity limb since less restrictive alternatives existed.

**Q6 — (a).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct — solid waste management appears in the Twelfth Schedule, inserted by the 74th Amendment Act, 1992. Statement 3 is wrong: Article 243W is **enabling**, not mandatory — the State Legislature "may, by law, endow" municipalities with these functions.

**Q7 — (c).** All three are correctly matched. Punatsangchhu = Sankosh, Wangchhu = Raidak, Amochhu = Torsa; all are south-flowing tributaries of the Brahmaputra system, as is the Manas (Drangme Chhu), Bhutan's largest river system.
