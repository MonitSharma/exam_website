### 2. SC: Homemakers are "Nation Builders" — new "Loss of Domestic Care" head
**Headline:** In *Shishu Pal @ Shish Ram v. Surjeet* (2026 INSC 634, decided 11 June), the Supreme Court created a standalone motor-accident compensation head — "Loss of Domestic Care" — and called homemakers "nation builders."

**Exam angle:** A landmark with both GS1 (Society/care economy) and GS2 (judicial pronouncement, women's rights) value. The Court monetised unpaid domestic labour, separating economic household management from loss of companionship — a quotable Mains line on the "invisible economy."

**Memorize:** Bench: Justices Sanjay Karol & N. Kotiswar Singh. Care valued at **₹30,000/month**; award enhanced from ₹8.43 lakh → **₹62.77 lakh**. Relied on **NSO Time Use Survey, 2019** → women's unpaid domestic work ≈ **15–17% of GDP**.

**Notebook:** GS1 Society (Role of women) / GS2 Polity
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS *Indian Society* → "Women / care economy"; NCERT Sociology. *Update: add the TUS-2019 stat and the "recognise–reduce–redistribute" (3R) care framework; cross-link to GS3 national-income (unpaid work outside GDP boundary).*

**Exam angle:** High S&T/defence value; UPSC repeatedly asks about missile-defence systems and indigenous missiles. India now joins the small club of nations with multi-layered BMD able to engage up to ICBM-class threats.

### Mains Question

**Q (GS1, 250 words).** "The Supreme Court's recognition of homemakers as 'nation builders' and its introduction of a 'Loss of Domestic Care' compensatory head reflect a shift towards valuing unpaid care work." **Examine** the significance of recognising unpaid domestic labour for India's economy and gender equity, and the challenges in mainstreaming it.

*Model-answer skeleton:*
- **Frame it:** Define the care economy / unpaid domestic work; anchor with NSO Time Use Survey 2019 (≈15–17% of GDP) and the SC verdict (*Shishu Pal*, 2026), which monetised domestic care at ₹30,000/month.
- **Significance:** Economic (recognising the "invisible subsidy" to the formal economy); legal (fairer compensation jurisprudence under the Motor Vehicles Act); social/gender (dignity, challenges the "housewife" framing, basis for redistribution of care).
- **Challenges:** Valuation/measurement difficulties; unpaid work sits outside the GDP production boundary; entrenched patriarchal norms; thin data; weak care infrastructure (childcare, parental leave, anganwadis).
- **Way forward:** Regular Time Use Surveys, satellite/care accounts, the "Recognise–Reduce–Redistribute" (3R) framework, investment in care infrastructure. Conclude that recognition must translate into fiscal and policy redistribution, not remain symbolic.

## ANSWERS

**Q1 — (c) All three.** France held the 2026 G7 presidency and hosted at Evian-les-Bains; India attended as an invitee/partner (not a member); the seven members are the US, UK, France, Germany, Italy, Japan and Canada.

**Q2 — (c) Both 1 and 2.** In *Shishu Pal v. Surjeet* (2026) the SC created the "Loss of Domestic Care" head and relied on the NSO Time Use Survey 2019 (unpaid domestic work ≈15–17% of GDP).

**Q3 — (c) Both 1 and 2.** The Children's Climate Risk Report 2026 is a UNICEF publication; it found ~92% of Indian children (≈392 million) exposed to extreme heat.

**Q4 — (c) All three.** AD-1/AD-2 are Phase-II BMD interceptors; NASM-MR is a naval anti-ship missile (maiden test); all three tests were from ITR Chandipur, Odisha (10–11 June 2026).

**Q5 — (c) Both 1 and 2.** Phase-I of India's BMD uses PAD (exo-atmospheric, high-altitude) and AAD (endo-atmospheric, low-altitude) interceptors, giving a two-layered intercept across both phases.

**Q6 — (b) The National Statistical Office (under MoSPI).** India's first comprehensive Time Use Survey was conducted in 2019 by the NSO under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation.
