## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Lok Sabha passes the Kerala (Alteration of Name) Bill, 2026 — Kerala becomes "Keralam" — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** Union Home Minister Amit Shah introduced the Kerala (Alteration of Name) Bill, 2026 in the Lok Sabha on 10 August 2026; the House passed it by voice vote on 11 August amid Opposition sloganeering, altering the State's name in the First Schedule from "Kerala" to "Keralam".

**Exam angle:** This is the cleanest live illustration of Article 3 you will get before Prelims 2027 — and the trap UPSC loves is the difference between *consent* and *views*. Parliament's power over State names is unilateral: the President must **refer** the Bill to the State Legislature, but that Legislature's opinion is purely advisory and the Union is free to ignore it. It also sets up the classic Ambedkar formulation of India as an "indestructible Union of destructible States", contrasted with the US federation. Watch the second trap: renaming a **State** needs only ordinary law (Art. 3 + Art. 4), but renaming a **language** in the Eighth Schedule needs a constitutional amendment — which is exactly why Orissa→Odisha in 2011 required *both* the Orissa (Alteration of Name) Act and the Constitution (113th Amendment) Act for "Oriya"→"Odia". Keralam needs no amendment because "Malayalam" is unchanged.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **Article 3** — Parliament may form new States and alter the area, boundaries or **names** of existing States. Two conditions: (i) prior **recommendation of the President** to introduce the Bill; (ii) President must **refer** it to the State Legislature for its views within a specified period. Views are **not binding**; **simple majority** suffices.
- **Article 4** — consequential changes to the **First Schedule** (names/territories of States) and **Fourth Schedule** (Rajya Sabha seat allocation) are **not deemed amendments** under Article 368.
- Kerala Legislative Assembly passed a unanimous resolution on **9 August 2023**, and a **revised resolution on 24 June 2024** after MHA pointed out technical defects (confining the request to Article 3). Union Cabinet approved on **25 February 2026**; the Assembly cleared the referred draft Bill in **July 2026**.
- "Keralam" is the Malayalam-language name of the State. Kerala was formed on **1 November 1956** under the **States Reorganisation Act, 1956** (Travancore-Cochin + Malabar district of Madras State + Kasaragod taluk of South Canara).
- Renaming precedents: Madras→Tamil Nadu (1969), Mysore→Karnataka (1973), Uttaranchal→Uttarakhand and Pondicherry→Puducherry (2006), Orissa→Odisha (2011).

### 2. Parliament passes the Bankers' Books Evidence Bill, 2026 — a 135-year-old colonial statute goes — Economy

**Headline:** The Rajya Sabha cleared the Bankers' Books Evidence Bill, 2026 on **10 August 2026** (Lok Sabha had passed it on 5 August), completing Parliamentary passage of a law that repeals and replaces the **Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891** and gives cloud-stored and digital bank records full evidentiary standing.

**Exam angle:** A quiet but genuinely high-yield item — it sits at the junction of GS3 Economy (banking, digital financial infrastructure) and GS2 (colonial-law repeal, ease of doing business). The larger story is India's evidence law catching up with a banking system where the "book" no longer exists physically: read it alongside the **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023**, which already made electronic records admissible as *primary* evidence. Also a good Mains line on cutting litigation cost — banks no longer have to produce or prove physical ledgers in recovery and fraud proceedings, which matters for **DRTs, IBC proceedings and cyber-fraud prosecutions**.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Bill No. **147 of 2026**; introduced in Lok Sabha **3 August 2026**, passed LS **5 August**, passed RS **10 August 2026**. Repeals the **Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891**.
- **"Bankers' books" redefined** to cover ledgers, day-books, cash-books, account books and all records used in ordinary banking business, kept in **physical, electronic, digital or cloud-based** form — including **back-up and disaster-recovery sites**, onsite or offsite/virtual.
- Electronic/digital records are **admissible, valid and legally enforceable** subject to conditions: the system was in regular use and operating properly, no unauthorised alteration or tampering, and the network/devices were secured against cyber risk.
- **"Legal proceeding" widened** to include court proceedings, **arbitration**, and investigations/inquiries conducted under law for collection of evidence.
- **Retained safeguard:** a bank officer cannot be compelled to produce a banker's book, or appear as a witness to prove its contents, in a proceeding to which the bank is **not a party** — except by order of a court or judge made **for a special cause**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Banking & Financial Sector Reform); cross-file in GS2 (Repeal of colonial laws)

**Static anchor:** **Mrunal — Pillar 3 (Banking / BFSI)**, banking-regulation sub-section. Add a short "evidence & recovery architecture" box, and **cross-link** it to your Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 note on Sections 61–63 (electronic records as primary evidence) so the two laws sit on one page.

### 3. Navy Chief in Mauritius, 10–13 August — the MAHASAGAR frame goes operational — International Relations

**Headline:** Chief of the Naval Staff **Admiral Krishna Swaminathan** began a four-day official visit to **Mauritius on 10 August 2026** (to 13 August), one of his first foreign engagements since taking charge, covering maritime security, defence cooperation, capacity building and military collaboration with the Mauritian leadership and National Coast Guard.

**Exam angle:** Mauritius is the diplomatic address at which India *announces* its Indian Ocean doctrine — SAGAR was unveiled there in **March 2015**, and **MAHASAGAR** was unveiled there in **March 2025**. So a CNS visit to Port Louis is a doctrinal statement, not a courtesy call. Use it to anchor the whole SAGAR→MAHASAGAR evolution: from *"Security and Growth for All in the Region"* (littoral security-first) to a broader development-plus-security compact covering the **Global South**. Pair it with India's role as Mauritius's principal security partner and the **Agalega** infrastructure, and you have a ready 150-word Mains paragraph on India as "first responder" and "preferred security partner" in the IOR.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **MAHASAGAR** = **M**utual and **H**olistic **A**dvancement for **S**ecurity **A**nd **G**rowth **A**cross **R**egions — announced by PM Modi in **Mauritius, March 2025**; successor/expansion of **SAGAR** (announced in Mauritius, **March 2015**).
- India–Mauritius: **CECPA (2021)** was India's **first trade agreement with an African country**; Mauritius has long been among the top sources of FDI inflows into India (Mauritius route + the revised DTAA protocol).
- India built and handed over the **airstrip and jetty at Agalega** (February 2024) — a two-island dependency ~1,100 km north of the Mauritian mainland.
- Mauritius is part of the **Mascarene Islands** (with Réunion and Rodrigues) and a member of **IORA**, the **African Union**, **SADC** and **COMESA**.
- Background: the **UK–Mauritius treaty of 22 May 2025** transferred sovereignty over the **Chagos Archipelago** to Mauritius with a 99-year lease for the UK–US base on **Diego Garcia** — India publicly supported Mauritian sovereignty.

### 4. A new deep-sea shark from off Kollam — *Apristurus drona*, the Arabian Slender Catshark — Environment

**Headline:** Scientists have described **_Apristurus drona_**, a new deep-sea catshark, from the **Kollam slope in the southeastern Arabian Sea off Kerala** — the first species of the genus *Apristurus* recorded from India's south-west coast.

**Exam angle:** UPSC asks "new species in news" almost every year, and this one carries a policy payload: the specimens came out of the **bycatch of deep-sea shrimp trawlers** at 400–650 m. That makes it a case study in the conservation blind spot of India's deep-sea fishery — species are being extracted before they are even described, and the Wildlife (Protection) Act's protection architecture is built around species we already know. Excellent for a Mains line on **data deficiency as a conservation risk**, and for the Blue Economy vs marine biodiversity trade-off under the Deep Ocean Mission.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **_Apristurus drona_**, common name **Arabian Slender Catshark**. Family **Pentanchidae** (deep-sea catsharks), order **Carcharhiniformes** (ground sharks).
- Habitat: **Kollam slope**, continental slope of the **southeastern Arabian Sea**, depth **400–650 m**. Black, slender-bodied, angular pointed snout, striking white eyes.
- **Four specimens** (2 males, 2 females): first encountered in **2022** in deep-sea shrimp trawler bycatch; further specimens collected in 2024 at **Sakthikulangara Fisheries Harbour, Kollam**. Identified through **morphology + DNA barcoding**; described in the journal **_Zootaxa_**.
- Rare, of **no commercial fisheries value**; represents a distinct lineage related to Pacific and New Zealand catsharks.
- Context for the answer sheet: India banned **shark finning** (2015 policy on fins-naturally-attached); whale shark, ganges shark and sawfishes are **Schedule I** under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972; requiem sharks and hammerheads were listed in **CITES Appendix II at CoP19 (Panama, 2022)**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Biodiversity (Marine biodiversity / Species in news)

**Static anchor:** **Shankar IAS Environment — "Marine Ecosystems" and "Species in News"**. Add *A. drona* to your species table with genus/family/depth/location, and **cross-link** to the Deep Ocean Mission page with a one-line caution: *deep-sea mining and deep-sea trawling operate in a zone whose species inventory is still incomplete.*

### 5. 'Vande Mataram' to be sung from the ramparts of the Red Fort for the first time — 150 years — Modern History / Art & Culture

**Headline:** Defence Secretary **Rajesh Kumar Singh** announced on 10 August 2026 that, to mark **150 years of Vande Mataram**, the National Song will for the **first time** be sung from the ramparts of the **Red Fort** on **Independence Day, 15 August 2026** — India's 80th — on the Prime Minister's arrival, before the flag is unfurled.

**Exam angle:** The news hook is thin; the **static payload behind it is very heavy**, and that is precisely why it belongs here. UPSC has repeatedly tested the National Song's *status* — and the single most examinable fact is that the **Constitution does not mention the National Song at all**. Article 51A(a) lists the National Flag and the National Anthem as objects of the fundamental duty to respect; the National Song is absent. So is it from the Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971. Its status rests entirely on **Rajendra Prasad's statement in the Constituent Assembly on 24 January 1950**. Lock that asymmetry down.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Written by **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay** in **1875** (the 150-year commemoration is reckoned from **7 November 1875**), in **Sanskritised Bengali**.
- Included in his novel **_Anandamath_** — serialised in his journal **_Bangadarshan_** in 1881–82, published as a book in **1882**. *Anandamath* is set against the **Sannyasi Rebellion** in Bengal.
- First sung at the **1896 Calcutta session of the Indian National Congress by Rabindranath Tagore**. Became the rallying cry of the **Swadeshi and anti-Partition of Bengal agitation, 1905**.
- **1937**: after Tagore's letter to Nehru objecting to the later stanzas, the **Congress Working Committee** resolved that only the **first two stanzas** be sung — the position that still holds.
- **24 January 1950**: on the last day of the Constituent Assembly, President **Rajendra Prasad** declared that Vande Mataram "shall be honoured equally with Jana Gana Mana and shall have equal status with it." **Jana Gana Mana** was adopted as the National Anthem the same day. **Neither is mentioned in the text of the Constitution as the National Song.**
- 2026 celebration details: an IAF **Mi-17** will carry a "150 Years of Vande Mataram" banner and shower flower petals; **~2,500 NCC cadets and MY Bharat volunteers** will form the words on Gyanpath. Theme: **Yuva Shakti**. The year-long commemoration was inaugurated by the PM on **7 November 2025**, with a commemorative stamp and coin.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Modern Indian History (National Movement) / Art & Culture (National Symbols)

**Static anchor:** **Spectrum, "Nationalism: The Beginnings" / the Swadeshi Movement chapter**, plus your National Symbols one-pager. Add the 1937 CWC two-stanza resolution and the 24 January 1950 declaration, and **cross-link** to Laxmikanth Ch. 8 (Fundamental Duties) with the note: *51A(a) covers the Flag and the Anthem — not the Song.*

### C. Mains-Style Question

**GS2 — Polity & Governance (250 words, 15 marks)**

> *"Article 3 of the Constitution makes India an indestructible Union of destructible States." In the light of recent legislation altering the name of a State, **examine** the constitutional procedure for such an alteration and the role assigned to the State Legislature in it.*

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the proposition.** Open with Article 1 ("Union of States") and Ambedkar's Constituent Assembly explanation — the Union is not the result of an agreement among States, and no State has a right to secede. Article 3 operationalises this: Parliament may unilaterally create, merge, diminish, enlarge or rename a State. Contrast with the US, where State boundaries cannot be altered without State consent (Art. IV, Sec. 3) — hence "indestructible Union of destructible States."

2. **Set out the procedure precisely.** (i) Prior **recommendation of the President** for introduction; (ii) mandatory **reference to the State Legislature** for its views within a specified period; (iii) passage by **simple majority** in both Houses; (iv) Presidential assent; (v) consequential changes to the **First and Fourth Schedules** under **Article 4**, expressly *not* an amendment under Article 368. Illustrate with the Keralam sequence: State Assembly resolutions (2023, revised 2024) → Union Cabinet approval (February 2026) → Presidential reference and Assembly clearance (July 2026) → introduction and passage in Lok Sabha (10–11 August 2026).

3. **Locate the State Legislature honestly.** Its role is **consultative, not consensual** — views are neither binding nor even required to be waited for beyond the specified period. Note the political counterweight: the Union has in practice moved on renaming only where the State itself asked (Odisha, Puducherry, Uttarakhand, now Keralam), so convention supplies the legitimacy that the text withholds. Add the *Berubari Union* (1960) distinction — cession of territory to a foreign State needs a constitutional amendment, not Article 3.

4. **Conclude on balance.** Article 3 reflects the Constitution's overriding concern with **national unity in a linguistically and territorially plural polity**, and the framers' memory of Partition. But the legitimacy of its exercise now rests on a well-settled convention of State initiative — a case of **cooperative federalism supplying the restraint that the text does not.**

## PART C — ANSWERS

**Q1 — (c) 1 and 3 only.** Statement 2 is wrong: the President must *refer* the Bill to the State Legislature for its **views**, but that Legislature's **consent is not required** and its views are not binding. Statements 1 and 3 restate the proviso to Article 3 and Article 4(2) correctly.

**Q2 — (b).** The Bill repeals the Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 1891 and makes electronic, digital and cloud-stored records — including back-up and disaster-recovery copies — admissible, valid and legally enforceable, subject to conditions on system integrity and cyber security. It creates no new tribunal and mandates no physical ledger.

**Q3 — (a).** *Apristurus drona*, the Arabian Slender Catshark (family Pentanchidae, order Carcharhiniformes), was described from four specimens taken at 400–650 m on the Kollam slope in the southeastern Arabian Sea — the first *Apristurus* from India's south-west coast.

**Q4 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct. SAGAR (2015) and MAHASAGAR (2025) were both announced in Mauritius; the India–Mauritius CECPA (2021) was India's first trade agreement with an African country; and Mauritius, with Réunion and Rodrigues, forms the Mascarene Islands.

**Q5 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statement 3 is the trap: **Article 51A(a) mentions only the National Flag and the National Anthem** — the National Song finds no mention in the Constitution. Its status derives from Rajendra Prasad's declaration in the Constituent Assembly on 24 January 1950.

**Q6 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statement 3 is wrong — an Article 3 law is passed by **simple majority**, and Article 4 expressly excludes the consequential Schedule changes from Article 368. Statements 1 and 2 correctly capture the "Union of States" formulation and Parliament's unilateral power over State areas.

**Q7 — (b) Only two.** Pairs 2 and 3 are correct. Pair 1 is wrong: **Agalega** lies in the **south-western Indian Ocean**, roughly 1,100 km north of the Mauritian mainland — not in the Arabian Sea.
