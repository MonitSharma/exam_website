## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. 'Shakti ki Sapt Dhara' — the Red Fort gets a new seven-stream framework for Viksit Bharat — Polity & Governance

**Headline.** From the ramparts of the Red Fort on the 80th Independence Day, PM Modi replaced the older slogan-set with a named framework — **"Shakti ki Sapt Dhara"** (Seven Streams of Strength) — invoking the Rigvedic *Sapta Sindhu* as its rhetorical anchor, and bundled it with three youth announcements.

**Exam angle.** Independence Day speeches are the single most reliable source of scheme names and target years for the next 12 months of Prelims. Note the deliberate framing: the seven *dharas* are not new schemes but an umbrella that re-labels existing missions (PM GatiShakti, Make in India, PLI, Deep Ocean Mission, semiconductor mission) into one narrative — exactly the kind of "which of the following are components of X" question UPSC likes. The Mains value is in the tension the framework papers over: manufacturing-led growth versus a services-heavy economy (see Item 2 today), and target-setting versus delivery capacity.

**Facts to memorize.**

- The **seven dharas**: (i) Manufacturing, (ii) Agriculture & Food Processing, (iii) Technology & Innovation, (iv) **Gati-Shakti** (logistics), (v) **Raksha Shakti** (defence), (vi) **Green & Blue Economy**, (vii) **Soft Power**.
- Stated horizon: *"in the coming 5 to 7 years, what could not be achieved in the last 5 to 7 decades"* — a quotable Mains line, and a usable one for critique.
- Youth announcements: **free online coaching** for competitive exams (a stated cost-of-coaching intervention, exam list not yet notified); **AI training for 1 crore youth**; a **nationwide sports talent-identification programme for the 5–15 age group**.
- Also stated: more **semiconductor plants in 7–8 years**; a target that at least one **Indian bank** enter the Fortune 500; India's move from the **"Fragile Five" (2013 coinage) to fastest-growing major economy in 12 years**.
- Ceremonial firsts: **Vande Mataram** sung from the Red Fort ramparts (covered 11 & 13 Aug — no re-explanation needed).

**Notebook.** GS2 — Governance (government policies & interventions); GS3 — Economy (growth & planning).

**Static anchor.** *Mrunal — Pillar 4 (Sectors of Economy) & Pillar 5 (Infrastructure)*. **Nudge:** open a single page titled "Sapt Dhara" and cross-link each of the seven streams to the scheme you already have notes on (Gati-Shakti → National Logistics Policy; Green & Blue → Deep Ocean Mission + National Green Hydrogen Mission), so you revise the umbrella and the parts together.

### 2. NITI Aayog's professional-services report: India's most-restricted service is **legal** — Reports & Indices

**Headline.** NITI Aayog released the **third report in its Services Thematic Series** (14 August), benchmarking India's regulatory framework in professional services against foreign jurisdictions and proposing a four-pronged roadmap.

**Exam angle.** This is a data-dense, low-competition report — the kind that produces a clean "match the sector to its restrictiveness score" or "which sector is most open" question. It also gives you the counter-argument to the manufacturing-first framing of Item 1: India's comparative advantage is in high-skilled services, and the binding constraint there is *domestic regulation*, not tariffs. Excellent for a Mains paragraph on services-led growth and the demographic dividend.

**Facts to memorize.**

- Professional services = **19.7% of India's services exports in 2023**; grew at **18% CAGR (FY15–FY25)**.
- On the import side: **10% of services imports**, growing at **8% CAGR**.
- **OECD Services Trade Restrictiveness Index (STRI)** scores for India — **Legal 0.865** (most restricted) > **Accounting 0.762** > **Architecture 0.537** > **Engineering 0.155** (most open). Remember the ordering, not just the numbers.
- India has about **1,700 Global Capability Centres (GCCs)** employing **over 1.9 million professionals** — the report's core question is whether GCCs can move up the value chain.
- Framing line: a well-designed regulatory ecosystem strengthens **service exports + cross-border mobility of professionals + ease of doing business**.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Economy (services sector, external sector); GS2 — Governance (regulatory reform).

**Static anchor.** *Mrunal — Pillar 4C (Services Sector) and Pillar 3 (BoP / Invisibles)*. **Nudge:** add a line under "Invisibles" distinguishing **Mode 4 (movement of natural persons)** barriers from tariff barriers — the STRI measures the former, and that distinction is the whole point of this report.

### 3. India and SACU sign Terms of Reference to open PTA negotiations — International Relations

**Headline.** On **12 August 2026** India and the **Southern African Customs Union (SACU)** signed Terms of Reference launching negotiations for a **Preferential Trade Agreement** — reviving talks that had collapsed after five rounds between **2002 and 2010**.

**Exam angle.** SACU is a high-probability Prelims target for two reasons: it is the **world's oldest existing customs union**, and it lets the examiner test the PTA/FTA/customs-union/common-market ladder in one question. Strategically, this would be India's **first major trade agreement with an African regional bloc**, and it sits inside the critical-minerals conversation — Botswana, Namibia and South Africa are relevant to India's mineral-security push (cross-link to the MMDR amendment of 13 Aug).

**Facts to memorize.**

- **SACU**: established **1910**; HQ **Windhoek, Namibia**; five members — **South Africa, Botswana, Namibia, Lesotho, Eswatini** (mnemonic: **B-L-E-N-S**). Common external tariff; revenues pooled and shared via the **SACU Revenue-Sharing Formula**.
- Combined market of roughly **65 million people**.
- India's exports to SACU ≈ **USD 7.5 billion in 2025–26**. India's asks: **automobiles, pharmaceuticals, machinery, electrical equipment**, plus cooperation on **critical minerals**.
- The proposed PTA has **eight chapters**: trade in goods & market access, customs procedures, **rules of origin**, **SPS**, **TBT**, trade remedies, dispute settlement (and general provisions).
- **The ladder** (learn this cold): **PTA** = tariff concessions on an agreed list → **FTA** = tariffs eliminated on substantially all trade → **Customs Union** = FTA + **common external tariff** → **Common Market** = + free movement of factors → **Economic Union** = + policy harmonisation.

**Notebook.** GS2 — IR (bilateral/regional groupings, India & Africa); GS3 — Economy (external sector).

**Static anchor.** *Laxmikanth is silent here — use Mrunal Pillar 3 (International Trade) + your GS2 "India–Africa" note.* **Nudge:** on the trade-blocs page, add SACU to your customs-union list alongside the EU and Mercosur, and tag it "oldest, 1910, B-L-E-N-S".

### 4. Black-necked Crane: 2,950+ dogs sterilised in Ladakh — conservation as waste management — Environment

**Headline.** The **Bombay Natural History Society (BNHS)** has sterilised **over 2,950 free-ranging dogs in Ladakh since 2022**, working with the **Indian Army**, the Animal Husbandry Department and the Department of Forest, Ecology & Environment, to cut predation on **Black-necked Crane** chicks in alpine wetlands.

**Exam angle.** Two exam-worthy layers. First, a clean species-plus-place cluster (crane → Ladakh → Changthang) that UPSC has already mined once (2012). Second, the *causal chain* — unmanaged food waste from settlements and tourism subsidises feral dog populations, which then prey on ground-nesting birds. That is a textbook example of an **indirect anthropogenic driver**, and it makes conservation a solid-waste-management problem rather than a fencing problem. Very usable in a Mains answer on human–wildlife conflict or high-altitude ecosystem fragility.

**Facts to memorize.**

- ***Grus nigricollis***; **IUCN: Vulnerable**; the **only alpine crane species** in the world.
- Global population ~**11,000–12,000**, mostly on the **Tibetan Plateau**; **fewer than 100** in India.
- **State bird of Ladakh** (UT).
- Key Indian breeding habitat: **Changthang Cold Desert Wildlife Sanctuary** — **Hanle, Tso Kar, Nyoma**; also the **Suru Valley** (Kargil). **Tso Kar is a Ramsar site.**
- Driver chain: **food waste → feral dog subsidy → chick predation + nest disturbance**. Response = **sterilisation + waste management + habitat protection**.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Environment (biodiversity, conservation); GS1 — Geography (cold desert ecosystems).

**Static anchor.** *Shankar IAS — "Conservation Efforts" and the "Wetlands / Ramsar" chapter*. **Nudge:** on your Ladakh page, cluster the three high-altitude Ramsar sites — **Tso Kar, Tsomoriri, Hanle (Tso Kar & Tsomoriri are Ramsar)** — with their flagship species (Black-necked Crane, Bar-headed Goose), and add "invasive/feral pressure" as a named threat category next to poaching and habitat loss.

### 5. Three Sanchar Mitras become ITU Generation Connect Youth Envoys (2026–30) — Science & Technology

**Headline.** Three Indian **Sanchar Mitras** — Ankit Kumar Pal (Delhi), Manish Kumar Mandal (Bengaluru) and Susmita Sain (Guwahati) — have been selected in the **third cohort** of the **ITU's Generation Connect Youth Envoys** programme for **2026–2030**, announced around **International Youth Day 2026** (theme: *"Different Contexts, Common Aspirations"*).

**Exam angle.** Small item, high Prelims yield: it links a **DoT scheme** to a **UN specialised agency**, and UPSC reliably asks "which ministry implements X" and "X is a programme of which organisation". The ITU connection is the trap — the ITU is the **oldest UN specialised agency (1865, as the International Telegraph Union)**, headquartered in **Geneva**, and is *not* under UNESCO or the UNDP. Also a ready-made example for a Governance answer on digital literacy and cyber-fraud prevention as a demand-side intervention.

**Facts to memorize.**

- **Sanchar Mitra Scheme** — implemented by the **Department of Telecommunications (DoT), Ministry of Communications**, through its **Licensed Service Area (LSA) field offices**; live in **all States/UTs** with **200+ participating institutions**.
- Sanchar Mitras are **students of technical institutions** in telecom, electronics, computer science, cybersecurity and allied fields; they do outreach on **telecom/cyber-fraud prevention, mobile security and responsible telecom usage**.
- **ITU**: UN specialised agency for ICTs; founded **1865**; HQ **Geneva**; **Generation Connect Youth Envoys (GCYE)** is its flagship youth-participation track. This is the **third cohort**; India's envoys serve **2026–2030**.
- Connects upward to Item 1's **AI training for 1 crore youth** — same demographic-dividend logic, different instrument.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Sci-Tech (ICT, digital governance); GS2 — Governance (international organisations, schemes).

**Static anchor.** *Your "UN system & specialised agencies" table in GS2 + the Telecom/IT section of your Sci-Tech notes.* **Nudge:** in the specialised-agencies table, tag ITU as "oldest, 1865, Geneva, ICT/spectrum" and put **WSIS** and **GCYE** in the adjacent programmes column — one row, three likely questions.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Economy, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **"India's next growth frontier lies less in cutting tariffs abroad than in dismantling regulatory barriers at home."** Examine this proposition in the light of recent assessments of India's professional services sector.

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points):**

1. **Set up the paradox.** Services contribute the largest share of GVA and professional services grew at 18% CAGR (FY15–FY25) to 19.7% of services exports — yet India ranks among the most restricted jurisdictions on the OECD STRI for legal (0.865) and accounting (0.762) services. Growth has happened *despite*, not because of, the regulatory regime.
2. **Diagnose the barriers as domestic, not external.** Entry restrictions on foreign professionals and firms, reciprocity conditions, bars on multidisciplinary practice and advertising, licensing fragmentation across State-level councils, and weak mutual-recognition agreements. These are **Mode 3 and Mode 4** constraints under GATS — tariff negotiation cannot touch them.
3. **Note the cost.** Deters GCCs (1,700 centres, 1.9 million professionals) from moving up the value chain from cost-arbitrage delivery to high-value advisory; limits cross-border mobility of Indian professionals; weakens India's negotiating hand in services chapters of FTAs, where India is the *demandeur* on Mode 4 while remaining closed on Mode 3.
4. **Balance and conclude.** The counter-case is real — regulation protects consumers, professional standards and domestic practitioners, and full liberalisation without capacity-building risks capture by global firms. The way forward is calibrated: sector-specific roadmaps, mutual recognition agreements, a unified regulatory architecture, and sequencing openness with domestic upskilling — so that India converts a demographic peak into a services export peak rather than either extreme.

### D. PYQ MIRROR

**Theme 1 — Trade agreements and blocs (Items 2 & 3).**
UPSC has repeatedly tested the *identity* of a named trade arrangement rather than its content. **Prelims 2017, Q82:** *"'Broad-based Trade and Investment Agreement (BTIA)' is sometimes seen in the news in the context of negotiations held between India and — (a) European Union (b) Gulf Cooperation Council (c) Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development (d) Shanghai Cooperation Organization."* **Answer: (a) European Union.** *(Verified.)* Expect the SACU story to be asked in exactly this shape — "India signed Terms of Reference for a preferential trade agreement with which grouping?" — so fix **SACU = five Southern African states, 1910, Windhoek** rather than memorising the eight chapters.

**Theme 2 — Species distribution in India (Item 4).**
**Prelims 2012:** *"Consider the following: 1. Black-necked crane 2. Cheetah 3. Flying squirrel 4. Snow leopard. Which of the above are naturally found in India?"* **Answer: 1, 3 and 4 only.** *(Verified.)* The pattern is "which of these occur naturally in India" — so for the Black-necked Crane the examinable facts are **occurrence (Ladakh/Arunachal), status (Vulnerable), and habitat type (high-altitude wetland)**, not population figures.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (c) Jal Shakti.** The seven streams are manufacturing, agriculture & food processing, technology & innovation, Gati-Shakti, Raksha Shakti, green & blue economy, and soft power. Jal Shakti is a ministry, not one of the seven *dharas*.

**Q2 — (b) Legal > Accounting > Architecture > Engineering.** Per the OECD STRI scores cited in the report: legal 0.865, accounting 0.762, architecture 0.537, engineering 0.155 — engineering is the most open of the four.

**Q3 — (c) 1 and 3 only.** SACU (est. 1910, HQ Windhoek) is the world's oldest existing customs union and operates a common external tariff with a revenue-sharing formula. Statement 2 is wrong: **Zimbabwe is not a member**; the five members are South Africa, Botswana, Namibia, Lesotho and Eswatini.

**Q4 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** The Black-necked Crane is the only alpine crane species, and its Indian breeding stronghold is the Changthang Cold Desert (Hanle, Tso Kar, Nyoma). Statement 2 is wrong: IUCN status is **Vulnerable**, not Endangered.

**Q5 — (b) Department of Telecommunications; International Telecommunication Union.** Sanchar Mitra is a DoT (Ministry of Communications) youth digital-outreach scheme run through LSA field offices; three Sanchar Mitras were selected as ITU Generation Connect Youth Envoys for 2026–30.

**Q6 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** Statement 1 describes a **free trade area**, not a PTA — a PTA only grants tariff concessions on an agreed list of products. Statements 2 and 3 correctly state the customs-union and common-market conditions.

**Q7 — (b) Only two.** Tso Kar (Ladakh) and Chandra Taal (Himachal Pradesh) are correctly matched. **Tsomoriri is in Ladakh, not Sikkim** — all three are Ramsar sites.
