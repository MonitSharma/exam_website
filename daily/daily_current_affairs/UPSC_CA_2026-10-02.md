### 1. West Bengal's first tiger reintroduction: a Valmiki tigress goes to Buxa — Environment
**Exam angle:** Buxa (Alipurduar, WB) is a tiger reserve where the 2018 census recorded zero tigers. A three-year-old tigress from Valmiki Tiger Reserve (Bihar) is being released in a 5-acre soft-release enclosure, timed with Wildlife Week (1–7 Oct). It is an inter-state translocation under the "source–sink" logic used earlier for Satkosia and Rajaji. The release was overseen by Union Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav (he reviewed the site on 1 Oct).
**Memorize:** Buxa lies between Assam's Manas and a Bhutanese sanctuary (a transboundary corridor). It is one of WB's two tiger reserves (the other is Sundarbans). Officials say it can hold 10–15 tigers. Tigress source: Valmiki, Bihar. Wildlife Week: 1–7 October.

### 2. Manufacturing PMI hits a seven-month high of 55.1 in September — Economy
**Exam angle:** The final HSBC India Manufacturing PMI rose to 55.1 (from 52.8 in August). Flash estimate was 55.7, so the final was revised down. Drivers: new orders, output, fastest hiring since May, and export orders from Brazil, Europe, UAE and the US. Input costs rose (electronic components, pharma, steel), but inflation stayed below its long-run average. This is a high-frequency indicator; do not confuse it with IIP.
**Memorize:** PMI > 50 = expansion, < 50 = contraction. Compiled by S&P Global for HSBC (survey-based, diffusion index). The same survey cycle put the flash composite at 56.5 and flash services at 55.8 (23 Sep).
**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (Growth/Industry indicators).
**Static anchor:** Ramesh Singh *Indian Economy* — Industry & Indicators; Mrunal *Economy* — National income & indices. **Nudge:** add a "PMI vs IIP vs Core Index" comparison box (source, frequency, base, coverage).

### 3. Rajasthan High Court warns Jaipur's Walled City could lose World Heritage status — Art & Culture
**Exam angle:** In a suo motu case, the Rajasthan HC (order of 26 Sep 2026) said the Walled City faces a "serious risk" if remedial action is not taken by December 2026. It ordered a survey of unauthorised construction (two weeks), an affidavit on safeguards (four weeks), and a phase-wise action plan, and asked why officials should not face action. UNESCO has flagged redevelopment in the core and buffer zones; the conservation report is due in December 2026 and review is expected at the 2027 World Heritage Committee session (per Outlook Traveller, March 2026).
**Memorize:** Jaipur Walled City inscribed 2019 (founded 1727). The World Heritage Committee decides on the List of World Heritage in Danger and on deletion. Tools: Heritage Impact Assessment, buffer zone, Reactive Monitoring. Only a handful of sites have ever been delisted globally.
**Notebook:** GS1 Art & Culture (Heritage conservation); GS2 Polity (judicial activism).
**Static anchor:** Nitin Singhania *Art & Culture* — UNESCO sites; NCERT *Class 11 An Introduction to Indian Art*. **Nudge:** add Jaipur to a "WH sites under UNESCO scrutiny" list with Hampi and Ahmedabad as comparators; cross-link to the Ancient Monuments Act (AMASR Act 1958/2010).

### Mains Question
**Critically examine the challenges of reconciling urban development with the conservation of heritage in India's World Heritage cities, with reference to recent concerns over the Walled City of Jaipur. (150 words, 10 marks, GS1)**
*Model-answer skeleton:*
1. **Intro:** the Walled City (2019) is under UNESCO and HC scrutiny over construction in core/buffer zones; this exposes the tension between development and Outstanding Universal Value.
2. **Challenges:** metro/infrastructure inside the core and buffer zone; unauthorised construction and haveli loss; a weak Heritage Cell and enforcement; no mandatory Heritage Impact Assessment; livelihood and housing needs of residents.
3. **Reconciliation:** mandatory HIAs; a binding management plan; adaptive reuse of havelis; community and funding incentives; coordination between ASI, urban local bodies and state.
4. **Way forward / conclusion:** timely compliance with UNESCO's December deadline; judicial oversight plus institutional capacity; heritage as an economic asset (tourism).
