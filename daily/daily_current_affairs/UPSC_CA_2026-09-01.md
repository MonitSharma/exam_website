### 1. Q1 FY27 GDP comes in at 7.8% — the first full quarter on the new 2022-23 base — Economy

**Headline.** MoSPI released the first-quarter estimates for 2026-27 on **31 August 2026**: real GDP grew **7.8% year-on-year** in April–June 2026, against **6.9%** in Q1 FY26 and a revised **8.6%** in Q4 FY26 — comfortably above the RBI's ~7% projection and market consensus of ~7.1%.

**Exam angle.** Three things make this more than a headline number. First, **real GVA (8.2%) grew faster than real GDP (7.8%)** — the wedge is net product taxes, so a squeeze on indirect-tax collections or a rise in subsidies mechanically drags GDP below GVA. UPSC has asked the GDP-vs-GVA relationship directly, and this quarter gives you a live illustration. Second, the **deflator is now very small**: nominal GDP grew ~10.3% against real 7.8%, implying an implicit deflator of only ~2.3% — consistent with soft WPI and a benign price environment, and the reason nominal-GDP-linked ratios (fiscal deficit/GDP, debt/GDP, tax buoyancy) behave the way they do this year. Third, this is estimated on the **new 2022-23 base year series**, so any comparison with pre-revision quarterly numbers you have memorised from older notes is invalid — rebuild that page.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **Real GDP growth: 7.8%** in Q1 FY27 (Apr–Jun 2026), vs **6.9%** in Q1 FY26 and a revised **8.6%** in Q4 FY26.
- **Real GDP level: ₹81.36 lakh crore**, against ₹75.46 lakh crore in Q1 FY26.
- **Nominal GDP: ₹88.27 lakh crore**, against ₹80.00 lakh crore — growth of **~10%**.
- **Real GVA: 8.2%**, at ₹73.82 lakh crore vs ₹68.21 lakh crore. **Nominal GVA: 11.5%**, at ₹80.53 lakh crore vs ₹72.24 lakh crore.
- Series basis: the **new National Accounts series with base year 2022-23** (replacing 2011-12).
- RBI's projection for the quarter was **~7%**; the print beat it.
- Static hook you must be able to write from memory: **GVA at basic prices + product taxes − product subsidies = GDP at market prices.**

### 2. Semicon 2.0 notified — ₹1,27,500 crore, six pillars, and a shift from fabs to the whole stack — Science & Technology

**Headline.** MeitY has **notified Semicon 2.0** (India Semiconductor Mission 2.0), an outlay of **₹1,27,500 crore** approved by the Union Cabinet in **July 2026**, restructuring support around **six pillars** and **ten categories** — and extending incentives beyond fabrication to chip design and indigenous IP, equipment and materials, advanced packaging, R&D, and talent.

**Exam angle.** The examinable shift is **from a capex subsidy for fabs to an ecosystem programme**. Semicon 1.0 (₹76,000 crore) was essentially a fiscal-support scheme to land fabs and ATMP units; Semicon 2.0 explicitly funds the parts of the value chain India does *not* have — semiconductor equipment, materials, EDA tooling for startups, and IP creation. That maps onto a standard GS3 argument: India is strong at chip *design services* (a large share of global design engineers work in India) but owns almost no design *IP*, and has no equipment or materials base at all, which is where the real chokepoints sit. The second angle is **supply-chain resilience and technology geopolitics** — export controls on lithography and advanced nodes make "assured access" a strategic, not commercial, question. Pair with critical minerals and the "critical and emerging technologies" tracks in India–US/India–EU dialogues.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **Outlay ₹1,27,500 crore**; Cabinet approval **July 2026**; notified end-August 2026; nodal ministry **MeitY**, implementing agency the **India Semiconductor Mission (ISM)**.
- Predecessor: **Semicon India Programme ("Semicon 1.0"), ₹76,000 crore**, approved 2021.
- **Six pillars:** (i) wafer fabrication, (ii) ATMP/OSAT — assembly, testing, marking and packaging, (iii) semiconductor equipment and materials, (iv) chip design and indigenous IP, (v) R&D and innovation, (vi) talent and resilient supply chains. Ten categories sit under these.
- **Capex support rates:** **40%** for silicon wafer fabs; **35%** for compound semiconductor, photonics, sensor/MEMS and display fabs; **35%** for advanced packaging; **25%** for conventional packaging.
- **Design startups/MSMEs:** Design Infrastructure Support — access to national **EDA** tools and **multi-project wafer (MPW)** services; seed funding up to **₹15 crore**, capped at **50%** of project cost.
- Eligibility widened from large firms to **startups, MSMEs, research bodies and training institutions**.
- **Talent:** the original target of 85,000 semiconductor engineers over ten years was met in about four; the new target is **1 lakh** more.
- Under Semicon 1.0, **10 projects worth roughly ₹1.60 lakh crore** had been approved across **six states** by December 2025.
- Glossary for Prelims: **OSAT** = Outsourced Semiconductor Assembly and Test; **ATMP** = Assembly, Testing, Marking and Packaging; **EDA** = Electronic Design Automation; **MPW** = multi-project wafer (several designs share one wafer to cut prototyping cost).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (indigenisation of technology, developing new technology) and Economy (industrial policy).

**Static anchor:** *Shankar/standard Sci-Tech notes* — **Semiconductors & Electronics chapter**; and *Mrunal* **Pillar 4A — industrial policy**. *Nudge: on your Semicon page replace the single ₹76,000-crore line with a two-row table — Semicon 1.0 (fabs + ATMP, capex subsidy) vs Semicon 2.0 (six pillars, whole stack) — and add one cross-link each to the PLI/MPMS page, the Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme, and the National Quantum Mission / IndiaAI institutional map you built on 30 August.*

### 3. V-Dem puts India's Electoral Democracy Index at 0.38 — its lowest since 1975 — Reports & Indices

**Headline.** The **V-Dem Institute's** Electoral Democracy Index for 2025 scores India at **0.38**, ranking it **106th of 179 countries** — below the score recorded in **1975**, the year the Emergency was proclaimed. V-Dem continues to classify India as an **"electoral autocracy"**, a category it has placed India in since 2017, and as an **"autocratising"** country.

**Exam angle.** For Prelims, treat this as a **"who publishes what"** item: V-Dem is a **Varieties of Democracy** research project hosted at the **University of Gothenburg, Sweden** — do not confuse it with the EIU's *Democracy Index* (The Economist Intelligence Unit) or Freedom House's *Freedom in the World*. For Mains, this is the cleanest available prompt for the **"how should India respond to global governance rankings?"** question, which UPSC likes because it forces you to argue both sides: the methodological critique (**expert-coded perception scores**, small country-expert panels, aggregation of qualitatively different indicators into a single number, no correction for the fact that a more assertive free press *generates* more reported incidents) against the substantive point that **institutional-autonomy indicators are internally consistent across independent indices**. The additional hook this year is the **UN CERD** review — the first since 2007 — which flagged voter exclusion during the Special Intensive Revision of electoral rolls. That lets you connect an index story to a treaty-body story to a live domestic controversy.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **V-Dem** = Varieties of Democracy; hosted at the **University of Gothenburg, Sweden**; measures democracy across **five dimensions — electoral, liberal, participatory, egalitarian, deliberative**.
- India's **Electoral Democracy Index: 0.38 (2025)**; **rank 106/179**; **lowest since 1975**. Denmark tops the index (~0.9).
- India has been classified an **"electoral autocracy"** since **2017**; V-Dem describes the trajectory as **autocratisation**, gradual since about 2009.
- Sub-indicators at multi-decade lows: **Election Management Body (EMB) autonomy**, **judicial accountability and corruption**, **impartial public administration**, **freedom of academic and cultural expression**.
- On the separate **Liberal Democracy Index**, India is placed around **100th of 179**.
- **UN CERD** (Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination) — the treaty body under the **International Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Racial Discrimination (ICERD, 1965)**, which India ratified in **1968** — held its **first review of India since 2007** and raised concerns about voter exclusion in the **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** of electoral rolls.
- Government of India's standing position on such indices: methodology relies on **subjective expert perception**, small samples, and ignores the scale and complexity of Indian electoral administration.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Election Commission, Fundamental Rights) and Governance (transparency and accountability); cross-tag GS2 IR for the treaty-body angle.

**Static anchor:** **Laxmikanth — "Election Commission"** chapter, and your **Reports & Indices** master sheet. *Nudge: your Reports & Indices sheet almost certainly has EIU Democracy Index and Freedom House but not V-Dem — add a row (publisher: V-Dem Institute, University of Gothenburg; five dimensions; India 2025: EDI 0.38, rank 106; classified electoral autocracy since 2017) and a one-line "how to attack an index in Mains" template you can reuse for Global Hunger Index, Press Freedom Index and World Happiness Report.*

### 4. VB-G RAM G's first two months — rural person-days roughly halve, and the right-to-work question returns — Polity & Governance

**Headline.** The **Viksit Bharat – Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) Act, 2025 ("VB-G RAM G")** replaced MGNREGA from **1 July 2026**. Data for its first months show a sharp contraction: **7.67 crore person-days in July 2026, down 49.94%** from 15.33 crore in July 2025, with beneficiary households down **51.45%** (68.94 lakh vs 1.42 crore). Measured against the five-year July–August average, the fall is steeper still — around **68%** on person-days. The Centre attributes part of the drop to the **monsoon sowing pause**; critics point to the Act's structural design.

**Exam angle.** This is the highest-value GS2 item available right now because it is simultaneously a **welfare-scheme story, a constitutional-doctrine story, and a fiscal-federalism story**. The constitutional core: the right to work sits in **Article 41 (DPSP)** — a directive, not a fundamental right, because the Constituent Assembly (K. T. Shah pressed for a fundamental right; **Ambedkar** argued it was fiscally unenforceable) deliberately made it aspirational. MGNREGA was the statute that gave that aspiration teeth. The argument now being made is the **doctrine of non-retrogression** — that the State cannot roll back a level of realisation already achieved — read together with **Olga Tellis (1985)**, where livelihood was read into Article 21, and **Sanjit Roy (1983)**, where paying below minimum wages on famine-relief work was held to violate Article 23. The fiscal-federalism layer: if the Union caps its fund release, the residual burden falls on States already bound by **FRBM** borrowing limits — so a design change at the Centre becomes a State fiscal-capacity problem. Write it as: *right → statute → repeal → doctrine → federal transmission.*

**Facts to memorise.**

- **VB-G RAM G Bill, 2025** introduced in **Lok Sabha on 16 December 2025**; the Act commenced in all rural areas from **1 July 2026**; **MGNREGA, 2005 stands repealed** from the same date.
- Guarantee: **125 days** of unskilled manual work per rural household per financial year (MGNREGA guaranteed 100); **unemployment allowance** if work is not provided within **15 days**.
- Works are restricted to **four thematic domains**: (i) water security, (ii) rural infrastructure, (iii) livelihood-related infrastructure, (iv) mitigation of extreme weather events.
- **July 2026 outcome: 7.67 crore person-days (−49.94% y-o-y); 68.94 lakh households (−51.45%).** Jul–Aug 2026 combined: about **14.94 crore person-days and 1.39 crore households**, against a five-year Jul–Aug average of roughly 44 crore person-days and 3.44 crore households — a fall of about **68%**.
- Average wage cited for 2026-27: about **₹282.5/day** (against ~₹210/day in 2021-22) — a nominal rise whose real value is contested.
- The **2009 delinking of MGNREGA wages from the Minimum Wages Act, 1948** is the origin of the persistent gap between statutory agricultural minimum wages and programme wages; VB-G RAM G does not restore the link.
- Constitutional anchors: **Article 41** (right to work, education, public assistance) and **Articles 39, 42, 43** (adequate livelihood, just conditions, living wage) — all DPSPs; **Article 21** via *Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation (1985)*; **Article 23** via *Sanjit Roy v. State of Rajasthan (1983)*.
- Criticisms of the new design: an **arbitrary fund cap** replacing MGNREGA's demand-driven funding obligation; wages not linked to minimum wages; a **denotification** provision that lets areas be taken out of coverage; and the reclassification risk for rural women, for whom programme wages were a distinct source of independent income.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (DPSPs, Articles 36–51) and Social Justice (welfare schemes, mechanisms for the vulnerable); cross-tag GS3 Economy (employment, inclusive growth) and GS1 Society (rural women's work participation).

**Static anchor:** **Laxmikanth — "Directive Principles of State Policy"** chapter, and your **welfare-schemes** register. *Nudge: your scheme register still says "MGNREGA — 100 days". Overwrite the entry: VB-G RAM G Act, 2025 → 125 days, four thematic domains, 15-day allowance trigger, in force 1 July 2026, MGNREGA repealed. On the DPSP page, add a three-line box on the doctrine of non-retrogression with Olga Tellis and Sanjit Roy as the case anchors — it is reusable for any "rolling back a welfare right" question.*

### 5. Rural solid waste — Majuli and Tawang show what the SWM Rules 2026 look like on the ground — Environment

**Headline.** With the **Solid Waste Management Rules, 2026** in force since **1 April 2026** and the Supreme Court pressing States on enforcement, attention has turned to the segment the Rules least reach: **rural waste**, which is essentially unmeasured. Two community-led models — **Majuli (Assam)** and **Tawang/Zemithang (Arunachal Pradesh)** — now offer documented, costed alternatives to door-to-door collection.

**Exam angle.** The examinable insight is economic, not moral: **primary collection and transport are the costliest steps in solid waste management — roughly half of total cost even in cities**, and far higher where settlements are scattered. Both models solve that by *shifting collection cost onto households* (a monthly community collection day instead of daily door-to-door) and recovering value through deep source segregation. That is a textbook decentralisation argument you can run through **Panchayati Raj institutions (11th Schedule, Entry 6 — "public health, sanitation, conservancy and solid waste management")**, funded partly by **Extended Producer Responsibility** flows from packaging producers. Note the honest limitation for a balanced Mains conclusion: both models are viable only because someone else absorbs long-haul transport cost — ₹45,000 a truckload from Arunachal to a recycler in Assam is not something a Gram Panchayat budget can carry.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **SWM Rules, 2026** — notified by **MoEFCC**, in full effect from **1 April 2026**, replacing the SWM Rules, 2016. Key changes: **four-stream segregation at source**, expanded obligations on **bulk waste generators (BWGs)**, mandatory digital monitoring through centralised portals, and stronger decentralised-management duties.
- **Supreme Court enforcement (2026):** directed **District Collectors** to identify all BWGs within six weeks, set up a dedicated **Special Cell** (including SPCB officials) in every district to monitor implementation, and designated councillors, mayors, chairpersons and ward members as lead facilitators for source-segregation awareness.
- **Scale:** India generates about **1.7 lakh tonnes of solid waste per day** (CPCB); roughly **22%** reaches landfills and about **17%** is unaccounted for. **UNEP:** of 2.7 billion people worldwide without waste collection, about **2 billion are rural**.
- **Majuli (Assam)** — world's largest river island, ~1.67 lakh people, annually flooded; waste historically dumped in the Brahmaputra. **Central Material Collection Facilities (CMCFs)** built in 19 of 20 panchayats plus mini-facilities in ~160 villages (2017–22) lay unused until an NGO-district partnership added logistics, worker training and market linkage in 2024. First waste boat-crossing of the Brahmaputra: **April 2025**. **82.4 tonnes** kept out of the river since 2024; **16.78 tonnes** ferried between April 2025 and May 2026, earning **₹1.47 lakh**.
- **Tawang / Zemithang (Arunachal Pradesh)** — altitude 6,900–8,000 ft, settlements too scattered for door-to-door collection. **"Swachchata Divas"**: a monthly community collection day; households segregate into **22 categories** at home, **Material Recovery Facilities** sort into **35**. Governance runs through a circle-level committee (Circle Officer, with a Lama as secretary) and village committees (Gaon Bura and treasurer), meeting quarterly and levying fines; households pay **₹50/month**, shops **₹100/month**. Over **20 tonnes** sold to recyclers for **₹3.53 lakh**; transport costs **₹45,000 per truckload** to Tezpur, Assam.
- Key terms: **EPR (Extended Producer Responsibility)**; **MRF (Material Recovery Facility)**; **bulk waste generator**; **11th Schedule, Entry 6** for the Panchayat mandate.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (environmental pollution and degradation; conservation); cross-tag GS2 Governance (local bodies, Panchayati Raj).

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS* — **"Waste Management"** chapter. *Nudge: your Shankar waste chapter is almost certainly still built around the SWM Rules 2016. Add a "Rules 2016 → Rules 2026" delta box (four-stream segregation, BWG expansion, District Collector enforcement, digital monitoring) and a one-line rural case-study pair — Majuli (river island, boat transfer) and Zemithang (mountain, monthly Swachchata Divas) — so you have concrete examples instead of generic ones. Cross-link to your Panchayati Raj page at 11th Schedule Entry 6.*

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Polity & Social Justice, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **The replacement of the Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005 with the Viksit Bharat – Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) Act, 2025 has revived an old constitutional question about the enforceability of the right to work. Critically examine the constitutional and fiscal implications of this transition.**

**Model-answer skeleton (four points):**

1. **Frame the constitutional status precisely.** The right to work is **Article 41**, a Directive Principle — non-justiciable by design. The Constituent Assembly made that choice consciously: K. T. Shah pressed for a fundamental right; Ambedkar held it fiscally unenforceable in a newly independent State. MGNREGA's significance was that it converted an aspirational directive into a **justiciable statutory entitlement** — so the question is not whether Article 41 is enforceable, but what happens when the statute that operationalised it is replaced.

2. **State the doctrinal argument on both sides.** Against the transition: the **doctrine of non-retrogression** — a level of rights realisation once achieved should not be rolled back; read with ***Olga Tellis (1985)*** (livelihood implied in Article 21) and ***Sanjit Roy (1983)*** (sub-minimum wages on public works offend Article 23). For the transition: Parliament is entitled to redesign a welfare statute; the new Act **raises the guarantee from 100 to 125 days**, retains the **15-day unemployment-allowance trigger**, and channels works into four outcome-oriented domains — arguably improving asset quality rather than diluting the right.

3. **Bring in the evidence, and be fair to it.** July 2026 person-days fell **49.94%** year-on-year (7.67 crore against 15.33 crore) and participating households **51.45%**. The Government attributes this to the monsoon sowing pause and transition friction; critics point to the **fund cap replacing demand-driven funding**, the absence of a **minimum-wage linkage**, and the **denotification** provision that permits withdrawal of coverage. Acknowledge that a single quarter cannot settle the question, then note the direction of the design change is what makes the number worrying.

4. **Close on fiscal federalism and a way forward.** A Union-side cap transmits the residual burden to States already constrained by **FRBM** limits, so a central design choice becomes a State fiscal-capacity problem — with the sharpest incidence on rural women, for whom programme wages were often the only independently earned income. Way forward: restore **demand-driven funding with a statutory funding floor**; **re-link wages to the Minimum Wages Act, 1948** with periodic indexation; make denotification reviewable and evidence-based; and publish disaggregated MIS data (by district, gender and social group) so the guarantee remains auditable.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 2 only.** Real GDP grew 7.8% in Q1 FY27 against 6.9% in Q1 FY26, so statement 1 is correct. Real GVA grew 8.2%, faster than real GDP's 7.8% — the wedge being net product taxes — so statement 2 is correct. Statement 3 is wrong: the estimates use the **new series with base year 2022-23**, not 2011-12.

**Q2 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** Semicon 2.0's outlay of ₹1,27,500 crore exceeds Semicon 1.0's ₹76,000 crore (statement 1 correct). Statement 2 is wrong — Semicon 2.0 is deliberately broader, covering six pillars including ATMP/OSAT and advanced packaging (35%) and conventional packaging (25%), alongside equipment and materials, design, R&D and talent. Statement 3 is correct: design startups and MSMEs get seed funding up to ₹15 crore (capped at 50% of project cost) plus national EDA tool access and multi-project wafer services.

**Q3 — (c) The V-Dem Institute, University of Gothenburg.** V-Dem (Varieties of Democracy) publishes the Electoral Democracy Index; India scored 0.38 in 2025, ranked 106/179 — its lowest since 1975. The EIU publishes the *Democracy Index*, Freedom House publishes *Freedom in the World*, and the IPU publishes parliamentary data — all distinct products, and a standard Prelims confusion set.

**Q4 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct. The Act commenced in rural areas on 1 July 2026 with MGNREGA repealed from that date; it guarantees 125 days of unskilled manual work per rural household per financial year (up from 100); and permissible works are confined to four domains — water security, rural infrastructure, livelihood-related infrastructure, and mitigation of extreme weather events.

**Q5 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct. The SWM Rules 2026 were notified by MoEFCC and replaced the 2016 Rules from 1 April 2026; they mandate four-stream source segregation; and 2026 judicial directions required District Collectors to identify bulk waste generators and set up dedicated Special Cells, including State Pollution Control Board officials, to monitor compliance.

**Q6 — (a) 1, 2 and 3 only.** Adequate means of livelihood (**Article 39(a)**), the right to work, education and public assistance (**Article 41**) and a living wage with decent conditions of work (**Article 43**) were all in the original Constitution. Worker participation in management of industries is **Article 43A**, inserted by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976** — hence 4 is excluded.

**Q7 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are correct and all three are standard national-accounts identities: GDP at market prices = GVA at basic prices + product taxes − product subsidies; real GDP uses base-year constant prices while nominal GDP uses current prices; and quarterly GDP estimates are released by MoSPI (through the National Statistical Office).
