### 1. India–Russia: the MEA Consultative Committee lays out connectivity, energy and trade — International Relations
**Exam angle.** The Parliamentary Consultative Committee on External Affairs reviewed ties with Russia on 3 Oct 2026. India repeated that it will help "bring the war to an end", building on PM Modi's line to Putin at the SCO summit in Bishkek (31 Aug): "endless war must now give way to an end of war". The static-rich part is connectivity: the Chennai–Vladivostok corridor, the INSTC and the Northern Sea Route. New sub-development only; the BRICS and SCO coverage from earlier this month is not repeated.

- Progress reported toward an India–Eurasian Economic Union FTA.
- Ukraine's FM Sybiha called India's proposal "the most comprehensive" of four peace proposals (reported by ANI).

### 2. First release of captive-bred Great Indian Bustards: two males enter the wild in Jaisalmer — Environment
**Exam angle.** Union Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav and the Wildlife Institute of India released two captive-bred male GIBs near Jaisalmer on 4 Oct 2026, after rewilding training. It is the first test of whether ex situ breeding can feed in situ recovery of a Critically Endangered, power-line-vulnerable species. Mains hook: limits of captive breeding, habitat and infrastructure conflict (overhead lines).
**Memorise.**
- Two males, tagged for monitoring, released into a restricted-to-humans enclosure.
- Run by WII with the Rajasthan Forest Department; breeding centres at Sam and Ramdevra (Jaisalmer).
- Globally, captive-bred bustard survival is about 48%, ANI reports; about 98 chicks bred so far.
- GIB: IUCN Critically Endangered; Schedule I, WPA 1972; Rajasthan's state bird; Desert National Park and Thar are its core range.
- Supreme Court (2021) directions on power lines; priority area about 14,013 sq km (as reported).
- Flag: sources differ on how many birds were planned (10 vs 2); only the two actually released are used here.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment.
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS "Biodiversity: species in news"; Mrunal Environment pillar, "Conservation: in situ vs ex situ". *Nudge:* add a GIB row to the species card; cross-link to the Buxa tigress item (rewilding vs reintroduction).

### 3. Poompuhar's seabed gives up a Mediterranean wine jar — Ancient History / Art & Culture
**Exam angle.** The third phase of underwater exploration at Poompuhar (Kaveripoompattinam), by the Tamil Nadu State Department of Archaeology with the Indian Maritime University, ended on 22 Sept 2026. The finds corroborate Sangam-age maritime trade with West Asia and the Mediterranean and show a submerged city. A natural fit for GS1 (Sangam Age, Indo-Roman trade) and Prelims "ports" questions.
**Memorise.**
- A West Asian torpedo-jar fragment (30–35 cm), used to carry wine and olive oil.
- Over 250 ceramic pieces, four terracotta ring wells; read as a buried palaeochannel swallowed by the sea (about 4th–5th century CE).
- Tools: side-scan sonar, sub-bottom profiler, multibeam, ROVs.
- Poompuhar = Chola port at the Cauvery mouth; other sites to pair: Arikamedu, Alagankulam, Muziris/Pattanam.

**Notebook:** GS1 Ancient History and Art & Culture.
**Static anchor:** NCERT Old (R.S. Sharma) "Sangam age"; Nitin Singhania "Ancient trade". *Nudge:* update your ports table (Poompuhar–Chola, Korkai–Pandya, Muziris and Tondi–Chera) with this find.

### 4. Status of Policing in India Report 2026: cybercrime victims and the bribe — Reports & Indices
**Exam angle.** The Lokniti-CSDS and Common Cause SPIR 2026 (8,306 respondents, 16 States) studied how police handle cybercrime. It reports that those who paid bribes recovered money more often than those who did not. This is a ready Mains example of weak FIR registration and bank–police coordination, and of how the burden falls on women, the rural and the poor.
**Memorise.**
- 49% of victims filed police complaints; 39% never complained.
- 27% of those complaining said they paid a bribe (women 35% vs men 22%; poor 51% vs affluent 12%).
- Full recovery: 41% for bribe-payers, 32% via personal contacts, 12% for non-payers.
- Recommendation: make FIR registration and bank–police coordination enforceable procedures.
- Other headline (from a digest, unverified): 63% of victims dissatisfied with police handling.

**Notebook:** GS3 Internal Security / GS2 Governance.
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth "Police reforms" and Ashok Kumar "Cyber security"; Mrunal Polity pillar. *Nudge:* add SPIR 2026's cybercrime numbers to your police-reform quote bank, with the Prakash Singh directions.

### 5. Monsoon 2026 closes at 87% of LPA — the driest since 2015, and the east is the worst hit — Geography
**Exam angle.** IMD's season summary (30 Sept): 759.4 mm, 87% of LPA, 4th lowest since 2001. The new angle versus the drought-watch item of 25 Sept is the full-season map: Central India was normal, while East/North-East and the South Peninsula were badly short. A model Mains case for El Niño years and regional variation.
**Memorise.**
- Seasonal: 759.4 mm = 87% of LPA (the IMD 'deficient' band is below 90%).
- East and North-East India 74% (lowest since 1901); South Peninsula 76%; North-West 94%; Central 97%.
- Monthly: June 65%, July 101%, August 84%, September 92%.
- Kerala onset 4 June (3 days late); withdrawal began 19 Sept; 17 of 36 subdivisions deficient, only Odisha in excess.
- Strong El Niño influence reported.

**Notebook:** GS1 Geography.
**Static anchor:** NCERT Class XI "India: Physical Environment", Ch. 4 Climate; Mrunal Geography pillar, "Monsoon mechanism". *Nudge:* add a "monsoon 2026" data box to your El Niño and IOD notes.

### C. Mains Question
**Analyse the spatial and temporal pattern of the 2026 South-West Monsoon and discuss the policy implications of an El Niño-year rainfall deficit for food, water and prices. (15 marks, 250 words)**

*Model-answer skeleton:*
1. Intro: 87% of LPA, 4th lowest since 2001; the national figure hides sharp variation (June 65%, July 101%).
2. Spatial: Central India 97% (normal) vs East/North-East 74% (lowest since 1901) and South Peninsula 76%; 17 of 36 subdivisions deficient.
3. Implications: kharif output and rural income risk in rain-fed east and south, reservoir stress for rabi, price pressure on pulses and vegetables, and a link to MSP and procurement choices.
4. Way forward: contingency crop plans, crop insurance (PMFBY), better sub-seasonal forecasting (onset forecast was 8 days off), groundwater and micro-irrigation, buffer-stock management.
