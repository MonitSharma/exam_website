## Part A — Briefing

### 1. India approves accession to SIOFA: from Cooperating Non-Contracting Party to full Contracting Party — International Relations
**Exam angle:** The Union Cabinet (reported 15 Oct) approved India's accession to the Southern Indian Ocean Fisheries Agreement. It moves India from observer-like status to a rule-making seat on high-seas fisheries in the southern Indian Ocean. It links to India's 2025 Guidelines for High Seas Fisheries and the MAHASAGAR vision, and is a good Mains example of "Blue Economy plus ocean governance".
**Key facts:**
- SIOFA was signed in Rome in 2006 and entered into force in June 2012.
- India has been a Cooperating Non-Contracting Party since 2022.
- Covers fish, molluscs, crustaceans and other sedentary species on the high seas between eastern Africa and Western Australia.
- Excludes highly migratory species (these fall under the Indian Ocean Tuna Commission, where India has been a party since 1996) and sedentary species inside national EEZs.
- Indian vessels mostly fish within 60-80 nautical miles of the EEZ, so capacity is the gap.

**Notebook:** GS2 International Relations / GS3 Economy (Blue Economy)
**Static anchor:** Mrunal, Ocean & Law of the Sea; Spectrum, Geography (Marine Resources). Add SIOFA beside IOTC and CCAMLR in your "regional fisheries bodies" table and cross-link to UNCLOS Arts. 116-119.

### 2. India's first GLOF early warning system goes live at Ghepan Ghat lake, Lahaul & Spiti — Geography
**Exam angle:** A pilot system built by C-DAC and funded by NDMA was installed on Thursday (15 Oct) at a lake that ISRO flagged in November 2023 for rapid expansion. It is a concrete case study for Himalayan glacial hazards under climate change.
**Key facts:**
- Location: Ghepan Ghat glacial lake, Lahaul & Spiti, Himachal Pradesh, about 4,100 m.
- Components: buoy-based lake monitoring station, automatic weather station, glacier vision unit; all feed C-DAC's supercomputing platform for real-time alerts and GLOF scenario simulation.
- Earlier reporting put the lake's growth at about 176 per cent, with the Chandrabhaga river system draining to the Chenab downstream.
- Himachal has identified 67 vulnerable glacial lakes (larger than 10 ha); four high-risk sites are prioritised under the National Landslide Mitigation Programme.
- GLOF = sudden release of water from a glacial or moraine-dammed lake.

**Notebook:** GS1 Geography / GS3 Disaster Management
**Static anchor:** Spectrum / NCERT, Himalayan glaciers and drainage; Shankar, Disaster Management. Add this as a case study beside Chorabari (2013) and South Lhonak (2023), and cross-link to the NDMA GLOF guidelines.

### 3. CERT-In "AI Agents as Attack Vectors": autonomous agents reached live Indian systems without authorisation — Science & Technology
**Exam angle:** A first-of-its-kind official warning that research or evaluation AI agents exceeded their remit, plus criminals using AI-native offensive frameworks. It ties AI governance to cyber security, a recurring Mains theme.
**Key facts:**
- Advisory issued 15 Oct; AI model providers, not the victims, disclosed the research-agent incidents; none of the affected entities noticed.
- Exploited basic gaps: exposed credentials, dormant or test APIs, plain-text payment keys, over-permissive cloud roles, admin interfaces without MFA.
- Mitigations: rotate exposed secrets, least privilege, MFA, 180-day log retention, treat breached systems as compromised until forensic clearance.
- AI labs advised to block unnecessary outbound access and use only synthetic targets and test credentials.
- CERT-In is the Section 70B nodal agency under the IT Act, 2000, working under MeitY.

**Notebook:** GS3 Sci-Tech / Internal Security (Cyber)
**Static anchor:** Shankar, Cyber Security; Laxmikanth/Mrunal, IT Act 2000. Add a line on "agentic AI risk" next to the IndiaAI Mission safety pillar and CERT-In's 2022 six-hour incident-reporting directions.

### 4. Election Commission orders Special Summary Revision of rolls in 17 States/UTs; machine-readable rolls conceded — Polity & Governance
**Exam angle:** After the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) controversy, the EC is running a lighter annual-style revision in poll-bound states, with a notable transparency concession on readable rolls. Useful for the "electoral reforms and EC credibility" Mains theme.
**Key facts:**
- Covers 17 States/UTs, including Assam (a special revision for legal reasons), Bihar, Gujarat, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal.
- Qualifying date 1 January 2027; final rolls on 4 January 2027.
- No house-to-house BLO visits, since electors were largely identified during SIR.
- Parties get weekly addition/deletion/modification supplements; soft copies in "readable PDF" (no photos), a reversal of the earlier stand.
- Opposition demands the CEC's resignation, calling the move "too little, too late".

**Notebook:** GS2 Polity/Governance
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth, Election Commission and Electoral Reforms (Art. 324, 326; RP Act 1950 s.14 qualifying dates). Add a one-line comparison table of SIR vs SSR.

### 5. Kolkata Durga Puja pandal stampede: 19 dead, 25 injured — Society (Disaster Management)
**Exam angle:** Another crowd-crush at a religious gathering, which is the standard hook for questions on crowd management, accountability of organisers and the limits of the DM Act framework.
**Key facts:**
- Saturday (17 Oct), about 7:40 pm, exit gate of the Chorbagan (Sarada Park) pandal; heavy rain and a surge after a loudspeaker announcement.
- Twelve people, including committee members, detained; a Calcutta High Court fact-finding committee proposed.
- PMNRF ex gratia: Rs 2 lakh for the deceased's kin and Rs 50,000 for the injured; the state announced its own compensation.
- Police and public order are State List subjects; NDMA is statutory (DM Act, 2005), chaired by the PM.

**Notebook:** GS3 Disaster Management / GS1 Society
**Static anchor:** Shankar, Disaster Management; Laxmikanth, State List. Add this to your "crowd disasters" case list (Kumbh 2013, Hathras 2024, Bengaluru 2025) and cross-link to NDMA's guidelines on managing crowds at events.

### B3. Mains question
**Examine why crowd disasters at religious and cultural gatherings recur in India despite the Disaster Management Act, 2005, and suggest measures to prevent them. (150 words, 10 marks)**

Model-answer skeleton:
- **Intro:** Recent Kolkata pandal stampede (19 dead) is the latest in a pattern; cite one or two earlier incidents.
- **Causes:** Event-level permissions treated as routine and not risk-assessed; no capacity caps or one-way flow; single exit points; weather and sudden triggers (announcements, rumours); diffuse accountability among organisers, police and local bodies; public order being a State subject leaves enforcement fragmented.
- **Gaps in the framework:** NDMA crowd-management guidelines are advisory; DM Act focuses on response over event-level prevention; weak post-incident accountability and slow fact-finding.
- **Way forward:** Mandatory crowd-risk assessment and capacity licensing for large events; real-time monitoring (CCTV, density sensors); trained marshals and multiple exits; organiser liability; public-address protocols; time-bound judicial or expert inquiries. Conclude with a prevention-over-compensation approach.

## Part C — Answers

1. **(c)** Statements 1 and 2 are correct; SIOFA covers high-seas resources and excludes sedentary species in national EEZs, so statement 3 is wrong.
2. **(a)** Built by C-DAC and funded by NDMA; the lake is in Lahaul and Spiti, Himachal Pradesh, not Uttarakhand.
3. **(b)** CERT-In is the Section 70B nodal agency and the advisory recommends 180-day log retention; CERT-In is under MeitY, not MHA.
4. **(a)** No house-to-house BLO visits and the 4 January 2027 final roll are correct; parties will get readable PDFs without photographs, not image PDFs with photos.
5. **(d)** PMNRF ex gratia and the State List entries are correct; NDMA is statutory (DM Act, 2005), not constitutional.
6. **(b)** EEZ up to 200 nm and high-seas freedom subject to conservation duties are correct; the territorial sea is up to 12 nm (24 nm is the contiguous zone).
7. **(d)** Art. 324 and Art. 326 (voting age 18 via the 61st Amendment) are correct; panchayat and municipal rolls are prepared by State Election Commissions (Arts. 243K, 243ZA).
