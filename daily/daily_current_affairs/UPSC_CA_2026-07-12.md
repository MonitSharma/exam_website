### 2. Union Cabinet approves additional ₹30,000 crore for NIIF — total government commitment doubles to ₹60,000 crore — Economy

**Headline:** The Union Cabinet approved a fresh **₹30,000-crore investment commitment in the National Investment and Infrastructure Fund (NIIF)**, taking the Centre's cumulative commitment to **₹60,000 crore**, to crowd in institutional/private capital and seed a **second flagship infrastructure fund**.

**Exam angle:** Perfect Prelims "institution" item — students routinely confuse NIIF with a ministry scheme. It is India's **quasi-sovereign wealth fund / infrastructure financing platform** set up in 2015, with the government holding **up to 49%** (rest from domestic and global institutional investors). Ties to the "infrastructure financing / crowding-in of capital" Mains theme and Viksit Bharat@2047.

**Key facts to memorize:** NIIF est. **2015**; Govt. share **≤49%** (a strategic, not majority, stake — so it is *not* a Public Sector Undertaking in the usual sense); manages ~₹40,000 crore across its funds; runs **four strategies — Master Fund (infrastructure), Private Markets Fund (fund-of-funds), Strategic Opportunities Fund, and India–Japan Fund**; the new corpus (~₹30,000 crore target) succeeds the existing Master Fund and targets transport, energy, digital, urban infra and e-mobility; has already returned ~₹12,000 crore to investors via exits.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure financing / mobilisation of resources).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal → Infrastructure & Investment Models pillar.* Under "who finances infrastructure," add NIIF's **four funds** and the **≤49% government stake** point; cross-link to the National Monetisation Pipeline (NMP) and the National Infrastructure Pipeline (NIP).

### 3. Supreme Court: the "Right to Walk" on safe, demarcated footpaths is a Fundamental Right — Polity & Governance (Judiciary)

**Headline:** In ***Maniyar Iliyaz @ Shaik Riyaz v. P. Ayyappan & Ors.* (2026 INSC 647, decided 19 June 2026)**, the Supreme Court held that the **right to walk on safe, demarcated footpaths is a Fundamental Right** flowing from **Article 21** read with **Article 19(1)(d)** (and 19(1)(a)–(c)), with pedestrian movement having **priority over motorised vehicles**. *(A landmark Article-21-expansion verdict not covered earlier in this feed; it re-entered the news cycle this week via legal commentary and Law-Commission referral.)*

**Exam angle:** A textbook illustration of the judiciary reading **new dimensions into the "right to life."** Sits in the same lineage as the right to a clean environment, right to privacy (Puttaswamy), right to health, right to shelter, right to sleep. The Court also imposed a **correlative positive duty** on municipal bodies/panchayats to build and maintain footpaths — a nice federal-governance and "positive rights" angle.

**Key facts to memorize:** Case = *Maniyar Iliyaz v. P. Ayyappan*, **2026 INSC 647**, decided **19 June 2026**; bench **Justices P.S. Narasimha & Atul S. Chandurkar**; basis = **Art. 21 + Art. 19(1)(d)**; footpaths get **priority over vehicles**; **correlative duty** on urban local bodies, municipalities and panchayats to demarcate/build/maintain footpaths; violation entitles citizens to **restitutionary/compensatory remedies** beyond the Motor Vehicles Act; judgment forwarded to the **Law Commission** and Ministries of Housing & Urban Affairs, Rural Development, and Road Transport.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Fundamental Rights / judicial interpretation of Article 21).

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth → Ch. "Fundamental Rights," Article 21 sub-section.* Add "Right to Walk (2026)" to your running list of **Article 21 expansions** (privacy, clean environment, health, shelter, sleep, speedy trial); cross-link to Directive Principle Art. 39 / 41-style positive duties on the State.

### 4. National CAMPA clears ₹3,000-crore "Aastha Van Sanrakshan Yojana" (AVSY) to conserve ~15,000 sacred groves — Environment

**Headline:** At the **7th National CAMPA Governing Body meeting** (Coimbatore, chaired by Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav), the government approved the **Aastha Van Sanrakshan Yojana (AVSY)** — a **₹3,000-crore, five-year (FY2026-27 to FY2030-31)** scheme to **conserve, restore and manage nearly 15,000 sacred groves ("Aastha Vans")** across India. *(What's new: the 11 July feed logged this CAMPA meeting only for its four species-recovery projects — AVSY, the sacred-groves scheme, is the distinct, previously-uncovered sub-development.)*

**Exam angle:** A high-probability Prelims item marrying **in-situ, community-based conservation** with **traditional ecological knowledge**. Sacred groves are classic examples of biodiversity protected by culture/religion rather than statute, and can be notified as **Community Reserves** under the Wildlife (Protection) Act. Funded via **CAMPA** (compensatory-afforestation money), which itself is a favourite exam topic.

**Key facts to memorize:** AVSY — **₹3,000 crore**, FY2026-27 to FY2030-31, ~**15,000 sacred groves**, funded through **National CAMPA** (statutory under the **CAF Act, 2016**); sacred groves = **in-situ** community conservation; regional names — **Orans/Kenkri (Rajasthan), Kavu/Sarpa Kavu (Kerala), Sarna (Jharkhand), Devrai/Deorai (Maharashtra), Dev Van (Himachal), Law Kyntang (Meghalaya), Devarakadu/Devara Kadu (Karnataka)**; can be protected as **Community Reserves** (added by the WLPA Amendment, 2002).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Biodiversity conservation / in-situ & community conservation).

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment → "Biodiversity Conservation" (In-situ methods) + "Environmental Acts."* Add AVSY under sacred groves; cross-link to CAMPA / CAF Act 2016 and the Community Reserve category (WLPA 1972, as amended 2002).

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Polity, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** "The Indian judiciary has progressively expanded the scope of Article 21 to convert it into a repository of unenumerated rights." In light of recent judgments, **examine** how this expansion balances individual liberty with the State's positive obligations. *(Directive: examine — investigate and weigh both sides.)*

**Model-answer skeleton:**
- **Intro:** Article 21 has evolved from a narrow "procedure established by law" reading (A.K. Gopalan) to a substantive due-process guarantee (Maneka Gandhi, 1978), spawning a family of unenumerated rights.
- **Body — expansion:** Illustrate with the arc — right to livelihood (Olga Tellis), clean environment (Subhash Kumar), privacy (Puttaswamy, 2017), health, shelter, and now the **right to walk on safe footpaths (2026)** — showing the "living document" approach.
- **Body — positive obligations:** These rulings increasingly impose *positive duties* on the State/local bodies (e.g., build and maintain footpaths), converting negative liberties into enforceable service-delivery mandates; note resource and federal-capacity constraints (municipal finances, separation of powers, judicial overreach debate).
- **Balance/Conclusion:** Judicial creativity fills governance gaps and deepens dignity, but must be tempered by feasibility, respect for the executive's domain, and clear, enforceable standards — a calibrated, not open-ended, expansion.

## PART C — ANSWERS

**Q1 — (d).** All three are correct: Strategic Partnership upgrade, first Indian PM visit in 40 years, and a Roadmap to 2030 with a trade-doubling (NZ$7 bn by 2030) target.

**Q2 — (b).** 1 and 3 correct. NIIF was set up in 2015 and runs several funds (Master Fund, Private Markets Fund, Strategic Opportunities Fund, India–Japan Fund). Statement 2 is wrong — the government holds **up to 49%**, a strategic minority stake, not a 74% majority.

**Q3 — (a).** The Court grounded the right to walk in **Article 21 read with Article 19(1)(d)** (freedom of movement).

**Q4 — (b).** AVSY is a ₹3,000-crore scheme to conserve and restore ~15,000 **sacred groves**, funded through **National CAMPA**.

**Q5 — (a).** ACITI covers AI, critical minerals/supply chains and green-energy tech; it is a technology-and-innovation framework, **not** a defence/peacekeeping arrangement — so 4 is wrong.

**Q6 — (d).** All correct — sacred groves are in-situ conservation, can be Community Reserves under the WLPA, and Orans/Kavu/Sarna are regional names.

**Q7 — (d).** All four have been read into Article 21 — privacy (Puttaswamy), clean environment, shelter (Olga Tellis line), and now the right to walk (2026).
