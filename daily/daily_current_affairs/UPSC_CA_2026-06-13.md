### 2. Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026
**Headline:** Bill proposes a government "Designated Authority" to take provisional (and eventually permanent) control of foreign-funded assets of NGOs whose FCRA registration ends.

**Exam angle (Polity/Governance — Bill with high MCQ potential):** Classic "new bill provisions" MCQ. Links civil-society regulation to fiscal procedure (Consolidated Fund) and to free-speech debate (extends the funding ban to anyone in news production/broadcast). Mains angle: state control vs. civil-society space.

**Memorize:**
- New **"Designated Authority"** → assets of NGOs with cancelled/surrendered/expired registration **provisionally vest** in it; can be **permanently vested** and disposed of.
- Sale proceeds → credited to the **Consolidated Fund of India**.
- **Section 14B**: automatic *cessation* of registration on expiry/non-renewal/refusal.
- Sub-granting allowed only to **other valid FCRA holders**; max imprisonment **cut from 5 years → 1 year**; **Centre's approval needed to start any investigation**. (Status: pending.)

**Notebook:** GS2 Polity / Governance.
**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth — there's no FCRA chapter, so anchor it under "Role of NGOs, Pressure Groups & Civil Society" + cross-link to "Consolidated Fund of India" (Art. 266) in the Parliament/Finance chapter.* Add a one-line note: "FCRA is administered by MHA, not MEA — don't confuse with FEMA (RBI/Finance Min.)."

### 3. India hits 20% ethanol blending (E20) — five years early
**Headline:** India achieved **20% average ethanol blending (E20)** in petrol in 2025, against an original 2030 target; nationwide E20 rollout now in force.

**Exam angle (Economy/Energy — a data point tied to a syllabus topic, top-priority type):** Energy security + import-bill reduction + agri-linkage (sugarcane, maize, surplus/damaged grains) + emissions. Prime for a "which statements are correct" Prelims set on targets and the Ethanol Blended Petrol (EBP) Programme.

**Memorize:**
- **E20 achieved in 2025**, ~5 years ahead of the 2030 target; target had been advanced to **ESY 2025-26** under the **National Policy on Biofuels, 2018 (amended 2022)**.
- Blending rose from **~1.5% (2014) → 20% (2025)**; ethanol capacity ~**under 2 bn L (2014) → ~20 bn L** now.
- E20 petrol spec: minimum **RON 95**; ESY = Ethanol Supply Year (Nov–Oct).
- Related schemes: **PM JI-VAN Yojana (2019)** for 2G ethanol; EBP Programme.

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (Energy) — cross-tag GS3 Environment.
**Static anchor:** *Mrunal Pillar #5 (Infrastructure/Energy) — "Biofuels & EBP" subsection,* and Shankar IAS Environment "Renewable energy/Biofuels." Update with the 2025 E20 milestone figure and the 1G/2G/3G biofuel definitions table.

### 5. SC: Homemakers are "nation builders"; ₹30,000/month notional income
**Headline:** Supreme Court (11 June 2026) recognised homemakers as "nation builders" and fixed a **national notional monthly income of ₹30,000** for valuing loss of domestic/care services in motor-accident compensation.

**Exam angle (GS1 Society / landmark verdict — quotable Mains line):** Gives statistical and judicial backing to the unpaid-care-work debate. Strong for a Society/gender Mains answer and a quotable line; MCQ-testable on the statute and the figure.

### C. Mains Question (GS1 — Society)

**Q.** *"The Supreme Court's recognition of homemakers as 'nation builders' once again foregrounds the invisibility of unpaid care work in India."* **Examine** the significance of recognising unpaid domestic and care work, and discuss the challenges in integrating the care economy into the formal/monetized economy. **(15 marks, 250 words)**

**Model-answer skeleton:**
- **Intro:** Define unpaid care work / care economy; anchor with the SC 2026 ₹30,000 notional-income ruling and Time Use Survey 2019.
- **Significance:** Economic (est. 15–17% of GDP, invisible subsidy), gender-justice (women do ~2.6× men's care work), legal-recognition value (compensation, social security), and policy-visibility (satellite accounts as in Australia/South Korea).
- **Challenges:** Measurement/valuation difficulty, social norms & patriarchy, fiscal cost of formal recognition, risk of "tokenism," care-infrastructure deficit (crèches, elder care).
- **Way forward:** Time-use surveys → GDP satellite accounts; invest in care infrastructure (a 2% GDP investment ≈ 11 mn jobs); redistribute via parental leave, recognise–reduce–redistribute (the "3 Rs") framework.
- **Conclusion:** Recognising care work is both an equity and a growth imperative; the SC verdict is a step toward mainstreaming it.

## ANSWERS

**Q1 — (a) 1 only.** France hosts the 2026 G7 Summit (Evian); India attends only as an *outreach/invitee* country, not a member. → Statement 2 wrong.

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The Bill creates a Designated Authority that vests NGO foreign-funded assets, credits disposal proceeds to the Consolidated Fund of India, and cuts the maximum jail term from 5 years to 1 year — all three are proposed.

**Q3 — (c) Both 1 and 2.** India achieved E20 in 2025 (~5 years early); the 2030 target was advanced to ESY 2025-26 via the 2022 amendment to the National Policy on Biofuels, 2018.

**Q4 — (a) Compensation for loss of domestic care services in motor-accident claims.** The Court fixed ₹30,000/month notional income as a separate head under the motor-accident compensation framework (Motor Vehicles Act, 1988).

**Q5 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** 1G ethanol = molasses/food grains; 2G ethanol = lignocellulosic residues; the 2018 Policy classifies biofuels (1G/2G/3G) to target incentives. All correct.

**Q6 — (d) Australia.** The G7 comprises the USA, UK, France, Germany, Italy, Canada and Japan (plus the EU). Australia is not a member.
