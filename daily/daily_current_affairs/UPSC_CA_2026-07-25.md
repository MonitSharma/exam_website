### 1. Cabinet clears a harsher Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) amendment; NTA sacks 47 officials — Polity & Governance

**Headline.** On 24 July 2026 the Union Cabinet approved a draft Bill to amend the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024, and the National Testing Agency terminated 47 officials in the wake of the NEET-UG 2026 paper-leak agitation, with criminal proceedings planned against several.

**Exam angle.** This is the cleanest available case study on *regulatory capture inside an examination body* and on the limits of penal deterrence in governance. It links the 2024 Act (a Prelims-favourite statute) to accountability of a *society* registered under the Societies Registration Act — the NTA is not a statutory body, which is the crux of the accountability gap. Expect Prelims questions on the Act's architecture and a Mains angle on institutional integrity in high-stakes selection.

**Key facts to memorise.**
- Parent Act: **Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024** (enacted February 2024).
- All offences are **cognizable, non-bailable and non-compoundable**.
- The Act **does not penalise candidates** — they remain governed by the exam authority's own rules.
- General unfair-means offences: **3–5 years** imprisonment + fine up to **₹10 lakh**.
- Organised crime: **5–10 years** + fine of **at least ₹1 crore**; institutional property may be **attached and forfeited** and proportionate exam cost recovered.
- Investigation by an officer **not below Deputy Superintendent / Assistant Commissioner of Police**.
- Reported thrust of the 2026 amendment: ceiling of **10 years' imprisonment and fine up to ₹10 crore** for organised paper leaks. *(Flag: reported from Cabinet briefings; the Bill text is not yet public, so treat the exact numbers as provisional until the Bill is tabled.)*
- **47 NTA officials terminated** on 24 July 2026.

**Notebook.** GS2 — Polity & Governance (statutory & non-statutory bodies; accountability).

**Static anchor.** Laxmikanth, *Indian Polity* — chapter on **Regulatory & Executive Bodies / non-constitutional bodies**. Add a one-line cross-link: "NTA = registered society under Ministry of Education, not statutory → contrast with UPSC (Art. 315) and SSC (executive resolution)." Also cross-link to the **Right to Education / Art. 21A** note on examination fairness as a facet of equality of opportunity (Art. 16).

### 2. India and Israel conclude the second round of FTA negotiations (20–23 July, New Delhi) — International Relations

**Headline.** The second round of negotiations for the proposed India–Israel Free Trade Agreement concluded at Vanijya Bhawan, New Delhi, covering goods, services, rules of origin, SPS, TBT, IPR and customs facilitation.

**Exam angle.** Adds a fourth live FTA track (after UK CETA in force, EFTA TEPA, and EU talks) to India's trade-agreement map — a reliable Prelims "which agreements are in force / under negotiation" area. The Israel track is distinctive for its *technology-and-agritech* content rather than tariff arbitrage, and it runs alongside a West Asian security environment in flux, which is the Mains hook on **de-risking versus de-coupling**.

**Key facts to memorise.**
- **Terms of Reference signed November 2025**; first round February 2026; **second round 20–23 July 2026, New Delhi**.
- India's chief negotiator: **Ajay Bhadoo**, Additional Secretary, Department of Commerce. Israel's: **Yifat Alon Perel**.
- **India–Israel merchandise trade: USD 3.93 billion in FY 2025-26.** (Note: Israel is *not* India's largest West Asian partner — the UAE is.)
- Chapters under negotiation include **rules of origin, SPS, TBT and IPR** — the standard "new-generation FTA" template.

**Notebook.** GS2 — International Relations (bilateral agreements); cross-post the trade data to GS3 — Economy (external sector).

### 3. SOFI 2026: India's undernourishment falls to 9.8%, but 520 million cannot afford a healthy diet — Reports & Indices

**Headline.** The *State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World* 2026, the UN's flagship food-security assessment, records a sharp long-run fall in Indian undernourishment while flagging the **cost of a healthy diet** as the binding constraint.

**Exam angle.** This is the single best data anchor for the "hunger vs nutrition security" distinction that UPSC now prefers over calorie-only framing. The report's 2026 theme — decomposing the cost drivers of the Healthy Diet Basket — gives you a ready analytical vocabulary for Mains on food-system reform, and the co-publisher list is a classic Prelims trap.

**Key facts to memorise.**
- Published **jointly by five UN agencies: FAO, IFAD, UNICEF, WFP and WHO.**
- India's **Prevalence of Undernourishment (PoU): 142.5 million people (9.8%)** in **2023–25**, down from **243.9 million (21.1%)** in 2004–06.
- **520.1 million Indians (35.5%)** could not afford a healthy diet in 2025.
- **Cost of a healthy diet: PPP $4.11 per person per day** in 2025 — up nearly **48% since 2017**.
- **Child stunting 32.9%; child wasting 18.7%** — wasting remains India's weakest indicator; childhood overweight, adult obesity and women's anaemia are all **rising** (the double burden of malnutrition).
- 2026 theme: **cost and affordability of healthy diets / Healthy Diet Basket cost drivers.**

**Notebook.** GS2 — Governance (health & nutrition schemes) and GS3 — Agriculture / Economy (food security).

**Static anchor.** Shankar IAS / GS3 notes on **Food Security & Nutrition** — update the report table (SOFI, Global Hunger Index, NFHS-5, Global Nutrition Report) with the 2026 SOFI figures. Nudge: write one line distinguishing **PoU (FAO, calorie-availability based)** from **GHI (composite: undernourishment + child stunting + wasting + child mortality)** — UPSC has tested exactly this difference, and add a cross-link to the **NFSA 2013** entitlements note.

### 4. Chandipura virus outbreak: nine paediatric deaths across Gujarat and Rajasthan — Science & Technology

**Headline.** Rajasthan intensified surveillance after two child deaths (Dungarpur and Sirohi districts), taking the current monsoon outbreak to **nine confirmed deaths — seven in Gujarat, two in Rajasthan** — as of 24 July 2026.

**Exam angle.** A vector-borne, monsoon-seasonal arbovirus with a *sandfly* vector rather than a mosquito — precisely the kind of discrimination Prelims rewards. It also plugs into the Acute Encephalitis Syndrome (AES) surveillance architecture (IDSP/IHIP, NCVBDC) and the One Health framing for Mains.

**Key facts to memorise.**
- **Chandipura virus (CHPV):** genus **Vesiculovirus**, family **Rhabdoviridae** (same family as rabies virus).
- First isolated **1965, Chandipura village, Nagpur district, Maharashtra.**
- Vector: **sandflies** — *Phlebotomus* and *Sergentomyia* spp. (**not** primarily mosquitoes); monsoon amplifies sandfly populations.
- Causes **Acute Encephalitis Syndrome**, overwhelmingly in **children under 15**; **case fatality 57–70%** in outbreaks.
- **No licensed vaccine and no specific antiviral** — response is vector control (fogging, plastering of mud-wall cracks where sandflies breed) plus supportive care.
- Endemic footprint: Maharashtra, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Telangana.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Science & Technology (health, biotechnology) and GS2 — Governance (public-health delivery).

**Static anchor.** Your **Sci-Tech / Health** notes on vector-borne diseases — add CHPV to the vector table beside dengue (*Aedes aegypti*), malaria (*Anopheles*), Japanese encephalitis (*Culex*), kala-azar (**sandfly** — *Phlebotomus argentipes*). Nudge: cross-link CHPV and kala-azar under "sandfly-borne" so the vector, not the disease, is what you recall first.

### 5. Puri temple secures trademarks over 'Jagannath Dham', 'Mahaprasad', 'Neelachakra' — Art & Culture

**Headline.** After earlier wordmarks for *Patitapabana* and *Ananda Bajara*, the Shree Jagannath Temple Administration's applications for **'Jagannath Dham', 'Shri Kshetra', 'Mahaprasad', 'Neelachakra' and 'Koili Baikuntha'** have cleared second-stage examination and been approved for publication in the Trademark Journal — a first for an Indian shrine.

**Exam angle.** A rare Art & Culture item with a hard legal spine: it forces the **trademark vs geographical indication** distinction (different statutes, different rationales) and raises the federal-cultural dispute over the Digha "Jagannath Dham" nomenclature in West Bengal. Excellent for a GS1 Art & Culture answer that needs a contemporary hook, and for GS3 IPR.

**Key facts to memorise.**
- Registering authority: **Intellectual Property India (Office of the Controller General of Patents, Designs and Trade Marks)**, under **DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce & Industry**.
- Governing statute: **Trade Marks Act, 1999**; registration valid **10 years, indefinitely renewable**. GIs are governed by a *separate* statute — the **Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999**.
- **Jagannath Temple, Puri:** 12th century, built by **Anantavarman Chodaganga Deva** of the **Eastern Ganga dynasty**; **Kalinga** sub-school of the **Nagara** style; sanctum tower is a **rekha deula**, the porch a **jagamohana**.
- Puri is one of the four **Char Dham** centres attributed to **Adi Shankaracharya** (Badrinath, Dwarka, Puri, Rameswaram).
- Trigger: Odisha's objection to West Bengal's use of "Jagannath Dham" for the Digha temple complex.

**Notebook.** GS1 — Art & Culture (temple architecture, religious traditions); GS3 — Science & Technology (IPR).

**Static anchor.** NCERT *An Introduction to Indian Art* (Class XI) — **chapter on Temple Architecture**, Nagara/Kalinga section. Nudge: annotate the Puri entry with "SJTA trademarks 2026 — Trade Marks Act 1999, *not* GI Act 1999" and cross-link to your **IPR** page where the GI list (Banaras brocades, Tirupati laddu, Kandhamal haladi) already sits.

### C. Mains-Style Question

**GS3 — Agriculture / Economy (250 words, 15 marks)**

*"India's food-security challenge has shifted from securing calorie availability to securing the affordability of a healthy diet." **Examine** this proposition in the light of recent global food-security assessments, and suggest policy correctives.*

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points)**

1. **Establish the shift with data.** Undernourishment has more than halved — 21.1% (2004–06) to 9.8% (2023–25), 142.5 million people — largely through the calorie-delivery machinery of NFSA/TPDS and PMGKAY. But 520 million Indians (35.5%) still cannot afford a healthy diet, and the cost of that diet has risen ~48% since 2017 to PPP $4.11/day. Conclusion: the binding constraint has moved from *availability* to *affordability and diet quality*.

2. **Diagnose why the calorie-first architecture cannot close this gap.** TPDS is cereal-centric (rice/wheat at ₹3/₹2/kg), while pulses, dairy, eggs, fruit and vegetables — the expensive components of the Healthy Diet Basket — sit largely outside it. Input and MSP incentives remain skewed to rice, wheat and sugarcane; horticulture and pulses face thin value chains, 30–40% post-harvest loss, and price volatility. Result: the double burden — stunting 32.9% and wasting 18.7% co-existing with rising adult obesity and women's anaemia.

3. **Note the structural cost drivers.** Fragmented cold chains and logistics margins, agri-input and energy price pass-through, water-intensive cropping patterns under climate stress, and the fiscal crowding-out of nutrition spending by cereal subsidy. Affordability is as much a *supply-chain and pricing* problem as an income problem.

4. **Policy correctives, sequenced.** (i) Diversify the entitlement basket — scale millets/pulses in TPDS, ICDS and PM POSHAN, building on nutri-cereal procurement; (ii) realign incentives via crop-neutral input subsidies and irrigation pricing to shift acreage toward pulses and horticulture; (iii) invest in cold chains, FPO-led aggregation and food-processing clusters to compress the farm-gate-to-plate margin; (iv) fortification, front-of-pack labelling and behaviour-change communication to address quality, not just quantity; (v) shift monitoring from PoU to composite affordability and dietary-diversity metrics so policy is measured against the right target. **Conclusion:** move from a food-*grain* security state to a food-*system* and nutrition-security state — consistent with SDG 2 and the Viksit Bharat human-capital agenda.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** All offences under the 2024 Act are cognizable, non-bailable and non-compoundable, and are investigated by an officer not below the rank of DSP/ACP. Statement 2 is wrong: the Act deliberately **excludes candidates** from its penal scope — they continue to be governed by the examination authority's own rules.

**Q2 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** The ToR were signed in November 2025 and FY 2025-26 merchandise trade was about USD 3.93 billion. Statement 2 is wrong: the second round (20–23 July 2026) was held at Vanijya Bhawan, **New Delhi**.

**Q3 — (c) 2 and 3 only.** SOFI 2026 puts India's undernourishment at 9.8% (142.5 million) in 2023–25 and reports that 520.1 million people (35.5%) could not afford a healthy diet in 2025. Statement 1 is wrong: **child wasting stands at 18.7%**, India's weakest nutrition indicator.

**Q4 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** CHPV is a *Vesiculovirus* of the family **Rhabdoviridae**, transmitted chiefly by **sandflies** (*Phlebotomus*, *Sergentomyia*). Statement 3 is wrong: there is **no licensed vaccine and no specific antiviral** — management is supportive plus vector control.

**Q5 — (c) 1 and 3 only.** Trademarks are registered under the **Trade Marks Act, 1999**, for a renewable ten-year term. Statement 2 is wrong: GIs are registered under a separate law, the **Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999**.

**Q6 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The Puri temple was built in the 12th century by **Anantavarman Chodaganga Deva** of the **Eastern Ganga** dynasty; **Kalinga** is a sub-school of the **Nagara** style; and the sanctum tower in Kalinga architecture is the **rekha deula** (with the *jagamohana* porch in front).

**Q7 — (c) 2 and 3 only.** NFSA covers up to 75% rural and 50% urban population under TPDS, and deems the eldest woman aged 18+ the head of household for ration cards. Statement 1 is wrong: **identification of eligible households is done by the State/UT governments**, within Centre-determined coverage ceilings.
