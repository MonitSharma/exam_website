## PART A — BRIEFING (5 items)

### 1. 7th National CAMPA Governing Body clears four new species-conservation projects — Environment
**Headline:** At the **7th Meeting of the Governing Body of National CAMPA** (Coimbatore, 11 July 2026), Union Environment Minister **Bhupender Yadav** approved four new wildlife projects — a **Conservation & Recovery Action Plan for River Dolphins**, **Project Snow Leopard Phase-II** (with a second population-estimation cycle), a **Conservation Action Plan for the Indian (one-horned) Rhinoceros**, and a **pan-India plan for the Wild Water Buffalo** — funded under a **new ₹3,000 crore scheme over five years**. A **National Human–Wildlife Conflict Portal** and a **Centre of Excellence for conflict management** were also launched; continued support for Manipur's **Sangai (brow-antlered deer)** was cleared.

**Exam angle:** High-yield Environment. Ties species conservation to the *funding mechanism* (CAMPA/Compensatory Afforestation Fund) — examiners love the "which authority funds this" link. Human–wildlife conflict is a recurring Mains theme. Note the flagship-species list — each animal maps to a specific habitat/state (rhino → Assam/Kaziranga; snow leopard → Himalayan trans-Himalaya; wild buffalo → Chhattisgarh/Assam; Gangetic dolphin → Ganga–Brahmaputra).

**Key facts to memorise:**
- CAMPA = **Compensatory Afforestation Fund Management and Planning Authority**, statutory under the **CAF Act, 2016**; money comes from **Net Present Value (NPV)** and compensatory-afforestation levies on diverted forest land (90% to States, 10% to Centre).
- **Gangetic dolphin** = India's **National Aquatic Animal** (2009); Schedule I, WPA 1972.
- Indian rhino population **>4,000** now (was <200 in early 20th century); ~70% in **Kaziranga**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Conservation).
**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment* → "Conservation Efforts / Compensatory Afforestation & CAMPA" and the flagship-species chapter. **Nudge:** cross-link CAMPA to the **CAF Act, 2016** and add a one-line note against each of the four species with its schedule status & prime habitat.

### 2. Centre waives basic customs duty on inputs for Li-ion cells, display assemblies & inductor modules — Economy
**Headline:** Via notifications dated **8 July 2026** (effective 9 July), the **Finance Ministry (Dept. of Revenue/CBIC)** exempted **Basic Customs Duty (BCD)** on specified imported inputs used to manufacture **lithium-ion cells, display assemblies and inductor coil modules** — covering display cells, FPCAs, backlight units, ACF, and an expanded list of **85 capital goods** for Li-ion cell lines. The exemption on display/inductor inputs runs **until 31 March 2029**.

**Exam angle:** Classic Economy + Sci-Tech overlap on **electronics manufacturing / import-substitution / value addition**. Reinforces the PLI-era logic: cut duties on *inputs* to deepen domestic assembly and cut the import bill for smartphones, EVs and wearables. Good for a "government took X step → intended effect" Prelims statement.

**Key facts to memorise:**
- **BCD** is levied under the **Customs Act, 1962**; exemptions are issued by the Dept. of Revenue via CBIC notifications.
- Aim: raise **domestic value addition** and cost-competitiveness in electronics & EV batteries; complements the **PLI schemes** and the earlier **components/PCBA** push.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Industry/Manufacturing & External Sector).
**Static anchor:** *Mrunal Economy* → "Sectors of Economy: Industry / Make-in-India & PLI" and BFSI-adjacent "Fiscal → Customs Duty" pillar. **Nudge:** add a running list of "duty-cut-to-boost-manufacturing" moves (mobile PCBA, Li-ion, now display/inductor) with dates.

### 3. Ministry of Tribal Affairs launches 'TribeX' — India's first digital platform for tribal arts & knowledge — Society / Art & Culture

It opens with **20 free certificate courses** (to expand past 100), a **Heritage Archive of 5,000+ multimedia resources**, and an **MoU with Sampurnanand Sanskrit University, Varanasi** for five UGC-recognised one-year PG diplomas (including **Santali/Ol Chiki**, museology, tribal textiles).

- **Santali** is written in **Ol Chiki** script and is in the **Eighth Schedule** (added by the **92nd Amendment, 2003**, along with Bodo, Dogri, Maithili).

### 4. Supreme Court flags "deep-rooted nexus" between banks, ARCs and borrowers; notice to Centre, RBI, SEBI — Polity & Governance / Economy
**Headline:** A bench of **CJI Surya Kant and Justice V. Mohana** (hearing ~10 July 2026) flagged a **"deep-rooted nexus" among public-sector banks, Asset Reconstruction Companies (ARCs) and borrowers**, observing that banks sell loan liabilities to ARCs "for peanuts." It issued **notice to the Centre, RBI and others** on a plea alleging that **₹1,537 crore** owed to PSBs was settled through two ARCs for just **₹73.5 crore**; the petition seeks a judicial commission / expert panel (RBI, SEBI, SFIO, ED, CBI) to probe the alleged fraud.

**Exam angle:** Prime Economy–Polity crossover on **NPA resolution, SARFAESI, ARCs and public-money accountability**. Connects the **SARFAESI Act, 2002**, the **RBI's ARC regulation**, and PIL-based judicial oversight — a strong Mains hook on "banking governance vs. moral hazard."

**Key facts to memorise:**
- **ARCs** are registered with and regulated by the **RBI** under the **SARFAESI Act, 2002**; they buy stressed assets (often via **Security Receipts**) at a discount.
- The **IBC, 2016** and **NCLT/NCLAT** are the parallel resolution track; here the Court is looking at *pre-insolvency* haircut abuse.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Judiciary/PIL) & GS3 — Economy (Banking/NPAs).
**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth* → "Supreme Court / Public Interest Litigation" and *Mrunal Economy* → "BFSI: NPA, SARFAESI, ARCs, IBC." **Nudge:** add a margin note distinguishing **SARFAESI (secured-creditor enforcement)** from **IBC (time-bound resolution)**.

### 5. IIT Delhi–DRDO demonstrate an indigenous low-cost tactical aerostat (payload to ~20 km) — Science & Technology / Internal Security
**Headline:** **IIT Delhi**, with **DRDO** and a domestic startup, demonstrated an **indigenous low-cost tactical aerostat** — a **lighter-than-air** platform that can loft surveillance and communication payloads to altitudes of up to ~**20 km**, cutting reliance on imported systems and extending persistent border/airspace monitoring beyond ground-radar line-of-sight.

**Exam angle:** Sci-Tech + Internal Security. Distinguish **aerostat (tethered, lighter-than-air, persistent)** from satellites and UAVs — a favourite Prelims discrimination. Ties to **border surveillance** and **indigenisation (Atmanirbharta in defence)**.

**Key facts to memorise:**
- An **aerostat** stays aloft via **buoyancy (helium)**, usually **tethered**, giving cheap, long-endurance high-altitude surveillance — unlike powered UAVs.
- Developed indigenously → reduces import dependence (India earlier relied on imported aerostats like the PGSS).

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology / Internal Security (border management tech).
**Static anchor:** *Shankar/So-called Sci-Tech notes* → "Defence Technology / Surveillance platforms." **Nudge:** add a 3-column compare — **aerostat vs UAV vs satellite** (endurance, cost, altitude, mobility).

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Environment, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *"Effective wildlife conservation in India increasingly depends less on protected-area boundaries and more on managing the human–wildlife interface."* **Examine** this statement in light of recent policy measures such as species-specific action plans and dedicated conflict-management institutions.

**Model-answer skeleton:**
1. **Context / intro:** India's conservation success (tiger, rhino recovery) has pushed wildlife into human-dominated landscapes → conflict is now a *central*, not peripheral, challenge. Cite the 7th National CAMPA move: species plans (snow leopard, rhino, river dolphin, wild buffalo) *plus* a **National Human–Wildlife Conflict Portal** and a **Centre of Excellence**.
2. **Why the interface matters:** habitat fragmentation, corridors cutting through farmland, retaliatory killings, crop/livestock loss, and climate-driven prey shifts; protected areas are too small/insular to hold recovering populations.
3. **Instruments & gaps:** CAMPA/CAF funding, WPA 1972 schedules, eco-sensitive zones, compensation schemes — but weak data, delayed compensation, and thin community incentives.
4. **Way forward:** data-driven early-warning (the new Portal), corridor protection, ex-gratia reform, community stewardship/eco-development committees, and coexistence models (e.g., Project Snow Leopard's landscape approach). **Conclude:** conservation must shift from "fortress" to "coexistence."

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (c) All four.** River dolphin, snow leopard (Phase-II), Indian rhino and wild water buffalo plans were all approved at the 7th National CAMPA meeting.

**Q2 — (b).** CAMPA funds come chiefly from **Net Present Value (NPV)** and compensatory-afforestation charges levied when forest land is diverted, under the CAF Act, 2016 (90% to States, 10% Centre).

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The July 2026 notifications waived BCD on inputs for lithium-ion cells, display assemblies and inductor coil modules.

**Q4 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** TribeX is a digital learning platform (correct) launched by the **Ministry of Tribal Affairs**, *not* the Ministry of Culture (so 2 is wrong); an MoU with Sampurnanand Sanskrit University includes a Santali-language diploma (3 correct).

**Q5 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The Court flagged the nexus among banks, ARCs and borrowers and sought responses from the Centre, RBI and others.

**Q6 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** ARCs are RBI-regulated under SARFAESI, 2002, and issue Security Receipts. Statement 3 is wrong — ARCs acquire stressed assets *directly* from lenders under SARFAESI, independent of any NCLT admission (that is the separate IBC track).

**Q7 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The Gangetic dolphin is India's National Aquatic Animal (2009), is in Schedule I of the WPA 1972, and is functionally blind, navigating by echolocation.
