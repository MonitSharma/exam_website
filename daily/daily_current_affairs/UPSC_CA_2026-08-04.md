## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Glaw Lake becomes India's 101st Ramsar Site — and Arunachal Pradesh's first — Environment

**Headline:** On 3 August 2026, Union Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav announced the designation of Glaw Lake, Lohit district, Arunachal Pradesh, as India's 101st Wetland of International Importance under the Ramsar Convention — the State's first-ever Ramsar Site.

**Exam angle:**
- This is the classic Prelims "Places in News" + "Ramsar tally" combination. UPSC has asked location-mapping questions on newly designated Ramsar Sites in three of the last five Prelims. The pairing of a *Ramsar Site inside a Tiger Reserve* is the examinable hook — it lets the examiner test whether you know that Ramsar designation is a **designation**, not a new legal protected-area category.
- For Mains GS3, this feeds the "wetlands as climate and water-security infrastructure" line — the Minister explicitly framed it as biodiversity + water security + sustainable livelihoods.
- A North-East first is also a Geography/regional-development angle: Arunachal previously had zero Ramsar Sites despite being in the Eastern Himalaya biodiversity hotspot.

**Key facts / figures to memorise:**
- India's Ramsar tally: **101** (up from **26 in 2014**) — the largest network in Asia.
- Glaw Lake: **~2.5 hectares**, a freshwater lake fed by perennial mountain streams, on the northern side of **Wakro town, Lohit district**, Arunachal Pradesh.
- It lies **inside the Kamlang Tiger Reserve and Wildlife Sanctuary** (Kamlang became a tiger reserve in 2016).
- Recorded biodiversity in the site and its catchment: **150+ tree species, 49 orchid species**.
- Cultural layer: the **Mishmi** tribes hold that four deities reside in the lake and protect it — a ready-made "sacred groves / community conservation" example.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Conservation & Biodiversity); cross-file under GS1 Geography (Places in News, North-East).

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment* — chapter on **Wetlands / Ramsar Convention**. **Update:** append Glaw Lake to your Ramsar Site table (State → site → distinguishing feature) and add a cross-link from the **Tiger Reserves** page for Kamlang, noting Arunachal's four tiger reserves — Namdapha, Pakke, Kamlang, Dibang. Also add a one-line cross-link to the **Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules, 2017** and the **Montreux Record**.

### 2. Rajya Sabha passes the MSMED (Amendment) Bill, 2026 — TReDS becomes mandatory for CPSEs — Economy

**Headline:** On 3 August 2026 the Rajya Sabha passed, by voice vote, the Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises Development (Amendment) Bill, 2026 — the first substantive overhaul of the MSMED Act, 2006, targeting delayed payments, dispute-resolution timelines and decriminalisation. It had been introduced in the Upper House on 28 July 2026 by MSME Minister Jitan Ram Manjhi.

**Exam angle:**
- Delayed payments to MSMEs are the single most-quoted structural constraint on India's manufacturing share of GDP — UPSC asked exactly this in **GS3 Mains 2023**. A statutory fix arriving in 2026 is a directly quotable update for that answer.
- The Bill is also a textbook **"decriminalisation of minor economic offences / ease of doing business"** case study, alongside the Jan Vishwas route.
- Prelims trap: the Bill **removes** the classification thresholds from the statute and shifts them to executive notification — a delegated-legislation angle for GS2.

**Key facts / figures to memorise:**
- **Classification:** thresholds deleted from the Act; the Centre may now classify MSMEs by (i) investment in plant, machinery or equipment and (ii) turnover, **specified by notification**. (Current notified limits, effective 1 April 2025: Micro ₹2.5 cr / ₹10 cr; Small ₹25 cr / ₹100 cr; Medium ₹125 cr / ₹500 cr.)
- **Registration:** filing the memorandum (Udyam) becomes **voluntary for all MSMEs**; Centre — and optionally States — to notify a digital platform.
- **TReDS mandatory:** every **Central Public Sector Enterprise** must settle MSME invoices on the **Trade Receivables Discounting System**, the RBI-regulated invoice-discounting platform. Centre/States may extend this to other public entities.
- **Timelines:** mediation to conclude in **90 days** from first appearance; reference to arbitration within **30 days** of mediation failing; award within **90 days** of completion of pleadings.
- **Deposit rule:** the existing 75% pre-deposit for challenging a Council award now also applies to mediated settlements; if a case is pending **beyond six months**, at least **50%** of the award must be released to the supplier.
- **Decriminalisation:** false information for registration → warning first, then penalty **₹1,000–₹50,000**; failure to disclose unpaid MSME dues in annual accounts → warning, then ₹10,000–₹50,000, then ₹50,000–₹1,00,000. Penalties auto-escalate **10% of the minimum every three years**. Development Commissioner is the adjudicating officer; appeal lies to the **MSME Secretary**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Industry/MSMEs, Inclusive Growth); cross-file under GS2 Governance (decriminalisation, delegated legislation).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* — **Pillar 4A/4B (Industry, MSMEs and Ease of Doing Business)**. **Update:** rewrite your MSMED Act, 2006 box — keep **Section 15** (payment within 45 days), **Section 16** (compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate), **Section 18** (reference to the Micro and Small Enterprises Facilitation Council), **Section 22** (buyer's disclosure duty) — and add a "2026 Amendment" column beside each. Cross-link to your **TReDS / Factoring Regulation (Amendment) Act, 2021** note.

### 3. Hyderabad hosts the 2nd BRICS Anti-Corruption Working Group and Asset Recovery Expert Network — International Relations

**Headline:** The Department of Personnel and Training is hosting the second meeting of the **BRICS Anti-Corruption Working Group (ACWG)** and the second meeting of the **BRICS Expert Network on Asset Recovery** in Hyderabad on **4–5 August 2026**, under India's BRICS Chairship of 2026. Inauguration by DoPT Secretary Ms Rachna Shah.

**Exam angle:**
- India's BRICS Chairship year is a guaranteed GS2/IR theme; the examinable distinction is **which ministry is nodal for which BRICS track** — DoPT for anti-corruption, unlike the more familiar MEA/Finance-led tracks.
- This is the "economic-offender extradition and asset recovery" theme that UPSC likes to pair with **UNCAC (2003)**, the **Fugitive Economic Offenders Act, 2018**, and Mutual Legal Assistance Treaties. Note the agenda language: cooperation is **"informal"** — i.e. agency-to-agency channels that run *alongside*, not in place of, formal MLAT/extradition routes.
- The FinTech and digital-asset corruption-risk agenda is a fresh GS3 crossover (crypto, virtual digital assets, PMLA reporting entity obligations).

**Key facts / figures to memorise:**
- Theme of India's BRICS Chairship 2026: **"Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability"**.
- Nodal department for the BRICS anti-corruption track in India: **DoPT, Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions**.
- Three agenda pillars: (i) informal cooperation to **trace fugitive offenders** and support extradition; (ii) operational information-sharing through the **Expert Network on Asset Recovery**; (iii) corruption risks from **FinTech, digital assets and emerging digital financial instruments**.
- Side event on 5 August: **"Ethical Governance through Innovation and Tech-Driven Systems"** — digital public procurement, citizen engagement, transparency.
- Anchor treaty: **UN Convention against Corruption (UNCAC), 2003** — India signed in 2005, ratified in **2011**.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (Groupings & Agreements involving India); cross-file under GS4/GS2 Governance (probity, transparency).

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth* — chapters on the **CVC** and the **Lokpal and Lokayuktas**, plus your BRICS institutional note. **Update:** add a "BRICS working-group architecture under India's 2026 Chairship" line to the BRICS page, and cross-link the **Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (as amended 2018)** and the **Fugitive Economic Offenders Act, 2018** from the anti-corruption page.

### 4. India plans a ₹45,000-crore railway build-out along the China, Pakistan and Bangladesh borders — Internal Security

**Headline:** Reported on 3 August 2026: the Centre plans to invest up to **₹45,000 crore (~USD 4.7 billion)** over the next **18–24 months** on new tracks, platforms and network strengthening in States along the borders with China, Pakistan and Bangladesh — roughly **22% of all planned railway infrastructure outlay** over that period.

**Exam angle:**
- This is the cleanest available example of **dual-use infrastructure**: civilian rail that doubles as military logistics capacity. UPSC's GS3 Internal Security syllabus explicitly lists "security challenges in border areas" and "linkages of development with the spread of extremism" — dual-use rail sits at both.
- Pair it with the **strategic-road/Border Roads Organisation** story rather than treating it as a Railways-budget item; the analytic point is *logistics depth on interior lines* versus China's Tibet rail network.
- The three-front framing (a steadied but unresolved China boundary, a Pakistan front after the 2025 conflict, and a recalibrating Bangladesh) is a ready Mains introduction.

**Key facts / figures to memorise:**
- Outlay: **₹45,000 crore**; horizon **18–24 months**; **~22%** share of the two-year railway infrastructure pipeline.
- States covered: **Punjab, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh, West Bengal, Assam** and several other North-Eastern States.
- Nature of works: **new tracks, platforms and network strengthening** — capacity augmentation rather than greenfield alignment alone.
- Concept to carry: **dual-use infrastructure** — civilian utility in peacetime, military mobility in contingency.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security (Border Management); cross-file under GS3 Infrastructure and GS1 Geography (border States, Siliguri Corridor).

**Static anchor:** Your **Border Management** note (Internal Security). **Update:** add a "connectivity" sub-head listing, in one line each, the **Border Roads Organisation**, **Vibrant Villages Programme (Phases I & II)**, **Integrated Check Posts under the Land Ports Authority of India**, and now the **border-States railway package** — and mark on your map the **Siliguri Corridor** and the Assam–Arunachal rail heads, which are where the strategic value concentrates.

### 5. TRAI relaunches 'MyCall' — crowdsourced, real-time voice-quality reporting — Science & Technology

**Headline:** On 3 August 2026, TRAI launched a fully revamped **MyCall** app letting subscribers rate every voice call in real time (1–5 stars) and flag specific defects. TRAI Chairman Anil Kumar Lahoti clarified the data will **not** be used punitively against operators.

**Exam angle:**
- The examinable idea is **crowdsourced regulation**: a regulator supplementing operator-reported drive-test data with citizen-generated data. Contrast this with the mandatory, penalty-backed reporting under the QoS Regulations — one is diagnostic, the other is enforcement.
- Prelims trap: TRAI runs a **family** of consumer apps — *MyCall* (voice quality), *MySpeed* (data speed), *DND* (unsolicited commercial communication). Examiners love mismatched-pairing questions here.
- GS2 angle: TRAI is a **statutory regulator whose recommendations are advisory, not binding**, with appeals lying to TDSAT — a standard "regulatory bodies" comparison against SEBI/CCI, which have adjudicatory teeth.

**Key facts / figures to memorise:**
- Defects reportable in-app: **call drop, echo, audio delay, cross-connection, voice breaking, one-way audio, delayed call connection**; plus multilingual support, feedback history and coverage tests.
- Explicitly **non-punitive** — feedback is fed to Telecom Service Providers to locate network deficiencies.
- Governing framework: **TRAI Act, 1997**; the **Standards of Quality of Service of Access (Wireline and Wireless) and Broadband Service Regulations, 2024**, in force from **1 October 2024** — monthly (not quarterly) monitoring, cell-level data, percentile-based measurement, and graded financial disincentives of **₹1 lakh to ₹10 lakh**.
- Appellate forum: **TDSAT**, created by the TRAI (Amendment) Act, 2000.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Telecom/ICT); cross-file under GS2 Governance (statutory regulatory bodies, citizen-centric governance).

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth* — **"Regulatory Bodies"** chapter (TRAI entry). **Update:** add a two-line row for the **QoS Regulations, 2024** under TRAI, and cross-link forward to the **Telecommunications Act, 2023** so that TRAI's post-2023 role is not left frozen at the 1997 statute.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Economy, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *"Delayed payments, not credit scarcity alone, are the binding constraint on India's micro and small enterprises."* **Examine** this statement in the light of recent legislative changes to the framework governing MSME payments and dispute resolution.

**Model-answer skeleton (3–4 points):**

1. **Frame the constraint precisely.** Working capital locked in receivables is functionally identical to a credit shortage but is invisible in credit-gap statistics. Section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006 already caps buyer credit at 45 days and Section 16 attaches compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate — yet enforcement, not entitlement, was the gap: MSEFC references were slow, awards were challenged, and the 75% pre-deposit gave suppliers a paper victory with no cash.

2. **Show what the 2026 amendment changes.** Hard timelines (mediation in 90 days; arbitration reference in 30 days; award in 90 days after pleadings); a release of at least 50% of the award to the supplier where a challenge has been pending beyond six months; and — the structural move — **mandatory TReDS settlement by central public sector enterprises**, which converts the largest and most chronically slow class of buyers into a discountable, dated receivable.

3. **Balance it with what remains unaddressed.** The mandate binds CPSEs, not private buyers or State entities, where a large share of dues sit; TReDS onboarding remains thin among mid-sized corporates; and voluntary Udyam registration, while easing compliance, weakens the data trail on which statutory remedies depend. Decriminalisation reduces deterrence even as it improves ease of doing business.

4. **Close on the growth link.** Manufacturing has been stuck near 17% of GDP; MSMEs supply roughly 40% of that. A receivables regime that clears predictably raises the effective return on MSME capacity expansion more cheaply than any interest subvention. Recommend extending the TReDS mandate to State PSUs and large private buyers, and publishing a public dues dashboard.

### D. PYQ Mirror

**Theme 1 — Wetlands and the Ramsar framework.**
*UPSC Prelims 2019* asked, verbatim: "Consider the following statements: 1. Under the Ramsar Convention, it is mandatory on the part of the Government of India to protect and conserve all the wetlands in the territory of India. 2. The Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules, 2010 were framed by the Government of India based on the recommendations of the Ramsar Convention. 3. The Wetlands (Conservation and Management) Rules, 2010 also encompass the drainage area or catchment regions of the wetlands as determined by the authority." **Answer: (c) 3 only.** The lesson repeats in 2026: Ramsar designation is a *wise-use commitment*, not a blanket domestic protection mandate — and the domestic rules are now the **2017** Rules, not the 2010 ones. Expect the examiner to test exactly that substitution.

**Theme 2 — MSMEs and the manufacturing share of GDP.**
*UPSC Mains 2023, GS Paper 3 (Q1, 10 marks):* "Faster economic growth requires increased share of the manufacturing sector in GDP, particularly of MSMEs. Comment on the present policies of the Government in this regard." The 2026 MSMED amendment is the newest item to add to the policy list in that answer — alongside PLI, Udyam, and the revised classification thresholds of April 2025 — and it lets you shift the answer from a list of schemes to an argument about *enforcement of existing entitlements*, which scores better.
