## Part A — Briefing

### 1. India and MERCOSUR launch talks to expand a 2004 PTA that covers barely 450 tariff lines — International Relations

**Headline.** On 14 September 2026, Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal and Uruguay's Foreign Minister Mario Lubetkin — Uruguay currently holds MERCOSUR's *Pro Tempore* Presidency — announced the launch of negotiations to expand the India–MERCOSUR Preferential Trade Agreement; the two sides are finalising the Terms of Reference that will define scope and structure. A separate protocol allowing electronic Certificates of Origin was also signed.

**Exam angle.** This is the cleanest available illustration of the difference between a *Preferential* Trade Agreement, an FTA and a customs union — the existing pact is a narrow, positive-list PTA covering a few hundred lines, while MERCOSUR itself is a customs union with a Common External Tariff, which is precisely why India cannot negotiate with Argentina or Brazil individually. It also plugs the perennial "India–Latin America" gap in Mains GS2, and connects to the critical-minerals story (the Lithium Triangle — Argentina, Bolivia, Chile — with Bolivia now a MERCOSUR full member).

**Facts to memorise.**

- Framework Agreement: Asunción, June 2003. PTA **signed 25 January 2004 at New Delhi; entered into force 1 June 2009**.
- Concessions: **India on 450 tariff lines, MERCOSUR on 452** — a fraction of the tariff universe.
- Bilateral trade **2025–26: about US$21.8 billion**.
- MERCOSUR: **Treaty of Asunción, 26 March 1991**; customs union formalised by the **Ouro Preto Protocol, 1994**. Founding four — Argentina, Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay. **Bolivia became a full member in July 2024**; **Venezuela has been suspended since December 2016**.
- Rotating six-monthly *Pro Tempore* Presidency — the reason Uruguay signed for the bloc.

**Notebook.** GS2 — International Relations (Groupings & agreements involving India).

**Static anchor.** Mrunal's Pillar on International Trade / BoP — the section on types of trade agreements. *Update:* add a one-page comparison table — PTA vs FTA vs Customs Union vs Common Market vs Economic Union — and cross-link India's live negotiations (EU, MERCOSUR, EFTA-TEPA, Oman, Chile) to the correct rung on that ladder.

### 2. Forex reserves hit a record $785.7 billion — India displaces Russia as the world's fourth-largest holder — Economy

**Headline.** RBI data released on 11 September showed reserves rising **$44.9 billion in the week ended 4 September 2026 — the largest weekly increase on record — to $785.7 billion**, moving India past Russia into fourth place globally, behind China, Japan and Switzerland.

**Exam angle.** The number matters less than the *composition of the inflow*: this is not a trade surplus or an FDI surge but **borrowed, redeemable, interest-bearing non-resident deposit money** mobilised through a concessional swap window for FCNR(B) deposits announced on 5 June 2026. Reserves built this way raise import cover and short-term buffer optics while simultaneously enlarging external liabilities — the standard Mains counterpoint that reserve adequacy must be read alongside the Net International Investment Position and short-term debt-to-reserves ratio, not in isolation.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **$785.7 billion** (week ended 4 Sep 2026); weekly jump **$44.9 billion**. Rank **4th**, after China, Japan, Switzerland.
- Reserves are up **over $100 billion since 5 June 2026**, when the concessional swap scheme was announced; FCNR(B)-window flows reported at roughly **$127 billion**, and total diaspora-deposit mobilisation at about **$136 billion**.
- **Four components:** Foreign Currency Assets (much the largest), Gold, SDRs, and the **Reserve Tranche Position in the IMF**. In the week ended 28 August, FCA stood at about **$600.7 bn**, gold **$116.4 bn**, SDRs **$18.8 bn**, RTP **$4.9 bn**.
- Reserves are an asset on the RBI's balance sheet; NRI deposits are a *liability* in the BoP capital account.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Economy (External sector / BoP).

**Static anchor.** Mrunal Pillar 3 — Balance of Payments and Forex Reserves; Economic Survey external-sector chapter. *Update:* add a two-line note distinguishing **reserve accretion from current-account earnings vs from capital-account borrowings**, and cross-link to the FCNR(B) swap-window note filed on 30 August.

### 3. The World Circular Economy Forum opens in Gandhinagar — its 10th edition and its first in South Asia — Environment

**Headline.** WCEF 2026 runs **15–18 September at Mahatma Mandir, Gandhinagar**, on the theme *"Circular economy: Transition for people and prosperity"* — convened by Finland's **Sitra** with the **Central Pollution Control Board**, supported by MoEFCC and the Gujarat government. Over 2,000 participants from 65-plus countries; 125-plus exhibitors. The Board of the **10-Year Framework of Programmes on Sustainable Consumption and Production (10YFP)** meets at Gandhinagar on 15 September, with India having recently taken over as **Co-Chair**.

**Exam angle.** Circularity is the connective tissue between SDG 12, the plastics-treaty negotiations, critical-mineral security and Mission LiFE — a single Mains paragraph can carry all four. The examiner's favourite distinction is **recycling (end-of-pipe, still linear) vs circularity (design-stage: durability, repairability, reuse, remanufacture)**, and the Indian policy instrument that operationalises it is **Extended Producer Responsibility**, which UPSC has already asked about directly.

**Facts to memorise.**

- WCEF launched by **Sitra (the Finnish Innovation Fund) at Helsinki in 2017**; 2026 is the **10th edition**, the first in South Asia.
- **10YFP** — the SCP framework adopted at **Rio+20 (2012)**, secretariat with UNEP; India now Co-Chair of its Board.
- India's architecture: **NITI Aayog's 11 circular-economy committees (March 2021)** across municipal solid and liquid waste, scrap metal, Li-ion batteries, tyres and rubber, gypsum, end-of-life vehicles, e-waste, hazardous industrial waste, used oil, agricultural waste and solar panels.
- EPR is embedded in the **Plastic Waste Management Rules 2016 (amended 2022)**, **E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022** and **Battery Waste Management Rules 2022** — but was *first introduced* in the **e-Waste (Management and Handling) Rules, 2011**.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Environment (Pollution & waste management) with a GS3 Economy spillover on resource security.

**Static anchor.** Shankar IAS *Environment* — Pollution / Waste Management chapter. *Update:* add the 11 NITI focus sectors as a memorised list, and cross-link EPR to the three waste rules plus the 2019 PYQ.

### 4. India's first Badri calf born through OPU-IVF — assisted reproduction turned toward a low-yield hill breed — Agriculture

**Headline.** A Badri female calf was born on **11 September 2026** at the Academic Dairy Farm, **GB Pant University of Agriculture and Technology, Pantnagar**, from an in-vitro-produced Badri embryo transferred into a **Sahiwal recipient cow** — a collaboration between **ICAR–NDRI Karnal** and GBPUAT, funded by the Uttarakhand Biotechnology Council, with the programme running since 2023.

**Exam angle.** The significance is the inversion of the usual logic of reproductive technology. OPU-IVF is normally deployed to multiply *high-yielding* genetics; here it is used to rescue a **low-yielding but hardy, climate-resilient indigenous breed** from dilution and decline — germplasm conservation, not productivity maximisation. That is the exact framing for a GS3 answer on conserving indigenous cattle, and it pairs naturally with the Rashtriya Gokul Mission and the National Livestock Mission.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **Badri** — Uttarakhand's **first cattle breed registered by ICAR–NBAGR (2016)**, accession INDIA_CATTLE_2400_BADRI_03040; reared across the Garhwal and Kumaon hills (Almora, Chamoli, Pauri, Champawat).
- Yield only **1–3 litres/day**, but milk reported at roughly **90% A2 beta-casein** — among the highest for any Indian breed; sure-footed on steep slopes.
- **OPU** = ultrasound-guided, **non-surgical, repeatable** aspiration of *immature* oocytes from a live donor; these are matured and fertilised **in vitro**, and the resulting embryo transferred to a recipient of any breed. Distinguish from **MOET** (superovulation + uterine flushing of *already fertilised* embryos).
- Scheme anchors: **Rashtriya Gokul Mission (2014)**, National Programme for Bovine Breeding and Dairy Development; **NBAGR, Karnal** is the registering authority for animal genetic resources.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Agriculture (Animal husbandry / Biotechnology applications).

**Static anchor.** Shankar IAS / Economic Survey agriculture chapter — Animal Husbandry; NCERT-level genetics on assisted reproduction. *Update:* add an OPU-IVF vs MOET vs AI (artificial insemination) vs sex-sorted semen comparison box, cross-linked to Rashtriya Gokul Mission.

### 5. 'Grass Mache' — Dal Lake's weed harvest becomes raw material for Kashmir's papier-mâché — Art & Culture

**Headline.** A Srinagar artisan, Simran Irshad, has developed a technique that dries and pulverises the aquatic weed routinely dredged out of Dal Lake, blends it with recycled paper pulp and a binder, and moulds it using the methods of Kashmir's GI-tagged **papier-mâché (kar-i-qalamdani)** craft — producing vases, ornaments and décor with a stone-like finish, with fireproof switchboards and panelling under trial.

**Exam angle.** A rare item that sits on three syllabus lines at once: a **traditional GI-registered handicraft** under strain from input costs; an **invasive-weed and eutrophication** problem in a Himalayan lake; and a **waste-to-wealth, green-livelihood** model of the kind Mains asks about under "conservation through local communities". Treat it as a case study, not a fact — it is a ready-made example paragraph rather than a Prelims target.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **Kashmiri papier-mâché** — introduced via Persia, traditionally associated with **Sultan Zain-ul-Abidin (Budshah, 15th century)**; two stages, *sakhtsazi* (moulding) and *naqashi* (painting); GI-registered.
- **Dal Lake**, Srinagar — an **urban, eutrophic** lake whose weed and algal load is driven by untreated sewage and catchment nutrient loading; managed by the J&K **Lakes Conservation and Management Authority** (successor to LAWDA). **Dal is *not* a Ramsar site** — J&K/Ladakh's Ramsar wetlands include **Wular, Hokera, Surinsar–Mansar, Hygam, Shallbugh** and **Tso Moriri**.
- The generalisable principle: **biomass removed for ecological reasons acquires value only when a downstream use exists** — compare Assam's *Pani Meteka* craft from water hyacinth.

**Notebook.** GS1 — Art & Culture (Indian handicrafts), with a GS3 Environment cross-reference.

**Static anchor.** Nitin Singhania *Indian Art & Culture* — chapter on Indian handicrafts / Indian paintings (Kashmiri papier-mâché). *Update:* maintain a running GI-crafts table (craft → state → year of GI) and add a cross-link from Dal Lake in the Shankar IAS wetlands chapter to this craft entry.

### C. Mains-Style Question

**Q7 (GS3, 15 marks, 250 words).** *"A circular economy is a question of product design, not of waste disposal."* **Examine** this proposition in the Indian context, and assess the adequacy of the present Extended Producer Responsibility framework in delivering circularity.

**Model-answer skeleton.**

1. **Frame the distinction.** Recycling is end-of-pipe: it recovers material after value has already been destroyed and is thermodynamically lossy (downcycling). Circularity intervenes upstream — durability, modularity, repairability, standardised components, remanufacture and reuse — so that material stays in its highest-value form. Anchor with WCEF 2026's Gandhinagar theme and India's Co-Chairship of the 10YFP Board.

2. **What India has built.** EPR as the principal instrument — first introduced in the e-Waste (Management and Handling) Rules, 2011, now extended to plastics (PWM Rules 2016, amended 2022) and batteries (BWM Rules 2022); NITI Aayog's 11 sectoral circular-economy committees (2021); Vehicle Scrappage Policy and the Battery/Critical Minerals recycling link; Mission LiFE as the demand-side complement.

3. **Where it falls short.** EPR is discharged largely through *tradable recycling certificates*, which price the recovery of waste but send no design signal — no eco-modulated fee that rewards a repairable product over a sealed one. Add: the informal sector processes the bulk of e-waste and plastic but is outside the certificate architecture; weak SPCB monitoring and certificate-fraud risk; no right-to-repair or minimum-durability mandate with teeth; municipal segregation still the binding constraint.

4. **Way forward and close.** Eco-modulated EPR fees; mandatory recycled-content thresholds; formalising aggregators and integrating waste-pickers (NAMASTE-style) into EPR value chains; product-passport style disclosure for electronics and batteries; and framing circularity as **critical-mineral security** — every kilogram of lithium or cobalt recovered is a kilogram not imported. Conclude that EPR is a necessary but insufficient condition: it internalises disposal cost, whereas circularity requires internalising *design* cost.
