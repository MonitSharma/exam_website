## PART A — BRIEFING (5 items)

### 1. DRDO flight-tests 'Kusha', India's first indigenous long-range SAM — Science & Technology

**Headline:** DRDO conducted the maiden flight test of the Kusha Long-Range Surface-to-Air Missile (LR-SAM) from Dr APJ Abdul Kalam Island, Odisha, on 23 July 2026, intercepting an electronic target simulating a high-speed, high-altitude aerial threat.

**Exam angle:** This is the top layer of India's emerging indigenous multi-tier air-defence grid — the domestic answer to the S-400 dependency. It slots directly into the "Atmanirbharta in defence" and "defence indigenisation" narrative that GS3 keeps returning to, and it is a clean Prelims fact-card (system name + test site + developer). For a Physics-optional aspirant, the interesting hook is that the system is not one missile but a *sensor-shooter chain*: long-range surveillance radar → fire-control radar → command-and-control → interceptor.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Full name: Kusha LR-SAM; developed by DRDO with Indian industry partners.
- Test site: Dr APJ Abdul Kalam Island (formerly Wheeler Island), off the Odisha coast.
- **All** elements — missile, launcher, surveillance and fire-control radars, and the command-and-control centre — are indigenously developed. This is the exam trap: no imported radar.
- Target set: fighter aircraft, cruise missiles, UAVs/drones, and large aircraft including AEW&C platforms.
- Defence Secretary and DRDO Chairman: Rajesh Kumar Singh. Defence Minister Rajnath Singh called it a "giant leap."

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology / Internal Security (Defence).

**Static anchor:** Your Sci-Tech notes on *Indian Missile Systems*. Update the SAM ladder table — QRSAM (short) → Akash / Akash-NG (medium) → MR-SAM/Barak-8 (medium-long) → **Kusha (long)** → S-400 (imported, long). Cross-link to the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP) page and note that Kusha is *outside* IGMDP, which closed in 2008.

### 2. Cabinet approves BHAVYA Rasayan — ₹3,030 crore for three dedicated Chemical Parks — Economy

**Headline:** The Union Cabinet on 24 July 2026 approved *Bharat Audyogik Vikas Yojana Rasayan* (BHAVYA Rasayan), a five-year scheme (FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31) to create three chemical parks with plug-and-play common infrastructure. It was announced in the FY 2026-27 Union Budget.

**Exam angle:** This is the cluster-based industrialisation template again — the same logic as PM MITRA textile parks, Bulk Drug Parks, Medical Device Parks and NIMZ. The examiner's angle is *why* clusters: shared effluent treatment and hazardous-waste infrastructure fix the two things that individually kill small chemical units — compliance cost and logistics cost. It also carries an import-substitution story, since India runs a large chemicals trade deficit with China.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Outlay **₹3,030 crore** = ₹3,000 crore for common infrastructure + ₹30 crore administrative.
- Central grant **up to ₹1,000 crore per park**, contingent on a **minimum ₹500 crore contribution by the State Government**.
- **Three parks**, selected from States via the **Challenge Route**.
- Each park: minimum contiguous **8 sq km (≈2,000 acres)** of encumbrance-free land.
- Common facilities include a **Common Effluent Treatment Plant (CETP)**, a **Treatment, Storage and Disposal Facility (TSDF)**, solvent recovery and distillation, steam generation, interconnected pipelines, warehousing.
- Framed under the *Viksit Bharat @ 2047* goal; downstream users named are agriculture, textiles, pharma, nutraceuticals, construction, automobile, electronics.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Industrial Policy / Infrastructure).

**Static anchor:** Mrunal Pillar 4A (Industry & Infrastructure) or Ramesh Singh's Industry chapter. Add BHAVYA Rasayan to your **industrial-cluster comparison table** alongside PCPIR, NIMZ, PM MITRA, Bulk Drug Parks and SEZ. Cross-link to Environment notes: CETP and TSDF are also Hazardous and Other Wastes (Management and Transboundary Movement) Rules, 2016 vocabulary.

### 3. India hosts the 3rd BRICS Ministerial on Disaster Risk Reduction under its 2026 Chairship — International Relations

**Headline:** The NDMA, under the Ministry of Home Affairs, hosted the 3rd BRICS Ministerial Meeting on Disaster Risk Reduction in New Delhi on 24 July 2026, chaired by MoS Home Nityanand Rai; a BRICS Joint Statement on DRR was adopted unanimously. Separately, India has invited Bangladesh PM Tarique Rahman as a special guest to the BRICS Summit in New Delhi (reported for 17–19 September 2026).

**Exam angle:** Two questions in one. First, India's BRICS chairship year — theme, deliverables, and the fact that BRICS now runs *sectoral ministerials*, not just leaders' summits. Second, the DRR angle lets you connect BRICS to the Sendai Framework and to the CDRI, which India itself launched — a strong Mains paragraph on India using plurilateral platforms to export its disaster-governance model.

**Facts to memorise:**

- India's **BRICS Chairship 2026** theme: *"Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability"* — note the acronym-friendly **BRICS** ordering.
- Nodal body: **National Disaster Management Authority (NDMA)**, under the **Ministry of Home Affairs** (not Earth Sciences — a standard trap).
- Outcome: **BRICS Joint Statement on DRR**, advancing the **BRICS DRR Work Plan (2025–2028)**.
- Bangladesh is **neither a BRICS member nor a partner country** — the invitation is as a special guest.
- Link forward: Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction 2015–2030 (four priorities, seven global targets); Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (CDRI), launched by India at the 2019 UN Climate Action Summit.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations; cross-tagged GS3 — Disaster Management.

**Static anchor:** Your IR notes on **BRICS** (members, New Development Bank, Contingent Reserve Arrangement, expansion and the "partner country" category). Add a one-line entry: *India Chair 2026 → DRR ministerial → Joint Statement*. Cross-link to the Disaster Management chapter: NDMA composition, DM Act 2005, Sendai, CDRI.

### 4. Sarnath inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List — India's 45th property — Art & Culture

**Headline:** On 25 July 2026, at the **48th Session of the World Heritage Committee at Busan, South Korea**, the "Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath" was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List — India's 45th World Heritage property.

**Exam angle:** A near-certain Prelims fact and a ready-made Mains example for Buddhist heritage diplomacy. The site is inscribed as a **serial property** with two components, under **cultural criteria (iii) and (vi)** — the "intangible association" criterion, which is the one examiners like to test. It also revives the Mauryan-art syllabus: the Ashokan pillar whose Lion Capital became India's State Emblem was excavated here.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Two component parts: **Chaukhandi Stupa** + **Archaeological Remains of Sarnath** (Dhamekh Stupa, Dharmarajika Stupa, Mulagandhakuti Vihara, Ashokan Pillar).
- Location: **Varanasi district, Uttar Pradesh**. Ancient names: **Rishipatana, Ishipatana, Mrigadava** ("Deer Park").
- Significance: the Buddha's first sermon — ***Dharmachakra Pravartana***, "Turning of the Wheel of the Law" — which founded the **Sangha** and set out the **Noble Eightfold Path**.
- Criteria **(iii)** and **(vi)**. Inscribed under India's **2025-26 nomination cycle**; on the Tentative List since **1998**.
- Numbers: **India = 45 sites**, ranked **6th globally** and **2nd in Asia-Pacific**. India has **69 sites on the Tentative List**. Each State Party may propose only **one** site per year.
- Predecessor: **Maratha Military Landscapes of India**, inscribed at the 47th Session (Paris, 2025). India hosted the WHC for the first time in **July 2024, New Delhi**, and was a Committee member 2021–25.
- Three of the four principal Buddhist pilgrimage sites are now World Heritage: **Lumbini (Nepal), Mahabodhi Temple Bodh Gaya (Bihar), Sarnath (UP)** — Kushinagar is the fourth, and is *not* yet inscribed.
- Nodal agency: **Archaeological Survey of India**. Sarnath Museum (1904) is the ASI's **oldest site museum**.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Art & Culture / Ancient History.

**Static anchor:** Nitin Singhania, chapter on **Indian Architecture — Mauryan art (pillars, stupas)** and the **World Heritage Sites** appendix. Update the running count to 45 and add Sarnath's two components. Cross-link to Ancient History notes on Ashoka's pillar edicts and to the **State Emblem of India (Prohibition of Improper Use) Act, 2005**.

### 5. Kaimur Wildlife Sanctuary expanded by gazette notification; on track to be Bihar's second tiger reserve — Environment

**Headline:** The MoEFCC has issued a gazette notification expanding the Kaimur Wildlife Sanctuary by about 30 sq km to **1,539.862 sq km** and enlarging its eco-sensitive zone, setting the stage for its declaration as Bihar's second tiger reserve.

**Exam angle:** Classic "Places in News" plus an ESZ-procedure question. The chain of authority is the testable part: a **wildlife sanctuary** is notified by the **State Government** under the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972; an **eco-sensitive zone** is notified by the **MoEFCC** under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986; a **tiger reserve** is notified by the State on the recommendation of the **NTCA**. Also a good corridor-connectivity case study — Kaimur links the Vindhyan landscape across Bihar, UP, MP and Jharkhand.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Located in the **Kaimur and Rohtas districts of Bihar**, on the **Kaimur Range** — the eastern extension of the **Vindhyas**.
- Bihar's **largest wildlife sanctuary**; new area **1,539.862 sq km** after a ~30 sq km addition.
- ESZ now covers roughly **108 villages in Rohtas and 144 in Kaimur** after 15 villages were added; industrial activity restricted within the notified belt.
- Would become **Bihar's second tiger reserve** after **Valmiki Tiger Reserve** (West Champaran, in the Terai). Final declaration needs **NTCA** clearance.
- Fauna: leopard, striped hyena, Indian wolf, sloth bear, chital, sambar, **four-horned antelope (chousingha)**, nilgai.
- Nearby: Karkatgarh waterfall, Durgavati reservoir; the sanctuary sits on the Son–Karmanasa drainage side of the plateau.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Ecology; GS1 — Geography (Places in News).

**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS Environment, chapter on the **Protected Area Network** and the **Eco-Sensitive Zone** section. Add Kaimur to your Bihar protected-areas card (Valmiki TR, Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary, Kanwar Taal Ramsar site) and to the tiger-reserve tally. Cross-link to Physical Geography notes on the **Vindhyan Range / Peninsular Plateau**.

### C. Mains-Style Question

**GS3 — Economy / Industrial Policy (250 words, 15 marks)**

> *"Cluster-based industrial parks address the cost of compliance more effectively than they address the cost of capital."* **Examine** this proposition with reference to India's recent chemical-park initiative.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the problem.** India is a top-six global chemicals producer but runs a persistent chemicals trade deficit; the binding constraints for small and mid-sized units are effluent-treatment compliance, hazardous-waste disposal, high logistics costs from dispersed location, and long land-acquisition timelines — not primarily the cost of debt.
2. **Where the cluster model delivers.** Shared CETP, TSDF and hazardous-waste infrastructure convert a fixed cost that is prohibitive per-unit into a shared variable cost; co-location of upstream, downstream and ancillary units cuts inter-firm logistics; pre-cleared encumbrance-free land removes the acquisition risk; centralised monitoring improves regulatory compliance and makes exports to standards-heavy markets viable. Cite BHAVYA Rasayan's ₹3,030 crore outlay, up to ₹1,000 crore Central grant per park with a ₹500 crore State match, and the 8 sq km land floor.
3. **Where it does not.** The scheme funds *common infrastructure*, not firm-level capex or working capital; it does not address feedstock security (naphtha/gas pricing, crude-linked volatility), tariff inversion on intermediates, or R&D depth in speciality chemicals. Only three parks are envisaged, so the demonstration effect is narrow, and the Challenge Route plus a ₹500 crore State match may favour fiscally stronger States, widening regional imbalance.
4. **Way forward and verdict.** Pair the parks with feedstock policy and PLI-type output-linked support for speciality chemicals; build in third-party environmental audit of the CETP/TSDF so shared infrastructure does not become a shared liability; ensure viability-gap support for lagging States. Conclude that the proposition largely holds — the model is a compliance-and-logistics instrument, and treating it as a substitute for capital-market and feedstock reform would overstate what it can deliver for Viksit Bharat @ 2047.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (b).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: the Ministry of Defence has stated that *every* major element — missile, launcher, surveillance and fire-control radars, and the command-and-control centre — is indigenously developed by DRDO laboratories with Indian industry partners.

**Q2 — (d).** All three are correct. Total outlay ₹3,030 crore (₹3,000 crore infrastructure + ₹30 crore administrative), Central grant up to ₹1,000 crore per park against a minimum ₹500 crore State contribution, three parks via the Challenge Route, each on at least 8 sq km (≈2,000 acres) of encumbrance-free land.

**Q3 — (c).** Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Statement 2 is wrong: the NDMA functions under the **Ministry of Home Affairs**, not the Ministry of Earth Sciences. The 3rd BRICS DRR Ministerial was held in New Delhi on 24 July 2026 and adopted a Joint Statement advancing the BRICS DRR Work Plan (2025–2028).

**Q4 — (c).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: Sarnath was inscribed under **cultural** criteria **(iii) and (vi)** at the 48th Session held at Busan, South Korea — not under natural criteria.

**Q5 — (a).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: the expansion notification was issued by the **Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change**. The NTCA's role comes later — its recommendation is required before a State can formally declare a tiger reserve.

**Q6 — (b).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct — the Sarnath Lion Capital originally carried a large Dharmachakra above the four addorsed lions, and Chunar sandstone was the standard material. Statement 3 is wrong: Ashokan pillars are **free-standing monolithic columns** quarried, dressed and polished at one site and then transported, unlike genuinely rock-cut architecture such as the Barabar caves.

**Q7 — (c).** Statements 1 and 2 are correct — the PM chairs the NDMA ex officio, and the NIDM is a statutory body under the Disaster Management Act, 2005. Statement 3 is wrong: the NDRF is headed by a **Director General**, an officer of the rank of an IPS-cadre DG drawn from the CAPFs, not by the Union Home Secretary.
