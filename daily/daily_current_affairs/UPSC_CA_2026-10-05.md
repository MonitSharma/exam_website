## Briefing

### 1. Swiss President Parmelin's state visit: Modi pitches "$100 billion and 10 lakh jobs" under India–EFTA TEPA — International Relations
**Exam angle.** Swiss President Guy Parmelin began a three-day state visit on 5 Oct 2026 (talks at Hyderabad House, Rajghat tribute). The summit is the first big political push on the India–EFTA TEPA after it came into force, with MoUs including migration and mobility, plus talk of cooperation in S&T, nuclear energy, transport and defence. Prelims angle: what EFTA is and who is in it. Mains angle: FTAs with non-EU European blocs, investment-linked trade deals, mobility provisions.
**Memorise.**
- TEPA signed 10 Mar 2024; in force 1 Oct 2025; India's first FTA with a European economic bloc (Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland).
- Target: $100 billion investment and 10 lakh (1 million) direct jobs in India over 15 years.
- Switzerland is the biggest EFTA trade and investment partner of India; Swiss focus sectors: biotech, life sciences, banking, insurance, food processing, sustainability.
- Second India–EFTA Prosperity Summit follows; reports give 7 Oct and 8 Oct (sources differ).
- Flag: one report says TEPA came into force in "October 2026"; this is wrong, it was 1 Oct 2025.

### 2. CAFE-III notified: tighter fuel-economy targets for 2027–32, with a 3x credit for EVs — Economy
**Exam angle.** The Ministry of Power notified the third phase of Corporate Average Fuel Economy norms for passenger cars on 30 Sept 2026. The fleet benchmark tightens about 16.7% over five years, and a credit-trading system lets over-achievers sell to others or to the Bureau of Energy Efficiency. Useful for energy security, climate (transport emissions), the auto industry and the EV/hybrid/ethanol policy mix. New sub-development versus the 22 Sept car-sales item: this is the regulatory lever behind that shift.
**Memorise.**
- Period: 1 Apr 2027 to 31 Mar 2032; two blocks (FY28–FY30, FY31–FY32); credits carry forward within a block and lapse after it.
- Target: from 3.996 to 3.3273 litres per 100 km (about 16.7% tighter).
- Super-credit multipliers: BEV and range-extended EV 3x; plug-in and strong-hybrid flex-fuel 2.5x; strong hybrid 1.6x; flex-fuel 1.1x.
- Buy-back price by BEE: ₹2,500 per g CO2/km in FY28 rising to ₹4,500 in FY32.
- Small makers (under 1,000 vehicles a year) are exempt from targets but must report.
- Legal base: Energy Conservation Act, 2001 (BEE is the implementing agency).

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (Energy, Infrastructure) and Environment.
**Static anchor:** Ramesh Singh "Energy / Climate change"; Shankar IAS "Vehicular pollution, BS norms". *Nudge:* add a CAFE I–II–III timeline next to your BS-VI and EV-policy notes and link it to the 22 Sept alt-fuel sales item.

### 3. IWC-70 in Hobart: India reaffirms science-based cetacean conservation as the 1946 whaling convention turns 80 — Environment
**Exam angle.** The International Whaling Commission's 70th meeting ran 28 Sept–2 Oct 2026 in Hobart, Australia, marking 80 years of the International Convention for the Regulation of Whaling. India stressed science-based conservation and better data on cetacean status, distribution and breeding, and pointed to the Wildlife (Protection) Act, marine protected areas and Project Dolphin. Typical Prelims "statement" fodder on international bodies.
**Memorise.**
- IWC: set up under the ICRW, 1946 (Washington); commercial-whaling moratorium in effect since 1986; not a UN agency.
- India joined in 1981 and supports conservation, not whaling.
- India's tools: WPA 1972 (highest protection for cetaceans), Project Dolphin, marine protected areas.
- Arabian Sea humpback whale is among the species flagged for priority action.
- Related: Indian Ocean Whale Sanctuary (1979) and Southern Ocean Sanctuary (1994), from general knowledge, not from today's reports.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment.
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS "International conventions and bodies: IWC, CITES, CMS"; Mrunal Environment pillar, "Marine conservation". *Nudge:* add IWC to your conventions table (year, HQ, India's status) and cross-link to the Buxa/GIB conservation entries.

### 4. PRIP's new 'Discovery Track': up to ₹50 crore for start-ups to build new medicines — Science & Technology
**Exam angle.** The Department of Pharmaceuticals opened a New Discovery Track under the Promotion of Research and Innovation in Pharma and MedTech (PRIP) scheme, alongside approving 41 first-round projects worth about ₹1,600 crore. It targets the early, risky end of drug discovery, where India is weak despite being the "pharmacy of the world". Mains hook: shifting from generics to innovation, and public money with private co-funding.
**Memorise.**
- PRIP outlay ₹5,000 crore; implemented by the Department of Pharmaceuticals (Ministry of Chemicals and Fertilizers).
- Three tracks: New Discovery (up to ₹50 crore per project, 25% institutional-investor co-funding), Early Stage (up to ₹5 crore), Later Stage (up to ₹100 crore).
- Discovery track targets startups and MSMEs at TRL 1-3 for new chemical or biological medicines.
- First round: 41 projects, about ₹1,600 crore; second call is open.

**Notebook:** GS3 Sci-Tech (Biotech/Health).
**Static anchor:** Ramesh Singh / Shankar IAS "Pharma sector, PLI schemes"; Mrunal Sci-Tech pillar, "Biotechnology policy". *Nudge:* put PRIP beside PLI (pharma) and BioE3 in your "pharma R&D schemes" comparison.

### 5. Delhi High Court: a valid visa is not a right of entry — Polity & Governance
**Exam angle.** On 1 Oct 2026 Justice Amit Mahajan dismissed a Turkmenistan national's plea after she was denied entry, holding that visa issue and permission to enter are separate and officers keep discretion on grounds like national security, foreign policy and document mismatches. A neat peg for the citizen–alien distinction in fundamental rights and the executive's sovereign power over entry.
**Memorise.**
- Visa gives permission to apply for entry; the final decision rests with the immigration officer.
- Petition failed due to mismatched personal details and no business documents for the stated purpose.
- Constitutional frame: Article 21 (and 14) apply to all "persons"; Article 19 freedoms, including residence and settlement, are for citizens only.
- Reported statutes: the court spoke of the statutory immigration framework; the exact Act cited was not confirmed in my sources.

**Notebook:** GS2 Polity.
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth "Fundamental Rights" and "Citizenship". *Nudge:* add a "rights of persons vs citizens" table and this case as a one-line example.

### C. Mains Question
**Examine how the CAFE-III norms can reconcile India's energy-security, climate and industrial-policy goals in the passenger-vehicle sector. (15 marks, 250 words)**

*Model-answer skeleton:*
1. Intro: CAFE-III (notified 30 Sept 2026) tightens the fleet benchmark from 3.996 to 3.3273 L/100 km over 2027–32 and introduces credit trading.
2. Energy security and climate: lower oil intensity per km, a push to EVs, hybrids and flex-fuel through 3x, 2.5x, 1.6x and 1.1x super-credits, and a carbon price signal via BEE buy-back (₹2,500 to ₹4,500).
3. Industry and consumers: technology-neutral credits help hybrid and ethanol routes, but compliance costs for small and heavy-SUV makers, possible price rises, and exemptions below 1,000 vehicles a year.
4. Way forward: charging and battery supply chain, clear credit-market rules, lifecycle emissions accounting, and aligning CAFE with ethanol blending and PLI auto schemes.
