### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Polity, 15 marks, ~250 words)

**Q.** Examine the constitutional distinction between the pardoning power of the President under Article 72 and that of the Governor under Article 161. In light of the Supreme Court's July 2026 ruling reaffirming that a Governor's Article 161 remission policy cannot be displaced by a subsequent statutory policy, discuss whether such an independent executive clemency power is compatible with the rule of law.

**Model-answer skeleton:**
1. **Constitutional basis and scope:** Article 72 (President) is broader — covers court-martial sentences, death sentences, and offences against Union laws; Article 161 (Governor) is narrower — confined to offences against State laws, and excludes court-martial and death-sentence cases.
2. **The July 2026 verdict's holding:** Article 161 power is "distinct and independent… uninfluenced by any other power, more so statutory in nature" — a statutory CrPC-based remission scheme cannot override it; the 2021 *Raj Kumar* verdict was declared per incuriam against the larger Bench in *Jagdish* (2010).
3. **Rule-of-law concerns:** Risk of inconsistent or politically-motivated remission decisions shielded from statutory checks; but the Supreme Court has consistently subjected mercy powers to judicial review for arbitrariness or mala fide (*Maru Ram*, *Kehar Singh*, *Epuru Sudhakar*).
4. **Way forward:** Codified, time-bound remission guidelines with reasoned, recorded orders and institutional safeguards (sentence review boards) to reconcile executive discretion with constitutional accountability.
