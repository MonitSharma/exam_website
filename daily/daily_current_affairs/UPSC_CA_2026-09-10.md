## Part A — Briefing

### 1. MHA puts a draft Article 371(K) on the table for Ladakh — a legislature without statehood — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** At a sub-committee meeting with the Leh Apex Body (LAB) and the Kargil Democratic Alliance (KDA) in New Delhi on **9 September 2026**, the Ministry of Home Affairs proposed inserting a new provision — reported as **Article 371(K)**, a new "Chapter K" in **Part XXI** of the Constitution — creating a **directly elected governing body with legislative powers** for the Union Territory of Ladakh.

**Exam angle:** This is the highest-value Polity item of the day because it sits at the intersection of three separate syllabus strands. First, it is a *third route* to Ladakh's demands: not statehood, not the Sixth Schedule, but a bespoke Part XXI special provision. Second, it tests the Article 239A / Article 240 distinction — Ladakh is a UT *without* a legislature, so the President currently legislates for it by regulation under **Article 240**; the proposal would carve legislative subjects out of that regulation-making power. Third, the design is explicitly modelled on **Articles 371A (Nagaland)** and **371G (Mizoram)**, where Parliamentary laws on land and customary practice do not apply to the State unless the local legislature so resolves — a "consent-gated" application of central law that Prelims loves. Note the honest gap: police, public order, finance and budgetary autonomy are reportedly **left undecided**, which is exactly where the Delhi (NCT) litigation history says the real fight will be.

**Facts to memorise:**

- Date of the sub-committee meeting: **9 September 2026**; interlocutors: **Leh Apex Body** and **Kargil Democratic Alliance**; nodal ministry: **MHA**.
- Proposal: **Article 371(K)** / Chapter K under **Part XXI** ("Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions", Articles 369–392).
- Ladakh became a **UT without legislature** on **31 October 2019** under the **J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019**; it retains **LAHDC Leh (1995)** and **LAHDC Kargil (2003)**.
- Proposed legislative domain: **land and transfer of property, culture and language, forests, environment, natural resources**.
- Explicit models cited: **Article 371A (Nagaland)** and **371G (Mizoram)** — religious/social practices, customary law, land ownership; central law needs Assembly assent.
- Left open: **finance and budget, executive decision-making, police and public order**.
- The letter "**K**" is the next unused sub-letter in the series: 371A Nagaland, 371B Assam, 371C Manipur, 371D–E Andhra Pradesh (and Telangana), 371F Sikkim, 371G Mizoram, 371H Arunachal Pradesh, 371I Goa, 371J Karnataka (Hyderabad-Karnataka).
- Demands *not* conceded: full statehood, **Sixth Schedule** inclusion.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Federalism / Special Provisions / Union Territories).

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth* — chapter on **Special Provisions for Some States** (Articles 371–371J) and the chapter on **Union Territories** (Articles 239, 239A, 239AA, 240, 241). **Update:** extend your 371A→371J table with a pencilled row "**371K — Ladakh (proposed, MHA, Sep 2026)**", and add a one-line margin note under Article 240: "regulation-making power for UTs without legislature — the 371(K) proposal would fence off land/forest/culture from it." Cross-link to your **Sixth Schedule** note (Shankar IAS / Laxmikanth) with the contrast: Sixth Schedule = *autonomous district councils inside a State*; the Ladakh proposal = *a UT-wide elected legislature*.

### 2. PM-KUSUM 2.0 is coming with a dedicated agrivoltaics component — 10 GW of solar *over* the crop — Agriculture

**Headline:** Speaking at the curtain-raiser for the **7th AgriVoltaics World Conference 2026** (New Delhi, **21–23 October 2026**), Union Minister for New and Renewable Energy **Pralhad Joshi** said **PM-KUSUM 2.0** will be announced shortly and is expected to carry a dedicated **agri-photovoltaics component of about 10 GW**.

**Exam angle:** Agrivoltaics is a textbook "land-use efficiency" answer: the same hectare yields a crop *and* electricity, so the **Land Equivalent Ratio** exceeds 1. That framing works in GS3 Agriculture (farm income diversification), GS3 Energy (decentralised RE), and GS3 Environment (land competition between food and fuel — the same argument that dogs biofuels). The policy logic is also a nice contrast with KUSUM 1.0: Component A already allowed ground-mounted plants on *barren or fallow* farmland; a dedicated agri-PV component is a shift to **cultivated** land, which changes the constraint from land availability to panel-height engineering and shade-tolerant cropping. The sceptical counterpoint worth writing: raised-structure agri-PV has materially higher capex per MW than conventional ground-mount, and shading cuts yields for C4 cereals more than for shade-tolerant horticulture — so this is a horticulture/fodder story, not a paddy story.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **PM-KUSUM** = *Pradhan Mantri Kisan Urja Suraksha evam Utthaan Mahabhiyan*, launched **2019**, under the **Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE)**; original outlay **₹34,422 crore**, target **34,800 MW** by March 2026.
- Three components: **A** — decentralised ground-mounted grid-connected solar on barren/fallow farmland (up to 2 MW each); **B** — standalone solar pumps replacing diesel pumps; **C** — solarisation of existing grid-connected pumps, including **feeder-level** solarisation.
- **PM-KUSUM 2.0**: expected dedicated **agri-PV component ~10 GW**; outlay expected to exceed version 1.0.
- **Agrivoltaics / agri-PV** = co-location of solar generation and agriculture on the same land; panels raised or spaced so cropping continues beneath.
- Conference: **7th AgriVoltaics World Conference 2026**, New Delhi, **21–23 October 2026**.
- India's solar capacity: about **3 GW (2016)** → over **160 GW (mid-2026)**.
- Sibling schemes to cross-reference: **PM Surya Ghar Muft Bijli Yojana** (rooftop), **PM Surya Sarovar Yojana** (floating solar), **National Solar Mission** under NAPCC.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (farm income, irrigation energy) with a spillover into GS3 Energy.

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* Pillar **4B / Infrastructure–Energy** (renewables) **and** your Agriculture notes on *irrigation and farm power subsidy*. **Update:** add "agrivoltaics / Land Equivalent Ratio" as a defined term beside the PM-KUSUM three-component box, and write the one-line link: "agricultural power subsidy → free/flat-rate pumping → groundwater over-draft → **solarising the pump changes the fuel, not the incentive**; agri-PV adds a *revenue* stream, which is the part that can change behaviour." Cross-link to the **6 September geothermal policy** note as the other "non-solar, non-wind RE" entry in the same pillar.

### 3. DRDO floats SHIELD — an S-band high-power microwave rig to find out what actually kills a drone — Science & Technology

**Headline:** DRDO has issued a **Request for Proposal** under its **Technology Development Fund (TDF)** for **SHIELD** — an **S-band High-Power Microwave Integrated Evaluation system for Lethality and Damage** — an indigenous test platform to measure how high-power microwave (HPM) radiation degrades drone electronics, sensors, radars and communication links under controlled conditions.

**Exam angle:** The examinable subtlety is that **SHIELD is not a weapon — it is an instrument.** Directed-energy counter-drone claims are easy to make and hard to verify; a national lethality-and-vulnerability evaluation rig is the metrology layer that lets India certify its *own* HPM weapons and, equally, test the hardening of its *own* electronics against an adversary's. That "test-before-you-trust" logic is a strong Mains line on defence R&D maturity. The technology hook is **Gallium Nitride (GaN)** solid-state power amplifiers: wide-bandgap semiconductors with higher breakdown voltage, power density and thermal tolerance than silicon — the same material story that runs through modern AESA radars and EW, so it connects the item straight to your semiconductor notes. Also note the **TDF route**: DRDO funding start-ups, MSMEs and private industry rather than building in-house is itself a policy shift worth quoting.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **SHIELD** = **S**-band **H**igh-power microwave **I**ntegrated **E**valuation system for **L**ethality and **D**amage; issued as an **RFP**, not a contract.
- Funding route: **Technology Development Fund (TDF)** — DRDO's industry-led innovation scheme, open to start-ups, **MSMEs** and private firms.
- Core technology: indigenous **Gallium Nitride (GaN) solid-state power amplifiers**; **modular and scalable** architecture, including drone-mounted configurations.
- **S-band** ≈ **2–4 GHz** (IEEE designation) — the band used by many surveillance radars and by some satellite links.
- Purpose: controlled-condition evaluation of HPM effects on **drone electronics, sensors, radars, communication nodes and command links** — i.e. a vulnerability/lethality testbed.
- Prior art: DRDO HPM trials have disabled quadcopter drones at about **1 km**.
- HPM belongs to the **Directed Energy Weapon (DEW)** family alongside **lasers** (e.g. DRDO's Mk-II(A) laser DEW) and **particle beams**; HPM is an *area* effect, lasers are a *point* effect.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Defence R&D, indigenisation) with an Internal Security cross-tag (counter-UAS).

**Static anchor:** Your GS3 Sci-Tech notes on **Directed Energy Weapons / Defence R&D**, plus the **semiconductor materials** page. **Update:** add a two-row comparison — *Laser DEW: point target, line-of-sight, weather-sensitive* vs *HPM: area effect, penetrates through apertures, non-kinetic electronics kill* — and write "GaN" beside it with one line on wide-bandgap advantages. Cross-link to the **7 September DRDO pulsed laser diode / proximity fuze** entry and to the **1 September Semicon 2.0** entry; three notes in ten days now share the compound-semiconductor thread.

### 5. A new lichen from Mathikettan Shola — *Buellia maharajensis*, and why lichens are a survey story — Environment

**Headline:** Researchers have described **_Buellia maharajensis_**, a new **saxicolous** (rock-dwelling) lichen species, from **Mathikettan Shola National Park** in **Idukki district, Kerala**, recorded at about **1,351 metres**; the wider park survey logged roughly **250 lichen species**, including about **60 new records for Kerala** and **15 new records for India**.

**Exam angle:** Two separate exam uses. As *Places in News*, Mathikettan Shola anchors the **shola–grassland mosaic** of the southern Western Ghats — stunted montane evergreen forest in sheltered valley folds, surrounded by frost-limited grassland — a landscape UPSC has repeatedly probed. As *concept*, lichens are the classic **pioneer species** of xerarch (lithosere) succession and among the most sensitive **bioindicators of SO₂**, because they absorb water and nutrients directly from the atmosphere and have no cuticle or stomata to regulate uptake. The survey number is the quiet story: 15 species new to *India* from a 12.82 km² park is a statement about how thinly India's non-charismatic taxa have been sampled — a usable line on the taxonomic-impediment problem in biodiversity governance.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **_Buellia maharajensis_** — new **saxicolous** lichen (grows on rock), family **Caliciaceae**; recorded at ~**1,351 m**; named for **Maharaja's College, Ernakulam** on its 150th anniversary.
- Survey totals in the park: ~**250 lichen species**; ~**60 first records for Kerala**; **15 first records for India**.
- **Mathikettan Shola National Park** — **Idukki district, Kerala** (Santhanpara, Udumbanchola taluk); notified **21 November 2003**; area **12.82 km²**; lies in the belt between **Eravikulam NP** and **Pampadum Shola NP**.
- Kerala's shola-belt national parks to know together: **Eravikulam, Anamudi Shola, Mathikettan Shola, Pampadum Shola** (plus Silent Valley and Periyar in the wider Western Ghats set).
- **Lichen** = symbiosis of a **fungus (mycobiont)** and an **alga or cyanobacterium (photobiont)**; growth forms **crustose, foliose, fruticose**; substrate types **saxicolous (rock), corticolous (bark), terricolous (soil)**.
- Ecological roles: **pioneer species** in primary succession on bare rock (lithosere); **bioindicator of SO₂** — lichen deserts form around SO₂-polluted urban-industrial cores.
- **Shola** = tropical montane evergreen forest occurring in valley folds above ~1,500 m in the southern Western Ghats, interspersed with montane grassland; the Western Ghats are a **UNESCO World Heritage Site** and one of the world's biodiversity hotspots.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Ecology (biodiversity, protected areas) with a GS1 Geography cross-tag for the shola landscape.

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment* — chapters on **Ecological Succession** and **Biodiversity / Protected Areas of India**. **Update:** add *Buellia maharajensis* and the "15 new-to-India records" figure to your Places-in-News map beside Mathikettan Shola, and beside the lichen entry in the succession chapter write the two-line prompt: "pioneer on bare rock (lithosere) **and** SO₂ bioindicator — UPSC has asked the symbiosis definition (2014); the pollution-indicator angle is the untested half." Cross-link to the **8 September vulture** entry as the other 2026 "species-in-news" note.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Polity & Federalism, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *"Examine the constitutional options available for accommodating the governance demands of the Union Territory of Ladakh, and assess whether a bespoke provision under Part XXI offers a durable settlement."*

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points):**

1. **Frame the deficit precisely.** Ladakh became a UT *without* a legislature on 31 October 2019; the LAHDCs (Leh 1995, Kargil 2003) are statutory, not constitutional, and their powers are subordinate to the LG. The result is an elected local tier without legislative competence over land, ecology and culture — the very subjects that drive the demand. Anchor the answer in **Article 239A / 240** and in the LAB–KDA charter (statehood, Sixth Schedule, PSC, parliamentary representation).
2. **Lay out the three routes and their costs.** (i) **Statehood** — Article 3, needs Parliamentary legislation; strongest but a precedent-setting reversal of the 2019 reorganisation. (ii) **Sixth Schedule** — designed for autonomous district councils *within* States (Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram); extending it to a whole UT is a category mismatch, though the NCST recommended it. (iii) **A Part XXI provision on the 371A/371G model** — consent-gated application of central law on land and custom, plus a directly elected legislative body. Note that the proposed Article 371(K) takes route (iii).
3. **Test durability against the missing pieces.** A settlement that leaves **police, public order, finance and budget** unallocated reproduces the structural conflict litigated repeatedly in the NCT of Delhi cases (Article 239AA; the 2018 Constitution Bench and the 2023 services judgment). Legislative competence without fiscal autonomy or executive control over the administrative machinery is a formula for recurring LG-versus-elected-body friction.
4. **Conclude with the design principle, not a verdict.** Asymmetric federalism is a legitimate constitutional technique — Articles 370, 371 and the Fifth/Sixth Schedules are all instances — and its success has depended on two things: clarity about the *residuary* holder of power, and a durable fiscal channel. A Part XXI provision for Ladakh can be a settlement rather than an interim device only if it settles those two questions on the face of the text, alongside ecological safeguards for a fragile cold desert and employment guarantees the LAB–KDA have made central to their case.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 3 only.**
Statements 1 and 3 are correct: the MHA proposal inserts a new provision in **Part XXI** (Temporary, Transitional and Special Provisions) and contemplates a **directly elected** territorial body with legislative competence over land, transfer of property, culture and language, forests, environment and natural resources. Statement 2 is incorrect — the proposal is explicitly an alternative to statehood and to Sixth Schedule inclusion, both of which the LAB and KDA have demanded and neither of which is conceded.

**Q2 — (b).**
SHIELD = **S-band High-power microwave Integrated Evaluation system for Lethality and Damage**, floated by DRDO as an RFP under the **Technology Development Fund**. It is an **evaluation platform**, not a fielded weapon: it measures how high-power microwave radiation degrades drone electronics, sensors and communication links under controlled conditions, using indigenous **GaN solid-state power amplifiers**.

**Q3 — (b) 2 and 3 only.**
Statement 1 is incorrect: PM-KUSUM is administered by the **Ministry of New and Renewable Energy**, not the Agriculture Ministry. Statement 2 is correct: **Component C** covers solarisation of existing grid-connected agricultural pumps, including feeder-level solarisation. Statement 3 is correct: PM-KUSUM 2.0 is expected to carry a dedicated **agrivoltaics (agri-PV) component of about 10 GW**.

**Q4 — (c).**
At the 9th AIMMAF (New Delhi, 9 September 2026) ministers welcomed **ASEAN 2045: Our Shared Future** and the ASEAN Food, Agriculture and Forestry Sectoral Plan 2026–2030, and looked forward to the **ASEAN–India Medium-Term Plan of Action for Cooperation in Agriculture and Forestry, 2026–2030**. No binding tariff schedule, rice reserve or common agricultural market was created — AITIGA is a separate track.

**Q5 — (a).**
*Buellia maharajensis* is a newly described **saxicolous** (rock-dwelling) lichen of the family Caliciaceae, recorded at about **1,351 m** in **Mathikettan Shola National Park**, Idukki district, Kerala. The wider park survey documented about 250 lichen species, including 15 new records for India.

**Q6 — (b) Only three.**
Pairs 1, 2 and 4 are correct: **371A — Nagaland**, **371F — Sikkim**, **371J — Karnataka** (Hyderabad-Karnataka region). Pair 3 is incorrect: **371G is Mizoram**; **Manipur is 371C**.

**Q7 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.**
All three are correct. A lichen is a symbiosis of a fungal **mycobiont** with an algal or cyanobacterial **photobiont** (asked directly in Prelims 2014). Lichens are classic **pioneer species** in primary succession on bare rock. Because they absorb water and nutrients directly from the atmosphere and lack a protective cuticle and stomata, they are acutely sensitive to **sulphur dioxide** and are standard bioindicators of it — "lichen deserts" mark SO₂-polluted urban-industrial cores.
