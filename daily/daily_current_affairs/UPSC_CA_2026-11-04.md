## Briefing

### 1. Cabinet (reported 4 Nov): 92 GWh grid-storage VGF scheme, critical-minerals processing scheme, statistical-system revamp — Economy
**Exam angle:** Three decisions briefed by Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw. (a) A viability gap funding (VGF) scheme for 92 GWh of grid-scale energy storage: 30 GWh battery, 60 GWh pumped storage, 2 GWh emerging technologies, to integrate renewables and use transmission lines better. (b) A capex scheme for processing the 24 identified critical minerals (lithium and nickel included) from mined and recycled feedstock, aimed at semiconductors, batteries, electronics and defence. (c) A statistical-system revamp scheme of ₹4,371 crore for better survey methods, validation and ground-truthing. Together they link energy transition, supply-chain security and data credibility.
**Memorize:**
- 92 GWh = 30 battery + 60 pumped + 2 emerging; period reported as FY27–FY31.
- Critical minerals scheme: ₹2,814 crore (reported), 24 minerals, mined plus recycled.
- Statistical revamp: ₹4,371 crore.
- *Source conflict:* storage VGF outlay is ₹14,500 crore in most reports, "up to ₹17,500 crore" in one. Do not memorise it until the PIB release is checked.

### 2. Ganga Water Treaty nears expiry; MEA points to the Joint Rivers Commission (3 Nov briefing) — International Relations
**Exam angle:** The 1996 India–Bangladesh Ganga Water Treaty expires in December 2026 (reported 12 Dec). MEA spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said both countries have a "structured system" and an established mechanism, the Joint Rivers Commission (JRC), to discuss water sharing. No renewal-talk dates have been announced; India has reportedly invited Bangladesh PM Tarique Rahman to visit New Delhi. Renewal is a test of neighbourhood diplomacy, with Teesta still unresolved.
**Memorize:**
- Signed 1996 by H.D. Deve Gowda and Sheikh Hasina; 30-year term (static knowledge).
- Governs dry-season Ganga flows at Farakka; JRC set up in 1972 (static knowledge).
- Teesta sharing is a separate, unsigned issue.
- Not verified: Bangladesh's formal position on renewal.

**Notebook:** GS2 International Relations (Neighbourhood).
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS *India–Bangladesh relations*; Mrunal *Water diplomacy*. Update: add the expiry date to the treaty timeline and cross-link to Teesta and the Indus Waters Treaty.

### 3. IndiaAI Mission releases EKA-1 and Dhīr 2B/5B foundation models (3 Nov) — Science & Technology
**Exam angle:** Under the IndiaAI Innovation Centre (Foundation Models) pillar, MeitY's IndiaAI Mission unveiled EKA-1 (Soket Labs; about 26.3 billion parameters, trained on about 7.5 trillion tokens; English plus all 22 scheduled languages; partial offline inference on smartphones) and Dhīr 2B and 5B (ZenteiQ; open-source, on-premise assistants; English plus 10 Indian languages). Both used subsidised national AI compute and are meant to be available via AIKosh. Exam hook: sovereign AI, language inclusion, compute access.
**Memorize:**
- EKA-1: 26.3 B parameters, about 7.5 T tokens, 22 scheduled languages.
- Dhīr: 2B and 5B variants, 10 Indian languages, open source.
- Platform: AIKosh (datasets and models repository).
- Not verified: the Mission's outlay (not stated in the source read).

### 4. Nagaland presses the Centre to scrap the Protected Area Permit regime before Hornbill Festival (reported 29 Oct) — Internal Security
**Exam angle:** CM Neiphiu Rio met the Union Home Minister seeking complete withdrawal of the PAP regime, saying it hurts foreign tourist arrivals, investment and growth. The Tourism Minister said the Home Minister promised to examine it quickly, while stressing "security and national issues are paramount". The 27th Hornbill Festival is in December 2026 (inaugural on 1 Dec reported). The MHA reinforced the PAP regime in December 2024 citing cross-border security concerns. Core tension: border security versus tourism and development.
**Memorize:**
- PAP: permit for foreign nationals in notified border areas, Centre-administered (static knowledge: Foreigners (Protected Areas) Order, 1958).
- ILP: for Indian citizens entering states like Arunachal, Mizoram, Nagaland, Manipur; Bengal Eastern Frontier Regulation, 1873.
- Do not conflate PAP (foreigners) with ILP (Indians).

**Notebook:** GS3 Internal Security (Border management) / GS2 Governance.
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth *Special provisions: Article 371A*; Ramesh Singh *Northeast insurgency & border areas*. Update: add a PAP vs ILP comparison table and cross-link to the 27 Oct Frontier Nagaland Territorial Authority note.

### 5. India's stance ahead of COP31, Antalya (9–20 Nov): undecided on "35 by 2035" electrification target — Environment
**Exam angle:** Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav said India has not decided whether to back the Turkish presidency's voluntary "35 by 2035" target (electricity's share of final energy demand from just over 20% to 35%). India argues climate goals must reflect national circumstances, immediate fossil-fuel phase-out is impractical, and voluntary initiatives should stay separate from formal negotiations; it will press for predictable climate finance and technology transfer.
**Memorize:**
- COP31: Antalya, Türkiye, 9–20 Nov 2026.
- Target proposed June 2026; voluntary under the Action Agenda.
- Principles invoked: common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities (CBDR-RC), national circumstances.

### B3. Mains question
**Q8.** *Examine* the challenges in renewing the Ganga Water Treaty with Bangladesh and suggest how India can balance its upper-riparian interests with neighbourhood diplomacy. (15 marks, 250 words)
**Model-answer skeleton:**
1. Intro: the 1996 treaty, a 30-year dry-season sharing arrangement at Farakka, expires in December 2026; MEA relies on the Joint Rivers Commission as the structured channel.
2. Challenges: Bangladesh seeks assured flows and a basin-wide approach including Teesta; climate change and upstream withdrawals alter dry-season flows; West Bengal's stake and Farakka siltation; domestic political change in Dhaka.
3. India's interests: Kolkata port navigability, irrigation and drinking water needs; a stable neighbourhood and a counter to external influence.
4. Way forward: data-sharing and joint flow monitoring, a basin-wide framework, early expert-level talks, linking renewal to a Teesta pathway and high-level political contact.

## Answers
- **Q1 — (d).** All three are correct as reported by Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw: 30 GWh battery + 60 GWh pumped storage + 2 GWh emerging technologies; 24 critical minerals (mined and recycled); statistical system revamp ₹4,371 crore. Note: the energy-storage outlay is reported as ₹14,500 crore by most outlets but up to ₹17,500 crore by one, so do not memorise the outlay.
- **Q2 — (c).** 1 and 2 are correct (signed 12 Dec 1996 by Deve Gowda and Sheikh Hasina; MEA spokesperson cited the Joint Rivers Commission on 3 Nov 2026). 3 is wrong: Teesta sharing has never been concluded and is outside this treaty, which deals with Ganga flows at Farakka.
- **Q3 — (c).** 2 and 3 are correct. 1 is wrong: EKA-1 (about 26.3 billion parameters) supports English and all 22 scheduled Indian languages. Dhīr 2B and 5B (ZenteiQ) support English and 10 Indian languages.
- **Q4 — (a).** 1 and 2 are correct (Chief Minister Neiphiu Rio raised it with the Home Minister; PAP is a Centre-notified regime for foreigners). 3 is wrong: the Inner Line Permit is for Indian citizens from outside the state and is issued by the state government under the Bengal Eastern Frontier Regulation, 1873.
- **Q5 — (b).** 1 and 3 are correct. 2 is wrong: the target, proposed by the Turkish presidency in June 2026, is a voluntary Action Agenda item aiming to lift electricity's share of final energy demand from about 20% to 35% by 2035; India wants voluntary initiatives kept separate from formal negotiations.
- **Q6 — (c).** 1 and 2 are correct (Manipur was brought under ILP in December 2019). 3 is wrong: the ILP regulates entry of Indian citizens from other states; foreigners need a Protected Area Permit or Restricted Area Permit.
- **Q7 — (b).** 1 and 2 are correct. 3 is wrong: the Indus Waters Treaty has no fixed expiry, unlike the 30-year Ganga treaty of 1996.
