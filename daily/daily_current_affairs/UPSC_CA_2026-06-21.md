## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. SC: Right to walk on a demarcated footpath is a Fundamental Right
**Subject: Polity & Governance | Notebook: GS2 — Polity (Fundamental Rights)**

- **What & when:** On **19 June 2026**, a SC bench of **Justices P.S. Narasimha and A.S. Chandurkar** held that the right to walk safely on a demarcated footpath is a fundamental right, with the pedestrian having **priority over motorised vehicles**. The Court retitled the matter *'Re: Fundamental Right to Walk and Footpath'* and pushed for a national pedestrian law.
- **Exam angle:** Classic case of **judicial expansion of Article 21** — the Court grounded the right in **Article 19(1)(d)** (free movement) read with Art 19(1)(a), (b), (c) **and Article 21**. It also read in a **positive state duty**: where a road exists, the State must demarcate and maintain a footpath. Pairs perfectly with the "Article 21 = bundle of derived rights" theme.
- **Key facts to memorise:** Bench = Narasimha + Chandurkar; basis = Art 19(1)(d) r/w 19(1)(a)/(b)/(c) + Art 21; origin = PIL traced to a fatal accident (5-year-old killed by a tanker where no footpath existed); thrust = pedestrian priority + national pedestrian law.
- **STATIC ANCHOR:** *Laxmikanth — "Fundamental Rights"* chapter, Art 21 sub-section. **Add a cross-link** in your Art 21 "derived rights" list (right to clean environment, livelihood, privacy, health, speedy trial → now **right to walk**).

### Mains-Style Question

**Q (GS2, 250 words).** *"Article 21 has evolved from a guarantee against arbitrary deprivation of life into a fountainhead of positive socio-economic rights."* **Examine** this statement in the light of recent judicial pronouncements, including the right to walk on safe footpaths.

**Model-answer skeleton:**
- **Intro:** From *A.K. Gopalan* (narrow, procedural) → *Maneka Gandhi* (1978: "procedure established by law" must be just, fair and reasonable) — the pivot that opened Art 21 to substantive content.
- **Expansion as negative + positive right:** Derived rights — clean environment (*Subhash Kumar*), livelihood (*Olga Tellis*), privacy (*Puttaswamy*, 2017), health, speedy trial, and now **safe footpaths / pedestrian priority (2026)** — show Art 21 imposing **positive duties** on the State.
- **Significance:** Bridges Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles; makes welfare obligations justiciable; deepens substantive due process.
- **Caveats / balance:** Risks of judicial overreach, resource/implementation burden on the executive, and the need to balance rights with separation of powers.
- **Conclusion:** A living, transformative Article 21 — judicial expansion must be matched by executive capacity and legislative backing (e.g., a national pedestrian law).
