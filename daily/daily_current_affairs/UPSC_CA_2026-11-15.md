## Part A — Briefing

### 1. India's October 2026 trade data: record exports, record imports, a 12-month-high merchandise deficit — Economy
**Exam angle:** Exports are the headline, but the deficit and the oil bill are the real story. Merchandise exports jumped 45% y/y (fastest in five years) on petroleum-product re-exports and electronics, while crude at a 14-year-high price pushed the merchandise gap up. The services surplus cushions the *overall* deficit. Separate RBI data: Q2 current account deficit (Sept) $7.4 bn; H1 FY27 CAD $25.8 bn.
**Memorise:**
- Merchandise exports $49.47 bn (record, +45%); imports $89.35 bn (record, +16.5%); merchandise deficit $39.88 bn (12-month high).
- Services exports $40.51 bn, imports $20.72 bn (surplus ~$19.79 bn); overall (goods + services) deficit $20.1 bn vs $25.1 bn a year ago.
- Crude imports +54% to $22.82 bn (Indian basket $119.91/bbl, 14-year high); petroleum-product exports ~doubled to $8.89 bn; gold imports −63% to $5.51 bn; electronics imports $14.32 bn, exports $6.64 bn.
- April–October FY27: exports $309.06 bn, imports $527.21 bn, merchandise deficit $218.15 bn. September CAD $7.4 bn; overall BoP surplus $8.8 bn; H1 CAD $25.8 bn. ICRA sees FY27 CAD ~0.9% of GDP.
**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (External sector).
**Static anchor:** Mrunal / Ramesh Singh — *Balance of Payments*: current account = merchandise trade + invisibles (services, income, transfers). *Nudge:* add an "Oct 2026" row to your BoP data card and a cross-link to "oil price shock → CAD → rupee (₹94.25/$ on 13 Nov)".

### 2. WHO Global TB Report 2026: domestic money now funds most TB spending as donors retreat — Reports & Indices
**Exam angle:** A funding-shift story: low- and middle-income countries are carrying the load while donor funding falls and research is far below target. India matters because South-East Asia holds about a third of the global burden and India anchors the TB-elimination push (target 2025 under the NTEP has already been missed — link to the 2030 SDG goal).
**Memorise:** (as reported by *The Hindu*, 14 Nov; India-specific 2026 figures not verified here)
- ~86% of TB spending in 2025 came from domestic sources; LMIC domestic funding $5.63 bn, of which BRICS-5 (Brazil, China, India, Russia, South Africa) gave $3.7 bn (65%).
- Need: $22 bn a year by 2027 (UN High-Level Meeting 2023 target). Donor funding fell 14% to $0.94 bn (2025). TB research $1.2 bn (2023) vs $5 bn target.
- WHO South-East Asia Region: 3.6 million new cases, 400,000 deaths in 2025 (about one-third of global total).
**Notebook:** GS2 Social Justice (Health) / Reports & Indices.
**Static anchor:** Spectrum/Laxmikanth — health schemes: NTEP, Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana, Pradhan Mantri TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan. *Nudge:* add "WHO Global TB Report" to your reports tracker with the issuing-body/annual-cycle line.

### 3. DPDP Act: Consent Manager framework goes live (13 Nov 2026) — Polity & Governance
**Exam angle:** The first operational piece of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 regime. A Consent Manager is a registered intermediary that lets a Data Principal give, manage, review and withdraw consent on one platform; it is accountable to the individual, not the Data Fiduciary. Mains hook: privacy (Art. 21) vs. innovation, and a data-protection ecosystem built on intermediaries (cf. the Account Aggregator model).
**Memorise:**
- Effective 13 Nov 2026 (Rule 4); Data Protection Board registers Consent Managers; MeitY Secretary launched the Board's portal.
- Eligibility: Indian company, minimum net worth ₹2 crore (First Schedule).
- Use of a Consent Manager is optional for fiduciaries. Substantive fiduciary obligations (notice, security, withdrawal handling) apply from 13 May 2027 (a MeitY proposal to shorten timelines was not confirmed as gazetted).
- Children: verifiable parental consent for under-18 data (DPDP Act).
**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (e-governance, rights) / GS3 cyber.
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth — *Fundamental Rights, Art. 21*; K.S. Puttaswamy (2017). *Nudge:* cross-link your Art. 21 note to DPDP Act 2023 and the Data Protection Board.

### B3. Mains question
**"The consent-manager model is touted as the backbone of India's data-protection regime. Critically examine its strengths and limitations in safeguarding the right to privacy." (15 marks, 250 words)**
Model-answer skeleton:
1. Intro: DPDP Act 2023; privacy as part of Art. 21 (Puttaswamy 2017); Consent Managers operational from 13 Nov 2026.
2. Strengths: single dashboard for giving/withdrawing consent; accountability to the Data Principal; interoperability (Account Aggregator parallel); regulated by the Data Protection Board with a ₹2 crore net-worth floor.
3. Limitations: use is optional for fiduciaries; consent fatigue and digital-literacy gaps; broad exemptions for the State; enforcement phased to 13 May 2027; concentration/security risk in intermediaries.
4. Way forward: purpose limitation, independent DPB capacity, vernacular consent UX, audit and liability for consent managers.

## Part C — Answers

1. **(b)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Merchandise deficit $39.88 bn vs overall $20.1 bn thanks to the services surplus; gold imports *fell* ~63% (statement 3 wrong).
2. **(b)** — About 86% of TB spending came from domestic sources (as reported). Donor funding fell 14%; SE Asia holds about one-third of global cases/deaths; the report is by WHO.
3. **(a)** — Consent Managers are accountable to the Data Principal and registered by the Data Protection Board; use is optional for fiduciaries, so 3 is wrong.
4. **(d)** — NIOT develops it; 3-person, 6,000 m rated sphere; part of Samudrayaan under the Deep Ocean Mission.
5. **(b)** — Birsa Munda (b. 15 Nov 1875), leader of the Munda Ulgulan.
6. **(d)** — FDI inflows are in the capital/financial account, not the current account.
7. **(c)** — K.S. Puttaswamy (2017) overruled M.P. Sharma and Kharak Singh on this point.
