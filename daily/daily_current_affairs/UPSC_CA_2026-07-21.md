### 1. Prevention of Insults to National Honour (Amendment) Bill, 2026 — statutory protection for *Vande Mataram* — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** On the opening day of the Monsoon Session (20 July 2026), Home Minister Amit Shah moved a Bill in the Rajya Sabha to extend to the National Song, *Vande Mataram*, the same criminal protection the 1971 Act gives the National Anthem.

**Exam angle:**
- This is the classic Prelims trap area — the Constitution names a National Anthem's *tune* nowhere; national symbols derive status from Constituent Assembly *statements* and ordinary law, not from any Article. UPSC loves the Anthem-vs-Song distinction.
- Mains angle: the tension between promoting national symbols and Article 19(1)(a); the *Bijoe Emmanuel v. State of Kerala* (1986) precedent that not singing the Anthem is not an offence if one stands respectfully.
- Timing is not incidental — it falls in the 150th-anniversary year of the composition of *Vande Mataram* (written 1875).

**Facts to memorise:**
- Parent law: **Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971**. Section 2 → insults to the **National Flag** and the **Constitution of India**; Section 3 → preventing the singing of / causing disturbance to the **National Anthem**. There is currently **no** provision for the National Song.
- Proposed penalty: up to **3 years' imprisonment, or fine, or both** — parity with the Anthem provision.
- *Vande Mataram*: composed by **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay** (1875), published in the novel **Anandamath (1882)**; first sung at the **1896 Calcutta session of the INC** (tune by Rabindranath Tagore).
- **24 January 1950** — Constituent Assembly President **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** stated that *Vande Mataram* "shall be honoured equally with *Jana Gana Mana* and shall have equal status with it." This was a *statement*, not a constitutional provision.
- Monsoon Session 2026: began **20 July**, scheduled for **25 days / 19 sittings**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Fundamental Rights & Duties; Parliament & legislation)
**Static anchor:** **Laxmikanth, Ch. 2 (Salient Features / National Symbols appendix) + Ch. 7 (Fundamental Rights, Art. 19) + Ch. 22 (Parliament — legislative procedure).** *Nudge: add a cross-link card "National Anthem vs National Song vs National Flag — legal basis of each" and file* Bijoe Emmanuel *beside Art. 19(1)(a) reasonable restrictions.*

### 2. WPI inflation hits 9.87% in June 2026 — a widening gap with 4.38% retail inflation — Economy

**Headline:** Wholesale price inflation rose to **9.87%** in June 2026 from 9.68% in May, driven by mineral oils, food articles and basic metals — even as CPI inflation stayed at 4.38%.

**Exam angle:**
- The **WPI–CPI divergence** is the single most examinable idea here: WPI has a heavy weight for fuel and manufactured goods and **contains no services**, while CPI is services-heavy and food-heavy at the retail end. A ~5.5-percentage-point gap is a textbook illustration.
- Policy consequence: the RBI targets **CPI**, not WPI — so a near-10% WPI print does *not* mechanically force a rate hike, but it signals producer-side cost pressure that can pass through to retail with a lag (margin squeeze first, prices later).
- Note the base-year context: India moved to a new **2022-23 base** WPI series, alongside new **Producer Price Indices**.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **WPI June 2026: 9.87%** (May: 9.68%). All-Commodities index **110.2** (May: 109.9).
- Group-wise YoY: **Primary Articles 7.0%** (May 4.99%), **Fuel & Power 27.41%** (May 30.33%), **Manufactured Products 7.48%** (unchanged).
- **WPI Food Index** — weight **24.99%** — inflation **6.14%** in June vs 4.49% in May.
- WPI is released by the **Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce & Industry**; CPI by the **NSO, MoSPI**. Base year of the new WPI series: **2022-23**.
- Contrast: **CPI June 2026 = 4.38%** → divergence of ~5.5 pp.

### 3. DRDO drawing up a strategic roadmap for ISRO's Kulasekarapattinam spaceport — Science & Technology

**Headline:** DRDO announced (CII Tamil Nadu Defence X Conclave, Chennai) that it is conceptualising "big programmes" built around ISRO's second spaceport at **Kulasekarapattinam**, Thoothukudi district, Tamil Nadu.

**Exam angle:**
- The **geography of launch sites** is the exam hook: Sriharikota, on the east coast, must fly a fuel-costly **"dogleg" manoeuvre** to reach polar/sun-synchronous orbits because a straight southward trajectory would overfly **Sri Lanka**. Kulasekarapattinam, on the southern tip, permits a **direct southward launch** over open ocean — more payload for the same fuel.
- Strategic overlay: cheaper polar access means small, rapid-response **military reconnaissance satellites** in Sun-synchronous orbit — a civil-military space convergence story worth a Mains line.
- Pair this with India's commercial-space push (IN-SPACe, NSIL, private launchers).

**Facts to memorise:**
- **Kulasekarapattinam** (also spelt Kulasekharapatnam), **Thoothukudi district, Tamil Nadu** — India's **second spaceport**, ~**2,300 acres** (~9.5 sq km), cost ~**₹950 crore**.
- Dedicated primarily to the **SSLV (Small Satellite Launch Vehicle)** — ~**500 kg payload to Low Earth Orbit**; designed for **on-demand, low-turnaround** launches.
- Designed capacity: about **25 launches per year**. **Commissioning targeted in FY 2026-27** (stated in a Lok Sabha reply by Union Minister Dr. Jitendra Singh).
- Existing site: **Satish Dhawan Space Centre (SDSC), Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh**.
- Key concept: **Sun-synchronous orbit (SSO)** — a near-polar, retrograde orbit (~98° inclination) in which the satellite crosses a given latitude at the same local solar time each pass; ideal for Earth observation.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Space) / Internal Security (space-based surveillance)
**Static anchor:** **Shankar IAS / NCERT Science & Tech — "Indian Space Programme: launch vehicles and launch sites."** *Nudge: add a small map-sketch cross-link — Sriharikota (dogleg) vs Kulasekarapattinam (direct southward) — and a one-line SSLV vs PSLV vs LVM3 payload comparison.*

### 4. Akash SAM exported to Tajikistan and Turkmenistan — India's defence footprint reaches Central Asia — International Relations

**Headline:** DRDO leadership confirmed that India's indigenous **Akash** surface-to-air missile system has been exported to **Tajikistan and Turkmenistan** — two states historically dependent on Russian air-defence equipment.

*(Note: the confirmation came via DRDO leadership remarks; a detailed MoD delivery statement is not yet in the public domain. Treat the transaction as reported, not as an itemised official disclosure.)*

**Exam angle:**
- This lands squarely in India's **"Connect Central Asia"** framework and the **India–Central Asia Dialogue / Summit** architecture — a region where India's leverage has historically been limited by the absence of direct land connectivity via Pakistan.
- Strategic significance: India entering a market long monopolised by Russia, in Russia's own near-abroad, while India simultaneously seeks Russian systems — a good "strategic autonomy" illustration.
- Defence-export policy angle: this feeds the target of **₹50,000 crore in defence exports by 2028-29**.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **Akash**: a **DRDO-developed medium-range surface-to-air missile (SAM)**, range ~**25 km**; production by **Bharat Electronics Limited (BEL)** and **Bharat Dynamics Limited (BDL)**; capable of engaging **multiple aerial targets simultaneously**; targets include aircraft, helicopters, UAVs and cruise missiles.
- **Armenia** was the **first foreign buyer** of Akash (Akash-1S). Newer variant under negotiation: **Akash-NG**.
- Central Asia geography for "Places in News": all five republics — **Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan** — are **landlocked**; **Uzbekistan and Liechtenstein are the world's only two doubly landlocked countries**. **Turkmenistan** borders the **Caspian Sea** (a lake, not an ocean outlet). **Tajikistan** is the only one sharing a border with **China** and **Afghanistan** both, via the **Wakhan Corridor** frontier region.
- India's connectivity workaround to the region: **Chabahar port (Iran)** and the **International North-South Transport Corridor (INSTC)**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India & its neighbourhood / bilateral groupings) | cross-file to GS3 — Internal Security (defence indigenisation & exports)

### 5. Belwa–Minapur Link Channel inaugurated — diverting Bagmati floodwater into the Burhi Gandak — Geography

**Headline:** Bihar inaugurated the **68.80-km Belwa–Minapur (Belwa Dhar) Link Channel**, built at **₹130.88 crore** under the Bagmati–Burhi Gandak river-linking project, to route surplus Bagmati floodwater away from vulnerable embankments.

**Exam angle:**
- North Bihar is India's most flood-prone plain — this is a live case study of **intra-basin river linking** as flood *moderation* rather than long-distance inter-basin transfer.
- Twin dividend framing for Mains: the same channel does **flood diversion in the monsoon** and **irrigation/aquifer recharge in the dry season** — the standard defence of link canals. Counterpoint to keep ready: siltation of Himalayan-origin rivers, embankment-induced waterlogging, and the argument that embankments *raise* riverbeds and worsen breaches over time.
- Strong "Places in News" map value: Sheohar, Muzaffarpur, East Champaran, Samastipur.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **Length 68.80 km; cost ₹130.88 crore.** Runs from **Belwa (Piprahi block, Sheohar district)** to **Minapur (Muzaffarpur district)**.
- Districts benefited: **Sheohar, Muzaffarpur, East Champaran (Purvi Champaran), Samastipur**.
- Design capacity: diverts up to **50,000 cusecs** of surplus flood discharge at peak monsoon; adds about **1,35,000 hectares** of assured irrigation.
- **Bagmati**: rises in the **Shivapuri hills near Kathmandu, Nepal**; enters Bihar in Sitamarhi district; joins the **Kosi** in Bihar. Considered sacred in Nepal (Pashupatinath sits on its bank).
- **Burhi Gandak**: rises in the **Someshwar hills, West Champaran district, Bihar**; a **left-bank tributary of the Ganga**, joining near **Khagaria**.
- Bihar's flood context: ~**73% of North Bihar** is flood-prone; the chief flood rivers are the **Kosi ("Sorrow of Bihar"), Gandak, Burhi Gandak, Bagmati, Kamla Balan and Mahananda** — all Himalayan-origin, high-silt, left-bank tributaries of the Ganga.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Geography (Indian drainage, floods) | GS3 — Disaster Management
**Static anchor:** **NCERT Class XI *India: Physical Environment*, Ch. 3 (Drainage System) + Shankar IAS Disaster Management chapter (Floods).** *Nudge: annotate the Ganga's left-bank tributary list with source hills and confluence points, and add a cross-link "structural vs non-structural flood measures" citing this channel as a structural example.*

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Economy, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *"Wholesale price inflation in India has climbed towards double digits even as retail inflation has remained comfortably within the central bank's tolerance band."* **Examine** the reasons for this divergence and **assess** its implications for monetary policy and for corporate profitability.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Set up the divergence with data.** WPI at 9.87% (June 2026) against CPI at 4.38% — a gap of roughly 5.5 percentage points. Anchor the answer in the June group-wise numbers: Fuel & Power at 27.41%, Primary Articles at 7.0%, Manufactured Products at 7.48%.

2. **Explain *why* the two indices can diverge.** (i) **Composition** — WPI carries no services at all, whereas services form a large share of the CPI basket; (ii) **Weights** — fuel and manufactured intermediates dominate WPI, food and housing dominate CPI; (iii) **Stage of transaction** — WPI captures producer/first-point-of-bulk-sale prices, CPI captures final retail prices, so a shock hits WPI first and CPI later; (iv) **Absorption in margins** — firms may absorb input-cost spikes rather than pass them on immediately, especially where demand is weak; (v) **Administered prices and taxes** wedge the two apart at the retail end.

3. **Monetary-policy implications.** Under the flexible inflation-targeting framework (RBI Act, 1934, as amended in 2016), the MPC's mandate is CPI at **4% ± 2%** — so a high WPI print does not by itself trigger tightening. But WPI functions as a **leading indicator** of pipeline pressure: sustained producer-price inflation raises the risk of second-round pass-through and of unanchored inflation expectations, which the RBI tracks through its household expectations surveys. The policy dilemma is between pre-emptive tightening and avoiding a growth sacrifice from a supply-side, largely imported (fuel-driven) shock.

4. **Corporate-profitability implications, and a way forward.** A high-WPI/low-CPI configuration is a classic **margin squeeze** — input costs rise faster than realisable output prices, hitting MSMEs hardest since they have the least pricing power and the thinnest working-capital buffers. Sectors with imported energy or metal inputs are worst placed. Conclude on remedies: supply-side management of fuel and food, strategic reserves, targeted input-cost relief for MSMEs, and continued use of the new **Producer Price Indices** (with the 2022-23 base) to read pipeline pressure more accurately than WPI alone permits.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The Bill extends 1971-Act-style protection to *Vande Mataram*; Sections 2 and 3 of the parent Act cover the Flag, the Constitution and the Anthem; the proposed penalty is up to three years, a fine, or both.

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** WPI rose to 9.87% from 9.68%; Fuel & Power at 27.41% was by far the highest group rate. Statement 3 is wrong — WPI is released by the **Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT (Ministry of Commerce & Industry)**; the NSO/MoSPI compiles the **CPI**.

**Q3 — (b).** Kulasekarapattinam, in Thoothukudi district of Tamil Nadu, is India's second spaceport, built primarily for SSLV launches, with commissioning targeted in FY 2026-27.

**Q4 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Akash is a DRDO medium-range SAM (~25 km) able to engage multiple targets; Armenia was its first export customer; both Tajikistan and Turkmenistan are landlocked Central Asian republics.

**Q5 — (b) Bagmati to Burhi Gandak.** The 68.80-km channel diverts surplus Bagmati floodwater into the Burhi Gandak, easing embankment pressure in North Bihar while adding irrigation.

**Q6 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** *Vande Mataram* was first sung at the 1896 Calcutta INC session, and *Bijoe Emmanuel* held that respectful standing without singing is no offence. Statement 2 is wrong — **no Article of the Constitution declares the National Anthem**; its status rests on the Constituent Assembly President's statement of 24 January 1950.

**Q7 — (b) Only two.** Pairs 1 and 2 are correct. Pair 3 is wrong — the **Kosi** rises in the Himalayas (formed by the confluence of the Sun Kosi, Arun and Tamur in Nepal), not on the **Amarkantak plateau**, which is the source of the Narmada, Son and Mahanadi.
