### 1. India takes the BRICS chair for a fourth time — the 18th Summit opens in New Delhi tomorrow — International Relations

**Headline:** India hosts the **18th BRICS Summit in New Delhi on 12–13 September 2026**, closing out a Chairship that began on **1 January 2026** under the theme **"Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability"** — and the first full-length Chairship to run the expanded **11-member** BRICS plus the new **Partner Country** track.

**Exam angle:** Do not write this up as "another summit". The examinable story is *institutional design under enlargement*. BRICS doubled its membership in two years (5 → 11) and then invented a second tier — 10 Partner Countries — precisely because consensus among eleven heterogeneous states is harder than among five. That gives you the cleanest available case study of the classic IR trade-off between **breadth of membership and depth of commitment**, and it is directly transferable to your notes on WTO paralysis, SAARC vs BIMSTEC, and UNSC reform. Second angle: BRICS is the principal *non-Western* vehicle through which India argues for IMF quota and World Bank voice reform — but India is simultaneously in the Quad, the I2U2 and the IPEF. That "multi-alignment" framing is the standard high-scoring Mains line. Third: note the sceptical counterpoint the PIB backgrounder itself invites — nearly every 2026 deliverable is a **voluntary platform, network or set of guiding principles**, not a binding obligation, and the bloc contains members on opposite sides of live sanctions and trade disputes.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **18th BRICS Summit: New Delhi, 12–13 September 2026.** India's Chairship ran from **1 January 2026**, spanning **350+ meetings across 25 cities**.
- India's theme: **"Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability."**
- This is India's **fourth** Chairship — earlier: **2012 (New Delhi, Delhi Declaration)**, **2016 (Goa)**, **2021 (virtual, BRICS@15)**.
- **Lineage:** term "BRIC" from a 2001 Goldman Sachs paper → first political grouping **2006** → first BRIC Summit **Yekaterinburg, Russia, June 2009** (note: *not* Rio) → **South Africa joins 2011**, becoming BRICS.
- **First expansion, January 2024:** Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia, UAE. **Second, January 2025:** Indonesia — the 11th member, and the first Southeast Asian one.
- **Current 11 members:** Brazil, China, Egypt, Ethiopia, India, Indonesia, Iran, Russia, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, UAE.
- **10 Partner Countries (category created 2025):** Belarus, Bolivia, Cuba, Kazakhstan, Malaysia, Nigeria, Thailand, Uganda, Uzbekistan, Vietnam.
- **Weight:** ~**49.5% of world population**, ~**40% of global GDP**, ~**26% of global trade** *(PIB backgrounder figures)*.
- **2026 Chairship deliverables to name-drop:** BRICS Centres of Excellence on Agro-Ecology; **Digital Agriculture Network (AGRIN)**; Healthy Lifestyle Mission and a **Mental Health Training Hub with NIMHANS as coordinator**; MSME Cooperation Portal, Incubator Network and Startup Innovation Fund; **Global Value Chains Action Plan 2026–2030**; Logistics Supply-Chain Cooperation Framework; Guiding Principles on Energy Storage and Smart Grids; **BRICS Urban Mobility Hub**.

### 2. The Tribal Affairs Ministry says it has no role in Gram Sabha consent — and the FRA's nodal-authority question reopens — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** An **office memorandum dated 31 August 2026** from the **Ministry of Tribal Affairs (MoTA)** states there is **"no provision" under the Forest Rights Act, 2006 for obtaining Gram Sabha consent at the Stage-II forest-clearance stage**, and that such matters fall outside the Ministry's purview. The **Campaign for Survival and Dignity (CSD)**, a national platform of Adivasi and forest-dweller organisations, has demanded its withdrawal as "factually and legally untenable".

**Exam angle:** This is the highest-value Polity/Governance item of the day, and the reason is structural: the memorandum is a **ministry disclaiming its own statutory nodal role**. Three things make it examinable. First, **allocation of business** — MoTA was assigned "all matters including legislation relating to the rights of forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes on forest lands" by a **March 2005 amendment to the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961**, and **Section 11 of the FRA** makes it the nodal agency; an executive OM cannot subtract from either. That is a textbook illustration of the limits of **administrative instructions versus statute** — a recurring Prelims and GS2 theme. Second, **judicial precedent**: the **Niyamgiri judgment, *Orissa Mining Corporation v. MoEF*, (2013) 6 SCC 476**, held that the Gram Sabha must decide on community and religious claims before bauxite mining could proceed in the Niyamgiri hills — the source of India's **free, prior and informed consent (FPIC)** standard. Third, **the trigger**: the OM followed a **Committee on Public Undertakings** report recording **NHPC's** complaint that consent requirements delay hydel projects, and the Committee suggested lowering the consent threshold from **100% to 70–75%**. That is a beautifully clean development-versus-rights conflict for a 250-word answer — and note the counter-datum CSD deploys: roughly **3,05,945 hectares** of forest land were cleared for non-forest use between **2008-09 and 2022-23** without full FRA compliance, which undercuts the claim that consent is the binding constraint on project timelines **(verify)**.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **Instrument:** MoTA **office memorandum, 31 August 2026**; position taken — no FRA provision requires Gram Sabha consent at **Stage-II** forest clearance.
- **Full title of the Act:** *Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, **2006***; came into force **31 December 2007**; Rules **2008**, amended **2012**.
- **Section 11, FRA:** MoTA is the **nodal agency** for implementation. Basis in the **Allocation of Business Rules, 1961** (amended **March 2005**).
- **Section 6, FRA:** the **Gram Sabha** is the authority that **initiates** the process of determining the nature and extent of individual and community forest rights — an *actual* Prelims 2013 answer.
- **Rights recognised:** Individual Forest Rights (IFR, up to **4 hectares**), Community Rights (CR), and **Community Forest Resource (CFR) rights** under **Section 3(1)(i)** — the right to protect and manage.
- **Eligibility:** Forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes, plus "Other Traditional Forest Dwellers" who have occupied forest land for **three generations (75 years)** prior to **13 December 2005** — the cut-off date.
- **Niyamgiri:** *Orissa Mining Corporation v. Ministry of Environment & Forests*, **(2013) 6 SCC 476** — FPIC; 12 (later 12 of 12) Gram Sabhas of Rayagada and Kalahandi rejected the project.
- **Two-stage clearance** under the **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980** — renamed **Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 1980** by the 2023 Amendment: **Stage-I** = in-principle approval with conditions; **Stage-II** = final approval once conditions (including, per the 2009 MoEF circular, FRA compliance and Gram Sabha consent) are met.
- **Read alongside:** **PESA, 1996** (Panchayats Extension to Scheduled Areas) — the Gram Sabha's constitutional footing in Fifth Schedule areas.
- Parliamentary trigger: **Committee on Public Undertakings** report citing **NHPC**; suggestion to reduce the consent threshold to **70–75%**.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (statutory bodies, welfare schemes for vulnerable sections, government policies and interventions) with a strong GS3 Environment spillover.

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment* — the chapter on **Forest Rights Act / Forest Conservation** — **and** *Laxmikanth* on the **Fifth Schedule and Tribal Areas**. **Update:** add a short margin box titled "**Who guards the consent step?**" with three lines — (i) Section 11 FRA = MoTA is nodal; (ii) 2009 MoEF circular = FRA compliance is a Stage-II precondition; (iii) **31 Aug 2026 OM** = MoTA disclaims the role. Cross-link to your **Niyamgiri** case card and to the **Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam, 2023** entry, and pencil a link to the **1 Sep 2026** VB-G RAM G note where the same "statutory entitlement vs executive circular" tension appears in the employment-guarantee context.

### 3. New e-commerce rules take aim at search manipulation, dark patterns and "fake" discounts — Economy

**Headline:** The **Department of Consumer Affairs** has notified the **Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026**, amending the 2020 Rules under the **Consumer Protection Act, 2019**. They come into force on **1 January 2027** and require, among other things, that a discounted item display both the reduced price and the **"prior price" — the lowest price in the preceding 30 days**.

**Exam angle:** The clean framing here is **information asymmetry**, the market-failure concept your Economy notes already carry. Every obligation in the amendment is a *disclosure* mandate rather than a price or entry control — sponsored listings must be labelled, search results must not be manipulated away from query relevance, imported goods must show country of origin, best-before dates must be visible. The State is not regulating what platforms sell; it is regulating what the consumer is allowed to *know*. That distinction is worth one sentence in any Mains answer on digital-market regulation. The second hook is **dark patterns** — interface designs that nudge users into choices they did not intend (drip pricing, confirm-shaming, forced action, basket sneaking, false urgency). The **CCPA's Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023** listed **13** such patterns; the 2026 Rules make an **annual self-audit** against them mandatory, with a displayed compliance certificate. And that is exactly where the critique bites: self-audit and self-certification, with a **15-month** runway before commencement, leaves enforcement capacity at the CCPA as the binding constraint. Quotable scale figure: the **National Consumer Helpline received 17,71,622 grievances in 2025, of which about 29% (5,11,196) concerned e-commerce**.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **Instrument:** **Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026**, amending the **E-Commerce Rules, 2020**; parent statute **Consumer Protection Act, 2019**; nodal body **Department of Consumer Affairs** (Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution). **In force from 1 January 2027.**
- **Key obligations:** no manipulation of search results away from query relevance; **prominent disclosure of sponsored listings**; display of the **prior price = lowest price in the preceding 30 days** alongside any reduction; best-before dates; return/refund/warranty/delivery/payment terms; **importer and country-of-origin** details for imported goods.
- **Dark patterns:** compliance with the **CCPA Dark Patterns Guidelines, 2023** (**13** specified patterns), an **annual self-audit**, and a displayed compliance certificate.
- **Consent and bundling:** consumer data may not be used for specified purposes without **express consent**; bundled fees for unrelated services are barred, subject to a **loyalty/membership exception**.
- **Grievance redressal:** every e-commerce entity must join the **National Consumer Helpline (NCH)** convergence process and share complaint copies with complainants.
- **Data point:** **NCH grievances 2025 = 17,71,622**; e-commerce share ≈ **29% (5,11,196)**.
- **Statutory architecture to keep straight (CPA 2019):** **CCPA** (Central Consumer Protection Authority, class-action regulator, *suo motu* powers); three-tier redressal — **District (up to ₹50 lakh), State (₹50 lakh–₹2 crore), National (above ₹2 crore)** Commissions; **product liability**; **e-filing** and **mediation** introduced by the 2019 Act.
- **Categories under the 2020 Rules:** *marketplace* e-commerce entity vs *inventory* e-commerce entity — different duty sets.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Indian economy and issues relating to planning, growth, development; e-commerce and consumer markets), with a GS2 Governance spillover on regulatory capacity.

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* — **Pillar 4** (Sectors of the Economy → Services/E-commerce) and the **Consumer Protection Act, 2019** card in your Governance notes. **Update:** replace the flat "CPA 2019 features" list with a two-column layout — *Rights created* | *Institutions created* — and slot the **2026 Amendment Rules** under a new sub-head "**disclosure-based regulation of platforms**", with the one-line note "prior price = 30-day low; sponsored = must be labelled; dark patterns = self-audit annually". Cross-link to the **DPDP Act, 2023** entry (the express-consent requirement overlaps) and to the **5 September NFRA advisory-panel** note, both being cases of a regulator leaning on **self-certification** rather than inspection.

### 4. PMMSY turns six with a record allocation — and the fisheries story is now about the value chain, not the catch — Agriculture

**Headline:** The **Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana (PMMSY)** completed **six years on 10 September 2026**, with a record **BE 2026-27 allocation of ₹2,500 crore**, and government figures showing fish production up from **141.64 lakh tonnes (2019-20) to a record 197.75 lakh tonnes (2024-25)** and fisheries exports up from **₹46,663 crore to ₹73,890 crore**.

**Exam angle:** The examinable shift is from **Blue Revolution to value chain**. The older Blue Revolution scheme was a *production* programme; PMMSY is built as an umbrella with a **Central Sector (CS)** and a **Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS)** component covering infrastructure, cold chain, exports, digital governance and fisher welfare — so it is the natural example when a question asks you to distinguish output-maximising from income-maximising agricultural policy. Second, it is your best live illustration of **formalisation as a delivery strategy**: **PM-MKSSY**, the sub-scheme, exists to bring informal fishers onto the **National Fisheries Digital Platform** so they become legible to credit and insurance systems — **37 lakh-plus registrations** as of 8 September 2026. Note honestly that this cuts both ways, and say so in the answer: registration-gated benefits systematically under-reach the small and traditional fishers who are hardest to formalise, and the government's **58 lakh jobs** figure is a self-reported scheme target, not an independently audited outcome. Third, the standing policy gaps — **deep-sea fishing regulation**, **overexploitation of coastal stocks**, the absence of a national marine fisheries law harmonised across State jurisdictions (territorial waters to **12 nautical miles** are State subject-matter; the EEZ beyond is the Centre's) — are what separates a scheme recital from an analytical answer.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **PMMSY launched 2020-21** (announced under the Aatmanirbhar Bharat package), building on the **Blue Revolution** scheme. **Nodal:** Department of Fisheries, **Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying**.
- **Outlay:** notified at **₹20,050 crore** — the figure standard UPSC material carries; the September 2026 PIB backgrounder cites **₹20,750 crore** **(verify — quote ₹20,050 crore unless you can confirm a revision)**. **BE 2026-27: ₹2,500 crore.**
- **Structure:** umbrella scheme with a **Central Sector (CS)** and a **Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS)** component.
- **Sub-scheme — PM-MKSSY** (*Pradhan Mantri Matsya Kisan Samridhi Sah-Yojana*), **2023-24 to 2026-27**, outlay **₹6,000 crore**: formalisation, insurance, institutional finance.
- **Approvals:** projects worth **₹21,394.88 crore** (Central share **₹9,510.89 crore**) as of **11 August 2026**.
- **Infrastructure:** **₹2,797 crore** of cold-chain and marketing infrastructure — **775** cold storages/ice plants, **24** wholesale and **117** retail fish markets, **28,000+** fish-transportation units.
- **2,195 Fish Farmers Producer Organisations** supported (**₹544.86 crore**); **100 Climate Resilient Coastal Fishermen Villages** at **₹200 lakh per village** (CS component).
- **National Fisheries Digital Platform**, launched under PM-MKSSY in **September 2024**: **37 lakh+ registrations** as of **8 September 2026**.
- **Techniques to know:** **Recirculatory Aquaculture System (RAS)** — filtered, recirculated water for intensive land-and-water-efficient farming; **Bio-floc** — microbial nutrient recycling with minimal water exchange ("green soup"); **cage culture**; **Integrated Multi-Trophic Aquaculture**.

### 5. Mercury is shrinking about 30% faster than we thought — and BepiColombo arrives in November to check — Science & Technology

**Headline:** A study from researchers at the **German Aerospace Centre (DLR)** finds that **Mercury's diameter may have contracted by as much as ~23 km (about 14 miles)** since the planet formed **4.5 billion years ago** — roughly **30% more** than estimates derived from **MESSENGER** data — because shrinkage wrinkles were being missed where impact debris had buried them.

**Exam angle:** Two clean Prelims hooks and one conceptual one. Conceptually, this is **planetary thermal evolution**: Mercury has the **largest iron core relative to its size** of any planet (roughly 85% of its radius, about 60% of its mass); as that core cools it contracts, and the rigid crust above it — a **single lithospheric plate**, since Mercury has no plate tectonics — must buckle. The resulting compressional landforms are **lobate scarps** and **wrinkle ridges**, i.e. surface thrust faults. That is a genuinely transferable idea: contraction produces *compressional* features, whereas the tension of a cooling, spreading surface produces *extensional* ones — the same logic your Geography notes use for terrestrial thrust faults and rift valleys. Methodologically, the new result came not from new spacecraft data but from **cross-referencing existing fault maps against newer surface-roughness maps** — a nice "reanalysis, not re-observation" line for an answer on the value of open planetary datasets. And the verification hook is dated: **BepiColombo**, the joint **ESA–JAXA** mission, is expected to enter **Mercury orbit in November 2026** and can test the estimate with its **laser altimeter (BELA)**. Given your Physics optional, the underlying result — thermal contraction of a differentiated body, and the crustal strain it imposes — is worth a line in your own words rather than rote memory.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **Finding:** Mercury's diameter has shrunk by up to **~23 km (14 miles)**; about **30% more** than prior estimates. Source institution: **German Aerospace Centre (DLR)** **(verify the precise institute name)**.
- **Method:** comparison of **surface-fault maps** (evidence of contraction) with newer **surface-roughness maps**; the roughest terrain showed *fewer* wrinkles, implying some are **buried under impact debris**.
- **Mercury basics:** smallest and innermost planet; diameter about **4,880 km**; **no substantial atmosphere** (a thin **exosphere**); **no moons**; the **densest planet after Earth**; a **3:2 spin-orbit resonance** (three rotations per two orbits); the only inner planet besides Earth with a **global magnetic field**.
- **Landforms of contraction:** **lobate scarps** and **wrinkle ridges** — compressional thrust-fault features. **Caloris Basin** is its largest impact structure.
- **Missions to Mercury — only three, ever:** **Mariner 10** (NASA; three flybys, 1974–75, first spacecraft to use a **gravity assist** to reach another planet); **MESSENGER** (NASA; orbit **2011–2015**, ended by deliberate impact); **BepiColombo** (**ESA–JAXA**, launched **2018**; **Mercury orbit insertion expected November 2026**).
- **BepiColombo carries two orbiters:** ESA's **Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO)** and JAXA's **Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter (Mio)**; the **BepiColombo Laser Altimeter (BELA)** is the instrument relevant to measuring contraction.
- **Contrast to hold:** Earth loses interior heat largely through **plate tectonics and mantle convection**; Mercury, a **one-plate planet**, loses it by conduction and pays for it in **global contraction**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (space, awareness in the fields of space technology), with a GS1 Geography link on faulting and crustal deformation.

**Static anchor:** *NCERT Class XI Geography, Fundamentals of Physical Geography* — the chapters on **the origin and evolution of the Earth** and on **faulting/folding** — plus your **space missions** table in S&T. **Update:** add a row to the missions table: "**BepiColombo — ESA + JAXA — Mercury — launched 2018, orbit insertion Nov 2026 — two orbiters, MPO and Mio**", right below Mariner 10 and MESSENGER. In the physical-geography chapter, pencil a one-line margin note beside *thrust faults*: "Mercury's **lobate scarps** = planetary-scale thrust faults from core cooling — compression, not tension." Cross-link to the **2 September Roman Space Telescope** entry so all your 2026 mission notes sit on one page.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Governance / Social Justice, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **Examine** the statutory and judicial basis for Gram Sabha consent in the diversion of forest land for non-forest use. In light of recent executive communications on the subject, discuss the risks that diluting this requirement poses for tribal rights and forest governance.

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points):**

1. **Establish the legal foundation, precisely.** Section 11 of the FRA, 2006 makes MoTA the nodal authority; the Allocation of Business Rules (amended March 2005) assign it all matters relating to forest-dwellers' rights; Section 6 makes the Gram Sabha the initiating authority for rights determination; Section 3(1)(i) creates CFR rights. Layer on PESA, 1996 for the constitutional footing in Fifth Schedule areas, and the Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980 two-stage clearance process into which consent is inserted.

2. **Add the judicial layer.** *Orissa Mining Corporation v. MoEF*, (2013) 6 SCC 476 (Niyamgiri) established free, prior and informed consent as a substantive, not procedural, requirement — the Gram Sabha decides on community and religious claims before clearance. Note that FPIC also tracks India's obligations under ILO and UNDRIP-adjacent norms, without overstating their binding force.

3. **State the dilution and evaluate it.** The 31 August 2026 OM; the CoPU recommendation to lower the threshold from 100% to 70–75%; the NHPC delay argument. Test it against evidence: roughly 3,05,945 ha diverted between 2008-09 and 2022-23 without full FRA compliance suggests consent is not the operative bottleneck. Make the constitutional point: an executive memorandum cannot narrow a statutory mandate.

4. **Set out the consequences and a balanced way forward.** Risks — erosion of the only enforceable pre-clearance veto held by forest communities; displacement without rehabilitation; litigation risk that *lengthens* rather than shortens project timelines; weakening of community forest management, which has demonstrable conservation value. Way forward — time-bound Gram Sabha proceedings with recorded minutes and video documentation, completion of CFR recognition *before* clearance applications are entertained, an independent grievance mechanism, and a genuine distinction between a consent requirement and a consent *process* that is badly administered.

### D. PYQ Mirror — how UPSC has asked these themes before

**Theme: Forest Rights Act, Gram Sabha.** **Prelims 2013** asked directly: *"Under the Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (Recognition of Forest Rights) Act, 2006, who shall be the authority to initiate the process for determining the nature and extent of individual or community forest rights or both?"* — with the answer being the **Gram Sabha** (not the State Forest Department, District Collector or Tahsildar). That single question tells you how UPSC frames the FRA: not by policy debate, but by *who holds which power at which step*. Today's item is the same question asked at a later step in the chain. **Mains GS2** has repeatedly asked variants of "Examine the role of the Gram Sabha in Fifth Schedule areas" and "Do government schemes for the upliftment of tribals adequately address their needs?" — treat Niyamgiri as your standing case study for both.

**Theme: BRICS.** **Prelims 2014** carried a statement-based question on BRICS whose examinable trap was the venue of the first summit — **Yekaterinburg, Russia (2009), not Rio de Janeiro** — paired with a statement on South Africa being the last to join (true at the time, superseded by the 2024–25 expansions). *(Approximate recall of the exact wording — flagged; verify the 2014 paper before you quote it.)* The lesson for 2027 is that the **venue-and-sequence** details are exactly what gets tested, which is why the dated ladder in the static-anchor note above matters more than the outcome list.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
Statement 1 is correct — the first BRIC Summit was at **Yekaterinburg, Russia, in June 2009**; Rio de Janeiro is the classic distractor. Statement 2 is correct — **Indonesia joined in January 2025** as the **eleventh** member, after Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi Arabia and the UAE joined in January 2024. Statement 3 is incorrect — the **Partner Country** category, created in 2025 for ten states, provides structured engagement **without** full membership.

**Q2 — (b).**
The MoTA office memorandum of **31 August 2026** stated that there is **"no provision" under the FRA, 2006 for Gram Sabha consent at the Stage-II forest-clearance stage** and that the matter lies outside the Ministry's purview. The Campaign for Survival and Dignity has sought its withdrawal, pointing to **Section 11** of the FRA and the **March 2005** amendment to the Allocation of Business Rules, both of which make MoTA the nodal authority, and to the **Niyamgiri** judgment, (2013) 6 SCC 476.

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.**
All three are correct. The Rules are made under the **Consumer Protection Act, 2019** and amend the **E-Commerce Rules, 2020**; they require display of the **"prior price"**, defined as the **lowest price in the preceding 30 days**, alongside any reduction; and they mandate an **annual self-audit** against the CCPA's **Dark Patterns Guidelines, 2023**, with a displayed compliance certificate. They take effect on **1 January 2027**.

**Q4 — (b) 1 and 3 only.**
Statements 1 and 3 are correct: PMMSY is run by the **Department of Fisheries**, Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry and Dairying, and **PM-MKSSY** (₹6,000 crore, 2023-24 to 2026-27) is its sub-scheme for formalisation, insurance and institutional finance. Statement 2 is incorrect — PMMSY is an **umbrella scheme with both a Central Sector (CS) and a Centrally Sponsored Scheme (CSS) component**; the 100-village Climate Resilient Coastal Fishermen Villages initiative, for instance, sits under the CS component.

**Q5 — (b).**
**Lobate scarps** are **compressional thrust-fault landforms** — cliff-like ridges where one block of crust has been pushed over another as the planet's crust buckles over a cooling, contracting core. On Mercury, which is a **single-plate planet** with an outsized iron core, they are the principal surface evidence of global contraction, and the recent DLR reanalysis argues that many have been obscured by impact debris, causing earlier estimates to undercount the shrinkage.

**Q6 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.**
All three are correct. **Section 6** makes the **Gram Sabha** the authority that initiates the determination of forest rights — the exact point tested in **Prelims 2013**. "Other Traditional Forest Dwellers" must show residence and dependence for **three generations (75 years) prior to 13 December 2005**. **Section 3(1)(i)** confers the right to **protect, regenerate, conserve and manage community forest resources**, which is the basis of CFR titles.

**Q7 — (b) 1 and 3 only.**
Statements 1 and 3 are correct: the **New Development Bank** was agreed at the **Fortaleza Summit (2014)** and is headquartered in **Shanghai**; BRICS has **no permanent secretariat**, coordination resting with the rotating Chair — which is why India's 2026 Chairship involved over 350 meetings. Statement 2 is incorrect: the **Contingent Reserve Arrangement** is a **US$100 billion treaty-based currency-swap framework among member central banks**, not a pool of capital deposited with the NDB; no money changes hands until a member draws on it.
