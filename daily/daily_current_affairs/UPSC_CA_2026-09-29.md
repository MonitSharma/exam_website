# PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. India climbs to 31st in the WEF Travel & Tourism Development Index 2026 — Reports & Indices
**Exam angle:** A headline rank that can be tested directly, plus a clean strengths-vs-constraints split that fits GS3 services and employment answers. Released 25 Sept at "Beyond Tourism Day" in Geneva, two days before World Tourism Day (27 Sept).
**Memorise:** India is **31st of 110 economies**, up nine places. The 2024 edition had India **39th of 119**, but WEF recalculated the 2024 figures under the new methodology, which is why reports say "up from 40th". Top 5: **Japan, US, Spain, Australia, France**. Advanced economies hold 9 of the top 10; China is the exception. Structure: **5 dimensions, 17 pillars, 102 indicators**, scored **1–7**. Prepared by WEF with **Zurich Insurance Group**. TTDI replaced the old TTCI in 2022. India's strengths: price competitiveness, natural assets and cultural resources (it is in the global top 10 for World Heritage cultural sites). Its constraints: **workforce skills, environmental sustainability and infrastructure quality**. Supporting figures from PIB for World Tourism Day: e-Visa now covers **172 countries**, **2.53 crore foreign tourist visits in 2025**, and tourism is about **5.22% of GDP**.
**Notebook:** GS3 – Economy (Services / Tourism); Reports & Indices sheet.
**Static anchor:** Mrunal Economy pillar, *Services sector → Tourism*; Ramesh Singh, *Indian Economy: Services*. *Nudge:* update your "India's rankings" table (TTDI 2024 → 2026) and cross-link Swadesh Darshan 2.0 / PRASHAD / e-Visa under the "infrastructure quality" weakness.

### 2. The Collegium recommends three High Court Chief Justices for the Supreme Court — Polity & Governance
**Exam angle:** This is the first batch of elevations since Parliament raised the Court's strength to 38. It also brings the appointment machinery back into focus: the Judges Cases, the Memorandum of Procedure, seniority and diversity.
**Memorise:** On **28 Sept 2026** the Collegium (**CJI Surya Kant** with Justices Vikram Nath, B.V. Nagarathna, M.M. Sundresh and P.S. Narasimha) recommended **Justice Sunita Agarwal** (CJ, Gujarat HC; parent HC Allahabad), **Justice D.K. Upadhyaya** (CJ, Delhi HC; from Allahabad) and **Justice Aparesh Kumar Singh** (CJ, Telangana HC; parent HC Jharkhand). The SC's working strength is **34 against a sanctioned 38**. If these three are appointed, it becomes **37 with one vacancy**. The strength went from 34 to 38 (CJI + 37) through the **Supreme Court (Number of Judges) Amendment Act, 2026**, which got presidential assent on 12 Aug 2026 and replaced the May 2026 Ordinance. Justice Agarwal would make **three women judges** on the SC. None of the three is in line to become CJI. *Mains line:* "Adding benches only helps if appointments keep pace. Sanctioned strength means little without timely elevations."
**Notebook:** GS2 – Polity (Judiciary).
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth, *Supreme Court* chapter → Composition (Art. 124(1): Parliament fixes the number by law) and Appointment of Judges (the Judges Cases). *Nudge:* change "34" to "38 (2026 Amendment Act)" in your notes and add this batch as a live example beside the Second/Third/Fourth Judges Cases.

### 3. Jharkhand's *Mandar* gets a GI tag — the clay drum of the Sarhul and Karma dances — Art & Culture
**Exam angle:** This is exactly the kind of GI item UPSC turns into a "which of these has GI status" question. It also covers a tribal musical instrument, which ties into the folk instruments and Jharkhand's festivals.
**Memorise:** The GI registration was reported on **28–29 Sept 2026**. The instrument is made in **Jarjatta village, Raidih block, Gumla district**, and the application came from a Raidih artisans' **producer company** through the **GI Registry, Chennai**. It is a **double-headed drum with a red-clay (terracotta) barrel** and two leather heads of unequal size. A black paste called ***Kiran*** on the smaller head tunes it, and it is **slung from the shoulder**. It is played at **Sarhul and Karma**, at weddings, and in the dances of the **Oraon, Munda, Santhal and Ho** communities. The dossier cited ethnographers **Sarat Chandra Roy** and **W.G. Archer**. It joins Jharkhand's earlier GI, **Sohrai–Khovar painting** (Hazaribagh).
**Notebook:** GS1 – Art & Culture (Music / Folk instruments; GI).
**Static anchor:** Nitin Singhania, *Indian Music → Folk instruments (membranophones/avanaddha vadya)*; NCERT Class 11 *Living Craft Traditions*. *Nudge:* add Mandar under "avanaddha vadya" and cross-link to your Jharkhand tribes & festivals sheet (Sarhul, Karma).

### 4. A single digital portal for livestock insurance under the National Livestock Mission — Agriculture
**Exam angle:** Risk management for livestock-dependent households is weaker than for crops. PMFBY has a mature digital system, but livestock cover was paper-heavy and prone to fraud. It fits GS3 questions on animal husbandry, rural income diversification and "digital public infrastructure in agriculture".
**Memorise:** Union Minister **Rajiv Ranjan (Lalan) Singh** launched it in New Delhi on **28 Sept 2026**. It is run by the **Department of Animal Husbandry & Dairying** as a web workflow under **NLM (Risk Management & Insurance)** and covers everything from policy issue to claim settlement. The farmer pays **15%** of the premium. The other **85%** is shared by the Centre and the States in a **60:40** ratio, or **90:10** in the Himalayan and North-Eastern States. The subsidised cap is **10 cattle units per household**, with a separate **5-unit** cap for pigs and rabbits. Covered animals: indigenous and crossbred cattle, **buffalo, horse, donkey, camel, sheep, goat, pig, rabbit, yak, mithun**. It works in **13 languages** (11 regional plus Hindi and English) and links to the **Bharat Pashudhan** database and the **12-digit ear-tag ID** to stop duplicate policies. RFID tagging is under consideration. In the last five years, **60.8 lakh animals were insured** and **₹161.07 crore** was paid in claims.
**Notebook:** GS3 – Agriculture (Animal husbandry; agri-insurance).
**Static anchor:** Mrunal Agriculture pillar, *Animal husbandry schemes (NLM, Rashtriya Gokul Mission, NADCP)*. *Nudge:* add a "Crop vs livestock insurance" comparison row (PMFBY 2%/1.5%/5% farmer share vs 15% under NLM).

### 5. Foundation stone for India's first port-based e-methanol plant at Kandla — Science & Technology
**Exam angle:** This is how India's green-hydrogen push is supposed to become an export product: a green marine fuel sold as bunkers on the Asia–Europe route. It is useful for questions on e-fuels, shipping decarbonisation and the National Green Hydrogen Mission. For a Physics-optional student it is also a clean electrolysis → CO₂-hydrogenation chain. *(This is a different story from the 28 Sept mega-port notification, even though Deendayal features in both.)*
**Memorise:** The foundation stone was laid on **28 Sept 2026** by Union Minister **Sarbananda Sonowal** with the CMs of **Gujarat** and **Assam**. The plant is at **Deendayal Port Authority (DPA), Kandla**, with capacity of **150 TPD** at a cost of **₹2,300 crore**. It is a JV of **DPA (76%)** and **Assam Petro-Chemicals Ltd (24%)**. **Phase I** is 50 TPD for ₹1,200 crore (target Jan 2027) and **Phase II** is 100 TPD for ₹1,100 crore (target Mar 2027). Feedstock: **renewable power + water (→ green H₂) + biogenic CO₂**. The target production cost is about **US$750/t**, against a global price of about **US$1,300/t**, and the plant is expected to create **3,500+ jobs**. Chemistry: CO₂ + 3H₂ → CH₃OH + H₂O. The fuel is "carbon-neutral" only because the CO₂ is biogenic. It still emits CO₂ when burnt.
**Notebook:** GS3 – Sci-Tech (Energy) and Environment (Climate mitigation).
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS, *Renewable Energy / Climate change mitigation*; NCERT Class 11 Chemistry, *Hydrogen* (production by electrolysis). *Nudge:* add "e-methanol / e-fuels" under the National Green Hydrogen Mission (Jan 2023, ₹19,744 crore, 5 MMT by 2030) and cross-link to the Deendayal green-hydrogen-hub note.

**Mains (GS3, 150 words, 10 marks)**
**Q8.** *"Green marine fuels could let India's ports export clean energy, not just handle cargo." Discuss this with reference to the e-methanol project at Kandla.*

*Model-answer skeleton:*
- **Intro:** Kandla's 150-TPD e-methanol plant (DPA 76 : APL 24, ₹2,300 crore) is India's first port-based e-fuel unit. It links the National Green Hydrogen Mission to bunkering on the Asia–Europe route.
- **Opportunities:** a cost edge (about $750/t vs about $1,300/t globally) from cheap solar and wind power; ports that already host green-H₂ capacity; demand from shipping's decarbonisation rules (IMO net-zero track) and from methanol-ready ships; 3,500+ jobs; lower dependence on imported bunker fuel.
- **Constraints:** a reliable supply of biogenic CO₂; electrolyser costs and round-trip efficiency losses; uncertain global rules (adoption of the IMO Net-Zero Framework was deferred in October 2025); methanol's roughly half energy density compared with fuel oil, which means bigger tanks; safety and toxicity handling standards.
- **Way forward:** long-term offtake contracts with shipping lines; certification of the CO₂ source and a "green corridor" MoU on the Asia–Europe route; production-linked support for electrolysers; cluster the plant with Deendayal's hydrogen hub and shipbuilding push. *Conclusion:* ports as energy exporters ("Maritime Amrit Kaal").

# PART C — ANSWERS

1. **(b)** India is 31st of 110, and the index scores on a 1–7 scale across 17 pillars. It measures the conditions that enable tourism development, not arrivals or receipts, so statement 3 is wrong.
2. **(c)** All three are HC Chief Justices (Gujarat, Delhi, Telangana), and the 2026 Amendment Act raised the strength to 38. After their appointment the Court would have 37, with one vacancy left, so statement 3 is wrong.
3. **(d)** Mandar is a red-clay, double-headed drum from Jarjatta, Raidih block, Gumla (Jharkhand), played at Sarhul and Karma.
4. **(a)** Cover extends to crossbred cattle, buffalo, sheep, goats, pigs, rabbits, yaks, mithuns, horses, donkeys and camels, not only indigenous cattle and buffaloes.
5. **(b)** Only statement 1 is correct. DPA holds 76%, APL 24%. Burning e-methanol still emits CO₂; it is called carbon-neutral only because that CO₂ was taken from a biogenic source.
6. **(a)** Art. 124(1) lets Parliament fix the number by law, and Art. 124(3) includes the distinguished-jurist route. The Constitution sets no minimum age, only the retirement age of 65.
7. **(c)** H₂ is released at the cathode (reduction), and SMR without CCS gives grey hydrogen. Blue = SMR with carbon capture; nuclear-powered electrolysis is usually called "pink" hydrogen.
