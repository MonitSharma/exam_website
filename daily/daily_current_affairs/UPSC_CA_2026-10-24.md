### 3. NTCA's 6th Management Effectiveness Evaluation of Tiger Reserves (MEE-TR 2026) makes solid-waste management a separate criterion — Environment
- **Exam angle:** Conservation governance is being measured on outcomes and ecology of waste, not just inputs. Ties to International Snow Leopard Day (23 Oct), which highlights big-cat conservation beyond tigers.
- **Facts to memorise:** Launched (inception workshop, with the Technical Manual) in late Sept 2026 by the Environment Minister; organised by **NTCA with the Wildlife Institute of India**. Covers all **58** tiger reserves. MEE began in **2006**, four-yearly; 2022 (5th cycle) covered 51 reserves with **Periyar** first (94.38%). Bands: Excellent ≥90%, Very Good 75–89%, Good 60–74%, Fair 50–59%. New in 2026: mandatory **solid waste management** criterion (forest posts, tourism zones, rescue centres, biomedical waste); more remote-sensing / geo-tagged patrol evidence; community and sociology experts on evaluation teams. Framework adapted from the IUCN-WCPA framework. Snow leopard: 718 (SPAI, WII), Ladakh 477; CITES Appendix I.
- **Notebook:** GS3 – Environment / Biodiversity.
- **Static anchor:** Shankar IAS – Protected Area Network, Project Tiger & NTCA; Spectrum – Wildlife conservation. *Nudge:* update the MEE-TR cycle timeline and cross-link to the Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (2006 amendment).

### 4. CISF moves from territorial to sector-based commands from 1 November — Internal Security
- **Exam angle:** A structural reform of a CAPF that now guards airports, ports, metros, atomic and space sites. The shift mirrors "threat-based" specialisation and the ISPS-Code role at seaports.
- **Facts to memorise:** Effective **1 Nov 2026**; replaces geographic sectors. New/renamed: Coal (Ranchi), Steel (Bhilai), Petroleum & Natural Gas (Guwahati), Power (New Delhi, Kolkata), GBMM & MRTS (government buildings, museums, monuments, mass rapid transit), **West Coast Port Sector (Mumbai)** and **East Coast Port Sector (Chennai)** covering ~250 ports. Aviation, Atomic Energy and Space sectors unchanged. CISF is the nodal force for seaport security (Recognised Security Organisation under the ISPS Code). Raised **1969** under the **CISF Act, 1968**; MHA; strength ≈ 2.7 lakh (as reported).
- **Notebook:** GS3 – Internal Security (CAPFs, critical infrastructure).
- **Static anchor:** Shankar IAS / Vajiram – Security forces & agencies; Laxmikanth – Union executive (MHA). *Nudge:* add CISF sectors to your CAPF comparison table (BSF, CRPF, ITBP, SSB, CISF, Assam Rifles).

### 5. Ladakh: Hill Development Councils extended to all eight districts via LAHDC (Amendment) Regulation, 2026 — Polity & Governance
- **What is new vs 21 Oct (MHA draft governance model rejected by Leh Apex Body/KDA):** the President-promulgated regulation is now in force, and gives a statutory route for hill councils across the UT. Not a Sixth Schedule extension, which remains the core local demand.
- **Exam angle:** Decentralisation in a UT without a legislature; statutory autonomous councils vs constitutional Sixth Schedule protection.
- **Facts to memorise:** Until now elected hill councils existed only in **Leh and Kargil**. Councils can now be set up in all **8 districts** (incl. newly created Sham, Nubra, Changthang, Zanskar, Drass, Sankoo). Constituencies per council: now **7–24** (earlier fixed at 26). Four nominated members (women / unrepresented groups). The Ladakh Lok Sabha MP is an ex-officio member of every council. "Governor/State" replaced by "Administrator/UT of Ladakh". State Election Commission to conduct polls; fixed five-year term; election disputes to the J&K and Ladakh High Court (45 days). Reported dates: promulgated 16 Oct, effective 19 Oct (sources differ slightly; verify before quoting dates).
- **Notebook:** GS2 – Polity / Governance (Union Territories, local governance).
- **Static anchor:** Laxmikanth – Union Territories; Sixth Schedule; Shankar IAS – special provisions. *Nudge:* add a "Sixth Schedule vs statutory councils" comparison table.

### B3. Mains Question (GS2 · 15 marks · 250 words)
**Critically examine** whether extending statutory hill development councils to all districts of Ladakh can substitute for Sixth Schedule protection.

*Model-answer skeleton:*
1. **Intro:** Ladakh (UT without legislature since 2019) had councils only in Leh and Kargil; 2026 regulation extends them to 8 districts, with the Sixth Schedule demand still pending.
2. **Gains:** wider decentralisation; district-level Panchayat powers; State Election Commission and fixed five-year terms; representation of women and unrepresented groups.
3. **Limits:** statutory (can be amended by executive regulation) vs constitutional protection; plans and bye-laws need administration approval; land, resource and identity safeguards absent; MP's ex-officio role and nominations dilute elected autonomy.
4. **Way forward:** consult Leh Apex Body/KDA; consider Sixth Schedule or Article 371-type safeguards; delimitation and phased elections; devolve funds, functions, functionaries.

## PART C — ANSWERS

1. **(b)** — India welcomed the peace agreement; Azerbaijan is not in NATO; Baku hosted COP29 (2024).
2. **(b)** — Fresh issue with no OFS; largest IPO at ≈ ₹30,213 cr vs Hyundai's ₹27,870 cr. Statement 3 is wrong.
3. **(a)** — 58 reserves and a separate waste criterion are correct; MEE is four-yearly and run by NTCA with WII.
4. **(b)** — CISF is under the MHA, not MoD; sector commands and two port sectors are correct.
5. **(b)** — Councils existed only in Leh and Kargil; the new regulation allows all eight districts; they are statutory, not Sixth Schedule bodies.
6. **(a)** — NTCA was made statutory in 2006; Project Tiger began in 1973; NTCA is chaired by the Union Environment Minister.
7. **(b)** — In a fresh issue, proceeds go to the company; in an OFS they go to the selling shareholders; an IPO is a primary-market event; SEBI regulates it.
