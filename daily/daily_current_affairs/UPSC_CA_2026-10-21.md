## Part A — Briefing

### 1. China tightens export controls on tunnel-boring machines from 18 November — International Relations / Economy
**Exam angle:** A clean case of "weaponised interdependence": India's metro, rail and highway tunnelling depends on imported TBMs, and China is adding them to its dual-use export-licence regime just as India–China ties are being normalised post-Galwan. Good for Mains answers on supply-chain resilience, Atmanirbhar manufacturing and India–China relations.
**Key facts:**
- China's Commerce Ministry (MOFCOM) announced the change, reported by Reuters; it takes effect **18 November 2026**. Exporters will need a licence under China's export-control law and dual-use rules. The same update reportedly adjusted controls on drone-related items and dredgers.
- The Hindu's digest adds that heavy machinery used above **3,000 m** (e.g., plateau-type shotcrete machines) is covered, with licences for sensitive or disputed destinations and prior registration for others; this detail rests on one source.
- Precedent: in **Sept 2025** Germany's ambassador said Chinese customs had held at least **three Herrenknecht TBMs** bound for Indian projects; they were later released, and Herrenknecht decided to **produce in India**.

**Notebook:** GS2 – International Relations (India–China); GS3 – Economy (infrastructure, manufacturing).
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS / Mrunal "India and its neighbourhood" – India–China; Mrunal economy pillar "Infrastructure". Add a cross-link: rare-earth and fertiliser curbs (2025) → TBM curbs (2026) as a pattern of China's export-control leverage.

### 2. Aditya-L1's VELC produces Doppler velocity images of the solar corona — Science & Technology
**Exam angle:** The coronagraph's spectroscopy yields plasma-velocity maps that show whether a coronal mass ejection (CME) is moving toward Earth. This ties space-weather forecasting to satellite and power-grid safety, and is a fair Prelims candidate (payloads, orbit, L1).
**Key facts:**
- Scientists at the **Indian Institute of Astrophysics (IIA), Bengaluru** generated Doppler images from the **Visible Emission Line Coronagraph (VELC)**, the largest of Aditya-L1's seven payloads. Positive velocity = plasma moving away from the observer; negative = toward.
- Aditya-L1: launched **2 Sept 2023** on **PSLV-C57**; halo orbit around Sun–Earth **L1** (~**1.5 million km** from Earth) since **6 Jan 2024**; design life 5 years.
- Payloads: **4 remote-sensing** (VELC, SUIT, SoLEXS, HEL1OS) and **3 in-situ** (ASPEX, PAPA, magnetometers).

**Notebook:** GS3 – Science & Technology (Space).
**Static anchor:** Spectrum "Science & Technology – Space" / Mrunal Sci-Tech pillar: ISRO missions. Add a table: Aditya-L1 payload → what it measures; cross-link to solar flares, CMEs and space weather in the Geography (Sun's layers) notes.

### 3. Reuters coins "heat storm" for supersized, long-lasting global heat events — Environment
**Exam angle:** A new framing for climate-extreme data that links to Mains GS1/GS3 questions on heat waves, El Niño (already in the 15 Oct briefing) and disaster preparedness. The concept is journalistic, not an official IPCC/WMO category, so avoid over-claiming in answers.
**Key facts:**
- A heat wave = at least five consecutive days of abnormally high temperature; a **heat storm** covers at least **1% of the globe** at its peak (about half the size of Canada).
- **"Vera"**: largest on record, **367 days** (2023–24), nearly **85%** of the globe. **"Raimundo"**: current event since **28 Feb 2026**, touching almost **three-quarters** of the planet.
- Heat storms averaged about **5 a year** in the 1960s and about **102 a year** this decade; a cascade begun on **6 July 2008** has not stopped.
- **IMD criteria (static)**: heat wave in plains when max temp ≥ **40°C** (37°C coastal, 30°C hills) with departure ≥ **4.5°C**, or an absolute ≥ **45°C**.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment (climate change); GS1 Geography (climatology).
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS "Climate Change – impacts"; NCERT Class XI *Fundamentals of Physical Geography*. Add the IMD heat-wave criteria box and link to NDMA heat-action plans.

### 4. Ladakh: Leh Apex Body and KDA reject the MHA's draft governance model — Polity & Governance
**Exam angle:** Tests Union Territory governance, the Sixth Schedule, constitutional safeguards and federal bargaining. The groups say the draft contradicts the understanding of **22 May**, which treated a UT with a legislature as the minimum.
**Key facts:**
- A **32-page** MHA draft was rejected "in principle" by the **Leh Apex Body (LAB)** and **Kargil Democratic Alliance (KDA)** at a sub-committee meeting in **Leh on 15 Oct 2026**. They called it an "archaic administrative model"; the draft envisages a directly elected UT-level body but, they say, leaves real power with the Lieutenant Governor.
- Earlier: **22 May** understanding (minutes signed **3 July**); on **9 Sept** the MHA floated a new **Article 371-type safeguard** (reported as "371(K)") giving an elected body powers over land, culture, forests and environment.
- Ladakh has been a **UT without a legislature since Aug 2019**. Local demands: Sixth Schedule, statehood, a Public Service Commission, reserved seats. Talks have run since 2023.
- Also in the news: the **Ladakh Bench of the J&K and Ladakh High Court** was inaugurated at Leh on **18 Oct** (hearings from 21 Oct).

**Notebook:** GS2 – Polity & Governance (UTs, Schedules).
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth ch. "Union Territories" and "Scheduled and Tribal Areas" (Fifth and Sixth Schedules; Art. 244, 239A, 371 series). Add a comparison table: Sixth Schedule vs Art. 371 vs Art. 239A, with Ladakh's demand mapped to each.

### 5. PM Internship Scheme after the March revamp: wider pool, higher stipend, attrition still the test — Economy (Schemes)
**Exam angle:** A scheme-evaluation staple: demand-side participation and drop-out rather than funding. Useful for GS3 (employment, skilling) and GS2 (scheme design).
**Key facts:**
- Launched as a pilot in **Oct 2024** with a goal of **1 crore** internship opportunities over five years; nodal ministry: **Ministry of Corporate Affairs**.
- March 2026 revamp (ThePrint): stipend **₹5,000 → ₹9,000**, age **21–24 → 18–25**, duration **12 months → 6–9 months**, employer pool **500 → ~2,000** firms; MSMEs, statutory bodies and GCCs now participate.
- Round 3 (from April; offers till December, joining till January 2027) has 30,000+ joiners against a 1.1 lakh target. Sources differ on past drop-out figures (ThePrint ~54% in Round 1, ~34% in Round 2; another digest reports completion of ~43% and ~39%), so treat exact attrition numbers as provisional.

**Notebook:** GS3 – Economy (employment and skilling).
**Static anchor:** Mrunal "Employment & Skilling" pillar; Economic Survey chapter on employment. Add a one-line row: PMIS vs PMKVY vs Apprentices Act, 1961, with the "completion rate" metric.

### B3. Mains question
**"Critically examine how the use of export controls on critical industrial equipment affects India's infrastructure ambitions, and suggest measures to reduce this vulnerability." (150 words / 10 marks)**
Model-answer skeleton:
- **Intro:** China's licensing of TBMs (from 18 Nov 2026) follows a 2025 episode of held machines; shows weaponised interdependence.
- **Impact:** metro, rail and road tunnelling timelines and costs; capital-goods dependence; leverage in border-linked bargaining.
- **Limits of the threat:** earlier machines were eventually released; global suppliers (e.g., Herrenknecht) are localising in India.
- **Measures:** domestic TBM manufacturing and PLI-style support for capital goods, supplier diversification (Europe, Japan), strategic stockpiles and long-term maintenance contracts, and a diplomacy track on predictable licensing.
