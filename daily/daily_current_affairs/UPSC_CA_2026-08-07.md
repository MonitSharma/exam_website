## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Agni-4 MRBM user trial from Chandipur — the canisterised leg of India's deterrent — Science & Technology

**Headline:** The Strategic Forces Command (SFC), with DRDO, carried out a successful user trial of the medium-range ballistic missile **Agni-4** from the Integrated Test Range, Chandipur (Odisha) on **6 August 2026**; all operational and technical parameters were validated.

**Exam angle:** This is a *user trial* by the SFC, not a developmental test by DRDO — the distinction matters, because a user trial signals that the system is already inducted and is being checked for reliability and operational readiness. The examiner's favourite hooks here are the **canisterised, road-mobile** configuration (which compresses launch preparation time and improves survivability, i.e. strengthens second-strike credibility under India's No First Use posture) and the **two-stage solid-propellant** propulsion (storable, instant-readiness, unlike liquid-fuelled early Agni variants). Pair this with India's declaratory nuclear doctrine — credible minimum deterrence + NFU + massive retaliation — which is where the Mains marks sit.

**Memorise:**

- Agni-4 — **two-stage, solid-propellant, surface-to-surface MRBM; strike range up to ~4,000 km; nuclear-capable; road-mobile and canister-launched.**
- Launched from **Integrated Test Range, Chandipur, Odisha** (Balasore district) — India's principal missile test facility, alongside Dr APJ Abdul Kalam Island (formerly Wheeler Island).
- Conducted under the **Strategic Forces Command**, raised in **2003**, the tri-service body under the **Nuclear Command Authority** that manages and administers India's nuclear arsenal.
- Agni family lineage: Agni-1 (~700–900 km), Agni-2 (~2,000 km), Agni-3 (~3,000 km), Agni-4 (~4,000 km), Agni-5 (5,000+ km, ICBM-class), Agni-Prime (next-generation canisterised SRBM/MRBM).
- The Agni programme began as a **technology demonstrator** under the **Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme (IGMDP, 1983)** — the other four were Prithvi, Trishul, Akash and Nag. IGMDP was formally closed in **2008**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (Defence) / Internal Security.

**Static anchor:** *Defence Technology* chapter of your Sci-Tech notes + **Mrunal Pillar 6 (Defence & Internal Security)**. Add a single comparison table of the entire Agni series — range, stages, propellant, canisterised (Y/N) — and cross-link it to your **nuclear doctrine** page (NFU, credible minimum deterrence, NCA structure). One table beats five paragraphs in Prelims.

### 2. Supreme Court: judicial officers are not government servants — states cannot block a 60→62 retirement age on cost grounds — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** On **5 August 2026**, a bench of **CJI Surya Kant, Justice Joymalya Bagchi and Justice V Mohana**, hearing the long-running *All India Judges Association* matter, observed that **judicial officers are not government servants and constitute a distinct and separate class**, and that States cannot oppose raising the district judiciary's retirement age from **60 to 62** merely by citing financial burden or parity with State government employees.

**Exam angle:** This is a separation-of-powers question dressed as a service-conditions dispute. The Constitution is **silent** on the retirement age of the subordinate judiciary — it is fixed by State rules framed under Articles 233–235, which is exactly why the Supreme Court has had to legislate-by-judgment through the *All India Judges Association* line of cases since the early 1990s. The Court's reasoning — that appointment *by* the State Government does not make a judicial officer an employee *of* the executive — is the quotable Mains line on **judicial independence at the district level**, the tier that handles the overwhelming bulk of India's litigation.

**Memorise:**

- Retirement ages: **Supreme Court judge — 65 (Art. 124)**; **High Court judge — 62 (Art. 217)**; **district judiciary — presently 60**, fixed by State rules; proposal is to raise it to **62**.
- **Article 233** — appointment of district judges by the Governor in consultation with the High Court; a person **not already in State service** needs **7 years** as an advocate or pleader plus a High Court recommendation.
- **Article 234** — recruitment of persons other than district judges to the judicial service (Governor, per rules, after consulting the State PSC and the High Court).
- **Article 235** — **control over the subordinate judiciary vests in the High Court** (posting, promotion, grant of leave).
- The **All India Judges Association** litigation (from 1991–93 onwards) is the vehicle through which pay, cadre strength and superannuation of the district judiciary have been settled.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Judiciary).

**Static anchor:** **Laxmikanth, Ch. 34 "Subordinate Courts"** (and Ch. 25 "High Court" for Art. 235). Add a margin note on the *All India Judges Association* line of cases and a one-line contrast: "SC/HC judges' tenure is constitutionally fixed; the district judiciary's is not — hence recurring litigation." Cross-link to your **judicial independence** and **pendency/judicial reforms** pages.

### 3. RBI holds repo at 5.25%; FY27 growth revised up to 6.7%, inflation down to 5.0% — Economy

**Headline:** The Monetary Policy Committee, meeting **3–5 August 2026** under Governor **Sanjay Malhotra**, voted **unanimously** to keep the policy repo rate unchanged at **5.25%** and retained the **'neutral'** stance, while raising the FY27 real GDP growth projection to **6.7%** and trimming FY27 CPI inflation to **5.0%**.

**Exam angle:** The examinable content is the *shape* of the forecast, not the rate. The RBI expects headline inflation to **peak in Q3 FY27 at 5.9%** on food and fuel before moderating — an inverted, hump-shaped path that explains why a "neutral" stance with an unchanged rate is internally consistent with a cut in the full-year inflation number. Note also the corridor arithmetic: with repo at 5.25%, the **SDF sits 25 bps below at 5.00%** and the **MSF and Bank Rate sit 25 bps above at 5.50%** — the standard symmetric **LAF corridor** that Prelims tests every few years.

**Memorise:**

- **Repo 5.25% (unchanged, unanimous) | SDF 5.00% | MSF & Bank Rate 5.50% | stance: Neutral.**
- **FY27 real GDP: 6.7%** (raised from 6.6%).
- **FY27 CPI inflation: 5.0%** (cut from 5.1%) — Q1 4.1%, Q2 4.7%, **Q3 5.9% (peak)**, Q4 5.5%.
- Statutory frame: the **inflation target is 4% CPI with a ±2% tolerance band**, notified by the Central Government under **Section 45ZA of the RBI Act, 1934**; the **MPC has 6 members** (3 RBI + 3 government-nominated externals), the **Governor chairs it and has a casting vote**, and it meets **at least four times a year**.
- Flagged external risk: continuing conflict in West Asia and its transmission through crude prices.

### 4. Clouded Leopard Conservation Action Plan — the species enters the Species Recovery Programme — Environment

**Headline:** India released a national **Clouded Leopard Conservation Action Plan**, announced around **International Clouded Leopard Day (4 August 2026)**; the plan was cleared at the **91st Standing Committee of the National Board for Wildlife** (Coimbatore, July 2026), and the clouded leopard has been **brought under the Species Recovery Programme of the Integrated Development of Wildlife Habitats (IDWH) scheme**, unlocking dedicated funding and monitoring.

**Exam angle:** Two testable layers. First, the *species*: **Neofelis nebulosa**, an elusive arboreal small cat of Northeast India's dense tropical and subtropical forests, **IUCN Vulnerable**, **Schedule I** of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972, **CITES Appendix I**, and the **State Animal of Meghalaya**. Second, the *architecture*: this is a textbook case of a species moving from generic protection to a **named recovery programme with a landscape approach** — the plan identifies **14 priority conservation landscapes** in the Northeast and leans on camera traps, occupancy surveys, genetics and **environmental DNA (eDNA)**. That last detail is the Sci-Tech–Environment crossover UPSC likes.

**Memorise:**

- **Clouded leopard (*Neofelis nebulosa*)** — IUCN **Vulnerable**; WPA 1972 **Schedule I**; **CITES Appendix I**; State Animal of **Meghalaya**; found in **Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Meghalaya, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram, Tripura** and parts of the eastern Himalayan foothills.
- Now added to the **Species Recovery Programme** under **IDWH**, a Centrally Sponsored Scheme of MoEFCC — joining species such as the snow leopard, Great Indian Bustard, dugong, hangul and Nilgiri tahr.
- CAP tools: **habitat security, forest connectivity/corridors, camera-trap and occupancy monitoring, genetics and eDNA, anti-poaching, community participation**; **14 priority landscapes** in Northeast India.
- Institutional hook: cleared at the **91st SC-NBWL**. The **National Board for Wildlife** is a **statutory** body under Section 5A of the WPA 1972, **chaired by the Prime Minister**; most business is transacted by its **Standing Committee, chaired by the Union Environment Minister**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Biodiversity.

**Static anchor:** **Shankar IAS Environment — "Conservation Efforts" / "Species in News"** chapter. Add the clouded leopard to your one-page "recovery-programme species" list with IUCN status, WPA Schedule and range States, and cross-link it to your **NBWL / SC-NBWL** institutional page and to the **wild cats of India** table (tiger, lion, snow leopard, clouded leopard, leopard, caracal, fishing cat, Asiatic golden cat, marbled cat).

### 5. Ganga Water Sharing Treaty enters its final months — MEA says renewal talks stay inside the bilateral mechanism — International Relations

**Headline:** With the **1996 India–Bangladesh Ganga Water Sharing Treaty set to expire on 12 December 2026**, the MEA (spokesperson **Randhir Jaiswal**, briefing of **4 August 2026**) stated that any discussion on the Treaty will be held **through the existing bilateral mechanisms, including the Joint Rivers Commission**, noting that India and Bangladesh share **54 rivers** and multiple technical-level structures.

**Exam angle:** This is the single highest-value India–Bangladesh theme available for CSE 2027, because the Treaty actually lapses inside the exam cycle. Three angles to hold: (i) the **federal** angle — West Bengal's consent problem, the same fault line that has stalled **Teesta** since 2011; (ii) the **hydrological** angle — the Treaty's dry-season formula was built on historic flow assumptions that upstream abstraction and a changing monsoon have since eroded, which is why commentary argues for an *ecological reset* rather than a mechanical renewal; (iii) the **diplomatic** angle — India's insistence on "existing bilateral mechanisms" is a deliberate signal against internationalisation of the river question.

**Memorise:**

- **Ganga Water Sharing Treaty signed 12 December 1996** by PM **H.D. Deve Gowda** and Bangladesh PM **Sheikh Hasina**; **30-year duration**; expires **December 2026**.
- It governs sharing at the **Farakka Barrage** during the **dry season (1 January – 31 May)** in **ten-day cycles**, with a guaranteed minimum flow to Bangladesh.
- **Farakka Barrage** — **Murshidabad district, West Bengal**, roughly **18 km** from the Bangladesh border; commissioned **1975**; diverts Ganga water via a feeder canal into the **Bhagirathi–Hooghly** to flush silt from **Kolkata Port**.
- The **Joint Rivers Commission (JRC)** was established in **1972** for cooperation on shared rivers, flood management and water resources.
- Unresolved companion issue: **Teesta** (rises in Sikkim, flows through West Bengal into Bangladesh); a deal came close in **2011** and again in **2017** but was never signed. The **Kushiyara** agreement (2022) remains the only recent water-sharing arrangement concluded.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India & its Neighbourhood) + GS1 Geography (Drainage).

**Static anchor:** Your **India–Bangladesh** country page + **Mrunal/Shankar Geography — Drainage: Ganga system**. Add a dated timeline strip (1972 JRC → 1975 Farakka commissioned → 1996 Treaty → 2011/2017 Teesta near-misses → 2022 Kushiyara → Dec 2026 expiry) and cross-link it to your **transboundary water diplomacy** page (Indus Waters Treaty, Mahakali Treaty with Nepal, Brahmaputra/China data-sharing MoUs) — UPSC likes to test these comparatively.

### C. Mains-Style Question

**GS2 — International Relations (250 words, 15 marks)**

> *"The impending expiry of the 1996 Ganga Water Sharing Treaty is less a legal deadline than a test of India's federal and hydrological readiness."* **Examine** the challenges before India in renegotiating the Treaty, and suggest a framework for a durable successor arrangement.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the deadline.** Treaty signed 12 December 1996 for 30 years; expires December 2026; governs dry-season (1 Jan–31 May) sharing at Farakka in ten-day cycles; MEA has confirmed renewal will be handled through existing bilateral mechanisms, chiefly the Joint Rivers Commission (1972). India and Bangladesh share 54 rivers — the Ganga is the only one with a long-term treaty.
2. **Challenge 1 — the federal constraint.** Water is a State subject (Entry 17, List II), while treaty-making is a Union power (Art. 253). West Bengal's consent is politically indispensable; its objections stalled Teesta in 2011 and 2017 and shape the Farakka calculus, including Bihar's separate grievance on siltation upstream of the barrage.
3. **Challenge 2 — the hydrological constraint.** The 1996 formula rests on historic dry-season flow assumptions now eroded by upstream abstraction, groundwater depletion, sedimentation at Farakka and a more erratic monsoon. A treaty that shares a shrinking quantum without addressing the quantum itself renews a dispute rather than resolving it.
4. **Challenge 3 — the strategic constraint.** Political volatility in Dhaka, third-party interest in the Brahmaputra–Ganga basin, and India's own position as a lower riparian to China make internationalisation undesirable — hence the insistence on bilateralism.
5. **Way forward.** A successor arrangement should build in: an **environmental-flow floor** rather than a purely distributive formula; **joint, transparent real-time gauging** and open data; a **basin-wide rather than point-wise (Farakka-only)** approach covering sediment and salinity ingress in the Sundarbans; a **review-and-adjust clause** indexed to measured flows; and **structured consultation with riparian States** before signature, so that federal buy-in precedes rather than follows diplomacy.

*Quotable line:* "A river treaty that allocates water without protecting the river merely schedules the next dispute."
