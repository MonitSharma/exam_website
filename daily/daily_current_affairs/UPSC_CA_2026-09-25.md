## Part A — Briefing

### 1. Deep depression 'Arnab' crosses the coast near Kalingapatnam — and the name never actually gets used — Geography

**Headline:** The Bay of Bengal system made landfall between Visakhapatnam and Gopalpur, close to **Kalingapatnam (Srikakulam district, Andhra Pradesh)**, at about **02:47 IST on 24 September 2026**, and IMD kept it at **deep depression** intensity — so "Arnab", the Bangladesh-contributed name next in line, was never formally assigned.

**Exam angle (2–3 lines):** This is the cleanest "Places in News" + disaster-classification combination of the season. UPSC's favourite trap is exactly this gap — a named system versus an unnamed one — because a name is assigned only once a system reaches **cyclonic storm** intensity (≥62 kmph). Pair it with the withdrawal story: the **southwest monsoon withdrew from Delhi on 23 September 2026**, two days ahead of the normal 25 September date and the earliest exit since 2002.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Landfall ~02:47 IST, 24 Sep 2026, between **Visakhapatnam and Gopalpur**, near **Kalingapatnam**; gusts to **75 kmph**; intensity held ~12 hours post-landfall.
- Remained a **deep depression** (50–61 kmph), below the 62 kmph cyclonic-storm threshold — hence no name used. **"Arnab"** was contributed by **Bangladesh** (Bengali: "ocean").
- Red alert districts: **Koraput, Gajapati, Ganjam (Odisha)**; spillover rain into West Bengal, Chhattisgarh, Telangana.
- Delhi monsoon withdrawal **23 Sep 2026** (normal: 25 Sep; earliest since **20 Sep 2002**); Safdarjung seasonal rain **728.6 mm** vs normal **629.7 mm** (+16%), still classed "normal".
- Withdrawal criteria applied: cessation of rainfall, falling lower-tropospheric moisture, and establishment of an **anticyclonic circulation**.

**Destination notebook:** GS1 — Geography (Climatology / Indian Monsoon) + GS3 — Disaster Management.

**Static anchor:** *NCERT Class XI Fundamentals of Physical Geography*, Ch. 10 "Atmospheric Circulation and Weather Systems" + *Shankar IAS / GC Leong* tropical cyclones. **Nudge:** on your cyclone page, write the full IMD intensity ladder (LPA → Depression 31–49 → Deep Depression 50–61 → Cyclonic Storm 62–87 → SCS 88–117 → VSCS 118–166 → ESCS 167–221 → Super Cyclone ≥222 kmph) and cross-link to the RSMC New Delhi naming note: 13-member WMO/ESCAP Panel, 169-name 2020 list, names never reused in the North Indian Ocean.

### 2. The Crop Weather Watch Group puts satellite numbers on a drought in six states — Agriculture

**Headline:** At the **Crop Weather Watch Group** meeting of **21 September 2026** (reported 24 September), the **Mahalanobis National Crop Forecast Centre (NCFC)** flagged drought/soil-moisture stress across **Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu**, with secondary stress in Punjab, Haryana, Kerala, Chhattisgarh and Odisha.

**Exam angle:** This is the item to use when a question asks *how* India actually detects agricultural drought — the answer is a remote-sensing stack, not just rainfall departure. It also sets up the kharif-shortfall → rabi-risk chain as the monsoon retreats, and gives you a concrete case study for both the Manual for Drought Management and the "technology in agriculture" GS3 slot.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Analysis window **1 June – 19 September 2026**; dry spells of **two to three weeks** (days with <2.5 mm rain) in several states.
- Data stack: **IMD** rainfall + **SMAP** root-zone soil moisture + **VIIRS** NDVI/LSWI at **500 m** + **Sentinel-1 SAR** backscatter + **CWC** reservoir levels.
- **NDVI down more than 30%** in **Jaisalmer, Bikaner, Barmer (Rajasthan)** and **Kutch (Gujarat)**.
- Impact reported: delayed paddy transplanting, with some farmers shifting out of paddy.
- **NCFC**, New Delhi, sits under the Department of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare and runs **FASAL** and **NADAMS**.
- Companion Agriculture nugget for the same day: **Z.T. Lalrithangi** of **Sangau, Lawngtlai district, Mizoram** named among FAO's **"100 Women Heroes in Agrifood Systems"** (100 selected from 600+ nominees across 131 countries; 2 Indians), under the UN's **International Year of the Woman Farmer 2026**; award at the World Food Forum, Rome, 12–16 Oct 2026.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (+ GS3 Disaster Management for the drought-declaration machinery).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* Agriculture pillar (irrigation, cropping patterns, drought) + *Shankar IAS* chapter on agriculture and climate. **Nudge:** add a one-page box to your drought notes distinguishing **meteorological / hydrological / agricultural** drought, and cross-link the **Manual for Drought Management (2016, revised 2020)** — mandatory indicator (rainfall deviation + dry spell) plus four impact-indicator classes, Trigger-1 and Trigger-2, declaration by the **State Government**, SDRF/NDRF assistance.

### 3. Edible-oil duties cut again — crude sunflower oil goes to zero — Economy

**Headline:** **Notification No. 31/2026-Customs dated 23 September 2026** (Department of Revenue) cut basic customs duty across the edible-oil basket **with effect from 24 September 2026**, taking **crude sunflower oil to 0%**.

**Exam angle:** A textbook illustration of tariffs used as an anti-inflation instrument, and of the **crude-versus-refined duty differential** as industrial policy — the gap was widened, which favours importing crude and refining at home. The trade-off is the classic one UPSC likes: consumer price relief versus protection for domestic oilseed growers under NMEO.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **Crude palm and crude soybean oil: 10% → 5%.** **Crude sunflower oil: 10% → 0%.**
- **Refined palm and refined soybean oil: 32.5% → 27.5%.** **Refined sunflower oil: 32.5% → 22.5%.**
- Effective **24 Sep 2026**; industry directed to pass the benefit through ahead of the festive season.
- Trigger cited in reporting: **WPI-based food inflation at 7.1% in August 2026, a 20-month high**.
- Crude–refined gap widened (palm/soy: 22.5 pp; sunflower: 22.5 pp) — protects domestic refining capacity.
- India imports **over half** of its edible-oil requirement; **palm oil** is the single largest imported edible oil.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (External Sector / Inflation) with an Agriculture cross-entry.

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* Economy pillar on inflation and on foreign trade/tariffs. **Nudge:** on your edible-oil page, tabulate **BCD + AIDC + Social Welfare Surcharge** as the three layers of an effective import duty, and cross-link **NMEO-Oil Palm (2021, ₹11,040 crore, North East and A&N focus)** and **NMEO-Oilseeds (₹10,103 crore, 2024-25 to 2030-31, under Krishonnati Yojana)** so the "protect the farmer vs cool the CPI" tension sits on one page.

### 4. DDU-GKY 2.0 begins its first batches — 2,015 batches, ~70,000 rural youth from October — Society

**Exam angle:** The most examinable feature is not the number but the *design shift*: from "train and count" to **placement-linked, industry-led training with mandatory post-placement support**. Use it as the rural-skilling counterpart to the urban skilling question, and as evidence in any answer on the trained-versus-placed gap.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **2,015 batches**, ~**70,000 youth**, from **October 2026**; **24 States/UTs** had allocated targets at announcement.
- **Over 9 lakh training slots for 2026–2028** across all **31 participating States/UTs**; **5.6 lakh** targets already distributed to **1,200+ Project Implementation Agencies (PIAs)**; PIA-allocation deadline **31 October 2026**.

- Target group **15–35 years**, from marginalised and vulnerable rural households; courses **3–12 months**, **NSQF-aligned**.
- Launched **25 September 2014**; sub-schemes **Roshni** (LWE-affected districts) and **Himayat** (J&K and Ladakh).
- DDU-GKY 2.0 adds industry-led curriculum design, post-placement support, upskilling and reskilling.

### 5. IEA's Special Report on Electrification lands in New Delhi — India's electrification rate 19% → 32% by 2035 — Reports & Indices

**Headline:** The **International Energy Agency** released its **Special Report on Electrification** on **23 September 2026** at a **Pre-COP dialogue in New Delhi**, commissioned by **Türkiye (COP31 President)** and **Australia (President of Negotiations)**; it projects India's electrification rate rising from about **19% to 32% by 2035**.

**Exam angle:** "Electrification rate" here means **the share of electricity in final energy consumption** — a definition worth memorising because it is the kind of term UPSC tests directly. The report converts decarbonisation into an *energy-security* argument, which is exactly the framing India uses, and gives you a quotable Mains line: electrification as import-bill insurance rather than as climate cost.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **India: ~19% today → 32% by 2035**; **Southeast Asia: 25% → 37%**; **global: ~23% → 35% by 2035** under the High Electrification Scenario.
- Fuel importers could cut import bills by about **a quarter by 2035** versus 2025 — roughly **USD 400 billion a year**; for India, savings of nearly **30% of its 2025 import bill**.
- India evidence cited: **EV sales up over 115%** (Mar–Jun 2026 vs 2025); **electric three-wheelers over 65%** of 2025 three-wheeler sales; **5 lakh induction cooktops** under a government procurement push.
- India + Southeast Asia share of global air-conditioning demand nearly doubles from **under 10% to 20%** by 2035.
- High-electrification pathway cuts global oil demand by **18 million barrels per day**.
- Türkiye's COP31 Presidency has proposed the **35%-by-2035** figure as a **voluntary** implementation benchmark.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Environment / Energy, plus a Reports & Indices ledger entry.

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment*, chapter on climate change and international climate negotiations (UNFCCC/COP). **Nudge:** update your COP table with **COP31 — Türkiye as host/President, Australia presiding over negotiations**, and cross-link the IEA's flagship set (**World Energy Outlook**, **Electricity 2026**, **Net Zero Roadmap**) so you never confuse an IEA report with an IRENA or IPCC one.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Disaster Management / Agriculture, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *Satellite-based soil-moisture and vegetation indices are now central to how India detects agricultural drought.* **Examine** the strengths and limitations of remote-sensing-based drought monitoring, and suggest how it can be better integrated with India's existing drought declaration framework.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the shift (intro, ~40 words):** Drought detection has moved from rainfall departure alone to a multi-sensor stack — IMD rainfall, SMAP root-zone soil moisture, VIIRS NDVI/LSWI at 500 m, Sentinel-1 SAR, CWC reservoir data — operationalised through NCFC's NADAMS and FASAL. The September 2026 assessment across six States is the live illustration.

2. **Strengths:** near-real-time and spatially continuous coverage down to sub-district level; captures *agricultural* drought (crop stress) rather than only meteorological deficit; SAR sees through cloud during the monsoon; detects intra-seasonal dry spells that seasonal rainfall averages mask; enables objective, contestation-resistant triggers for relief.

3. **Limitations:** indices measure vegetation vigour, not yield or income loss — irrigated fields can look healthy while groundwater is being mined; 500 m pixels blur India's small and fragmented holdings; NDVI saturates in dense canopy and confuses weeds with crop; cloud cover degrades optical data exactly when it is most needed; declaration remains a State political decision, so better data does not guarantee faster relief; thin ground-truth networks limit calibration.

4. **Way forward:** statutorily hard-wire the Manual for Drought Management's impact indicators into Trigger-2 verification; densify automatic weather stations and soil-moisture probes for calibration; move to higher-resolution and hyperspectral data plus crop-specific yield models; link drought flags automatically to PMFBY claim settlement and fodder/water contingency plans; publish district dashboards so declaration becomes evidence-led rather than negotiated.

5. **Conclusion:** Remote sensing has solved the detection problem; the binding constraint is now institutional — converting a satellite signal into a timely, rule-bound entitlement.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** Landfall near Kalingapatnam, between Visakhapatnam and Gopalpur, ~02:47 IST on 24 Sep 2026; 'Arnab' is Bangladesh's contribution to the North Indian Ocean list. Statement 2 is wrong — IMD kept it a **deep depression** (50–61 kmph), below the 62 kmph cyclonic-storm threshold.

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Crude sunflower 10% → 0%; crude palm and crude soybean 10% → 5%. Statement 3 is wrong — the crude–refined differential was **widened** (refined palm/soy fell only to 27.5%, refined sunflower to 22.5%), which favours importing crude and refining domestically.

**Q3 — (b) International Energy Agency (IEA).** Released 23 Sep 2026 at a Pre-COP dialogue in New Delhi, commissioned by Türkiye (COP31 President) and Australia (President of Negotiations).

**Q4 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** DDU-GKY is implemented by the **Ministry of Rural Development**, not MSDE — that is the single most common confusion on this scheme. Target group 15–35 years; Roshni (LWE districts) and Himayat (J&K and Ladakh) are its sub-schemes.

**Q5 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** NCFC's September 2026 assessment combined IMD rainfall, SMAP root-zone soil moisture, VIIRS NDVI/LSWI at 500 m, Sentinel-1 SAR backscatter and CWC reservoir levels.

**Q6 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** A name is assigned only on reaching cyclonic-storm intensity (≥62 kmph), which is precisely why 'Arnab' went unused; RSMC New Delhi is the naming authority for the North Indian Ocean. Statement 3 is wrong — unlike the Atlantic, North Indian Ocean names are **retired after use and not repeated**.

**Q7 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** NMEO-Oil Palm (2021) focuses on the North East and A&N Islands; NMEO-Oilseeds (2024) runs to 2030-31 with a ₹10,103 crore outlay under Krishonnati Yojana. Statement 3 is wrong — India is a large **net importer**, meeting over half its edible-oil demand through imports, with palm oil the largest single import.
