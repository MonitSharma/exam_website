## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. A barrier lake in Tibet breaches; the Bhote Koshi–Trishuli valley is evacuated a second time — Geography

**Headline (28 Aug):** A landslide-dammed **barrier lake** that had formed in Tibet near the confluence of the **Chhochen Khola** and **Purepu Tsangpo** breached on Friday, forcing a fresh round of evacuations along Nepal's **Trishuli** valley — two days after the 26 August **Bhotekoshi flood** that has so far yielded **475 recovered bodies**, with the Nepal Tourism Board listing **667 tourists and travellers still missing** (127 of them Nepali residents).

**Exam angle.** This is the cleanest available case study of a **transboundary, cascading water hazard**: an upstream landslide/debris dam in Chinese territory, a downstream victim state with no treaty-backed right to real-time data, and an Indian consular-evacuation response (EAM Jaishankar called the rescue of Indians "highest priority"). It also lets you separate two hazards students routinely conflate — a **GLOF** (moraine-dammed *glacial* lake) versus a **landslide/debris-dammed barrier lake**, which is what actually failed here. Expect it in Prelims as a river-mapping question and in Mains as a hydro-diplomacy/DM question.

**Facts to memorise.**
- The Chhochen Khola and Purepu Tsangpo **rise in Tibet**, merge, and cross into Nepal as the **Bhote Koshi** of Rasuwa district — which becomes the **Trishuli**, joins the Kali Gandaki at **Devghat** to form the **Narayani**, and enters Bihar as the **Gandak**. (Note the trap: this Rasuwa Bhote Koshi is *Gandaki* basin, **not** the Koshi basin — a *different* Bhote Koshi in Sindhupalchok feeds the Sun Koshi.)
- China's Ministry of Water Resources put the lake at **~2 million m³** on Thursday, with **~3 million m³** more inflow expected over three days.
- Districts affected: **Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading**; the corridor is the **Rasuwagadhi–Kerung (Gyirong)** trade point and a Kailash Manasarovar Yatra route.
- India has **no water treaty with China**; only two **MoUs on hydrological data sharing** (Brahmaputra and Sutlej), renewable and seasonal. There is **no early-warning arrangement covering Nepal's Tibetan headwaters**.

**Notebook:** GS1 Geography (drainage / geophysical hazards) + GS3 Disaster Management, with a GS2 IR spillover (India–Nepal, India–China).

**Static anchor:** *NCERT Class XI 'India: Physical Environment', Ch. 3 "Drainage System"* — on your Himalayan-rivers page, redraw the **Gandaki system** (Kali Gandaki + Trishuli → Narayani → Gandak) and label Devghat; then add a two-column box "**GLOF vs landslide-dammed barrier lake**" beside your existing **South Lhonak (Sikkim, Oct 2023)** GLOF note. Cross-link to *Shankar IAS Environment*, disaster chapter.

### 2. Nilekani task force opens public consultation on reforming India's public examination system — Polity & Governance

**Headline (28 Aug):** The **High-Powered Task Force (HPTF) on Examination Reforms**, chaired by **Nandan Nilekani** and constituted on **26 July 2026**, has thrown open a nationwide public consultation — students, parents, teachers, exam bodies, technologists and civil society can send inputs until **13 September 2026** through a **DoPT-hosted portal**.

**Exam angle.** Two GS2 hooks in one story. First, **regulatory capacity**: the NTA/recruitment-exam integrity problem is a textbook case of an institution scaled faster than its process controls. Second, **consultative rule-making** — the HPTF is running a structured pre-legislative-style consultation, which is exactly the "citizen participation in policy formulation" line the syllabus asks for. Note that the portal sits on **dopt.gov.in**, not the Education Ministry — signalling a mandate wider than NTA, covering **central recruitment examinations** too (relevant to your SSC CGL backup).

**Facts to memorise.**
- **Chair:** Nandan Nilekani. Reported members include **Tapan Deka** (former IB Director), **S. Somanath** (former ISRO Chairman), **V. Kamakoti** (Director, IIT Madras), **Anita Karwal** (former Education Secretary) and **Amrit Lal Meena**.
- **Consultation window closes 13 September 2026**; portal `examreforms-taskforce.dopt.gov.in`; also via helpline **1800-180-4747**, WhatsApp and email; submissions accepted in **English, Hindi and regional languages**.
- **Six mandate heads:** conduct & security of exams; exam design and assessment methodology; governance and accountability; prevention/detection/redressal of malpractice; technology (secure **computer-based testing**) and infrastructure; inclusion, transparency, grievance redressal and **student well-being**.
- Distinguish this from the **earlier (2024) high-level committee chaired by K. Radhakrishnan** on NTA reform — different body, different year.
- Statutory backdrop: the **Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024** (assent Feb 2024, in force **21 June 2024**).

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (institutional reform, transparency & accountability, citizen participation).

Manufacturing rose to **127.4** (from 125.3) and electricity to **134.8** (from 132.3), while **mining collapsed to 94.4 from 110.6**.

Also note the **composition** story: renewables inside the electricity index jumped to **186.1 from 164.5** while non-renewable *fell* to 119.6 — a usable data point for any "energy transition is visible in the data" Mains line.

### 4. India's nuclear pathway is laid out: PFBR criticality, the SHANTI Act, and 100 GW by 2047 — Science & Technology

**Headline (28 Aug):** A government explainer consolidates India's nuclear trajectory: the **Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor (PFBR) at Kalpakkam attained first criticality in April 2026**, formally opening **Stage II** of Bhabha's three-stage programme, against a **Nuclear Energy Mission** target of **100 GW by 2047** enabled by the **SHANTI Act, 2025**.

**Exam angle.** Given your Physics optional, this item pays twice. The three-stage programme is the single most repeated Sci-Tech theme in UPSC (Prelims fertile/fissile distinctions; Mains "self-reliance and energy security"). The **legal** half is newer and under-prepared: SHANTI **repeals both the Atomic Energy Act, 1962 and the Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act, 2010**, ending NPCIL's operating monopoly while keeping enrichment, heavy-water production and off-site spent-fuel management with the Centre (DAE).

**Facts to memorise.**
- **Current fleet:** **24 reactors, 8.78 GW** installed; **9 units / 7.5 GW** under construction; **10 indigenous PHWRs approved in fleet mode**; pre-project work on **two 500 MWe FBRs**.
- **PFBR:** 500 MWe, **MOX**-fuelled, designed by **IGCAR** Kalpakkam, ~**90% indigenous** equipment; first criticality **April 2026**.
- **Three stages:** (I) **PHWR on natural uranium** → reprocess spent fuel for **Pu-239**; (II) **Fast Breeder Reactors** on Pu-239, breeding more fissile material and converting **Th-232 → U-233**; (III) **Th-232/U-233 thermal breeders**. **Th-232 is fertile, not fissile**; **U-233 is fissile**.
- **Thorium geography:** monazite-bearing **coastal beach sands of Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, West Bengal**, plus **Jharkhand**. India's uranium is low-grade → imports needed; hence the thorium route.
- **SMRs:** **₹20,000 crore** under the Nuclear Energy Mission (Budget 2025-26); **BSMR-200 (220 MWe, BARC+NPCIL)**, **SMR-55 (55 MWe)**, and an **HTGR for hydrogen**; target of **at least five indigenous SMRs by 2033**. SMR ≤ **300 MWe**; micro-reactors ≤ **20 MWe**.
- **SHANTI Act, 2025** in force **21 December 2025**; permits Indian private companies and JVs to build/own/operate/decommission plants; the definition of "company" **excludes companies incorporated outside India** (no FDI route as such).
- India is among the few countries with **vitrification** technology for high-level waste; it follows a **closed fuel cycle**.

**Notebook:** GS3 Science & Technology (indigenisation) + GS3 Energy/Environment (clean baseload, NDC).

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment, "Energy" chapter* + your Sci-Tech nuclear page — draw the three-stage flow diagram with **fertile/fissile labelled at each arrow**, then add a fresh "**Law**" box: Atomic Energy Act 1962 + CLNDA 2010 → **repealed by SHANTI Act 2025**. Cross-link to your 25 Aug DRDO tech-transfer note under the common theme "private industry entering strategic sectors".

### 5. Ayyankali's 163rd birth anniversary — the Villuvandi Yatra and the making of Dalit assertion in Travancore — Modern History

**Headline (28 Aug):** The Prime Minister and Home Minister paid homage to **Mahatma Ayyankali** on his **163rd birth anniversary** (b. **28 August 1863, Venganoor**), the same day Kerala marked the birth anniversary of **Sree Narayana Guru**.

**Exam angle.** UPSC has a durable appetite for **anti-caste and social-reform movements outside the Bengal/Bombay axis**, and Kerala is where the syllabus's "social empowerment" strand meets Modern History. Ayyankali is the sharper answer-writing example than Narayana Guru for one reason: he shifted the register from **temple-entry and spiritual reform to civic rights, education and labour** — road access, school admission, and India's arguably **first agricultural labourers' strike**.

**Facts to memorise.**
- **Villuvandi Yatra, 1893** — Ayyankali rode a bullock cart through Venganoor in upper-caste attire, asserting Dalit (Pulaya) right to public roads. By **1900**, Pulayars had access to most roads except those leading to Hindu temples.
- **Sadhu Jana Paripalana Sangham (SJPS), 1907** — his organisational vehicle; the same year he led an **agricultural labourers' strike** demanding **school admission for Dalit children**.
- **1912** — nominated to the **Sri Mulam Popular Assembly** (Travancore), among the earliest Dalit legislators in India.
- **Kallumala agitation, 1915** — Pulaya women's right to discard the stone-bead necklace and wear upper-body cloth.
- Contrast set: **Sree Narayana Guru** — **Aruvippuram Pratishta, 1888**; **SNDP Yogam, 1903**; "**One caste, one religion, one God for man**"; Ezhava social mobility. Ayyankali worked among **Pulayas**; Narayana Guru among **Ezhavas**.
- Later Kerala chain to remember: **Vaikom Satyagraha (1924-25)** → **Guruvayur Satyagraha (1931-32)** → **Temple Entry Proclamation (1936)**.

**Notebook:** GS1 Modern History (socio-religious reform) + GS1 Society / GS2 Social Justice.

**Static anchor:** *Spectrum, "Socio-Religious Reform Movements" chapter* — add a **Kerala column** to your reform-movements table with three rows (Ayyankali / Narayana Guru / Vaikom–Temple Entry) and columns for community, method, and whether the demand was **spiritual or civic**. Cross-link to your Ambedkar page under "Mahad Satyagraha (1927) — the same civic-rights logic, three decades later."

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Governance, 250 words, 15 marks)

**"Repeated failures in the conduct of high-stakes public examinations are less a problem of law and more a problem of institutional design." Critically examine this statement in the light of recent reform initiatives in India's examination ecosystem.**

*Model-answer skeleton:*

1. **Frame the stakes.** Public examinations are the principal instrument of social mobility and of merit-based entry into the state; a breach is therefore not an administrative lapse but an erosion of the state's claim to fairness. Note scale — crores of candidates across NTA, SSC, RRB, IBPS and central departments.

2. **The legal layer, and its limits.** The Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024 supplies deterrence: cognizable, non-bailable, non-compoundable offences; 3-5 years for unfair means; 5-10 years and ₹1 crore minimum fine for organised crime; DySP/ACP-level investigation. But criminal deterrence operates *ex post*; it does not fix question-paper custody chains, centre allotment, vendor accountability or grievance redressal — i.e. it punishes the leak, it does not prevent it.

3. **The institutional-design deficit.** Argue the substance: exam bodies scaled volume faster than process controls; heavy outsourcing to private vendors without commensurate audit; opaque centre-allocation and no independent process audit; weak, slow grievance redressal; no security-classification discipline over papers. Invoke the 2nd ARC's "Citizen Centric Administration" logic — service standards, accountability, and redressal must be designed in, not bolted on.

4. **The current reform response, and a balanced verdict.** The Radhakrishnan Committee (2024) on NTA reform and the Nilekani-led High-Powered Task Force (2026) — running an open consultation to 13 September 2026 across security, assessment design, governance, technology (secure computer-based testing), inclusion and student well-being — mark a shift from punishment to process. Conclude: law and design are complements, not substitutes; deterrence without an auditable, technology-secured and consultatively legitimated process will keep producing the same failures. Add the caveat that technology (CBT, biometrics) itself creates new attack surfaces and access barriers, so inclusion must be engineered alongside security.

### D. PYQ Mirror — how UPSC has asked these themes before

**Theme: India's nuclear programme and fast breeder reactors** — *verified.*
> **UPSC Mains GS3, 2013:** "Give an account of the growth and development of nuclear science and technology in India. What is the advantage of fast breeder reactor programme in India?" (250 words, 15 marks)

The 2026 update to that answer is the PFBR's **April 2026 first criticality** plus the **SHANTI Act, 2025** — i.e., the 2013 answer's "advantage of FBR" argument (breeding fissile material, bridging to thorium) can now be written with a completed milestone rather than a promise.

**Theme: landslides and mass-movement hazards** — *verified.*
> **UPSC Mains GS1, 2021:** "Describe the various causes and the effects of landslides. Mention the important components of the National Landslide Risk Management Strategy."

Today's barrier-lake breach is the ideal contemporary illustration for that answer: a landslide *dams* a river, the impoundment fails, and the hazard propagates downstream across an international boundary — landslide risk and flood risk are not separate files.

The pattern to internalise: UPSC tests **who compiles which index**, not the month's reading. Keep a single table of IIP (NSO/MoSPI), CPI (NSO/MoSPI), CPI-IW (Labour Bureau), WPI (Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT), and the forthcoming Index of Services Production (MoSPI). *Treat the exact year/wording of this one as approximate — verify against the official 2015 paper before quoting it.*

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Statements 1 and 2 are correct: the two Tibetan streams merge and enter Nepal in Rasuwa as the Bhote Koshi, which becomes the Trishuli, joins the Kali Gandaki at Devghat to form the Narayani, and enters India as the Gandak — a *Gandaki*-basin river despite the "Koshi" name. Statement 3 is wrong: this was a **landslide/debris-dammed barrier lake**, not a moraine-dammed glacial lake, so the event is not a GLOF.

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The HPTF is chaired by Nandan Nilekani and its consultation portal is hosted on a DoPT domain, indicating a mandate spanning central recruitment examinations, not only NTA tests. Statement 3 is wrong — its mandate is to recommend reforms across conduct, security, design, governance and inclusion; abolishing or replacing the NTA is not its stated remit.

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The series uses base year 2022-23 with NIC 2025; manufacturing's weight is 76.062; and in July 2026 mining fell to 94.4 (from 110.6) while electricity rose to 134.8 (from 132.3).

**Q4 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** PFBR first criticality was in April 2026, opening Stage II; SHANTI, 2025 repealed both the 1962 and 2010 Acts. Statement 3 is wrong — **enrichment of nuclear material, heavy-water production and off-site spent-fuel management remain with the Central Government (DAE)**; private participation is confined to building, owning, operating and decommissioning plants.

**Q5 — (b).** The Villuvandi Yatra (1893) was Ayyankali's bullock-cart ride through Venganoor asserting the Pulaya right to public roads. Aruvippuram (1888) was Narayana Guru's consecration; Phule and Vaikunda Swami relate to different movements.

**Q6 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** All three are provisions of the 2024 Act: offences are cognizable, non-bailable and non-compoundable; unfair means attracts 3-5 years plus fine up to ₹10 lakh; and investigation is by an officer of DySP/ACP rank or above.

**Q7 — (c) All three.** Trishuli is a Gandaki-system river; the Arun (rising in Tibet as the Phung Chu/Bum-chu) is one of the seven Koshi tributaries; the Bheri is a major left-bank tributary of the Karnali.
