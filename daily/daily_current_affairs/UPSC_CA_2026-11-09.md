## Briefing

### 1. SC holds National Book Trust is "State" under Article 12 (reported 6 Nov) — Polity & Governance
**Exam angle:** In *Chairman, National Book Trust India v. A.R. Abdul Gaffar* a Bench of Justices Prashant Kumar Mishra and Augustine George Masih — dismissed the NBT's appeal and upheld the Delhi High Court: a society formed by government resolution is "State" where government control is deep and pervasive. The Court applied the cumulative financial–functional–administrative test (*Ajay Hasia*, *Pradeep Kumar Biswas*) and held that registration as a society and some self-earned revenue do not take a body out of Art. 12. It also drew limits: performing public functions alone does not make a body "State", and NBT staff do not become civil servants (no Arts. 309–311 protection).
**Memorise:** Government appoints the NBT Chairman and nominates governing-body members; CAG audits it; consequence = fundamental rights (Part III) are enforceable against it (writ jurisdiction, Art. 32/226). Landmarks: *Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal* (1967, "other authorities"), *Ajay Hasia* (1981), *Pradeep Kumar Biswas v. IICB* (2002, "pervasive control").
**Notebook:** GS2 Polity/Governance (Fundamental Rights, Judiciary).
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth — *Fundamental Rights* → Art. 12 "State"; Shankar IAS — Fundamental Rights. *Nudge:* add "NBT (2026)" as a worked example under the Pradeep Kumar Biswas test and cross-link to Art. 309–311.

### 3. Aditya-L1's VELC finds a solar eruption expanded asymmetrically (study reported 4–5 Nov) — Science & Technology
**Exam angle:** A Coronal Mass Ejection (CME) observed on 5 Aug 2024 by the Visible Emission Line Coronagraph (VELC) did not expand as a symmetric bubble; two Doppler-shifted emission branches on opposite sides of the line showed plasma moving towards and away from the observer. Combining VELC spectra with NASA SDO images and 3D modelling, the study shows VELC can constrain a CME's 3D geometry from a single vantage point. Why it matters: CME shape affects drag and hence Earth-arrival-time forecasts (space weather).
**Memorise:** Authors from the Indian Institute of Astrophysics, Bengaluru (Tripathi, Samanta, Joshi); published in *The Astrophysical Journal Letters*; VELC observations covered 1.05–1.5 solar radii. Aditya-L1: launched 2 Sep 2023 (PSLV-C57), placed in a halo orbit around Sun–Earth L1 (~1.5 million km) on 6 Jan 2024; seven payloads.
**Notebook:** GS3 Sci-Tech (Space).
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS/Mrunal — Space technology (India's missions); NCERT Class 11–12 Physics (Sun, magnetic fields; Physics optional: solar physics/plasma). *Nudge:* add a "space weather: CME, solar flare, geomagnetic storm" box next to Aditya-L1 payload list.

### 4. UN peacekeeping mission in Western Sahara (MINURSO) ends after 35 years (31 Oct–1 Nov) — International Relations
**Exam angle:** MINURSO (set up in 1991 to monitor the ceasefire and prepare a referendum) lapsed after Russia vetoed a US draft that would have extended it by a year, dropped "referendum" from its name and made Morocco's autonomy proposal the sole basis for talks — despite support from 11 of 15 Council members; a rival Russian draft was also rejected. UN assets began returning to troop contributors from 1 Nov (liquidation phase). Useful for UNSC functioning, the P5 veto and peacekeeping-mandate politics, and India's stance on self-determination vs territorial integrity.
**Memorise:** Morocco claims the territory since 1975; Polisario Front (Algeria-backed) seeks an independent Sahrawi republic; Algeria expressed "deep regret"; UN Secretary-General Guterres warned of escalation risk and urged maximum restraint.
**Notebook:** GS2 IR (UN/multilaterals; Africa).
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS/Mrunal — UN and its organs (UNSC voting, peacekeeping); India and UN peacekeeping. *Nudge:* add MINURSO (1991–2026) to the peacekeeping-missions list and "mandate lapse by veto" to the UNSC-reform argument.

### 5. SEBI (Settlement of Administrative and Civil Proceedings) Regulations, 2026 take effect (7 Nov) — Economy
**Exam angle:** New settlement framework (under Sec. 15JB of the SEBI Act, 1992) replaces the SEBI (Settlement Proceedings) Regulations, 2018. It is a regulatory-governance and capital-market reform: faster, formula-based settlement of enforcement cases, with an online portal and a settlement calculator.
**Memorise:** Notified 8 Oct 2026, in force 7 Nov 2026 (SEBI PR No. 70/2026); settlement amount = base amount × stage/regulatory-action/gravity/aggravating/mitigating factors; floor ₹3 lakh (first-time) and ₹7 lakh (others); application fee ₹25,000 (natural persons) and ₹35,000 (others); fast-track route for amounts up to ₹10 lakh; wilful defaulters, fraudulent borrowers and fugitive economic offenders cannot settle; pending 2018-regime matters can be refiled within 90 days (by 4 Feb 2027); decisions by the Panel of Whole Time Members after an Internal Committee and a High Powered Advisory Committee.

### B3. Mains question
**Q8.** *Examine* how the judicial tests for determining "State" under Article 12 have evolved, and discuss the significance of the Supreme Court's recent ruling on the National Book Trust. (15 marks, 250 words)
**Model-answer skeleton:**
1. Intro: Art. 12 defines "State" for Part III; "other authorities" has been expanded from *Rajasthan SEB v. Mohan Lal* (1967) to *Ajay Hasia* (1981, instrumentality/agency test) and *Pradeep Kumar Biswas* (2002, "pervasive control" — a cumulative, not rigid, test).
2. Tests: financial dependence, deep and pervasive government control, functional character (public functions), administrative control (appointments, audit, rules); contrast with bodies held not to be State (e.g., BCCI in *Zee Telefilms*, 2005) to show the fact-specific nature.
3. NBT ruling: government-resolution origin, Chairman appointed by government, CAG audit — registration as a society and self-earned revenue do not defeat the test; limits: public function alone insufficient; no civil-servant status (Arts. 309–311).
4. Significance and way forward: fundamental rights (Arts. 14, 16, 21) enforceable against quasi-government bodies, curbing arbitrariness; clearer statutory criteria would reduce litigation; conclude with accountability of the "outsourced" State.

## Answers
- **Q1 — (a).** NBT held to be "State" under Art. 12 on the *Ajay Hasia*/*Pradeep Kumar Biswas* cumulative test; the Court expressly said its staff do not become civil servants or gain Arts. 309–311 protection, so 3 is wrong.
- **Q2 — (b).** *Rajatarangini* (Kalhana, 12th century) and the Mysuru *Arthashastra* manuscript are correct; regional inscription does not automatically confer International Register status, so 3 is wrong.
- **Q3 — (c).** VELC is a coronagraph on Aditya-L1 and the 5 Aug 2024 CME showed asymmetric expansion; VELC studies the corona, whereas near-UV photosphere imaging is done by SUIT, so 3 is wrong.
- **Q4 — (d).** MINURSO (1991) lapsed after Russia's veto of the US draft, which would have made Morocco's autonomy plan the basis for talks.
- **Q5 — (a).** The 2026 Regulations replace the 2018 Regulations and bar wilful defaulters, fraudulent borrowers and fugitive economic offenders; the fee is ₹25,000 for natural persons but ₹35,000 for others, so 3 is wrong.
- **Q6 — (b).** Art. 12 applies "in this Part" (Part III), names no courts expressly, and includes "other authorities", which courts have extended to statutory and government-created bodies.
- **Q7 — (c).** Substantive matters need 9 of 15 affirmative votes with no negative vote by a P5; abstention is not a veto, and procedural matters need only nine votes.
