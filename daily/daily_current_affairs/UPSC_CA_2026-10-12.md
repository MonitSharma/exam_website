## Part A — Briefing

### 1. SC refuses to revive Andhra Pradesh's 34% BC quota in local bodies — the 50% ceiling holds — Polity & Governance
**Exam angle:** On 9 Oct 2026 a bench of Justices Dipankar Datta and Sheel Nagu dismissed the State's appeal (*State of A.P. v. Thandava Yogesh*) against the High Court's order quashing two GOs that gave 34% reservation to Backward Classes in rural and urban local bodies. The HC held the quota pushed total reservation past 50% and that the State had no empirical data to justify crossing the ceiling; the Bench called its approach "very reasonable" and questioned the legal basis of the "Unified Family Survey" used as data. Fits the Maratha / Jat / local-body quota line of cases.
**Memorize:** 50% ceiling = *Indra Sawhney* (1992), "a rule, not absolute", exceptions only in extraordinary situations with data. Local-body OBC quota needs the **"triple test"** (*K. Krishna Murthy* 2010, applied in *Vikas Kishanrao Gawali* 2021): dedicated commission → local-body-wise proportion → total (SC+ST+OBC) within 50%. Articles 243D(6) and 243T(6) enable backward-class reservation in Panchayats/Municipalities.
**Notebook:** GS2 Polity/Governance.
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth — *Panchayati Raj* (Art 243D) and *Municipalities* (Art 243T) chapters + *Fundamental Rights* (Art 15, 16). **Nudge:** add a "triple test" box and cross-link to the Maratha quota (2021) and Bihar 65% quota cases.

### 2. RBI creates a Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve (FERR) and slashes unhedged derivative limits — Economy
**Exam angle:** On 10 Oct 2026 the RBI tightened rupee FX-derivative rules to curb speculation and keep the market orderly. Authorised Dealers must now hold **cash with the RBI equal to 20% of the rupee notional** on qualifying rupee FX derivative contracts above **US$2 million** that hedge current-account exposures where users buy foreign currency against the rupee. Daily reporting via CIMS; splitting trades to dodge it is a violation. Separate circular cuts the limit on FX derivative positions with **no underlying exposure from US$100 million to US$5 million**, including on exchanges.
**Memorize:** 20% | >$2 mn | $100 mn → $5 mn | held daily till contract ends | an administrative (not rate) tool — contrast with the 7 Oct repo hike to 5.50%. *Outcome sources agree on figures; fuller effective-date wording to be checked against RBI circulars 25 & 26.*
**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (Monetary/External sector).
**Static anchor:** Mrunal Economy — *Forex market, forwards/futures/options, FEMA*; Ramesh Singh — *External Sector*. **Nudge:** add "FERR (2026)" under macro-prudential tools next to CRR/SLR.

### B3. Mains question (GS2, 150 words)
**"Critically examine whether the 50% ceiling on reservations is a binding constitutional limit or a judicial rule of prudence, with reference to recent rulings on local-body quotas."**
Model-answer skeleton:
1. **Intro:** Ceiling originates in *Balaji* (1962), settled in *Indra Sawhney* (1992) as a "rule" with exceptions in extraordinary situations; not in the constitutional text.
2. **Case for flexibility:** Demographic variation, States such as Tamil Nadu (69%, Ninth Schedule), EWS 10% upheld in *Janhit Abhiyan* (2022) without breaching the ceiling debate fully; social realities differ by region.
3. **Case for the ceiling:** Preserves equality of opportunity (Art 14, 16(1)); recent AP order shows insistence on **empirical data + triple test** for local bodies — quotas cannot rest on generic surveys.
4. **Way forward:** Periodic, quantifiable backwardness data (caste census debate), dedicated commissions, transparent evidence; Parliament/judiciary to settle the exceptions standard.
