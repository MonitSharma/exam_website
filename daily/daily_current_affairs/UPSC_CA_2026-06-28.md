### 1. Draft National Food Security (Amendment) Bill, 2026 — AAY entitlement shifts from "per-household" to "per-person"
**Subject:** Polity & Governance (Food Security) · **Notebook:** GS2 Governance / GS3 Economy

**Headline:** The Department of Food and Public Distribution (Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & PD) has put out a **draft National Food Security (Amendment) Bill, 2026** for public consultation (comments open till **13 July 2026**), proposing to change how the poorest beneficiaries — **Antyodaya Anna Yojana (AAY)** households — receive grain.

**Exam angle:** Tests the *design logic* of welfare targeting — the move from a flat **household** entitlement to a **per-capita** one, and the equity question it raises (small AAY households could get *less* than today). High-yield because the NFSA framework itself is static-syllabus core.

**Key facts to memorize:**
- Proposal: each AAY member gets **7 kg/month**, subject to an overall ceiling of **35 kg per household** → 1-member = 7 kg, 2 = 14, 3 = 21, 4 = 28, **5+ = 35 kg** (the current flat figure).
- Today under **NFSA, 2013**: AAY = flat **35 kg/household/month**; **Priority Households (PHH)** = **5 kg/person/month** (no household cap).
- NFSA covers up to **75% of rural** and **50% of urban** population (≈ two-thirds of India). Grain is currently **free** under PMGKAY (extended 5 years from Jan 2024).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal Economy → Food Security/PDS pillar* and *GS3 Economy → NFSA chapter*. **Nudge:** add a one-line cross-link "per-capita vs per-household entitlement (2026 draft)" beside your NFSA notes, linking to TPDS, AAY, PHH and One Nation One Ration Card.

### 2. World's first nuclear-heat hydrogen plant (Cu–Cl cycle) commissioned at IGCAR, Kalpakkam
**Subject:** Science & Technology · **Notebook:** GS3 Sci-Tech / Energy

**Headline:** On **26 June 2026**, the **Department of Atomic Energy (DAE)** inaugurated the **world's first hydrogen-production facility using the Copper–Chlorine (Cu–Cl) thermochemical cycle powered by nuclear process heat** from the **Fast Breeder Test Reactor (FBTR)** at the **Indira Gandhi Centre for Atomic Research (IGCAR), Kalpakkam (Tamil Nadu)**.

**Exam angle:** Connects the **three-stage nuclear programme** (Stage-II FBRs) to the **clean-hydrogen** push — a perfect "linkage" question. The Cu–Cl process is indigenous (developed by **BARC, Mumbai**), so it also fits *Aatmanirbhar* science.

**Key facts to memorize:**
- **Cu–Cl thermochemical cycle** splits water using heat (not just electricity) → lower operating temperature and higher thermodynamic efficiency than electrolysis; **no CO₂** emissions.
- Built jointly by **BARC + IGCAR**; inaugurated by **Ajit Kumar Mohanty** (Secretary, DAE & Chairman, Atomic Energy Commission). It is a **technology demonstrator** for future large-scale, carbon-free hydrogen tied to advanced reactors.

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS / Sci-tech notes → India's three-stage nuclear programme (Homi Bhabha) + Kalpakkam (FBTR/PFBR)*. **Nudge:** cross-link to the **National Green Hydrogen Mission** and note "pink/clean hydrogen = nuclear-powered" vs green (renewables) vs grey (fossil).

### Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Science & Technology / Energy)

**Q.** *"The commissioning of a nuclear-heat-driven hydrogen plant marks a convergence of India's three-stage nuclear programme with its decarbonisation goals."* **Examine** the significance and the challenges of leveraging nuclear energy for clean hydrogen production in India. *(15 marks, 250 words)*

**Model-answer skeleton:**
- **Context (1 line):** IGCAR's Cu–Cl facility (2026) — first to make hydrogen from nuclear process heat; ties Stage-II FBR ecosystem to the National Green Hydrogen Mission.
- **Significance:** carbon-free, round-the-clock baseload heat (unlike intermittent renewables); higher thermodynamic efficiency than electrolysis; energy security via indigenous Cu–Cl (BARC) and thorium roadmap; supports hard-to-abate sectors (steel, fertiliser, refining).
- **Challenges:** high capital cost & long gestation; safety/regulatory and public-acceptance concerns; materials handling (corrosive chlorine chemistry); scale-up from demonstrator to commercial; water and siting constraints.
- **Way forward:** phased scale-up linked to PFBR/advanced reactors; certification/standards for "pink" hydrogen; integrate with the Green Hydrogen Mission's offtake and export targets.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (b) Only two.** Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Statement 2 is wrong: a 4-member AAY household would get **28 kg** (4 × 7 kg), not 35 kg; only households of **5+ members** hit the 35 kg ceiling.

**Q2 — (b).** It uses the **Copper–Chlorine (Cu–Cl) thermochemical cycle** driven by heat from the **FBTR** at IGCAR — the world's first such nuclear-heat hydrogen facility; not electrolysis or fossil-based.

**Q3 — (b).** The **RSSC** is a BRICS Earth-observation data-sharing constellation, used for disaster management, climate monitoring and socio-economic applications.

**Q4 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** IBCA is HQ'd in **New Delhi** (1✓) and Bangladesh joined as the **27th member** in 2026 (3✓). Statement 2 is wrong — of the **seven** big cats, the **jaguar and puma are not found in India** (only five are).

**Q5 — (b).** The report flagged ~**65% import dependence on China** for **APIs and Key Starting Materials**, and proposed pharma chapters in FTAs.

**Q6 — (c) All three.** Correct description of Stage-I (PHWR), Stage-II (FBR breeding Pu-239) and Stage-III (Th-232 → U-233) of the three-stage programme.

**Q7 — (c) Jaguar.** The jaguar (Americas) is not found in India; India hosts tiger, lion (Gir), leopard, snow leopard and cheetah (reintroduced).
