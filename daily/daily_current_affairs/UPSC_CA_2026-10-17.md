## Part A — Briefing

### 1. SC: children have a fundamental right to internet access; safety and freedom "are not at odds" — Polity & Governance
**Exam angle:** A bench of Justices J.B. Pardiwala and K.V. Chandran, hearing reports of paid promotion of child sexual abuse material (CSEAM) through redirect links, reportedly rejected age-based blanket restrictions on children's internet use. This extends the line of cases on the internet and Articles 19(1)(a) and 21, and it is a good Mains example of balancing rights against protection.
**Key facts:**
- The Centre must frame guidelines for national databases of perceptual hashes and URLs.
- Safeguards are required against wrongful deletion, with a grievance redressal mechanism for wrong matches.
- The databases must not be repurposed to remove other kinds of content.
- Platforms must speed up detection and removal, and close the gap where content removed on one platform is not flagged on others.
- Quote: "Our children are entitled to both" (safety and freedom).
- Reports cite Articles 21 and 19(1)(a); the case name was not given in the sources I could open.

**Notebook:** GS2 Polity/Governance
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth, Fundamental Rights (Art. 19 and 21). Add a line for *Anuradha Bhasin* (2020), *Puttaswamy* (2017) and this order, and cross-link to the POCSO Act and IT Act s.67B.

### 2. India–New Zealand FTA enters into force on 20 October — International Relations
**Exam angle:** India's latest FTA, signed on 27 April 2026, is a mobility and market-access deal with a developed economy. India kept its sensitive dairy sector out.
**Key facts:**
- Zero duty on Indian exports to New Zealand.
- New Zealand will give 5,000 Temporary Employment Entry visas and 1,000 Working Holiday visas a year, with post-study work rights.
- Commitments cover about 118 services sectors.
- New Zealand has committed to facilitate US$20 billion of investment in India.
- Dairy, sugar, edible oils and most meat are excluded.
- Apples, kiwifruit and Manuka honey get limited access under tariff rate quotas.
- Bilateral trade is about US$1.1 billion; the aim is to double trade by 2030 (the target figure is quoted differently across sources, so verify it before memorising).

**Notebook:** GS2 International Relations
**Static anchor:** Mrunal, India's Trade Agreements and Shankar IAS, International Economic Organisations. Update the FTA table (India–UK, EFTA TEPA, NZ) and add a cross-link to the "TRQ" concept in the Economy notes.

### 3. Gaganyaan: Crew Module–Service Module separation system validated — Science & Technology
**Exam angle:** ISRO completed ground tests of crew and service module separation at VSSC, Thiruvananthapuram. Pyro charges sever the umbilicals and the modules are pushed apart. The uncrewed G1 flight with the humanoid Vyommitra has no announced date; ISRO has said Q4 2026, and the first crewed flight is targeted for 2027.
**Key facts:**
- The Service Module supplies power and fluids and is jettisoned before re-entry.
- Systems checked after the test: avionics, inertial navigation, thermal protection, the umbilical shield, the uprighting system and thermal-humidity control.
- Earlier tests: uprighting and umbilical mechanisms, plus the April parachute drop test of the G1 crew module.

### 4. WPI inflation jumps to 11.39% in September, a 49-month high — Economy
**Exam angle:** Wholesale inflation is now far above retail inflation (CPI 5.54% in the 13 Oct note). The gap shows cost-push pressure from fuel and metals that has yet to pass through to consumers. It matters for RBI's recent repo hike and for input costs.
**Key facts:**
- Headline WPI was 11.39%, against 9.92% in August.
- Fuel and power: 25.85%. Manufactured products: 9.76%. Primary articles: 8.67%.
- WPI Food Index: 8.66%.
- Basic metals: 16.96%. Chemicals: 16.74%.
- April–September cumulative: 9.85%.
- The release uses the new 2022-23 base, per the report I read.

**Notebook:** GS3 Economy
**Static anchor:** Ramesh Singh, Inflation chapter, or Mrunal's Inflation pillar. Add a WPI vs CPI vs PPI comparison table (coverage, base year, services) and link it to the monetary policy notes.

### 5. J&K and Ladakh HC bans fuel motorboats and jet skis on Dal Lake — Environment
**Exam angle:** A Division Bench (Chief Justice Pushpendra Singh Bhati and Justice Rajesh Sekhri) barred fuel-powered motorboats and jet skis, including motorised shikaras, "until further orders". It is a case study in judicial environmentalism and in tourism versus conservation trade-offs.
**Key facts:**
- Exempt: administration boats for emergency, law and order, and disaster management.
- Enforcement lies with the Lakes Conservation and Management Authority (LCMA), the Tourism Department and the Police. A violation is treated as contempt.
- The government must file a comprehensive motorised-boat policy and explore solar or electric engines.
- Next hearing: 30 October.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS, Environment and Wetlands chapters. Add Dal Lake to the Himalayan lakes and Ramsar notes as a pollution and eutrophication example. Cross-link to the Wetlands Rules 2017.

### B3. Mains question

**Q8.** *Critically examine* how the State can reconcile children's right to access the internet with the need to protect them from online abuse, in the light of the Supreme Court's October 2026 observations. (15 marks, 250 words)

**Model-answer skeleton:**
1. **Rights framing:** Internet access is linked to Articles 19(1)(a), 19(1)(g) and 21 (*Anuradha Bhasin*, *Puttaswamy*). Children are rights-holders, and a blanket age ban is disproportionate. Add Article 21A (education) and Article 39(f) (DPSP on children).
2. **Protection side:** CSEAM, grooming and paid redirect links are real harms. The statutory tools are POCSO, IT Act s.67B and the IT Rules 2021. The Court's hash and URL database is a platform-agnostic detection tool.
3. **Safeguards:** Proportionality means due process before takedown, a grievance mechanism, and no function creep. The risks are over-blocking, privacy loss and mass surveillance.
4. **Way forward:** Age-appropriate design, parental controls, digital literacy, platform accountability with audits, and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act's rules on children's data.
