### 1. India–Kyrgyzstan open the "Manas and Mahabharata" Centre for Civilizational Studies, Bishkek — International Relations

**Headline:** An Indian delegation (visit 4–7 July 2026, reported 9 July) helped inaugurate the **International Centre for Civilizational Studies "Manas and Mahabharata"** in **Bishkek, Kyrgyzstan**, and unveiled the **first-ever Hindi translation of the Kyrgyz national epic *Manas***.

**Exam angle:** A clean "civilizational/soft-power diplomacy" case study for GS2 IR and GS1 Art & Culture. Shows India using epic and cultural heritage as a bridge to **Central Asia**, a region India engages through the **SCO**, the **India–Central Asia Summit** and the **"Connect Central Asia" policy**. Good Mains line on culture as an instrument of foreign policy.

**Key facts to memorize:**
- Centre set up by the **Manas National Academy** (Kyrgyzstan) with the **Centre for Studies of International Relations (CSIR), New Delhi**.
- Trilateral cooperation agreements signed with **7 leading Kyrgyz universities** (KNU, BSU, AUCA, Ala-Too, etc.).
- ***Manas*** = Kyrgyz national epic (one of the world's longest); **Mahabharata** = India's — hence the twin naming.
- Kyrgyzstan capital: **Bishkek**; both India and Kyrgyzstan are **SCO** members.

**Destination notebook:** GS2 (IR — India–Central Asia); secondary GS1 (Art & Culture).

**Static anchor:** In your IR notes under **"India–Central Asia / SCO,"** add a cross-link: *"Cultural/civilizational diplomacy — Manas–Mahabharata Centre (Bishkek, 2026); pair with Connect Central Asia policy + India–Central Asia Summit."* Also tag your Art & Culture "epics" page.

### 2. Astra Mk-1 becomes India's first BVR air-to-air missile export — Indonesia is the first foreign buyer — Science & Technology

**Headline:** India and Indonesia signed an agreement on **7 July 2026** (during PM Modi's Indonesia visit) for supply of the **Astra Mk-1 Beyond-Visual-Range Air-to-Air Missile (BVRAAM)** — making Indonesia the **first foreign buyer** of India's first fully indigenous BVRAAM, part of a broader **~US$630 million** defence package that also covers **BrahMos** cooperation.

**Exam angle:** Anchors the "India as a defence *exporter*" story (record **₹38,424 crore** defence exports in FY2025-26) onto a concrete, indigenous, combat-proven platform — a favourite Sci-Tech/Internal-Security and Mains theme (Atmanirbhar Bharat in defence). Distinct from the earlier "Modi Indonesia leg" item; the *new* substance here is the missile export itself.

**Key facts to memorize:**
- **Astra Mk-1** — developed by **DRDO**, manufactured by **Bharat Dynamics Limited (BDL)**; indigenous **RF seeker**; speed **~Mach 4.5**; range **~80–110 km** (extendable up to ~160 km); integrated on the **Su-30MKI** (and the Navy's MiG-29K).
- India's **first indigenous BVRAAM**; interest boosted by its use in **Operation Sindoor**.
- Enabling policy stack: **Positive Indigenisation Lists**, **iDEX**, **DAP-2020**, **Defence Production & Export Promotion Policy 2020**, FDI up to **74% automatic / 100% government route**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 (Sci-Tech — defence technology) / GS3 (Internal Security — indigenisation & exports).

**Static anchor:** In your Sci-Tech "**Missiles of India**" table (Agni/Prithvi/BrahMos/Akash/Astra/Nag), flag *Astra = air-to-air, DRDO+BDL*; add a one-liner under Defence Exports: *"First BVRAAM export → Indonesia, 2026."*

### 3. IMF's July 2026 WEO Update trims India's FY27 growth to 6.4%, keeps it the fastest-growing major economy — Economy

**Headline:** In its **July 2026 World Economic Outlook (WEO) Update**, the **IMF** projected India's GDP growth at **6.4% for FY2026-27** — **10 bps below** its April 2026 estimate of 6.5% — citing **higher energy prices and global trade uncertainty**, while **raising FY2027-28 to 6.7%**. India remains the **fastest-growing major economy**.

**Exam angle:** Perfect "data-point" MCQ material and a Mains hook on external-sector risks. Lets you revise the IMF's flagship publications and the Bretton Woods architecture. Contrast with domestic data already noted this fortnight (IIP +5.1% in May).

**Key facts to memorize:**
- **FY27: 6.4%** (down from 6.5%); **FY28: 6.7%** (up from 6.5%).
- Drivers of the cut: **higher energy prices, weaker external demand, trade uncertainty**; support from strong private consumption and services.
- IMF flagship reports: **WEO**, **Global Financial Stability Report (GFSR)**, **Fiscal Monitor**; India's quota-based voice and **Article IV** consultations.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 (Economy — growth & international institutions).

**Static anchor:** In your Mrunal "**BoP / International Economic Institutions**" pillar, refresh the IMF card (HQ Washington DC; SDR basket USD-EUR-CNY-JPY-GBP; WEO/GFSR/Fiscal Monitor) and log *"WEO Jul-2026: India FY27 6.4%."*

### 4. 'PRAGATI' launched to build 20,000 agri-entrepreneurs for 20 lakh smallholder farmers — Agriculture

**Headline:** Union Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare Minister **Shivraj Singh Chouhan** launched **PRAGATI** (7–8 July 2026), a **multi-stakeholder initiative** to create a nationwide ecosystem of **20,000 agri-entrepreneurs** (adding to an existing 26,000+) and directly support **~20 lakh smallholder farmers** through **regenerative agriculture**, advisory, mechanisation, finance, insurance and market linkages.

**Exam angle:** Strong GS3 Agriculture item on **agri-extension, agri-entrepreneurship and farmer-income** models — and a rare government + corporate + global-foundation coalition. **Note the name clash:** this PRAGATI is *not* the PM's 2015 **PRAGATI (Pro-Active Governance And Timely Implementation)** e-governance platform — a classic prelims trap.

**Key facts to memorize:**
- Targets: **~30% rise in farmer incomes**, **15–20% higher yields**, **regenerative agriculture** adoption by ~20% of participating farmers, financial inclusion for ~50%.
- Delivery via **20,000 entrepreneur-led kiosks**.
- Backers include **PepsiCo Foundation, SBI Foundation, Gates Foundation, IDH, Heifer International, EDF, TRIF, AEGF**.
- Focus states: **MP, Bihar, UP, Assam, West Bengal, Jharkhand, Maharashtra, Rajasthan**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 (Agriculture — extension, FPOs, farmer income).

**Static anchor:** In your Shankar IAS / GS3 "**Agricultural Extension & Farmer Income**" topic, add PRAGATI beside FPOs and DAY-NRLM; write the nudge: *"PRAGATI (agri-entrepreneurs) ≠ PRAGATI (PM's governance platform) — don't confuse."*

### 5. West Bengal becomes the 36th State/UT under Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY — nationwide rollout completed — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** Following an MoU between the **National Health Authority (NHA)** and the state, **West Bengal** became the **36th State/UT** to implement **Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY**, completing the scheme's **100% nationwide coverage** and bringing an estimated **~1.24 crore eligible families** into the fold.

**Exam angle:** Textbook **cooperative federalism + welfare-delivery** item: since **health is a State subject**, states join PM-JAY *voluntarily via MoU* — WB's entry closes the last gap. Good for a GS2 governance/federalism MCQ and a "universal health coverage" Mains angle.

**Key facts to memorize:**
- **PM-JAY** = ₹**5 lakh per family per year** cover for **secondary & tertiary** hospitalisation; world's largest government-funded health assurance scheme.
- Two pillars of **Ayushman Bharat (2018)**: **Health & Wellness Centres / Ayushman Arogya Mandirs** + **PM-JAY**; implemented by the **NHA**.
- **36 States/UTs** now on board (WB the last major hold-out to join).

**Destination notebook:** GS2 (Governance — health schemes / federalism).

**Static anchor:** In Laxmikanth's federalism notes and your Ayushman Bharat scheme card, add: *"WB = 36th/last State-UT (2026) → 100% national PM-JAY coverage; opt-in reflects health = State subject."*

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Sci-Tech / Internal Security, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *"India's shift from being the world's largest arms importer to an emerging defence exporter marks a structural change in its security-industrial strategy."* **Examine** the factors driving this transition and the challenges that remain.

**Model-answer skeleton:**
- **Frame the shift:** Record defence exports (~₹38,424 crore, FY2025-26); the **Astra Mk-1** becoming India's **first BVRAAM export** (to Indonesia, 2026) as a symbolic inflection point.
- **Drivers:** Positive Indigenisation Lists and import embargoes; **iDEX**, **DAP-2020** and the **Defence Production & Export Promotion Policy 2020**; corporatisation of the OFB into 7 DPSUs; **FDI up to 74% (automatic)/100% (govt route)**; defence corridors (UP & TN); combat-proven credibility (Operation Sindoor) building buyer confidence.
- **Challenges that remain:** Continued import dependence for **critical components/aero-engines**; the R&D-to-mass-production gap; limited private-sector scale and MSME integration; export financing, quality assurance and after-sales support; geopolitical sensitivities of end-users.
- **Way forward:** Deepen indigenous **design** capability, sustain iDEX-to-procurement pipelines, build reliable supply-chain partnerships, and pursue the government's higher export target — converting a policy push into a durable, technology-led export base.
