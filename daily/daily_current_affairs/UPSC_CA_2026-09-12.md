### 1. 2,800 registered parties, 750 contesting — and an Election Commission that cannot de-register any of them — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** An analysis of Election Commission and ADR filings has found that **six Gujarat-based Registered Unrecognised Political Parties (RUPPs) received about ₹1,700 crore in donations in FY 2023-24** while fielding just **15 candidates** in the 2024 Lok Sabha election — more than the **₹1,480 crore** received by the five national parties that between them fielded **893 candidates**. One RUPP alone took in roughly **₹620 crore**.

**Exam angle:** Do not read this as a corruption story; read it as a **statutory-power story**, which is what UPSC actually tests. The ECI has an explicit power to *register* a party under **Section 29A of the Representation of the People Act, 1951** — and no corresponding power to *de-register* one. That asymmetry was settled by the Supreme Court in ***Indian National Congress (I) v. Institute of Social Welfare & Ors* (2002)**, which held that the ECI's registration function is quasi-judicial but that cancellation of registration is not within its powers, save on three narrow grounds. So the ECI is reduced to **administrative "delisting"** — striking a party off the active list — which does *not* dissolve the legal entity and does *not* automatically end its **Section 13A Income Tax Act** exemption. That is the precise gap. The second examinable thread is the **fiscal loophole**: 100% tax exemption on contributions for the party under Section 13A, plus a **Section 80GGC** deduction for the donor, with no vote-share qualification attached to either — an open invitation to round-tripping. Third thread: this sits squarely in the post-**Electoral Bonds judgment (*ADR v. Union of India*, February 2024)** conversation about political-finance transparency, so you can bolt it onto notes you already have.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **Section 29A, RP Act 1951** — registration of political parties with the ECI. **Section 29C** — annual contribution reports for donations **above ₹20,000**. Donations **above ₹2,000** must come through banking channels.
- **Election Symbols (Reservation and Allotment) Order, 1968** — the instrument under which a party qualifies as a **Recognised National** or **Recognised State** party; failing the vote/seat threshold leaves it an **RUPP**.
- **Benefits an RUPP still enjoys:** 100% income-tax exemption on voluntary contributions (**S.13A, IT Act**); a **common election symbol** across constituencies; **20 star campaigners** whose travel expenditure is not charged to candidates' expense limits.
- **Scale:** **2,800+ RUPPs** by mid-2026; only about **750** contested the 2024 Lok Sabha election — over **70% electorally inactive**.
- **Compliance:** ADR finds only about **26–27%** of RUPPs file or publish annual audit reports on time. The ECI has **delisted 800+** non-compliant entities.
- **The three grounds on which de-registration *is* permitted** (per the 2002 judgment): registration obtained by **fraud or forgery**; the party **ceases to profess allegiance to the Constitution**; the Union Government **declares it unlawful** under the UAPA.
- **Reform proposals on the table:** **Law Commission's 255th Report on Electoral Reforms (2015)** and the **ECI's 2016 proposals** — statutory power to de-register a party that has not contested for **10 consecutive years** or repeatedly defaults on financial disclosure.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Representation of the People Act; Election Commission; salient features of electoral reform).

**Static anchor:** **Laxmikanth — "Election Commission" chapter and the "Political Parties" chapter.** *Update:* add a three-line box titled **"Register ≠ De-register"** — S.29A gives registration power; INC v. Institute of Social Welfare (2002) denies cancellation power; delisting is an administrative workaround that leaves the legal entity and its tax exemption intact. **Cross-link** to your **Electoral Bonds (ADR v. UoI, 2024)** note and to the **Law Commission 255th Report** entry, so that "political finance" reads as one continuous argument rather than three unconnected news items.

### 2. DILRMP 3.0 gets its operational guidelines — ₹565.50 crore, and land records become a "Land Stack" — Economy

**Headline:** The Union Minister for Rural Development released the **Operational Guidelines for the Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme (DILRMP) 3.0** at Krishi Bhawan, New Delhi — a **Central Sector Scheme with an outlay of ₹565.50 crore for 2026–2031**, shifting land administration from digitising legacy records to an **integrated, GIS-enabled Land Stack**.

**Exam angle:** The high-value framing is **Digital Public Infrastructure applied to land**, and the reason it matters economically is credit. India's land-titling system is **presumptive, not conclusive** — a registered sale deed evidences a transaction, not indefeasible title — which is why land disputes clog the courts and why banks discount land as collateral. DILRMP 3.0's three moving parts each attack one leg of that problem: **ULPIN/Bhu-Aadhaar** fixes *identity* of the parcel, **georeferenced cadastral maps** fix its *location*, and **RCCMS integration** makes pending litigation visible *at the point of transaction*. Pair this with **SVAMITVA** (rural inhabited-area drone survey, property cards) and **NAKSHA** (urban cadastral mapping) and you have a complete "land as DPI" answer. The standard critique to include: land is a **State subject (Entry 18, State List)** and land revenue is **Entry 45, State List**, so a 100%-centrally-funded scheme can incentivise but cannot mandate — which is exactly why the guidelines use **performance-linked releases**. The deeper unfinished agenda is conclusive titling on the **Torrens** model, which DILRMP still does not deliver.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **DILRMP 3.0: Central Sector Scheme, ₹565.50 crore, 2026–2031, 100% central funding**, phased and **performance-linked** releases routed through **PFMS** and monitored on the DILRMP-MIS portal.
- **Nodal body: Department of Land Resources (DoLR), Ministry of Rural Development.**
- **Lineage:** launched as the **National Land Records Modernisation Programme (NLRMP) in 2008**; renamed DILRMP and converted into a **Central Sector Scheme in April 2016**.
- **ULPIN / "Bhu-Aadhaar":** a **14-digit Unique Land Parcel Identification Number** for every geo-referenced land parcel.
- **Registration Seva Kendras:** **75 high-footfall Sub-Registrar Offices** to be upgraded, explicitly modelled on **Passport Seva Kendras**; end-to-end online registration backed by a central Registration Repository.
- **RCCMS** — Revenue Court Case Management System — to be linked to land records so that pending litigation and court orders surface automatically against a parcel.
- **Land Stack:** each State builds its own stack by integrating land datasets through **APIs**; **NAKSHA** (urban property mapping pilot) to be scaled.
- **Constitutional placement:** **Land — Entry 18, State List**; **Land revenue — Entry 45, State List**. Registration of deeds and documents is **Entry 6, Concurrent List**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (land reforms, infrastructure, ease of doing business) with a GS2 e-governance spillover.

**Static anchor:** **Mrunal Pillar #4 (Agriculture & Land Reforms)** — the land-reforms sub-topic. *Update:* build a single ladder entry — **NLRMP 2008 → DILRMP (CS Scheme) 2016 → SVAMITVA 2020 → NAKSHA 2025 → DILRMP 3.0 (2026-31)** — and add one line on why **presumptive vs conclusive title** is the thing none of these has yet fixed. **Cross-link** to the **2nd ARC's 11th Report, "Promoting e-Governance"**, and to your **Economic Survey** note on land as a factor-market constraint.

### 3. India's research paradox: 3rd in papers, 19th by h-index, 3% university patent grant rate — Reports & Indices

**Headline:** A compilation of Scopus, Patent Office and ranking data shows India published **1.6 million Scopus-indexed papers between 2020 and 2025** — ahead of China, the US and the UK on annual volume — while sitting **19th globally on aggregate h-index (925, against the United States' 3,213)**, with university patent **grant rates stuck at about 3%** and the **commercial working rate of granted patents collapsing from 12.1% in 2022-23 to 1.6% in 2024-25**.

**Exam angle:** This is the most quotable data set of the day and it slots into three different answers. (i) **GS3 Sci-Tech / indigenisation:** the invention-to-innovation gap, and why gross R&D expenditure numbers understate the problem — the bottleneck is *translation*, not output. (ii) **GS2 Governance:** a clean case of **metric capture** — the **NIRF** assigns **30% weight to Research and Professional Practice** measured largely by *counts* of papers and patent *filings*, so institutions rationally optimise for filings they never intend to prosecute or work. (iii) **GS4 Ethics:** research integrity, predatory journals and paper mills, with India now **3rd in the world for retractions** and a retraction rate that **more than doubled from 1.5 to 3.5 per 1,000 papers between 2012 and 2022**. The clean Mains line: *India has built a paper fortress — impressive from the outside, empty within.* The institutional fix to name is the **Anusandhan National Research Foundation (ANRF)**, established under the **ANRF Act, 2023**, which subsumed SERB and is meant to crowd in private R&D funding.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **Volume:** **1.6 million Scopus-indexed papers, 2020–2025**; India **3rd globally** by annual volume. Private-university publications grew **over 760%**.
- **Impact:** **19th by aggregate h-index — 925**, against the **US at 3,213**.
- **University patent filings:** **11,150 (2020-21) → 45,776 (2024-25)** — roughly a fourfold jump. **Grants fell 67% in 2024-25.**
- **Grant rate:** about **3% for universities**, against a **national average of about 24%**.
- **Commercial working rate of granted patents: 12.1% (2022-23) → 1.6% (2024-25).**
- **Integrity:** India **3rd globally in retractions**; rate **1.5 → 3.5 per 1,000 papers (2012 → 2022)**; about **3,244 retractions over two decades**.
- **Rankings divergence, 2026:** **QS World University Rankings 2026** — **54 Indian institutions** featured, the 4th-most-represented country. **THE World University Rankings 2026** — only **4** Indian universities in the global top 500, **IISc Bengaluru** highest in the **201–250** band.
- **Incentive structure:** **NIRF gives 30% weight to Research and Professional Practice (RPP)**. **ANRF** (ANRF Act, 2023) is the apex funding body; the **IIT model** (chairs, lab grants, equity in incubator spin-offs) is contrasted with the **IIM model** (direct per-paper cash bounties).
- **Institutional gap:** India has **no independent statutory research-integrity authority**; the **UGC** has no investigatory powers over academic fraud.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (indigenisation, IPR, achievements of Indians in S&T); GS2 — Education/Governance; GS4 — Ethics (professional integrity).

**Static anchor:** **Mrunal Pillar #6 (Human Development — Education)** plus your **IPR / National IPR Policy 2016** note. *Update:* add a two-column card — *Volume metrics* (papers, filings, QS presence) vs *Impact metrics* (h-index, grant rate, working rate, retractions) — because every question on this theme is really asking you to distinguish the two. **Cross-link** to **ANRF (2023)**, the **NEP 2020** research-university provisions, and the **RDI Scheme** entry.

### 4. India gets its first independent photonics industry body — Science & Technology

**Headline:** The **India Photonics Association (IPA)**, the country's **first independent, ecosystem-led industry body for photonics**, was launched on **9 September 2026** with **65 founding members** — including **SFO Technologies, Tejas Networks, GX Quantum Photonics and Lightspeed Photonics** — inaugurated in the presence of the **Chief Technology Officer of the India Semiconductor Mission**.

**Exam angle:** Learn the concept first, the body second. **Photonics** is the generation, detection and manipulation of **photons** — light — as the information carrier, the way electronics uses electrons. It matters right now for one blunt reason: as AI data-centres scale, **electrical interconnects hit a power and bandwidth wall**, and **silicon photonics** — optical links fabricated on a silicon chip — is the way around it. That makes photonics the natural **third leg** of India's deep-tech triad alongside the **India Semiconductor Mission** (Semicon 2.0) and the **National Quantum Mission** — and note the overlap: photonic qubits are one of the four platforms the NQM funds, and quantum key distribution is pure photonics. Exam-relevant applications to list: **optical fibre and BharatNet backhaul, LiDAR for autonomous systems and DRDO's laser directed-energy work, photonic integrated circuits, medical imaging and optical biosensors, and QKD for secure communication**. The governance angle worth one line in a Mains answer: this is an **industry-led standards and policy-input body**, not a government mission — the same self-organising model that CII/NASSCOM played for IT services, applied to hardware.

**Facts to memorise:**

- **India Photonics Association (IPA)** — launched **9 September 2026**; **first independent, ecosystem-led** photonics industry body in India; **65 founding members**.
- Positioned as a **neutral platform** giving technically grounded inputs to policymaking; **not** a government body and **not** a statutory regulator.
- Founding members to be able to name: **SFO Technologies, Tejas Networks, GX Quantum Photonics, Lightspeed Photonics**.
- **Photonics, definition:** the science and technology of generating, detecting and manipulating **photons**; **silicon photonics** integrates optical components onto a silicon substrate using CMOS-compatible processes.
- **Adjacent missions:** **India Semiconductor Mission** (under MeitY; Semicon 2.0 notified 2026) and the **National Quantum Mission** (2023, ₹6,003.65 crore, DST) — **photonic qubits** are one of the NQM's platforms.
- Distinguish from the older **Indian Laser Association** — a scientific/professional society, not an industry association.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (developments in and applications of new technology; indigenisation of technology).

**Static anchor:** Your **Sci-Tech notes on semiconductors and quantum technology**. *Update:* open a new one-page **"Photonics"** entry — definition, silicon photonics, PICs, LiDAR, QKD, optical interconnects — and cross-link it to the **ISM/Semicon 2.0** and **National Quantum Mission** pages so all three read as one supply-chain story. **Cross-link** also to the **Chennai Declaration** adopted at the **14th BRICS Science, Technology and Innovation Ministerial** held in Chennai under India's 2026 Chairship, which lists photonics-adjacent areas — AI, HPC, quantum, nanotechnology — as joint-research priorities.

### 5. A Tibetan snowtrout surfaces in Tawang — *Schizopygopsis waddellii* recorded in India for the first time — Environment

**Headline:** Scientists from **ICAR's coldwater fisheries research institute at Bhimtal, Uttarakhand**, working with the **Arunachal Pradesh Department of Fisheries** and the local **Shar-Tso Committee**, have recorded the high-altitude Tibetan fish ***Schizopygopsis waddellii*** in India for the first time — **ten specimens collected in July 2026** from **Pangkang Teng Tso lake (about 12,800 ft) in Tawang district, Arunachal Pradesh**.

**Exam angle:** Species-in-news questions are almost always testing three things — **family, habitat, and why the record matters** — so anchor on those. Family is **Cyprinidae**, and within it the **schizothoracine** group, the "snowtrout" of the Himalaya (note the trap: snowtrout are **carps, not trout**; true trout are Salmonidae and are largely *introduced* in India). Habitat is **oligotrophic, cold, high-altitude lacustrine** water — low temperature, low dissolved oxygen, low nutrient. Why it matters: the species was previously known only from the **Tibetan and Chinese plateau**, with **Yamdrok (Yamdok) Lake in Tibet** as its type locality, so this is a **range extension** that ties Eastern Himalayan headwaters biogeographically to the Tibetan Plateau — and it was confirmed by **integrative taxonomy**, combining morphology with **mitochondrial DNA** analysis, which is itself a testable methodological point. Broader hook: this is the **Eastern Himalaya biodiversity hotspot**, and high-altitude lakes are among the least-surveyed and most climate-exposed freshwater systems in India.

**Facts to memorise:**

- ***Schizopygopsis waddellii*** — family **Cyprinidae** (carps and minnows); **schizothoracine / snowtrout** group; a **new record for India**, not a newly described species.
- **Where:** **Pangkang Teng Tso lake, ~12,800 ft, Tawang district, Arunachal Pradesh.** **Ten specimens, July 2026.**
- **Who:** **ICAR** coldwater fisheries institute, **Bhimtal** + **Arunachal Pradesh Dept of Fisheries** + **Shar-Tso Committee**.
- **Method:** **integrative taxonomy** — morphological examination **plus mitochondrial DNA** analysis.
- **Prior range:** Tibetan and Chinese plateau; **type locality Yamdrok Lake, Tibet**.
- **Adaptations:** **subterminal mouth with a hard, horny plate on the lower lip** for scraping algae and biofilm off rocks; **deeply forked caudal fin** for high-velocity cold currents; **nuptial tubercles on males** (marked sexual dimorphism); tolerance of near-freezing, low-oxygen, **oligotrophic** water.
- **Context to attach:** **Eastern Himalaya biodiversity hotspot**; Tawang also hosts **Sela Lake** and the **Pangchen Tsangpo/Nyamjang Chu** systems.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (conservation, biodiversity); GS1 — Geography (Eastern Himalaya, high-altitude lakes).

**Static anchor:** **Shankar IAS — "Biodiversity" chapter**, the endemic/freshwater fauna section. *Update:* add a short list of Himalayan **snowtrout genera — *Schizothorax*, *Schizopygopsis*, *Ptychobarbus*** — with the one-line trap note "**snowtrout are Cyprinidae, not Salmonidae**", and cross-link to your **Biodiversity Hotspots** page (Eastern Himalaya) and to your **high-altitude Ramsar sites** list (Tso Moriri, Chandra Taal, Tsomgo).

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Polity, 250 words, 15 marks)

**"The Election Commission of India can register a political party but cannot de-register one." Examine the implications of this statutory asymmetry for the transparency of political finance in India, and evaluate the reforms proposed to address it.**

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Establish the asymmetry precisely.** Section 29A of the RP Act, 1951 confers registration power; *Indian National Congress (I) v. Institute of Social Welfare* (2002) held the ECI's registration function is quasi-judicial but that no power of cancellation exists, save on three grounds — fraud/forgery in obtaining registration, cessation of allegiance to the Constitution, and a UAPA declaration. Note that the ECI's fallback, **administrative delisting**, extinguishes neither the legal entity nor its tax status.

2. **Trace the consequences with data.** 2,800+ RUPPs against roughly 750 that contested in 2024; only about 26–27% filing audit reports on time; six Gujarat-based RUPPs receiving ~₹1,700 crore in FY 2023-24 against ~₹1,480 crore for five national parties fielding 893 candidates. Explain the mechanism: **Section 13A** exemption for the party plus **Section 80GGC** deduction for the donor, with no vote threshold attached to either, converts a dormant party into a tax-efficient conduit. Add that **token participation** — nominating a handful of candidates — is enough to defeat the delisting criterion.

3. **Locate it in the wider political-finance debate.** Connect to the striking down of the Electoral Bonds Scheme (*ADR v. Union of India*, 2024) and the residual opacity of the ₹2,000 anonymous-cash threshold — the argument that disclosure reform is incomplete while the *entity* side of the system remains unpoliced.

4. **Evaluate reforms, with a balancing caveat.** For: amend S.29A along the lines of the **Law Commission's 255th Report (2015)** and the **ECI's 2016 proposals** (de-registration after ten years of non-contestation or repeated disclosure default); tie S.13A exemption to a minimum vote threshold; automate ECI–CBDT–FIU-IND data sharing; mandate audit by CAG-empanelled auditors. Against/caveat: **Article 19(1)(c)** protects freedom of association and new or regional parties must not be squeezed out, so any de-registration power needs a judicially reviewable, reasoned-order procedure rather than administrative discretion. **Conclude** on the balance — regulate the *finance*, not the *formation*, of parties.

### D. PYQ Mirror — how UPSC has asked these themes before

**Theme 1 — Election Commission and political parties.** *Prelims 2017* asked a three-statement question in which the only correct statement was that the **Election Commission resolves disputes relating to splits/mergers of recognised political parties** (the distractors claimed the ECI is a five-member body, and that the Ministry of Home Affairs sets the election schedule); the answer was **(d) 3 only**. On the Mains side, *GS2 2022* asked: **"Discuss the role of the Election Commission of India in the light of the evolution of the Model Code of Conduct."** (250 words, 15 marks). The lesson for today's item: UPSC's interest in the ECI runs to **what powers it does and does not have**, and the source of those powers — statutory, constitutional (Art. 324) or merely conventional. Today's RUPP story is exactly that question in a new dress.

**Theme 2 — Research in Indian universities.** *GS3 2014* asked: **"Scientific research in Indian universities is declining, because a career in science is not as attractive as are business professions, engineering or administration, and the universities are becoming consumer oriented. Critically comment."** (200 words, 12.5 marks). Twelve years on, the paradox has inverted in form — output is no longer declining, it is booming — but the underlying question, whether Indian universities produce *research* or merely *research-shaped output*, is identical. If this theme returns, the 2026 data in Item 3 is the differentiator.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
Statements 1 and 2 are correct. A party registered under **S.29A of the RP Act, 1951** that fails the recognition thresholds of the **Election Symbols Order, 1968** is an RUPP, and it retains **100% income-tax exemption on voluntary contributions under S.13A**. Statement 3 is the core error the news item turns on: the RP Act confers **no express de-registration power**, and *INC (I) v. Institute of Social Welfare* (2002) confined cancellation to fraud/forgery, cessation of allegiance to the Constitution, or a UAPA declaration. The ten-year non-contestation trigger is a **proposal** — Law Commission 255th Report and ECI 2016 — not existing law.

**Q2 — (b) 2 and 3 only.**
Statements 2 and 3 are correct: DILRMP 3.0 provides for the **14-digit ULPIN / Bhu-Aadhaar** and is run by the **Department of Land Resources, Ministry of Rural Development**. Statement 1 is incorrect — it is a **Central Sector Scheme with 100% central funding**, released on a phased, **performance-linked** basis through PFMS; there is no 60:40 sharing.

**Q3 — (b).**
The IPA, launched on **9 September 2026 with 65 founding members**, is India's **first independent, ecosystem-led industry body** for photonics. It is an industry association, not a statutory regulator (ruling out (a)), not a BRICS inter-governmental body (ruling out (c)), and not a government mission — photonic qubits fall under the **National Quantum Mission**, which is a separate DST mission (ruling out (d)).

**Q4 — (a).**
*Schizopygopsis waddellii* is a **cold-adapted freshwater fish of the family Cyprinidae** (schizothoracine snowtrout group), recorded in India for the first time from **Pangkang Teng Tso lake, ~12,800 ft, Tawang district, Arunachal Pradesh**, from ten specimens collected in July 2026 and confirmed by morphology plus mtDNA. Note that it is a **new record for India**, not a newly described species — its type locality is **Yamdrok Lake, Tibet**. Option (c) describes *Doronicum sanctum*, a different Tawang discovery.

**Q5 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
Statements 1 and 2 are correct: India ranks **3rd globally** by annual volume of Scopus-indexed publications (1.6 million papers, 2020–2025), and the **university patent grant rate of about 3%** is far below the **national average of roughly 24%**. Statement 3 is incorrect — the **NIRF assigns about 30% weightage to Research and Professional Practice**; the criticism is not that research is ignored but that it is measured by **counts of papers and filings** rather than citations, grants or commercialisation.

**Q6 — (b) 1 and 3 only.**
Statements 1 and 3 are correct: registration is under **S.29A of the RP Act, 1951**, and the ECI is the authority that decides **splits and mergers of recognised parties** under the Election Symbols Order, 1968 — the exact point tested in **Prelims 2017**. Statement 2 is incorrect and is the inverse of the actual holding: in *Indian National Congress (I) v. Institute of Social Welfare* (2002) the Supreme Court held there is **no implied power of cancellation**, except on the three narrow grounds noted above.

**Q7 — (c) Only three pairs.**
Pairs 1, 3 and 4 are correct — **Tso Moriri** is in **Ladakh** (a Ramsar site), **Tsomgo/Changu** is in **Sikkim**, and **Sela Lake** is in **Tawang district, Arunachal Pradesh**. Pair 2 is wrong: **Chandra Taal** is in the **Lahaul-Spiti area of Himachal Pradesh**, not Uttarakhand, and is also a Ramsar site.
