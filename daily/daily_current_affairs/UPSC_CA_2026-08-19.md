## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. India launches its first-ever Guide to Grasslands and Other Open Natural Ecosystems at UNCCD COP17 — Environment

**Headline:** At a side event of UNCCD COP17 in Ulaanbaatar on 17 August 2026, India released its first national *Guide to Grasslands and Other Open Natural Ecosystems (ONEs) of India*, formally pushing savannas, scrublands and ravines out of the "wasteland" category.

**Exam angle.** This is the highest-value environment item of the fortnight because it attacks a *classification error* that runs through Indian land-use policy: ONEs have been treated as degraded forest-in-waiting, which made them the default destination for compensatory afforestation, solar parks and plantation drives. The Guide reframes them as climax ecosystems in their own right — a ready-made Mains argument. It also sits at the intersection of three conventions (UNCCD's Land Degradation Neutrality, CBD's Kunming-Montreal targets, UNFCCC's carbon-sink target), which is exactly how Prelims likes to test conventions.

**Key facts to memorise.**

- Launched **17 August 2026** at **COP17 to the UNCCD**, **Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia** — COP17 runs **17–28 August 2026**, theme **"Restoring Land, Restoring Hope"**. This is the first UNCCD COP hosted by Mongolia.
- Released under the aegis of **MoEF&CC**; publication and event organised by **ATREE** (Ashoka Trust for Research in Ecology and the Environment) with **IUCN**, the **Centre of Excellence on Sustainable Land Management (CoE-SLM)**, **ICFRE**, and **SAC (ISRO)**.
- **Coverage:** Himalayan and Terai grasslands, the **Thar** and **Banni** landscapes, **Deccan savannas**, rocky plateaus, ravines, scrublands, coastal ecosystems, desert landscapes.
- **Thematic sections:** pastoralism, **fire ecology**, and **carbon sequestration** (ONEs store carbon largely *below ground*, in soil — unlike forests).
- Minister **Bhupender Yadav** (video message): these ecosystems "have for too long been judged by the absence of trees"; he invoked the traditional **Oran** and **gauchar** commons of Rajasthan.
- **Anchor number:** India's UNCCD pledge, raised at **COP14 (New Delhi, 2019)**, is to restore **26 million hectares** of degraded land by 2030 — the operational face of its **Land Degradation Neutrality (LDN)** commitment.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Conservation / Ecosystems) · GS1 — Geography (Natural vegetation) for the biome side.

**Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment* — the "Ecosystems" chapter (grassland ecosystem) and the "International Conventions" chapter (UNCCD). **Nudge:** in the UNCCD entry, add a cross-link box "ONEs ≠ wasteland" listing Banni, Thar, Deccan savanna, Terai; and in the grassland-ecosystem page, add a line that India's LDN restoration target is 26 mha by 2030 so the convention and the biome sit on the same page.

### 2. India and Armenia sign a defence MoU on R&D, innovation and shared production — International Relations

**Headline:** Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh signed an MoU with Armenia in Yerevan on **17 August 2026** covering research, development, innovation and **shared production** in the defence industry — the relationship moving from platform sales to co-production.

**Exam angle.** Armenia is the cleanest available illustration of India's arms-export pivot and of the counter-balancing logic in the South Caucasus, where **Azerbaijan–Türkiye–Pakistan** alignment faces **India–Armenia–Iran/France**. Note the vocabulary shift: an MoU on "shared production" is qualitatively different from a sale, and maps directly onto the *Aatmanirbhar Bharat* export target. Pair this with the **INSTC** and **Chabahar** — Armenia is a potential western leg of the corridor bypassing Azerbaijan.

**Key facts to memorise.**

- Signed **17 August 2026 in Yerevan** by **Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh** with Armenia's **Minister of High Tech Industry, Davit Tadevosyan**; scope = **research, development, innovation and shared production** in the defence industry.
- Two-day visit (16–17 August); Singh also called on **Defence Minister Suren Papikyan** and **Chief of the General Staff Lt Gen Edvard Asryan**; **Ambassador Nilakshi Saha Sinha** participated.
- **Geography for the map question:** Armenia is **landlocked**, in the **South Caucasus**, bordering **Turkey (W), Georgia (N), Azerbaijan (E and, via Nakhchivan, SW), Iran (S)**. Capital **Yerevan**; **Lake Sevan**; Mt **Ararat** lies across the border in Turkey. The **Nagorno-Karabakh** enclave (Azerbaijani territory) is the core dispute; the **Zangezur corridor** issue concerns Armenia's **Syunik** province.
- Armenia has been publicly reported as the **largest buyer of Indian arms** in recent years (Pinaka MBRL, Akash-1S SAM, ATAGS, Swathi weapon-locating radars). Treat the platform list as reported, not officially itemised.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (Bilateral groupings and agreements; India's extended neighbourhood).

### 3. SEBI launches the 'Incident Reporting' and 'Cyber Suraksha' portals — Economy

**Headline:** At the inaugural of its **Symposium on Cyber Defence** in Mumbai on **17 August 2026**, SEBI launched two web portals — a revamped **SEBI Incident Reporting** portal aligned to the **FSB's FIRE** format, and **Cyber Suraksha**, a central hub for threat and vulnerability information sharing.

**Exam angle.** Cyber resilience of financial market infrastructure is now a *systemic-risk* topic, not an IT topic — which is why the design is anchored to an FSB standard. The examinable idea is **interoperability of incident reporting**: a common format means a cyber incident at an Indian intermediary can be read by a foreign regulator without translation, cutting the friction that lets contagion spread faster than reporting. For RBI Grade B this is close to certain material.

**Key facts to memorise.**

- Launched by SEBI Chairman **Tuhin Kanta Pandey**, **17 August 2026**, Mumbai. The **Symposium on Cyber Defence** runs **17–21 August 2026** with delegates from **15 IOSCO jurisdictions**.
- **SEBI Incident Reporting portal** — structured, timely, actionable cyber-incident reporting; aligned with the **Financial Stability Board's Format for Incident Reporting Exchange (FIRE)**, which standardises cross-border incident reporting.
- **Cyber Suraksha portal** — central hub for cybersecurity knowledge, **vulnerability warnings**, policy measures and incident insights.
- Pandey also inaugurated the **RegTech Lab at NISM's Patalganga campus** (NISM = National Institute of Securities Markets, set up by SEBI).
- Background to hold: SEBI's **Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework (CSCRF)** is the umbrella under which regulated entities report; **FSB** is the Basel-based body created out of the **2009 G20 London Summit** (successor to the Financial Stability Forum); **IOSCO** is the global standard-setter for securities regulators.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Capital markets, financial regulation) · overlaps GS3 Internal Security (cyber security).

**Static anchor:** *Mrunal* Pillar 1D (Financial regulators — SEBI). **Nudge:** on the SEBI page add a three-line block — CSCRF → Incident Reporting portal (FIRE) → Cyber Suraksha — and cross-link to your FSB/IOSCO/BIS "global financial architecture" card so you can name the parent body under pressure.

### 4. Navy leases two more MQ-9B Sea Guardians; MHA orders the Indrajaal Ranger anti-drone vehicle — Internal Security

**Headline:** MoD signed a **₹1,943 crore, 30-month** contract with **General Atomics Aeronautical Systems (GA-ASI)** on 17 August 2026 to lease **two MQ-9B Sea Guardian** HALE RPAS for the Indian Navy; separately, the **Indrajaal–Sigma consortium** won **₹155 crore** in MHA orders for the **Indrajaal Ranger** mobile anti-drone patrol vehicle.

**Exam angle.** Read the two together and you get the whole drone question in one item: at the top end India *leases* foreign HALE ISR because indigenous MALE/HALE is not ready; at the bottom end it *buys Indian* counter-UAS because that threat is domestic and immediate. That contrast — capability gap above, indigenisation below — is the argument UPSC actually rewards. Also note the acquisition model: **lease**, not purchase, under **COCO** (Contractor Owned, Contractor Operated), which the Defence Acquisition Procedure explicitly allows.

**Key facts to memorise.**

- **MQ-9B Sea Guardian:** contract signed **17 August 2026** at **Kartavya Bhawan-2, New Delhi**, in the presence of **AS & DG (Acquisition) A. Anbarasu**. **₹1,943 crore** (~$203 million), **30 months**, **two** aircraft, for **Maritime Domain Awareness** and persistent ISR over the Indian Ocean Region. HALE = High-Altitude Long-Endurance; RPAS = Remotely Piloted Aircraft System. The Navy already operates leased MQ-9Bs on the **COCO** model.
- Distinguish this from the separate **31 MQ-9B** *purchase* case (Navy 15 Sea Guardian + Army and IAF 8 Sky Guardian each) — the lease is a bridging arrangement, not the purchase.
- **Indrajaal Ranger:** **₹155 crore** in orders from the **Ministry of Home Affairs**; India's only **ARDTC (Anti-Rogue Drone Technology Committee)**-approved mobile **C-UAS** platform; built by **Indrajaal Autonomous Defense Systems** in **Hyderabad, Telangana**, on a **Toyota Hilux** chassis; roles = border patrolling, VVIP protection, urban policing, airports, critical infrastructure.
- Regulatory static: **Drone Rules, 2021** (MoCA) classify UAS by **all-up weight** — nano ≤250 g, micro >250 g–2 kg, small >2–25 kg, medium >25–150 kg, large >150 kg; the **Digital Sky** platform and the green/yellow/red airspace map.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security (Border management; role of technology in internal security) · GS3 Sci-Tech for the platform detail.

**Static anchor:** your GS3 Internal Security file — "Border management & non-traditional threats". **Nudge:** add a "counter-UAS in India" card (ARDTC approval route → DRDO D4 system → Indrajaal Ranger) and cross-link it to the Drone Rules 2021 weight table, so the 2023 UAV Mains question has a one-page answer source.

### 5. Karnataka announces 'Vidyarthi Sahayahasta' — 7.5% inside the 15% rural quota — Society

**Headline:** Karnataka CM **D.K. Shivakumar** announced on **15 August 2026** a scheme reserving **7.5% of professional-course seats within the existing 15% rural reservation** for students who studied **Class I to Class X entirely in rural government schools**.

**Exam angle.** The interesting bit is the *design*, not the announcement. This is a **compartmentalised sub-quota** — a reservation carved inside a reservation, and the eligibility criterion is **type of schooling**, not caste. That makes it a live test case for two doctrines: the sub-classification permitted in **State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh (2024)**, and the older question of whether a benefit keyed to educational disadvantage is defensible under **Article 15(4)/15(5)**. Expect a Mains-style framing on "equality of opportunity vs. equality of outcome in higher education access".

**Key facts to memorise.**

- Announced **15 August 2026** at **Manekshaw Parade Ground, Bengaluru**, during Independence Day celebrations. Name: **Vidyarthi Sahayahasta**.
- **Design:** **7.5%** carved out **within** the existing **15% rural reservation**; eligibility = continuous study **Class I–X in rural government schools**; applies to **professional courses**, including **medical and engineering**.
- Constitutional pegs: **Article 15(4)** (special provision for socially and educationally backward classes/SC/ST), **Article 15(5)** (reservation in educational institutions including private unaided, inserted by the **93rd Amendment, 2005**), **Article 16(4)**; **Article 46** (DPSP — educational and economic interests of weaker sections).
- Case law to keep alongside: **Indra Sawhney (1992)** — 50% ceiling and the creamy-layer principle; **Ashoka Kumar Thakur (2008)** — upheld 27% OBC quota in central institutions; **Janhit Abhiyan (2022)** — upheld EWS, a quota not keyed to caste; **State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh (2024)** — States may sub-classify within SCs.
- Distinguish **vertical** reservation (SC/ST/OBC/EWS — the social categories) from **horizontal** reservation (women, PwD, rural — cuts across all verticals). A rural quota is classically horizontal; a sub-quota inside it is a further compartment.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Society (Social empowerment, education) · GS2 — Polity (Fundamental Rights, Articles 15 and 16).

**Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth* — "Fundamental Rights", Article 15 and 16 sub-sections; plus your Society notes on educational inequality. **Nudge:** on the Article 15 page, add a two-line "vertical vs horizontal reservation" distinction with an example each, and cross-link to Davinder Singh (2024) — you will need this the moment a State quota is challenged in the news.

### C. Mains-Style Question

**GS3 — Environment (250 words, 15 marks)**

> India's grasslands, savannas, scrublands and ravines have long been administratively classified as "wastelands". **Examine** how this classification has shaped land-use decisions, and **discuss** the measures required for a science-based conservation of Open Natural Ecosystems.

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points):**

1. **Establish the category.** ONEs are not degraded forests but distinct climax ecosystems — Thar and Banni, Deccan savannas, Terai and Himalayan grasslands, rocky plateaus, ravines, coastal systems. They store carbon largely in soil, regulate soil and water processes, and support pastoral and livestock livelihoods. India's first *Guide to Grasslands and ONEs* (UNCCD COP17, August 2026) is the first official document to map them as a category.
2. **The cost of the "wasteland" label.** Being judged by the absence of trees made ONEs the residual land bank: default sites for compensatory afforestation and plantation drives, for solar and infrastructure siting, and for revenue-department diversion. Consequences — loss of endemic fauna (Great Indian Bustard, Indian wolf, blackbuck, lesser florican), erosion of commons such as *Oran* and *gauchar*, and displacement of pastoralist tenure. Afforesting a savanna is not restoration; it is conversion.
3. **Why the policy grammar is missing.** No statutory category for ONEs under the Wild Life (Protection) Act or the Forest (Conservation) Act; land-degradation assessment methods penalise low tree cover; pastoralist rights are weakly recognised even under the Forest Rights Act; fire is treated as damage rather than as an ecological process.
4. **Way forward.** Adopt ONE-specific mapping and degradation metrics (SAC-ISRO, ICFRE) rather than canopy-cover proxies; recognise community and pastoral tenure over commons; site renewables and afforestation using ONE-aware screening; align restoration accounting with the **26 million hectare LDN target by 2030** so that grassland restoration counts; and fund a dedicated grassland-ecosystem programme with community-led rangeland governance.

**Quotable line:** *An ecosystem judged by what it lacks will always be managed as something to be replaced.*

### D. PYQ Mirror

**Theme 1 — UNCCD and desertification.** UPSC has asked this directly.

> **Prelims 2016 (GS1):** *"What is/are the importance/importances of the 'United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification'?"* — Statements: (1) it aims to promote effective action through innovative national programmes and supportive international partnerships; (2) it has a special focus on South Asia and North Africa, and its Secretariat facilitates the allocation of a major portion of financial resources to these regions; (3) it is committed to a bottom-up approach, encouraging the participation of local people in combating desertification. **Answer: 1 and 3 only.** Statement 2 fails because the Convention's full title gives priority to **Africa**, not South Asia.

**Theme 2 — Grassland/savanna ecology.** UPSC tests the biome mechanism, not the news.

> **Prelims 2021 (GS1):** *"The vegetation of savannah consists of grassland with scattered small trees, but extensive areas have no trees. The forest development in such areas is generally kept in check by one or more or a combination of some conditions."* Options included burrowing animals and termites, fire, grazing herbivores, seasonal rainfall, soil properties. The accepted answer set is **fire, grazing herbivores and seasonal rainfall**. This is exactly the "fire ecology" section of today's Guide, asked five years early.

**Theme 3 — UAVs and internal security.**

> **Mains 2023, GS3:** *"The use of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) by our adversaries across the borders to ferry arms/ammunitions, drugs, etc., is a serious threat to internal security. Comment on the measures being taken to tackle this threat."* The Indrajaal Ranger MHA order is fresh evidence for the "measures being taken" half of that answer.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** The Guide was launched on 17 August 2026 at a UNCCD COP17 side event in Ulaanbaatar and carries thematic sections on pastoralism, fire ecology and carbon sequestration. Statement 2 is wrong: it explicitly covers the Thar and Banni landscapes, Deccan savannas, rocky plateaus, ravines, scrublands, coastal and desert landscapes.

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The MoU signed in Yerevan on 17 August 2026 by Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh with Armenia's Minister of High Tech Industry covers research, development, innovation and shared production in the defence industry. Armenia borders Turkey, Georgia, Azerbaijan and Iran.

**Q3 — (b) SEBI.** Both portals were launched by SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey on 17 August 2026 at the inaugural of SEBI's Symposium on Cyber Defence in Mumbai; the Incident Reporting portal is aligned to the FSB's Format for Incident Reporting Exchange (FIRE).

**Q4 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** The ₹1,943 crore contract with General Atomics Aeronautical Systems is a **30-month lease** of two MQ-9B Sea Guardian HALE RPAS for the Indian Navy, aimed at Maritime Domain Awareness over the IOR — not an outright purchase. The separate 31-aircraft MQ-9B case is a purchase.

**Q5 — (b).** Vidyarthi Sahayahasta, announced by Karnataka CM D.K. Shivakumar on 15 August 2026, gives a 7.5% sub-quota within the existing 15% rural reservation for admission to professional courses, to students who studied Class I–X in rural government schools.

**Q6 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The UNCCD (1994) describes itself as the sole legally binding international agreement linking environment and development to sustainable land management. LDN is SDG target 15.3 and is tracked through the UNCCD reporting process. The Convention's full title is "…in Those Countries Experiencing Serious Drought and/or Desertification, **Particularly in Africa**".

**Q7 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Article 15(5) was inserted by the 93rd Amendment (2005). Women and PwD quotas are horizontal. Statement 3 inverts the holding: in *State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh* (2024) the Supreme Court held that States **may** sub-classify within the Scheduled Castes.
