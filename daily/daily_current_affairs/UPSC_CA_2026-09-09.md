## Part A — Briefing

### 1. DAC clears ~₹1.10 lakh crore of Acceptance of Necessity — and the AoN-to-induction gap is the real story — Internal Security

**Headline:** The Defence Acquisition Council, chaired by the Raksha Mantri, on 7 September 2026 granted Acceptance of Necessity (AoN) to capital acquisition proposals worth about ₹1,10,000 crore across the Army, Navy and Air Force, with the Government stating roughly 98% of value will be sourced from Indian industry.

**Exam angle:** The high-yield idea is not the number but the *stage*. AoN is the first, in-principle administrative approval in the Defence Acquisition Procedure (DAP) 2020 pipeline — it is not a signed contract and not an induction. UPSC likes questions that test whether you can distinguish an approval from a delivery. The Navy's Marine Gas Turbine item is listed for "design and development," which is a quiet admission that warship propulsion remains an import-dependent segment even inside a 98%-indigenous headline. Note also that the 98% figure is a share of *procurement value*, not of critical sub-systems, seekers or engines.

**Facts to memorize:**

- AoN value ≈ **₹1,10,000 crore**; indigenous sourcing **≈98%** (government-stated).
- Approving body: **Defence Acquisition Council (DAC)** — apex body for capital procurement, chaired by the **Defence Minister**.
- Framework: **DAP 2020**, which replaced the Defence Procurement Procedure (DPP) framework and prioritises **Buy (Indian-IDDM)**.
- Acquisition cycle: **AoN → Request for Proposal → Trials & Evaluation → Contract Negotiation → Contract Signing**.
- Army items: CBRN Reconnaissance Vehicles, High Mobility Vehicles, Self-Propelled Mechanical Mine Layers, Advanced Light Helicopters, Trawl Tanks, **Sarvatra** Bridge System.
- Navy: **Arudhra Radars** (replacing Air Route Surveillance Radars at naval air stations) and design & development of indigenous **Marine Gas Turbines (MGTs)**.
- Air Force: fighter/transport/helicopter upgrades, a **Ground-Based Multi-Purpose Jammer**, and the RFID-based **DEFSAC** smart-card access system.
- **CBRN** = Chemical, Biological, Radiological and Nuclear.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security (Defence procurement & indigenisation).

**Static anchor:** Your GS3 Internal Security notes on *Defence Production and Indigenisation*. **Update:** add a five-box flow strip for the DAP 2020 acquisition cycle with AoN marked as stage one, and cross-link it to the 4 September RAKSHA defence-diplomacy note (exports pillar) and the 29 August Javelin FMS note (import route) — three entries that together give you the full "buy vs make vs import" triangle for a Mains answer.

### 2. NITI Aayog launches PACT and the ZET Marketplace — solving freight electrification as a coordination problem, not a subsidy problem — Economy

**Headline:** At the 5th e-FAST India Summit on 8 September 2026, NITI Aayog launched **PACT (Platform for Aggregating Clean Transport)** and the linked **ZET Marketplace** to pool freight demand from shippers and build commercial and financing linkages for electric medium- and heavy-duty trucks.

**Exam angle:** This is a clean case study of the *market-failure-as-coordination-failure* framing, which is very usable in GS3 infrastructure and environment answers. Individual e-truck pilots fail not because the technology is absent but because no single shipper can guarantee route-level demand, no financier can price residual value, and no charge-point operator will build corridor infrastructure without visible volumes. PACT is explicitly **not a subsidy scheme** — it is a demand-aggregation and matchmaking platform, which makes it a good contrast case against FAME/PM E-DRIVE-style incentive instruments. The honest counterpoint: ~3,000 e-MHD trucks against a national commercial fleet in the millions is still a rounding error.

**Facts to memorize:**

- **PACT** = Platform for Aggregating Clean Transport; parent platform **e-FAST India** (Electric Freight Accelerator for Sustainable Transport), NITI Aayog-led.
- Partners: **MoRTH** and **Ministry of Heavy Industries**.
- **ZET Marketplace** links e-truck makers, logistics providers, **charge-point operators (CPOs)**, financiers and technology firms.
- Deployment data cited at launch: **201 e-freight vehicles in FY25 → 826 in FY26**; over **3,000 e-MHD trucks** now operating nationally.
- **e-MHDV** = electric medium- and heavy-duty vehicle.
- Function: demand aggregation + market/financing linkage — **not a direct subsidy**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure & clean mobility) / Environment (transport decarbonisation).

**Static anchor:** Mrunal's Infrastructure & Transport pillar, plus the Shankar IAS chapter on *Climate Change — mitigation in the transport sector*. **Update:** open a running sub-page titled "Freight decarbonisation instruments" and file PACT under *coordination platforms*, distinct from *fiscal incentives* (FAME/PM E-DRIVE) and *mandates* (fuel-efficiency norms). Cross-link to the 5 September green-shipping-corridor entry — both are demand-side coordination plays on different modes.

### 3. NCSK gets its 10th Commission — and the body still has no statute — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** The Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment appointed a new Chairperson, Vice-Chairperson and three Members to the **National Commission for Safai Karamcharis (NCSK)** with effect from **6 September 2026**, with **Shri Bhagwat Prasad Makwana** as Chairperson and **Shri Kaishab Bihari** as Vice-Chairperson, completing the tenure of the present 10th Commission up to **31 March 2028**.

**Exam angle:** This is the single best live illustration of the *constitutional vs statutory vs non-statutory body* distinction — a classification UPSC tests almost every year. NCSK is the trap: it *was* statutory (1994–2004), lost that status when its parent Act lapsed, and has since survived only on periodically renewed executive resolutions. That matters substantively, not just for trivia: a body without a statute has no independent enforcement teeth, which is precisely the gap the Supreme Court has been filling by judicial direction. Pair the appointment with *Dr. Balram Singh* (2023) and you have a ready-made "institutional design failure" paragraph for a GS2 Social Justice answer.

**Facts to memorize:**

- Constituted **12 August 1994** under the **National Commission for Safai Karamcharis Act, 1993**, initially for three years; extended by amendments in 1997 and 2001.
- The **1993 Act lapsed on 29 February 2004** — NCSK has been **non-statutory** ever since, its tenure extended by Central Government resolutions. Present extension: **up to 31 March 2028**.
- Nodal ministry: **Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment**.
- Contrast set: **NCSC (Art. 338), NCST (Art. 338A), NCBC (Art. 338B)** are *constitutional*; **NHRC, NCW, NCPCR** are *statutory*; **NCSK** is *non-statutory*.
- Governing law on the subject: **Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation (PEMSR) Act, 2013**, which replaced the Employment of Manual Scavengers and Construction of Dry Latrines (Prohibition) Act, **1993**.
- **Safai Karamchari Andolan v. Union of India (2014)** — ₹10 lakh compensation for sewer deaths since 1993.
- **Dr. Balram Singh v. Union of India, 20 October 2023** — compensation for sewer deaths raised to **₹30 lakh**; directions on mechanisation, accountability and a national dashboard.
- Scheme: **NAMASTE** (National Action for Mechanised Sanitation Ecosystem), MoSJE + MoHUA, which subsumed the **Self Employment Scheme for Rehabilitation of Manual Scavengers (SRMS)**.
- Constitutional hooks: **Article 17** (abolition of untouchability), **Article 21**, **Article 23** (prohibition of forced labour), **Article 46** (DPSP).

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity & Governance (statutory & non-statutory bodies) / Social Justice.

**Static anchor:** **Laxmikanth, Chapters on the National Commissions** (SC/ST/BC) — the chapter block where the constitutional commissions sit. **Update:** add a boxed "the odd one out" note at the end of that block: NCSK, the only major welfare commission in this family without either constitutional or statutory backing, with the 1994 → 2004 → executive-resolution timeline. Cross-link to your GS2 Social Justice notes on manual scavenging.

### 4. An Indian officer is re-elected to head the Asian-Pacific Postal Union — and the UPU's "Restricted Union" architecture is the point — International Relations

**Headline:** **Dr. Vinaya Prakash Singh**, a 1988-batch Indian Postal Service officer, was declared re-elected **unopposed, by acclamation**, as Secretary-General of the **Asian-Pacific Postal Union (APPU)** at the **14th APPU Congress in Bangkok on 4 September 2026**, for a second four-year term running **January 2027 – December 2030**.

**Exam angle:** Skip the personality; UPSC will ask about the institution. APPU is a **Restricted Union under Article 8 of the Universal Postal Union (UPU) Constitution** — the UPU being one of the oldest UN specialised agencies. "Restricted Union" is a genuinely testable concept: regional postal groupings that member states may form *without* leaving the UPU framework, with the UPU itself remaining the universal single postal territory. This is also a good example of India securing leadership positions in second-tier multilateral technical bodies, a recurring Mains theme on India's multilateral footprint.

**Facts to memorize:**

- **APPU:** intergovernmental organisation, **32 member states**, headquartered in **Bangkok** (moved from Manila in 2002).
- A **Restricted Union** under **Article 8 of the UPU** — the UPU is a **UN specialised agency**, headquartered at **Berne, Switzerland**.
- Constituted in present form by the **Asian-Pacific Postal Convention, signed at Yogyakarta on 27 March 1981**; formation date **1 July 1982**; origins trace to **1961**.
- Runs the **Asian-Pacific Postal College (APPC)**, founded **1970**, also at Bangkok.
- Members include India, China, Japan, Pakistan, Iran, Australia, New Zealand, Nepal, Bhutan, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Maldives, Afghanistan.
- Other restricted unions for contrast: **Postal Union of the Americas, Spain and Portugal (PUASP)**; **Caribbean Postal Union**; **CEPT** (Europe).

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (International institutions & India's role).

**Static anchor:** Your GS2 IR note on *UN specialised agencies and India's representation*. **Update:** add APPU to the "regional bodies nested inside a UN specialised agency" row alongside the Asia-Pacific Telecommunity (under the ITU umbrella) — and cross-link to your running list of Indians heading international organisations, which is a classic Prelims one-liner source.

### 5. Places in News: the Boma–Badingilo Migratory Landscape — a World Heritage Site inscribed and endangered on the same day — Environment

**Headline:** South Sudan's **Boma–Badingilo Migratory Landscape** — the setting of the **Great Nile Migration**, the largest land mammal migration on Earth — is back in focus as the country's **first-ever UNESCO World Heritage Site**, inscribed in **July 2026** through an **emergency procedure** and placed on the **List of World Heritage in Danger** simultaneously.

**Exam angle:** Two separately testable things. First, the *mechanism*: UNESCO's emergency inscription procedure, and the fact that a property can enter the World Heritage List and the In-Danger List in the same breath — a nuance that makes for an excellent statement-based Prelims question. Second, the *ecology*: this is a migration roughly **triple** the Serengeti wildebeest migration in animal numbers, and it demonstrates why corridor connectivity, not isolated protected areas, is the operative conservation principle — a point that maps directly onto India's own tiger-corridor and elephant-corridor debates and onto Target 3 (30x30) of the Kunming-Montreal GBF.

**Facts to memorize:**

- Comprises **Boma National Park + Badingilo (Bandingilo) National Park + connecting wildlife corridors**, South Sudan; also called the **Great Nile Migration Landscape (GNML)**.
- **South Sudan's first World Heritage Site**; inscribed **July 2026** under **emergency procedure** and simultaneously on the **List of World Heritage in Danger**.
- Migration scale (2024 aerial survey): **~6 million antelope** — white-eared kob (~5,000,000), Mongalla gazelle (~350,000), tiang (~300,000), Bohor reedbuck (~160,000). More than **double** the Serengeti wildebeest migration (~2 million).
- Known migration range: **~122,774 sq km**, extending into **Gambella National Park, Ethiopia**.
- Terrain: savanna, seasonal floodplain, wetland and woodland; migration runs **January–June**, reversing **November–January**.
- Also holds **African savanna elephant** (Endangered), **Beisa oryx**, lion, cheetah, giraffe (Vulnerable), and the **Critically Endangered Nubian giraffe**.
- Managed since **2022** under a 10-year agreement between the Government of South Sudan and **African Parks**.
- Threats cited in the In-Danger listing: commercial poaching, wildlife trafficking, infrastructure and extractive development, climate change, limited conservation resources.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Biodiversity & conservation) / GS1 — Geography (Places in News).

**Static anchor:** **Shankar IAS Environment**, chapter on *Biodiversity Conservation — International Conventions*, sub-topic **World Heritage Convention, 1972**. **Update:** add a two-line entry for Boma–Badingilo under "recent natural-site inscriptions," and beside it write the emergency-procedure rule (inscription + In-Danger listing can be simultaneous) — that is the exam-relevant mechanism. Cross-link to your migration/corridor note and to the 27 August IBCA snow-leopard entry, since both turn on transboundary connectivity.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Internal Security, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **"Acceptance of Necessity is only the first milestone in India's defence capital acquisition process."** *Examine* the stages that follow, and analyse the structural reasons why headline indigenisation figures do not automatically translate into indigenous capability.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the pipeline, don't just list it.** AoN → RFP → trials & evaluation → contract negotiation (CNC) → contract signing → delivery/induction. Make the point that AoN is *administrative in-principle approval*, and that the announced value is an intent figure, not committed expenditure. Anchor with the September 2026 DAC clearance of ~₹1.10 lakh crore.
2. **Where the pipeline leaks.** Trial delays and single-vendor situations; repeated RFP retractions; capacity constraints in the CNC stage; the long tail between signing and first delivery. Note that DAP 2020's categories (Buy Indian-IDDM, Buy & Make Indian, Make-I/II/III) determine both the indigenous-content threshold and the timeline realism.
3. **Why 98% indigenous ≠ 98% self-reliant.** Distinguish *value of procurement* from *technology depth*. Critical sub-systems — engines, marine gas turbines, seekers, focal-plane arrays, high-end semiconductors — often remain imported inside platforms counted as Indian. The same DAC list carrying a 98% figure also carries an MGT item at the *design and development* stage, which makes the point without editorialising.
4. **Way forward, calibrated.** Front-load R&D funding (iDEX, TDF, the 25% defence R&D allocation to private industry route); shift from AoN-count metrics to induction-time and indigenous-content-audit metrics; build design authority in the private sector rather than only assembly capacity; use offsets and the export push (RAKSHA roadmap) to give domestic lines the volumes that domestic orders alone cannot.

**Quotable line for the intro or conclusion:** *Indigenisation is measured at the point of purchase but proved at the point of failure — the test is not who assembled the platform, but who can repair, upgrade and re-engineer it without a foreign licence.*

### D. PYQ Mirror — how UPSC has asked these themes before

**Theme: manual scavenging and rehabilitation (Item 3).**
**UPSC Prelims 2016** asked: *"'Rashtriya Garima Abhiyaan' is a national campaign to —"* with options covering the homeless and destitute, sex workers, manual scavengers, and bonded labourers. **Answer: (c)** — eradicate the practice of manual scavenging and rehabilitate manual scavengers. *Reading:* UPSC has historically approached this theme through **campaigns and schemes** rather than through the commission itself. That makes today's news the *complementary* angle — expect the institutional question (statutory status of NCSK) or the judicial one (*Balram Singh*, ₹30 lakh) as the fresher framing.

**Theme: defence indigenisation and the private/foreign role (Item 1).**
**UPSC Mains 2014, GS Paper 3** asked: *"Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) in the defence sector is now set to be liberalized. What influence this is expected to have on Indian defence and economy in the short and long run?"* (12.5 marks). *Reading:* the 2014 framing was about **opening the sector**; a 2026-27 framing will more likely be about **whether opening it delivered depth** — which is exactly the AoN-vs-induction and value-vs-technology distinction above. Same theme, inverted question.

**Theme: heritage and wetland site identification (Item 5).**
**UPSC Prelims 2022** carried a matching question on wetlands and their locations (Hokera, Renuka, Rudrasagar, Sasthamkotta), with the answer being *only two pairs correctly matched*. *Approximate — verify the exact option wording against the official 2022 paper.* *Reading:* UPSC's preferred device on international designations is **pair-matching and statement-based mechanism questions**, not "which country got a new site." So for Boma–Badingilo, drill the **emergency-procedure rule** and the **In-Danger list mechanics**, not the news event.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**Q1 — (b) 3 only.**
Statement 1 is wrong: AoN is the *first, in-principle administrative approval*, several stages before contract signing. Statement 2 is wrong: the DAC is chaired by the **Defence Minister (Raksha Mantri)**, not the CDS. Statement 3 is correct: **Buy (Indian-IDDM)** — Indigenously Designed, Developed and Manufactured — is the highest-priority category under DAP 2020.

**Q2 — (b).**
PACT (Platform for Aggregating Clean Transport) and the ZET Marketplace were launched by **NITI Aayog** at the 5th e-FAST India Summit on 8 September 2026, to aggregate freight demand and connect e-truck makers, logistics providers, charge-point operators and financiers. It is a coordination platform, **not a subsidy scheme**.

**Q3 — (c) 1 and 3 only.**
Statements 1 and 3 are correct: NCSK was constituted on 12 August 1994 under the NCSK Act, 1993; the Act lapsed on **29 February 2004**, and the body has since functioned as **non-statutory**, with tenure extended by Central Government resolutions (currently to 31 March 2028). Statement 2 is wrong: NCSK works under the **Ministry of Social Justice and Empowerment**, not MoHUA.

**Q4 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
APPU is a **Restricted Union under Article 8 of the UPU** (a UN specialised agency), headquartered at **Bangkok**. Statement 3 is wrong — **India is a member**, and an Indian Postal Service officer, Dr. Vinaya Prakash Singh, was re-elected its Secretary-General at the 14th APPU Congress on 4 September 2026.

**Q5 — (b).**
The Boma–Badingilo Migratory Landscape (Great Nile Migration Landscape) spans Boma and Badingilo National Parks and their connecting corridors in **South Sudan**, hosts roughly **6 million migrating antelope**, and became **South Sudan's first UNESCO World Heritage Site** in July 2026.

**Q6 — (a) 1 and 2 only.**
Statements 1 and 2 are correct: under the **emergency procedure**, a property may be inscribed on the World Heritage List and placed on the **List of World Heritage in Danger** simultaneously — precisely what happened with Boma–Badingilo — and **IUCN** is the advisory body for natural properties (ICOMOS for cultural, ICCROM for conservation training). Statement 3 is wrong: the World Heritage Committee **may inscribe a property on the In-Danger List without the State Party's consent**; consent is not a precondition.

**Q7 — (b) Only two.**
Pair 1 is correct — NCBC became a **constitutional body** through **Article 338B**, inserted by the 102nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2018. Pair 2 is correct — the National Commission for Women is **statutory**, under the NCW Act, 1990. Pair 3 is wrong — NCSK is **non-statutory**, its parent Act having lapsed in 2004. Pair 4 is wrong — the NCPCR is **statutory**, established under the Commissions for Protection of Child Rights Act, 2005. So only two pairs match.
