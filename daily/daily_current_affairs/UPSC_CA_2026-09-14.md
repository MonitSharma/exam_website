## Part A — Briefing

### 1. Wholesale inflation edges up to 9.92% in August — and the CPI–WPI wedge becomes the story — Economy

**Headline.** The Office of the Economic Adviser released the provisional WPI for August 2026 today: the all-commodities index stood at **110.8**, with year-on-year wholesale inflation at **9.92%**, up from **9.78%** in July.

**Exam angle.** This is the cleanest available illustration of why India targets CPI and not WPI. WPI is a *producer-side, goods-only* index — it has no services at all — so a near-double-digit WPI sitting alongside a sub-5% retail print means input costs are running far ahead of consumer prices. That is a margin-compression and pass-through story, not by itself a rate-hike trigger: the RBI's flexible inflation targeting mandate under Section 45ZA of the RBI Act is written on **CPI-Combined (4% ±2%)**. Prelims will test the definitional difference; Mains will ask what a persistent producer–consumer wedge does to corporate margins, MSME viability and the eventual pass-through to households.

**Facts to memorise.**
- WPI Aug 2026 (provisional, **base year 2022-23**): index **110.8**, inflation **9.92%** YoY (July: 9.78%). Weighted response rate **84.4%**.
- Group-wise: **Primary Articles 7.76%** (July 8.52%, *easing*); **Fuel & Power 22.93%** (July 20.05%, *the driver*); **Manufactured Products 8.37%** (July 8.29%).
- **WPI Food Index 7.05%** (July 6.65%).
- Major contributors: mineral oils (petroleum products), food articles, manufacture of food products, basic metals, non-food articles, chemicals & chemical products.
- Last confirmed retail print: **CPI July 2026 = 4.45%** (June 4.38%), CFPI 5.52% — a 19-month high. *(August CPI was scheduled for release on 14 September; treat the August retail number as pending, not as a figure you can quote.)*
- Structural: WPI is compiled by the **Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce & Industry**; CPI by **NSO, MoSPI** (current CPI series base **2024 = 100**).

**Notebook.** GS3 — Economy (Inflation & Monetary Policy).

**Static anchor.** *Mrunal Pillar 1 (Inflation) / Ramesh Singh — Inflation chapter.* Update the WPI page to the **2022-23 base series** and pin the August 2026 print. Add a cross-link from your WPI page to the MPC/FIT page: one line on *why* the statutory target is CPI-C even when WPI is at 9.9%.

### 2. 'Operation Deep Manifest' — 362 tonnes of Pakistan-origin dry dates, routed through Jebel Ali — Internal Security

**Headline.** The Directorate of Revenue Intelligence seized **more than 362 metric tonnes** of Pakistan-origin dry dates at Nashik, in a case built entirely on the *route* the cargo took rather than on what was in the containers.

**Exam angle.** This is the economic-security convergence question in a single case study. The goods were legal, the quantity unremarkable and the paperwork clean — the offence was **country of origin**. That makes it a rules-of-origin and trade-enforcement story as much as a security one, and it is the natural hook for a question on how a security prohibition is actually operationalised through customs machinery (DGFT notification → import policy → CAROTAR verification → DRI intelligence). The second angle is **transshipment as an evasion architecture**: a third-country port used purely to launder provenance is the same technique used in sanctions evasion globally.

**Facts to memorise.**
- **>362 MT** of dry dates, **13 containers**, importer a Mumbai-based firm, intercepted at **Container Freight Station (CFS) Ambad, Nashik**. Goods valued at roughly **₹9 crore** (per DD News).
- Declared country of origin: **UAE**. Actual route: **Karachi Port → Jebel Ali Port (transshipped into a fresh set of containers, loaded onto a different vessel) → India**, allegedly facilitated through entities run by Pakistani nationals.
- Legal basis: **DGFT Notification No. 06/2025-26 dated 2 May 2025**, prohibiting the **direct or indirect** import or transit of all goods originating in or exported from Pakistan — imposed after the **Pahalgam terror attack**, in the interest of national security.
- **DRI** is the apex anti-smuggling intelligence agency under the **Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs (CBIC), Department of Revenue, Ministry of Finance**.
- DGFT issues such notifications under the **Foreign Trade (Development and Regulation) Act, 1992**.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Internal Security (economic offences / security–economy interface), cross-filed to GS3 Economy (External Sector).

**Static anchor.** *Internal Security notes — chapter on money laundering & economic offences; Mrunal External Sector — FTP/DGFT.* Add a fresh box on **rules of origin and CAROTAR, 2020** (Customs Administration of Rules of Origin under Trade Agreements Rules) and cross-link it to the FTA-utilisation notes — origin fraud is the same concept whether it is used to dodge a ban or to claim an undeserved FTA concession.

### 3. Dryland Congress 2026 closes with the 'Delhi Declaration on Drylands' — Agriculture

**Headline.** The three-day Dryland Congress 2026 in New Delhi ended on 12 September with the adoption of the **Delhi Declaration on Drylands (3D)**, endorsed at the closing plenary by participating governments, institutions and delegates.

**Exam angle.** Drylands are where India's agricultural vulnerability actually lives, and this declaration frames the problem as a **Global South knowledge-sharing** problem rather than a subsidy problem. Two examinable hooks: the institutional one (**ICAR–ICRISAT**, CGIAR, the proposal to mandate **ICAR-NIAP** with foresight work on dryland agriculture), and the diplomatic one — the closing day was deliberately aligned with the **UN Day for South-South Cooperation (12 September)**, making this a legitimate GS2 illustration of India using agricultural science as soft power.

**Facts to memorise.**
- Theme: **"Transforming Dryland Agriculture with South–South Cooperation."** Outcome document: **Delhi Declaration on Drylands (3D)**. Over **800 global experts**, six themes.
- Hosted around the **ICAR–ICRISAT** partnership, which marked **50 years** at the Congress. ICRISAT HQ: **Patancheru, Hyderabad** (est. 1972); a CGIAR centre.
- **M.L. Jat** (Secretary DARE & DG, ICAR) called for **ICAR-NIAP** (National Institute of Agricultural Economics and Policy Research) to lead foresight work, plus robust data systems and **"living laboratories"** linking research, practice and policy. **Himanshu Pathak** is DG, ICRISAT.
- **ISSCA** — International Solidarity for South-South Cooperation in Agriculture. Its inaugural **ISSCA Global Awards 2026** drew **170 nominations from 34 countries**; the Mega Award (Transformative Excellence) went to **ICARDA (Lebanon)**, **CIMMYT (Mexico)** and the **Odisha Livelihoods Mission**.
- New MoUs announced with the **Global Crop Diversity Trust**; ICRISAT signed with NIFTEM, Wipro, Avior Life Sciences and the Angul and Rayagada district administrations (Odisha).
- India context (NRAA): rainfed farming covers roughly **51–55% of net sown area**, contributes about **40–46% of foodgrain production**, and accounts for ~**85% of nutri-cereals, 83% of pulses, 70% of oilseeds and 65% of cotton**.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Agriculture (cropping patterns, dryland & rainfed farming), with a GS2 IR cross-tag for South-South cooperation.

**Static anchor.** *Agriculture notes — "Dryland and Rainfed Farming" topic.* Add the 3D Declaration as the 2026 marker, and cross-link to your **watershed programmes** page (NWDPRA 1990-91 → IWMP → **WDC-PMKSY 2.0**) and to **NRAA (est. 2006)**. Also add a one-line CGIAR mandate table: ICRISAT (sorghum, pearl millet, chickpea, pigeonpea, groundnut), ICARDA (dry areas), CIMMYT (maize & wheat), IRRI (rice).

### 4. India endorses the 'Call to Action for 6G Leadership and Security' — Science & Technology

**Headline.** India has endorsed the multi-country **Call to Action for 6G Leadership and Security**, committing to shape the next generation of wireless around openness, interoperability, security, resilience and innovation-led competition. The **Department of Telecommunications** will lead India's engagement and coordinate follow-up across agencies, industry and academia.

**Exam angle.** The substance here is not the technology — 6G is a 2030s rollout — but **who writes the standards**. Endorsing a plurilateral "trusted networks" framework before the standard is frozen is India positioning itself inside the rule-making room rather than accepting whatever ITU/3GPP produce. Pair it with the **Bharat 6G Vision** target and the **ITU IMT-2030** framework and you have a complete answer on technology standards as geopolitics.

**Facts to memorise.**
- Endorsement announced on the sidelines of the **G20 Innovation Ministers' Meeting at Chapel Hill, North Carolina, USA** (MoS Jitin Prasada attended for India). **DoT** is the nodal agency for follow-up.
- Existing endorsers include the **United States, United Kingdom, Japan, Republic of Korea, France, Germany, Italy, Canada, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Estonia, Latvia, Romania, Greece, Czechia, Costa Rica, Honduras and Paraguay** — roughly two dozen governments in all. *(Counts vary slightly across reports; safest to say "around twenty governments including the US, UK, Japan and Korea.")*
- Core commitments: **openness, interoperability, security, resilience, innovation-led competition** — i.e. the Open RAN / trusted-vendor philosophy carried forward from 5G.
- Static pairing: **Bharat 6G Vision** (unveiled March 2023) aims for India to contribute about **10% of global 6G patents and standards**; **Bharat 6G Alliance**; **TSDSI** is India's telecom standards development organisation; the **ITU's IMT-2030 framework** (approved June 2023) defines 6G usage scenarios.
- Related domestic instrument: the **National Security Directive on the Telecommunication Sector (2021)** and the **Trusted Telecom Portal**.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Science & Technology (ICT), with a GS2 IR tag for standards diplomacy.

**Static anchor.** *Sci-Tech notes — Telecom / 5G-6G topic.* Add IMT-2030 and the 6G Call to Action under a new sub-head "standards geopolitics," and cross-link to **Semicon 2.0** (your 1 September entry) — both are the same argument about India moving from consumer to rule-maker in a technology stack.

### 5. A Colombo Security Conclave table-top exercise at Visakhapatnam — International Relations

**Headline.** A three-day **Table Top Exercise (TTX)** under the aegis of the **Colombo Security Conclave** concluded on **11 September 2026** at **Eastern Naval Command, Visakhapatnam**, on the theme *"Coordinated Response to Illicit Maritime Activities in the Indian Ocean Region."*

**Exam angle.** The CSC is the minilateral UPSC keeps circling because it is the one Indian Ocean grouping where India is the effective anchor and the agenda is explicitly **non-traditional security**. A TTX is a low-cost, high-signal instrument: no ships sail, but SOPs get harmonised and information-sharing channels get tested — which is exactly what **Maritime Domain Awareness** means in practice. The framing under **MAHASAGAR** also lets you show that India's Indian Ocean doctrine has a 2025-onward vocabulary, not just SAGAR from 2015.

**Facts to memorise.**
- Held **9–11 September 2026** at ENC, Visakhapatnam; opened by **RAdm Manoj Jha, Chief Staff Officer (Operations), ENC**; over **72 hours** of simulated operational scenarios with delegations from CSC member and observer states.
- Threats war-gamed: **maritime terrorism, narcotics smuggling, human trafficking, and illegal, unreported and unregulated (IUU) fishing**. Deliverables: reviewed **SOPs** and streamlined **information-sharing** to strengthen collective **Maritime Domain Awareness (MDA)**.
- Aligned with **MAHASAGAR** — *Mutual and Holistic Advancement for Security and Growth Across Regions* — the successor framing to **SAGAR (2015)**.
- **CSC statics:** began **2011** as an India–Sri Lanka–Maldives **NSA-level trilateral**; revived 2020; **Mauritius** joined as a full member (2021-22); **Bangladesh** was welcomed as the **fifth member in July 2024** at the 8th Deputy NSA-level meeting; **Seychelles** is also a full member — six members in all. The **Charter and the MoU establishing the Secretariat** were signed in **August 2024**; the **Secretariat is at Colombo, Sri Lanka**.
- **Five pillars of cooperation:** (i) maritime safety & security; (ii) countering terrorism & radicalisation; (iii) combating trafficking & transnational organised crime; (iv) cyber security and protection of critical infrastructure & technology; (v) humanitarian assistance & disaster relief.

**Notebook.** GS2 — International Relations (India & its neighbourhood; regional groupings), cross-filed to GS3 Internal Security (maritime).

**Static anchor.** *IR notes — "Indian Ocean groupings" page.* Put CSC beside **IORA, IONS and BIMSTEC** in a single comparison table (members / level / secretariat / agenda), and add a one-line cross-link from your **SAGAR** entry forward to **MAHASAGAR** so the doctrinal succession is visible at a glance.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Agriculture, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **Rainfed and dryland areas account for a majority of India's net sown area but a disproportionately small share of its marketed surplus. Examine the reasons for this gap, and evaluate whether India's watershed-led policy approach is adequate to close it.**

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points):**

1. **Establish the asymmetry with data, not adjectives.** Rainfed covers roughly 51–55% of net sown area yet yields only about 40–46% of foodgrain output; it carries ~85% of nutri-cereals, 83% of pulses, 70% of oilseeds and 65% of cotton. So the deficit is not in *area* or in *nutritional importance* — it is in yield, stability and market linkage.

2. **Diagnose the gap across four layers.** *Agro-ecological* — moisture stress, dry spells, degraded soils, low organic carbon. *Technological* — research and seed-replacement effort historically concentrated on irrigated rice-wheat; dryland crops have thinner varietal pipelines. *Institutional* — MSP procurement is effective mainly for rice and wheat, so dryland crops face price risk without a procurement floor. *Market* — fragmented holdings, weak aggregation, poor cold-chain and processing near dryland districts.

3. **Evaluate the watershed approach on its merits.** NWDPRA (1990-91) → IWMP → WDC-PMKSY 2.0 have demonstrably raised groundwater recharge, cropping intensity and in-situ moisture. But watershed treatment is a *water-availability* intervention: it does not by itself fix varietal pipelines, procurement gaps or market access. Evidence of benefit capture by better-endowed farmers within a watershed is a standing equity critique.

4. **Argue for a systems answer, and close on 2026 policy.** Watershed treatment must be bundled with climate-resilient varieties, crop diversification incentives, expanded pulses/oilseeds procurement, FPO-led aggregation and processing. The **Delhi Declaration on Drylands (2026)** and the proposed foresight mandate for ICAR-NIAP point exactly this way — treating drylands as an innovation and knowledge-systems problem, shared across the Global South, rather than a purely hydrological one.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The current WPI series uses base year 2022-23. Fuel & Power at 22.93% far exceeded Primary Articles (7.76%) and Manufactured Products (8.37%). Statement 3 is wrong: Primary Articles inflation *eased* from 8.52% in July to 7.76% in August.

**Q2 — (b).** 'Operation Deep Manifest' is the DRI's enforcement drive against Pakistan-origin goods brought in through third-country transshipment and misdeclared origin; the September 2026 action seized over 362 MT of dry dates at CFS Ambad, Nashik.

**Q3 — (c).** The 3D Declaration was adopted at the closing plenary of the Dryland Congress 2026 in New Delhi, convened around the ICAR–ICRISAT partnership, on the theme "Transforming Dryland Agriculture with South–South Cooperation."

**Q4 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** The Call to Action is a voluntary political commitment among endorsing governments, not an ITU treaty — statement 1 is wrong. DoT is the designated Indian lead, and the commitments do cover openness, interoperability, security and resilience.

**Q5 — (b).** The TTX was held under the Colombo Security Conclave at Eastern Naval Command, Visakhapatnam, 9–11 September 2026.

**Q6 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** WPI covers goods only, with no services component; food carries a substantially larger weight in CPI-Combined than in WPI. Statement 3 is wrong: WPI is compiled by the **Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT (Ministry of Commerce & Industry)** — it is the **CPI** that NSO/MoSPI compiles.

**Q7 — (b) Only two.** Pairs 1 and 2 are correct. Pair 3 is wrong: **ICARDA** is the International Center for Agricultural Research in the **Dry Areas**; rice-based systems of the humid tropics are the mandate of **IRRI**.
