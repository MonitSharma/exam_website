### 5. FCRA (Amendment) Bill, 2026 — asset-vesting clause under fire
**Exam angle (Polity/Governance, with IR link via foreign funding):** Bill No. 97 of 2026 (introduced in Lok Sabha 25 Mar 2026; passage deferred amid Opposition protests, notably Kerala). Its new **Section 16A** lets the State take over an NGO's foreign-funded assets — a textbook GS2 case study on civil-society regulation, property rights and federalism.

**Memorise:** FCRA is administered by the **Ministry of Home Affairs**; foreign funds must be received in a designated **SBI, New Delhi** account; registration needs 3 years' existence. The Bill's **Section 16A**: on cancellation/surrender/cessation of registration, foreign contributions and assets derived from them **"provisionally vest"** in a govt-appointed **Designated Authority** (automatic, no prior adjudication); permanent vesting if no fresh registration; sale proceeds to the **Consolidated Fund of India**. Constitutional flashpoint: **Article 300A** (Right to Property — a constitutional/legal right, *not* a fundamental right since the **44th Amendment, 1978**).

**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (cross-tag GS2 Polity)
**Static Anchor:** *Polity notes → "Right to Property / Article 300A"* and *Governance → "NGOs & Civil Society / FCRA."* Add a cross-link: FCRA ⇄ Article 300A ⇄ role of NGOs (2nd ARC) so the property-rights angle and the civil-society angle sit on one page.

### Mains-Style Question (GS2 / GS3, ~250 words)

**Q.** "The regulation of foreign funding of voluntary organisations involves a delicate balance between safeguarding national security and preserving the democratic space for civil society." In the light of the FCRA (Amendment) Bill, 2026, **critically examine** this trade-off.

*Model-answer skeleton:*
1. **Frame the balance:** FCRA, 2010 (MHA-administered) seeks transparency + security in foreign funding; the 2026 Bill is the latest tightening. State the core tension up front.
2. **Case for the State:** curb diversion/misuse of foreign funds, prevent "asset-stripping" by shell NGOs, accountability via "key functionaries," Section 16A vesting; align with FATF transparency norms.
3. **Concerns:** automatic vesting without prior adjudication tests **Article 300A** (proportionality, compensation); chilling effect on advocacy/minority & religious institutions; federal/political friction (Kerala); risk to genuine grassroots work.
4. **Way forward:** time-bound, reasoned adjudication and judicial oversight; distinguish bona-fide NGOs from offenders; graded penalties over confiscation; draw on 2nd ARC recommendations and SC jurisprudence on property/proportionality. Conclude on calibrated regulation, not expropriation.

## ANSWERS

**Q1 — (c) All three.** RudraM-II is indigenous, air-launched from the Su-30 MKI, and is an anti-radiation/SEAD weapon that homes onto enemy radars.

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Repo held at 5.25% with a neutral stance. Statement 3 is wrong — the rate is set by the 6-member MPC, not the Governor alone.

**Q3 — (b) Centre for Science and Environment (CSE).** Published annually with *Down To Earth*; the 2026 edition / "In Figures" was released around World Environment Day 2026.

**Q4 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** FCRA is under the MHA, and Section 16A introduces provisional vesting of assets. Statement 3 is wrong — the Right to Property is a constitutional/legal right under Article 300A, not a Fundamental Right (since the 44th Amendment, 1978).

**Q5 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** India chairs BRICS 2026 and hosts the Summit; the NDB is HQ'd in Shanghai and funds infrastructure/sustainable development.

**Q6 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** MPC has 6 members (3 RBI incl. the Governor as chair + 3 Govt-nominated); the inflation target is 4% ± 2%.

**Q7 — (c) Article 300A.** The 44th Amendment Act, 1978 deleted the Right to Property from Part III; it is now a constitutional/legal right under Article 300A.
