### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Environment, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Directive: Discuss.**
"India's tiger conservation strategy is shifting from maximising headline population numbers to addressing 'source-sink' imbalances across reserves. Discuss the ecological rationale behind this shift and suggest measures to strengthen landscape connectivity between tiger reserves."

**Model-answer skeleton:**
1. **Rationale for the shift:** population is concentrated — just 10-12 "source" reserves hold ~36% of India's tigers, while 12 reserves have fewer than three tigers each and three (Kawal, Kamlang, Dampa) have none; high-density reserves see dispersal-driven human-tiger conflict, while low-tiger reserves have intact habitat but poor prey base.
2. **The concept:** source-sink/metapopulation dynamics — isolated small populations face inbreeding depression and local extinction risk; genetic exchange via connected corridors sustains long-term viability.
3. **Measures:** consolidate the 13 identified source populations; build landscape-level connectivity through territorial forests and mixed-use corridors; treat reintroduction as a last resort guided by rigorous habitat/prey assessment and community acceptance (contrast Panna's success with Satkosia's failure due to local resentment); prioritise the Central India-Eastern Ghats landscape (most reserves flagged) and unlock the high-potential Northeast Hills-Brahmaputra floodplain forests.
4. **Conclusion:** the paradigm shift — from "counting tigers" to "connecting tigers" — aligns conservation with the National Wildlife Action Plan's landscape approach and evidence-based, NTCA-WII index-driven governance.
