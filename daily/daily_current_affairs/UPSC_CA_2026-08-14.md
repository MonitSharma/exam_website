### 1. Retail inflation edges up to 4.45% in July 2026 — food does all the work — Economy

**Headline:** NSO/MoSPI released the July 2026 CPI on 12 August: headline retail inflation at **4.45%** (provisional), up from **4.38%** in June, driven almost entirely by food.

**Exam angle:** This is the cleanest live illustration of the *headline vs core* distinction — the print is rising while the pressure is narrow and food-specific, not broad-based. It also sits inside the RBI's post-6-August posture (repo held at 5.25%, FY27 CPI projected at 5.0% with a Q3 peak): a food-led uptick that stays inside the 4±2% band does **not** by itself force a rate action, because the MPC targets headline CPI over a *medium-term* horizon and looks through transient supply shocks. The rural–urban inversion is the second story: rural inflation is running a full ~88 bps above urban, because food carries a heavier weight in the rural basket.

**Key facts / figures to memorise:**

| Metric (July 2026, provisional) | Value |
|---|---|
| All-India headline CPI inflation | **4.45%** (June: 4.38%) |
| Consumer Food Price Index (CFPI) | **5.52%** |
| Rural headline / Urban headline | **4.84% / 3.96%** |
| Rural food / Urban food | **5.79% / 5.05%** |
| Series base | **2024 = 100** (revised from 2012 = 100) |
| RBI tolerance band | 4% ± 2% (Sec. 45ZA, RBI Act; notified by Centre) |

- Compiled by the **National Statistical Office (NSO)** under **MoSPI**; CPI is released around the 12th of the following month.
- The 2024-base series draws its weights from the **Household Consumption Expenditure Survey (HCES) 2022-23**, which lowered the food group's weight relative to the 2012 series. *(Verify the exact revised weights from the MoSPI base-revision note before quoting a number in Mains.)*

### 2. Great Nicobar Tribal Council alleges the Shompen were told to "adopt a modern lifestyle" — Society

**Headline:** On 14 August the Tribal Council of Great and Little Nicobar publicly alleged that a former A&N Islands Chief Secretary had said the **Shompen** — a Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group — must adopt the "modern lifestyle" the Great Nicobar Island (GNI) project would bring.

**Exam angle:** This is the *consent* question, not the *forest-clearance* question, and that is where the exam value lies. The Tribal Council granted consent to the GNI project in **August 2022** and **withdrew it in November 2022** on learning that pre-tsunami ancestral villages fell inside the footprint — which makes it a textbook case of consent that was procured once and treated as permanent. For a PVTG in "voluntary isolation", free, prior and informed consent is a *continuing* obligation, and epidemiological vulnerability (no acquired immunity to common pathogens) means increased contact is itself the harm, not merely a route to it.

**Key facts / figures to memorise:**

- **Shompen** — PVTG; semi-nomadic hunter-gatherers of the interior forests and river valleys of **Great Nicobar Island**; **Census 2011 population: 229**. Staple food: the **pandanus** fruit, called *larop*. Distinct from the coastal-dwelling **Nicobarese** (a Scheduled Tribe, relocated after the 2004 tsunami to Campbell Bay / Afra Bay).
- **PVTG framework:** identified on the **Dhebar Commission (1960–61)** recommendation; 52 groups declared in **1975**, expanded to **75** groups (across 18 States/UTs) — a sub-category of Scheduled Tribes, *not* a separate constitutional category.
- **GNI project** — "Holistic Development of Great Nicobar": **transhipment port at Galathea Bay + international airport + township + power plant**, ~166 sq km, of which ~130 sq km is forest. Piloted by **NITI Aayog**; implemented by **ANIIDCO**.
- Great Nicobar hosts the **Great Nicobar Biosphere Reserve** (UNESCO MAB network, 2013), containing **Campbell Bay** and **Galathea** National Parks.
- Legal hook: **FRA 2006, Section 3(1)(e)** — the right to *habitat and habitation* for PVTGs and pre-agricultural communities — the only Indian statute that recognises a collective territorial right for such groups.

**Mains line:** *"Consent obtained once is not consent held forever — for a group in voluntary isolation, the burden is on the State to keep re-establishing it, not on the community to keep withdrawing it."*

**Notebook:** GS1 — Indian Society (Tribal communities, vulnerable sections); overlaps GS2 (Vulnerable sections / Statutory rights) and GS3 (Environment vs development)

**Static anchor:** **NCERT XI–XII Indian Society** + Laxmikanth ch. "Scheduled and Tribal Areas" (Fifth & Sixth Schedules). → Under your PVTG note, add a three-column card: *Shompen | Jarawa | Sentinelese* — island, population, contact policy, and the specific legal instrument protecting each (ANPATR 1956 for the Andaman tribes). ### 3. First subterranean fish from the Gangetic Basin — *Gangaichthys indonepalicus*, from Bihar's borewells — Environment

**Headline:** A **new genus and species** of miniature blind earthworm eel, *Gangaichthys indonepalicus*, has been described from the alluvial aquifers beneath the Indo-Gangetic Plain in **Bihar** — the first subterranean (stygobitic) fish ever recorded from the Gangetic Basin.

**Exam angle:** Environment questions increasingly reward the *habitat* rather than the species. The point here is **stygofauna** — animals living permanently in groundwater — which act as biological indicators of aquifer health. That matters enormously in the Indo-Gangetic Plain, the world's most heavily exploited aquifer system: an endemic, non-dispersing, blind fish confined to alluvial groundwater is precisely the organism that over-extraction and aquifer contamination extinguish silently, because no survey looks there. Note also the discovery route — all specimens surfaced through **borewells**, and the trail began with a social-media video, a nice line on citizen science.

**Key facts / figures to memorise:**

- Family **Chaudhuriidae** (earthworm eels), order **Synbranchiformes**; described in ***ZooKeys*, 2026**. It is the **first stygobitic member of the family Chaudhuriidae anywhere**.
- ~**4 cm** long; troglomorphic adaptations — **reduced eyes covered by skin, complete absence of body pigmentation, apparent absence of ribs**.
- Habitat: **alluvial (unconsolidated) aquifers** of the Indo-Gangetic Plain — contrast with the *karst/laterite* subterranean habitats that host India's other cave fishes.
- India's other notable subterranean fishes, all from **Kerala's lateritic aquifers**: ***Aenigmachanna gollum*** (Gollum snakehead — a new family, **Aenigmachannidae**, 2019) and ***Horaglanis*** blind catfishes.
- Conservation gap: most freshwater fish are **not** listed in the Schedules of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972, so a species like this has effectively no species-level statutory protection — its only protection is groundwater regulation.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment & Ecology (Biodiversity, species in news, groundwater)

**Static anchor:** **Shankar IAS Environment — "Biodiversity" and "Water Pollution / Groundwater"**. → Open a running "Subterranean & deep-water species in news" list (this + *Apristurus drona*, 11 Aug) and tag each with habitat type. Cross-link to the Central Ground Water Board / Atal Bhujal Yojana note in Mrunal's Agriculture pillar — that is where the *policy* half of this answer lives.

### 4. Srikalahasti temple seeks ₹114 crore under PRASHAD — the Vayu sthalam of the Pancha Bhoota five — Art & Culture

**Headline:** Temple authorities have submitted a **₹114.06 crore** proposal to the Centre under the **PRASHAD** scheme for pilgrim and tourist amenities at the Srikalahasteeswara temple, Andhra Pradesh.

**Exam angle:** UPSC almost never asks "which scheme funded which temple" — it asks the *pairing*. Srikalahasti is the **Vayu (air) sthalam** of the **Pancha Bhoota Sthalams**, and that five-way matching set is one of the most reliably examinable clusters in Art & Culture. The second angle is dynastic layering: a single temple carrying a 5th-century core, an 11th-century Chola superstructure and a 16th-century Vijayanagara Rajagopuram is a compact illustration of how Dravidian temple complexes grew *outward* over centuries — inner sanctum oldest, outer gopurams newest.

**Key facts / figures to memorise:**

**Pancha Bhoota Sthalams (memorise as a set):**

| Element | Temple | Place | State |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vayu** (air) | Srikalahasteeswara | Srikalahasti | **Andhra Pradesh** |
| **Prithvi** (earth) | Ekambareswarar | Kanchipuram | Tamil Nadu |
| **Appu** (water) | Jambukeswarar | Tiruvanaikaval (Tiruchirappalli) | Tamil Nadu |
| **Agni/Tejo** (fire) | Arunachaleswarar | Tiruvannamalai | Tamil Nadu |
| **Akasha** (sky/ether) | Nataraja | Chidambaram | Tamil Nadu |

- Srikalahasti: **Tirupati district**, on the **Swarnamukhi** river, built adjoining a hill (the hill itself forms part of the temple wall); called **"Dakshina Kailasam"**. Style: **Dravidian**.
- Chronology: inner temple **5th century**; outer temple and main structure **11th century, Rajendra Chola I**; entrance gopurams under **Kulottunga Chola I**; the ~**120 m Rajagopuram** built by the **Vijayanagara** empire in the 16th century.
- Cross-link: the town is also the home of **Srikalahasti Kalamkari** — the *pen* (freehand, temple-narrative) tradition, as against the *block-printed* Machilipatnam Kalamkari.
- **PRASHAD** — Ministry of **Tourism**, **Central Sector** scheme, launched **2014-15** as PRASAD; renamed **PRASHAD** (National Mission on Pilgrimage Rejuvenation and Spiritual Heritage Augmentation Drive) in **October 2017**, when the **HRIDAY** scheme of MoHUA was closed and heritage-city projects folded in. Distinguish from **Swadesh Darshan 2.0** (thematic circuits, also Ministry of Tourism).

**Notebook:** GS1 — Art & Culture (Temple architecture); scheme detail cross-files to GS2 (Government schemes)

### 5. Exercise Udara Shakti 2026 at Subang — the Rafale's first Malaysian deployment — International Relations

**Headline:** The **IAF and the Royal Malaysian Air Force** began **Exercise Udara Shakti 2026** at **Subang Air Base, Selangor, Malaysia** on **13 August** — a five-day bilateral air exercise, and the first time Indian **Rafales** have deployed to Malaysia.

**Exam angle:** Read this as Act East infrastructure, not as a drill. Malaysia flies the **Su-30MKM**, a variant closely related to India's Su-30MKI — which gives India a genuine sustainment-and-MRO offer that no other partner can match, and turns a routine exercise into a defence-industrial opening. Layer on the **Enhanced Strategic Partnership** (upgraded in 2024) and Malaysia's position astride the **Strait of Malacca** — the chokepoint through which most of India's eastward trade and energy flows — and you have the standard Mains framing: interoperability with littoral ASEAN states as the practical content of maritime security cooperation in the Indo-Pacific.

**Key facts / figures to memorise:**

- **Venue:** Subang Air Base, Selangor. **Dates:** 13–17 August 2026 (five days). **Scale:** ~83 personnel, 11 aircraft.
- **India:** 4 × **Rafale** (101 Squadron) + 2 × **C-17 Globemaster III** (81 Squadron). **Malaysia:** 2 × **Su-30MKM** (No. 12 Sqn), 2 × **F/A-18D Hornet** (No. 18 Sqn), 1 × **A400M** (No. 8 Sqn).
- **Content:** Dissimilar Basic Fighter Manoeuvres (DBFM), Dissimilar Air Combat Training (DACT), Air-to-Air Refuelling (AAR), PHOTEX, and Subject Matter Expert Exchanges.
- **Udara Shakti** is the **bilateral air** exercise (earlier editions 2018, 2022, 2024). Do not confuse with **Harimau Shakti** — the bilateral **army** exercise, 5th edition held in **Rajasthan, December 2025**.
- Institutional frame: **Malaysia–India Defence Cooperation Meeting (MIDCOM)**; relationship upgraded to an **Enhanced Strategic Partnership**; Malaysia is an **ASEAN** member and chaired ASEAN in 2025.

**Mains line:** *"India's defence diplomacy in Southeast Asia is shifting from symbolic port calls to platform commonality — the Su-30MKM is a better instrument of influence than any communiqué."*

### C. Mains-Style Question

**GS1 — Indian Society (250 words, 15 marks)**

> **Examine** the adequacy of India's existing legal and institutional safeguards in securing the free, prior and informed consent of Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups when large infrastructure projects are proposed in their habitats.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the vulnerability, briefly and concretely.** PVTGs are a sub-category of Scheduled Tribes identified on the Dhebar Commission's recommendation — 75 groups, marked by declining/stagnant populations, pre-agricultural technology and extremely low literacy. For groups in relative isolation such as the Shompen (Census 2011: 229 persons), the injury from a project is not only displacement but epidemiological exposure and the collapse of a foraging economy that cannot be compensated in cash.

2. **Set out what the law already provides — and where each instrument stops.** FRA 2006 §3(1)(e) recognises habitat rights for PVTGs but is under-implemented and rarely notified; FRA §4(5) bars eviction until settlement of rights; PESA 1996 mandates Gram Sabha consultation but does not extend to the Sixth Schedule areas or to the A&N Islands in the same form; the EIA Notification 2006 provides for public consultation, which can be diluted or exempted; the Fifth/Sixth Schedules and Article 244 create administrative protection but vest wide discretion in the Governor/Administrator. Add the ANPATR 1956 for the Andaman tribes as an island-specific instrument.

3. **Identify the structural gap: consent is treated as a one-time procurement, not a continuing standard.** Illustrate with the Great Nicobar Tribal Council's consent of August 2022 and its withdrawal in November 2022 after the ancestral-village footprint became known. Add the informational asymmetry problem — consent given without a translated, comprehensible disclosure of scale is not "informed" — and the absence of any statutory mechanism to *revoke* consent once granted.

4. **Conclude with calibrated reform, not rejection of development.** Suggest: statutory notification of PVTG habitat rights *prior* to any clearance; a revocable, time-bound consent standard with an independent anthropological monitor (drawing on the ILO Convention 169 principle of FPIC without over-claiming India's ratification — India has ratified ILO C-107, not C-169); mandatory public disclosure of project footprint maps in the community's language; and a health-surveillance protocol for isolated groups. Close on the constitutional balance: Article 21 and Article 244 read together make cultural survival a component of the right to life, not a cost line in a project appraisal.
