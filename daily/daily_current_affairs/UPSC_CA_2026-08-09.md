### 1. Lok Sabha passes the Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 — the legal door to MDR on UPI opens — Economy

**Headline.** The Lok Sabha passed the Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Bill, 2026 on 7 August, replacing the Income-tax (Amendment) Ordinance, 2026 and amending three statutes at once — the **Income-tax Act, 2025**, the **Finance Act, 2026** and the **Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007**.

**Exam angle.** This is the rare Bill that touches Prelims (statutes amended, MDR, REITs/InvITs, BIS), GS2 (legislative scrutiny — passed amid disruption with limited debate) and GS3 (external capital, digital public infrastructure sustainability). The headline controversy is the payments clause: the PSS Act amendment **empowers the Central Government to permit banks and payment service providers to levy charges on UPI and other notified electronic payment modes**, replacing the existing statutory prohibition. Zero-MDR on UPI and RuPay debit cards has been the policy since 2020, anchored in **Section 269SU of the Income-tax Act, 1961**; the argument for change is that the ecosystem is financially unsustainable as UPI heads toward 100–150 billion monthly transactions. Write the trade-off both ways: fiscal sustainability of DPI versus the risk of pushing small merchants and low-income users off digital rails.

**Key facts to memorise.**

- Three statutes amended: Income-tax Act **2025**, Finance Act **2026**, Payment and Settlement Systems Act **2007**. It replaces the Income-tax (Amendment) Ordinance, 2026.
- Electronics tax holiday for foreign suppliers of capital goods / contract manufacturing **extended by ten more years, to 2040-41**; eligible goods explicitly defined (mobile phones, laptops, servers, wearables).
- **FIIs and the Bank for International Settlements (BIS)** exempted from income tax on interest and capital gains from **government securities**.
- REIT/InvIT unit-holders get **tax-free dividend income restored**; the surcharge on **SPVs of business trusts rises from 10% to 25%**.
- Data centres: multi-layered approvals for foreign cloud companies removed; Indian data centres may now operate on a **leased** basis, not only under direct ownership.
- Foreign fund managers may relocate to India without making their foreign funds taxable — with anti-abuse safeguards against **round-tripping**.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Economy (External Sector / Fiscal Policy); cross-tag GS2 Governance for the scrutiny point.

**Static anchor.** *Mrunal Pillar 3 (Taxation) — the "Direct tax exemptions and tax expenditure" section*, plus Pillar 4A on payment systems. **Update:** add a sub-heading "Zero-MDR: 2020 → 2026 reversal" under your UPI notes and cross-link it to Section 269SU and to the definition of *tax expenditure*; the same page should now carry the REIT/InvIT surcharge change (10% → 25%) so you have one place for all business-trust taxation.

### 2. Centre deploys a National Joint Outbreak Response Team for the Chandipura virus outbreak in Gujarat and Rajasthan — Science & Technology

**Headline.** The Union Health Ministry has deployed a **National Joint Outbreak Response Team (NJORT)** to Gujarat and Rajasthan for an ongoing **Chandipura Virus Disease (CHPV)** outbreak, and activated the NCDC's **Public Health Emergency Operations Centre (PHEOC)**.

**Exam angle.** Two things make this examinable beyond the virus itself. First, the vector: CHPV is spread by a **sandfly**, not a mosquito — the single most common error on this topic and exactly the kind of distinction UPSC builds a statement-pair around. Second, the **One Health** design of the response: NJORT includes the **Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying** alongside NCDC and ICMR, and **ICAR-NIVEDI (Bengaluru)** sits in the research network — human, animal and environmental health treated as one system. For Mains this is a ready-made illustration in any pandemic-preparedness answer.

**Key facts to memorise.**

- Virus: family **Rhabdoviridae**, genus **Vesiculovirus** — the same family as rabies; bullet-shaped, single-stranded **negative-sense RNA**.
- First isolated **1965**, **Nagpur district, Maharashtra** — named after the village of the isolates.
- Vector: **female phlebotomine sandfly**, genus *Phlebotomus*.
- Clinical profile: **acute encephalitis syndrome**, overwhelmingly in **children under 15**; case fatality reported at **57–70%** in past outbreaks; no specific antiviral.
- NJORT = NCDC + ICMR + Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying. Surveillance runs through the **Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme (IDSP)**, coordinated by NCDC with State Surveillance Units.
- Research network: ICMR-NIV Pune, ICMR-NIE Chennai, ICMR-NIVCR Puducherry, ICMR-NIPCR Hyderabad, ICAR-NIVEDI Bengaluru.
- Geography note: historic outbreaks clustered in Maharashtra, MP and Andhra Pradesh — a **Gujarat–Rajasthan** outbreak shows the affected zone is not static.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Science & Technology (Health/Biotechnology); cross-tag GS2 Governance (health surveillance architecture).

**Static anchor.** *Shankar IAS Environment — "Zoonoses / One Health"*, and your vector-borne disease table. **Update:** add a row to the vector table — Chandipura → sandfly → Rhabdoviridae → AES in under-15s — sitting beside kala-azar (also sandfly-borne) so the two sandfly diseases are learned together, and cross-link to the National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme page.

### 3. A 12th-century Veeragallu at Neeragunda may fix the death date of Hoysala king Vishnuvardhana — Art & Culture

**Headline.** A **Veeragallu (hero stone)** bearing an **Old Kannada inscription**, unearthed during restoration at the Hoysala-era **Lakshmi Narayana Swamy Temple in Neeragunda village, Arsikere taluk, Karnataka**, records the death of Hoysala ruler **Vishnuvardhana** at **Bankapura** — and dates it to **11 December 1141 CE**, against the 1149 CE or 1152 CE previously proposed by scholars.

**Exam angle.** Epigraphy-plus-architecture items are classic Prelims fodder and Art & Culture has been thin in your last fortnight. The inscription was raised by **Maravve** in memory of her husband **Boppa**, a Hoysala soldier who died shortly after the king — an instance of the Hoysala **Garuda** tradition of royal bodyguards. Note the source-criticism angle for Mains: a single memorial stone revising a dynastic chronology shows how epigraphic evidence outranks later literary tradition.

**Key facts to memorise.**

- **Veeragallu / hero stones**: memorials to those who died defending king, village or cattle; typically **three panels** — battle scene, ascent to heaven, worship before a deity (symbolising *moksha*).
- **Vishnuvardhana**, originally **Bitti Deva**, defeated the Cholas at the **Battle of Talakad (1116 CE)**; consecrated the **Chennakeshava Temple at Belur in 1117 CE**, originally called **Vijaya Narayana Temple**.
- Influenced by **Ramanujacharya**; embraced **Srivaishnavism** while continuing to patronise Jainism and Shaivism.
- Hoysalas: 11th–14th century CE across parts of Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh; capitals **Belur**, later **Halebidu (Dwarasamudra)**.
- Architecture: **Vesara style** — stellate (star-shaped) plans, raised **jagati** platform, tiered friezes, soapstone carving. The **Sacred Ensembles of the Hoysalas (Belur, Halebidu, Somanathapura)** were inscribed as a **UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2023**.
- Inscription date recorded as Ekadashi of Pushya Masa, Durmathi Samvatsara, Year 1064 (Saka) = **11 December 1141 CE**.

**Notebook.** GS1 — Art & Culture (Temple Architecture / Epigraphy).

**Static anchor.** *NCERT/Nitin Singhania — Temple Architecture, "Vesara school"*. **Update:** on your Vesara page, add a one-line box "Hoysala chronology now contested: Neeragunda Veeragallu, 1141 CE" and cross-link the Belur Chennakeshava entry to the Battle of Talakad (1116) so the temple is remembered as a **victory monument**, not just a style specimen.

### 4. INCOIS puts El Niño persistence at 70–90% to February 2027; IMD's second-stage forecast cuts the monsoon to 90% of LPA — Geography

**Headline.** In a parliamentary reply, the Ministry of Earth Sciences confirmed that **El Niño conditions developed in May 2026** and are likely to persist from **June 2026 to February 2027** at **70–90% probability** per **INCOIS**, while IMD has revised the season from **92% of LPA** (First Stage, 13 April 2026) to **90% of LPA** (Second Stage).

**Exam angle.** The high-value fact is the corrective, not the alarm: since 1950 there have been **16 El Niño years, of which only 7 saw below-normal Indian monsoon rainfall**. That single ratio kills the lazy "El Niño = drought" assumption and is exactly what a Prelims statement-pair tests. Note also the **seasonal asymmetry** — the inverse relationship is strongest in the *later* half of the season, particularly **September**, which matters for late-sown kharif and reservoir filling. And the honest admission in the reply: MoES has issued **no district-wise risk and impact assessment or State guidelines** on El Niño for 2026 — a clean Mains line on the gap between forecasting skill and actionable local advisory.

**Key facts to memorise.**

- El Niño developed **May 2026**; persistence **June 2026 – February 2027** at **70–90%** probability (INCOIS, using an indigenous **AI/ML ENSO prediction system** with the **Global Ocean Data Assimilation System**).
- IMD LRF: **First Stage 13 April 2026 → 92% of LPA (±5%)**; **Second Stage → 90% of LPA (±4%)**. "Below normal" — always quote the model error.
- **16 El Niño years since 1950; only 7 below-normal monsoons.**
- Other drivers: **Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD)**, **Madden–Julian Oscillation (MJO)** (30–60 day cycle), regional ocean–atmosphere interactions. A **positive IOD can offset El Niño entirely**.
- Map the four MoES institutions to their cities: **IMD — New Delhi; INCOIS — Hyderabad; IITM — Pune; NCMRWF — Noida**.
- **Mission Mausam**: Multi-Model Ensemble and coupled dynamical models, more Doppler Weather Radars, lightning network, AWS/ARGs, wind profilers.

**Notebook.** GS1 — Geography (Climatology / Indian Monsoon); cross-tag GS3 Disaster Management and Agriculture.

**Static anchor.** *NCERT Class XI Fundamentals of Physical Geography, Ch. 12 (World Climate) + GC Leong on ENSO*. **Update:** on your ENSO page write the ratio "16 : 7 (since 1950)" in the margin next to the Walker Circulation diagram, and add a small MoES institution-to-city table — those four names in four cities are pure Prelims marks.

### 5. Parliament's Commerce Committee tables its 200th Report — "Evaluation of India–US Trade Relations" — International Relations

**Headline.** The **Department Related Parliamentary Standing Committee on Commerce**, chaired by **Dola Sen, MP (Rajya Sabha)**, presented its **200th Report** on *Evaluation of India–US Trade Relations* to both Houses on **6 August 2026**, examining exposure to US tariff measures across marine products, leather, automotive, textiles, gems and jewellery, chemicals, steel and agriculture.

**Exam angle.** Two exam hooks in one item. The **IR/economy** hook: the Committee wants the proposed **India–US Bilateral Trade Agreement** concluded at the earliest, a time-bound roadmap for **Mission 500**, and negotiated **complete exemption for generic medicines, critical minerals and smartphones** from future US tariff increases. The **polity** hook: DRSC composition and powers are a near-annual Prelims topic, and a committee report is persuasive, not binding — the leverage is that evidence from ministries, regulators and industry goes on the record simultaneously. The buried diagnosis is the best data point: India's services *surplus* with the US is narrowing because imports are growing faster than exports.

**Key facts to memorise.**

- **24 DRSCs**: **16** serviced by the Lok Sabha, **8** by the Rajya Sabha. Each has **31 members — 21 Lok Sabha (nominated by the Speaker) + 10 Rajya Sabha (nominated by the Chairman)**; **one-year term**; **a Minister cannot be a member**. System began functioning **April 1993**.
- India's **services exports** to the US: **USD 51.20 bn**, CAGR **11.58%** since 2020. **Services imports**: **USD 47.32 bn**, CAGR **18.68%** — imports growing faster.
- Total India–US services trade: **USD 40.53 bn (2014) → USD 98.52 bn (2024)** — more than doubled in a decade.
- **Mission 500** = double bilateral trade to **USD 500 billion by 2030** (announced February 2025). **TRUST** = *Transforming the Relationship Utilizing Strategic Technology*, also February 2025 — AI, semiconductors, quantum, biotech, defence, energy, space.
- Recommendations worth quoting: a **DGFT real-time watch desk** on US customs audits; fast-tracked **Mutual Recognition Agreements** for professional services, pharma and agri standards; **friend-shoring** into US supply chains in electronics, semiconductors and clean energy; USFDA-aligned **pre-clearance and joint testing facilities in India**.

**Notebook.** GS2 — International Relations (India–US) and Polity (Parliamentary Committees); cross-tag GS3 External Sector.

**Static anchor.** *Laxmikanth, Ch. 23 "Parliamentary Committees" — the DRSC section*. **Update:** in the margin write "Commerce = **RS** committee (hence RS chair), but membership is still LS-majority 21:10" — that asymmetry is the trap. Then cross-link from Laxmikanth to your Mrunal external-sector notes with the Mission 500 / TRUST pair, both dated February 2025.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Economy, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *Zero Merchant Discount Rate made UPI ubiquitous but left its infrastructure without a revenue base.* **Examine** the case for and against permitting charges on UPI transactions, and suggest a design that protects small merchants while making India's digital payments backbone financially sustainable.

**Model-answer skeleton.**

1. **Frame the tension in one line.** Zero-MDR since 2020 (statutorily anchored in Section 269SU of the Income-tax Act, 1961) delivered scale — UPI now serves a majority of retail digital transactions and is heading toward 100–150 billion monthly — but the cost of switches, fraud control and settlement infrastructure has been borne by banks, PSPs and episodic government incentives rather than by users. The 2026 amendment to the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 shifts that from prohibition to executive discretion.
2. **The case for charges.** Cost-recovery and investment capacity for PSPs; reduced dependence on discretionary budgetary subvention; better fraud-control and grievance infrastructure; parity with card rails where MDR already exists; a price signal that discourages frivolous micro-transactions loading the switch.
3. **The case against.** Regressive incidence — small merchants operate on thin margins and may revert to cash; risk of reversing formalisation and the tax-trail benefit; UPI as a **digital public good** and an export of India's DPI model; possible concentration if only large PSPs can absorb the compliance cost.
4. **Design that reconciles the two.** A calibrated slab — zero MDR retained below a transaction-value or merchant-turnover threshold, charges only on high-value or large-merchant P2M flows; a statutory cap set by the RBI rather than negotiated bilaterally; transparent disclosure to the payer; a sunset-and-review clause; and interoperability safeguards so pricing does not become a barrier to entry. Close on the principle: **the objective is to fund the rails, not to ration access to them.**
