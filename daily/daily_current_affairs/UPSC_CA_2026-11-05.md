## Briefing

### 1. India and Israel vote against the Pakistan–Belarus draft on regional conventional arms control (UNGA First Committee, reported 3–4 Nov) — International Relations
**Exam angle:** India opposed the draft's South Asia-specific tilt, arguing that security threats are not confined to regions and that the UN Disarmament Commission's guidelines (1993, 1999, 2017) already exist, so fresh guidelines are redundant. It is a clean example of India's principle that disarmament frameworks should be global, not region-specific, and of the India–Pakistan contest at multilateral fora. The same vote pattern has recurred (India was the lone "no" on a similar draft in 2024).
**Memorise:** UNGA **First Committee** = Disarmament and International Security. Draft sponsored by Pakistan + Belarus. India and Israel voted "no". UNGA resolutions are recommendatory, not binding.
**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India and the UN; multilateral disarmament).
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS / Mrunal — UN system → UNGA committees (six Main Committees); Disarmament architecture (CD, UNDC, First Committee). *Nudge:* add a box "India's stand on disarmament: global, non-discriminatory, non-regional" and cross-link to NPT/CTBT/CCW notes.

### 2. Cabinet extends Agniveer tenure by two years: a path to six years of service (briefed by CDS, 4 Nov) — Internal Security
**Exam angle:** The first Agnipath batch (scheme introduced 14 June 2022) is nearing the end of its four-year term. The 75% who would have exited get an additional two-year engagement, and those willing can continue in the Armed Forces afterwards. About 25% are enrolled in the regular cadre on merit after performance evaluation. The move answers two long-standing criticisms: the loss of trained manpower and the uncertainty for exiting Agniveers. The extension comes with higher remuneration (no figure reported).
**Memorise:** Agnipath introduced 14 Jun 2022; original term 4 yrs; ~25% retention in regular cadre; extension = +2 yrs for those due to exit; scheme under periodic review.
**Notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security (defence reforms, manpower policy).
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth/Mrunal Internal Security → Defence reforms (CDS, DMA, theatre commands, Agnipath). *Nudge:* update the Agnipath note with the 2026 extension and add a Mains line: "tour-of-duty model vs. professional-force trade-offs".

### 3. National Urbanisation Policy debate: Census 2027 will retain the 2011 urban-classification criteria — Geography / Society
**Exam angle:** The first Census in 16 years keeps the old definition of "census town", which can undercount urbanisation. The Economic Survey 2026, Union Budget and 16th Finance Commission all point to the same gap: growth spills beyond municipal limits. Ties neatly to the 74th Amendment, municipal finance and airshed-level governance (over 65% of Delhi's winter PM2.5 originates outside the city).
**Memorise:** Census 2011: 377 million urban residents. Census town = population ≥5,000 + density ≥400/sq km + ≥75% male main workers in non-agricultural work. Peri-urban growth 4.15% p.a. vs 2.9% in core areas. ~52% of statutory towns and ~76% of census towns lack master plans. Municipal revenue ≈1% of GDP; property tax ≈0.15%. 16th FC: ₹10,000 crore "urbanisation premium" (to encourage merging peri-urban villages with municipalities). Budget 2026: ₹5,000 crore for City Economic Regions. UN: ~44% urban, >720 million by 2050.
**Notebook:** GS1 — Urbanisation/Geography; GS2 — Local governance.
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth — *Urban Local Government* (74th Amendment, Part IXA, Arts 243P–243ZG, Twelfth Schedule); Spectrum/NCERT — Human Settlements. *Nudge:* add a comparison table "Census town vs statutory town" and a cross-link to Smart Cities/AMRUT.

### 4. Cabinet continues Fast Track Special Courts for rape and POCSO cases till 2031 (4 Nov) — Polity & Governance
**Exam angle:** Centrally Sponsored Scheme continued for five years (1 Apr 2026–31 Mar 2031) with ₹3,788.04 crore; the Centre's ₹2,353.15 crore comes from the Nirbhaya Fund, states/UTs give ₹1,434.89 crore. Courts rise from 779 to 850, allotted by case pendency rather than evenly. The scheme began on 2 Oct 2019 after the Criminal Law (Amendment) Act, 2018 and a 25 Jul 2019 SC direction (exclusive POCSO courts in districts with >100 pending cases). Good Mains material on judicial infrastructure, pendency and child protection.
**Memorise:** 779 → 850 courts; 399 exclusive POCSO courts among the 779; 29 states/UTs; 4,02,457 cases disposed so far; Department of Justice monitors via an online dashboard.
**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity/Governance (judiciary, vulnerable sections).
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth — *Subordinate Courts; Statutory bodies/Acts* (POCSO Act 2012, Sec 28 Special Courts). *Nudge:* add a one-line scheme card "FTSC: CSS, Nirbhaya Fund, 2019 launch, extended to 2031".

### 5. Fish and buffalo meat now lead India's farm exports (Apr–Sep 2026) — Economy / Agriculture
**Exam angle:** Livestock and fisheries are becoming the growth engine of agri-trade, even as field-crop trade wobbles (India became a net cotton importer: cotton imports $1.9 bn vs exports $560.7 mn in 2025-26). Risks: FMD, antibiotic-residue rejections, effluent concerns, West Asia freight disruption (RELIEF scheme under ECGC addresses freight costs).
**Memorise:** Agri exports Apr–Sep 2026: $27.1 bn (+4.7%). Marine products $4.5 bn (+13.2%); buffalo meat $3.4 bn (+53.8%). Livestock + fisheries ≈40% of agri GDP (up from ~30% a decade ago). Seafood exports record $8.4 bn in FY26. Top buffalo-meat markets: Vietnam, Egypt, Malaysia.
**Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture / Economy (trade).
**Static anchor:** Mrunal/Ramesh Singh — *Agriculture: Revolutions (Blue, White, Pink), Livestock, Agri-trade*; PMMSY. *Nudge:* add "Pink Revolution = meat/poultry; buffalo meat export data" to your Revolutions table.

### B3. Mains question
**Q8.** *Examine* the case for a comprehensive National Urbanisation Policy in India, in light of the decision to retain 2011 urban-classification criteria in Census 2027. (15 marks, 250 words)

**Model-answer skeleton:**
1. *Intro:* 377 million urban in 2011; ~44% urban per UN today vs "functional" urbanisation higher; old definitions undercount spillover (Bhiwandi: 60 villages, 91.8% non-farm jobs, yet governed as rural).
2. *Gaps:* peri-urban growth 4.15% vs 2.9%; no master plans for ~52% statutory / ~76% census towns; municipal revenue ~1% of GDP, property tax ~0.15%; fragmented governance and air-quality spillovers (Delhi PM2.5).
3. *Case for policy:* single national framework, National Urbanisation Council, periodic review of settlement status, peri-urban zoning, airshed-level governance, financing (16th FC urbanisation premium ₹10,000 cr; City Economic Regions ₹5,000 cr).
4. *Conclusion:* operationalise the 74th Amendment (Twelfth Schedule functions, empowered municipalities, SFCs); treat cities as growth engines for Viksit Bharat.
