### 1. India rejects OIC resolutions on J&K; says Pakistan "misused" the grouping — International Relations

MEA (19 Oct) rejected them as "malicious, biased and false" and said the OIC has "no locus standi whatsoever" on India's internal matters. It urged members to "reform this organisation". ### 2. Zero sugar diversion for ethanol in 2026-27: OMC tender bars sugar-based feedstock and cuts the maize price — Economy / Agriculture
- **Exam angle:** Oil marketing companies (tender by BPCL, issued 16 Oct) seek about 11 billion litres for the supply year from November for E20. Ethanol from sugarcane juice, sugar syrup and B-heavy molasses is excluded; the government set sugar diversion to zero for 2026-27 because of weak rain in key States and high retail sugar prices. Food-versus-fuel trade-off is a classic Mains theme.
- **Memorize:** Allowed feedstocks: maize, broken rice, FCI surplus rice, damaged foodgrains, C-heavy molasses. Maize ethanol price cut to ₹68.86/litre from ₹71.86. India has reached E20; E25/E27 and flex-fuel options are being explored.
- **Notebook:** GS3 Economy / Agriculture

### 3. India and carbon-market coalitions: the Open Coalition on Compliance Carbon Markets (OCCCM) and India's CCTS — Environment
- **Exam angle:** The OCCCM was constituted on 7 May 2026 in Florence by the EU, Brazil and China (Brazil first proposed it at COP30; Brazil is president, China and the EU co-chairs). Members include Germany, France, Canada, UK, Norway, New Zealand and Türkiye. It targets interoperable compliance markets and aims to avoid carbon protectionism. A work plan was due at Wuhan (Sept). India is not a member, though its Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS) is now operational (about 500 entities in seven sectors filed compliance reports; first credits expected in October; exchange trading expected Nov–Jan, per The Hindu).
- **Memorize:** OCCCM: 7 May 2026, Florence; EU–Brazil–China. CCTS: Energy Conservation (Amendment) Act 2022; administered by the Bureau of Energy Efficiency.

### 4. SC: fees for the whole professional course must be notified before admission; mid-course hikes only in exceptional cases — Polity & Governance
- **Exam angle:** *Dr. B.C. Roy Engineering College v. State of West Bengal* (Justices P.S. Narasimha and Alok Aradhe; reported 15 Oct 2026). The Court upheld the High Court's view that a mid-course fee revision was impermissible, rejected the college's reliance on a prospectus clause (equity/estoppel), and said fee committees must finish their work before admissions begin. Connects to commercialisation of education and Article 21A / 19(1)(g).
- **Memorize:** Facts: a state notification of 16 Oct 2023 raised the fee ceiling to ₹1.10 lakh a year; the college then raised fees about 37.5% from the second semester. Exception allowed only for unforeseeable, extraordinary economic conditions or statutory change. Frameworks cited: AICTE fee regulations 1994, NEP 2020.
- **Notebook:** GS2 Polity & Governance (Education)
- **Static anchor:** Laxmikanth "Fundamental Rights – Art. 19(1)(g), 21A" and "Education policy". *Nudge: add TMA Pai (2002), Unni Krishnan (1993) and this 2026 ruling in a timeline of fee-regulation case law.*

### 5. Mega-constellations and radio astronomy: SNIFFLES study finds LEO satellites leak unintended radio emissions — Science & Technology
- **Exam angle:** Study in *Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society* tracked Starlink, OneWeb and Guowang satellites for about 376 hours (from April 2024) using the Mopra telescope and the Australia Telescope Compact Array. Emissions were classed as intended, unwanted (e.g., harmonics) or unintended (stray electronics). Some signals were up to 100 billion times brighter than typical astronomical sources; a OneWeb signal at 1,613.19 MHz can interfere with hydroxyl (-OH) studies. Useful for the space-governance/ITU spectrum debate and India's own constellation plans (IN-SPACe, SATCOM spectrum policy).
- **Memorize:** LEO ≈ up to 2,000 km; GEO ≈ 35,786 km. Three emission classes: intended, unwanted, unintended. (Source: The Hindu digest of 19 Oct; figures not independently verified beyond that.)
- **Notebook:** GS3 Science & Technology (Space)
- **Static anchor:** Spectrum/Shankar IAS "Space technology – orbits, satellites, space debris". *Nudge: add a "radio quiet zones / spectrum interference" line next to space-debris notes.*

### B3. Mains question
**Q8.** *Examine* how the Supreme Court's insistence on fee certainty in professional courses balances the autonomy of private institutions with students' right to education. *(GS2, 150 words, 10 marks)*

**Model-answer skeleton:**
1. **Context:** *Dr. B.C. Roy Engineering College* (2026): full-course fee to be notified before admission; mid-course hike only for extraordinary circumstances.
2. **Autonomy side:** Right to establish institutions under Art. 19(1)(g) and 26 (TMA Pai 2002, Inamdar 2005), but education is not a profit-making trade; capitation is barred (Unni Krishnan 1993).
3. **Student side:** Art. 21A/21, families plan loans around fees; estoppel cannot rest on a prospectus clause; fee committees must act as continuing bodies and decide before admissions.
4. **Way forward:** Pre-admission notification, transparent cost audits, narrow inflation-linked adjustment mechanism, High Court oversight, NEP 2020 regulator alignment.

## Part C — Answers

- **Q1 — (c)** Headquarters is Jeddah and it was founded in 1969 (Rabat summit). India is not a member or observer (statement 2 is wrong); India was only invited as guest of honour in 2019.
- **Q2 — (a)** Statements 1 and 2 match the tender and directive. Statement 3 is wrong: the maize ethanol price was cut to ₹68.86/litre from ₹71.86.
- **Q3 — (c)** The OCCCM was constituted by the EU, Brazil and China on 7 May 2026; India is not a member; the CCTS rests on the Energy Conservation Act, 2001 as amended in 2022.
- **Q4 — (b)** The Court said fees for the whole course must be notified before admission and mid-course hikes are exceptional; the brochure clause and retrospective application were rejected.
- **Q5 — (a)** LEO extends up to about 2,000 km and stray emissions affect radio astronomy; Starlink is a LEO constellation, not geostationary.
- **Q6 — (d)** All three statements are correct for the 2018 policy (target of 20% by 2030, later advanced to 2025-26).
- **Q7 — (a)** TMA Pai was an 11-judge Bench and recognised the Art. 19(1)(g)/26 right; it held capitation fee impermissible.
