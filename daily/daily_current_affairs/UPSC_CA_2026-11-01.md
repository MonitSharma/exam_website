### 3. NPPA caps trade margins on 137 anti-cancer drugs at 30%; new prices from 9 November — Society (Health policy)
**Exam angle:** this is a price-regulation case study. The measure covers non-scheduled drugs, which sit outside the National List of Essential Medicines (NLEM) price ceilings, and it targets the trade margin rather than the manufacturer's price. Mains angle: affordability against supply risk, and the limits of Para 19.
**Memorize:**
- Gazette notification on 17 Oct 2026 under Para 19 of the Drugs (Prices Control) Order, 2013; revised prices take effect 9 Nov 2026.
- Cap: trade margin at 30% of MRP; MRPs expected to fall by up to 70%.
- Covered examples: pembrolizumab, bevacizumab, osimertinib, denosumab. The list is to be updated periodically.
- Precedent: a 2019 pilot on 42 drugs cut MRPs of 526 brands by up to 90%.
- Manufacturers must file revised prices within 7 days.

*Verification note:* the details are from News on AIR (19 Oct) and other reports. The notification is two weeks old and is included because its effective date is close; check NPPA's gazette for the final list.
**Notebook:** GS2 Governance (health, regulation) / GS3 Economy.
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth, Directive Principles (Art 47, public health); Ramesh Singh, pharma sector. Add DPCO 2013 and NLEM to the health-regulation notes, with a cross-link to the Essential Commodities Act.

### 4. Direct mutual-fund plans become the default on AMC digital platforms from 1 November — Economy (Capital markets)
**Exam angle:** SEBI Chairman Tuhin Kanta Pandey said (28 Oct) that Direct Plans will be the default option on AMCs' own digital platforms from 1 Nov. Investors who choose Regular Plans will first be told about their higher expense ratios. This is an investor-protection and "cost-transparency" measure, relevant to RBI Grade B Phase 1 and 2.
**Memorize:**
- Direct Plan: no distributor commission, lower TER, separate and higher NAV than the Regular Plan.
- SEBI: set up in 1988, statutory since the SEBI Act, 1992.
- The disclosure nudge works through "defaults" (behavioural economics), not a ban on Regular Plans.

*Verification note:* the only source is a Cafemutual report of the Chairman's remarks. The implementing circular was not found, so check sebi.gov.in.
**Notebook:** GS3 Economy.
**Static anchor:** Ramesh Singh, Capital Market chapter; Mrunal, SEBI pillar. Add the Direct vs Regular distinction to the mutual-fund notes and link it to nudge theory in Ethics.

### Part B3 — Mains question
**Critically examine whether capping trade margins, as the NPPA has done for 137 anti-cancer drugs, is an effective way to improve access to essential medicines in India. (15 marks, 250 words)**
Model-answer skeleton:
1. *Context:* high out-of-pocket health spending; Para 19 of DPCO 2013 allows margin caps on non-scheduled drugs; 2019 pilot (42 drugs, 526 brands, prices cut up to 90%).
2. *Merits:* quick price relief, targets the distribution chain instead of the manufacturer, and fits Art 47 and the right to health under Art 21.
3. *Limits:* it does not touch manufacturer pricing, risks supply withdrawal or switching to unlisted brands, depends on enforcement, and the list is only periodically updated.
4. *Way forward:* expand NLEM-based price control, strengthen Jan Aushadhi and bulk procurement, apply trade-margin rationalisation to more drugs, and publish price-monitoring data.

## Answers

**Q1. (c)** — 1 and 3 are correct. Statement 2 is wrong: the G20 has no permanent secretariat; continuity is kept by the troika (previous, current and next presidencies). The AU was admitted as a permanent member at the 2023 New Delhi Summit under India's presidency.
**Q2. (a)** — 1 and 2 are correct. Gross GST was about ₹2.31 lakh crore (+22.9%); import revenue rose 42.2% against 15.8% for domestic. Net = gross − refunds (about ₹2.03 lakh crore). Statement 3 is wrong: refunds rose only 6.5% (₹27,934 crore vs ₹26,225 crore).
**Q3. (d)** — 1 and 3 are correct. The 137 drugs are NON-scheduled drugs, so statement 2 is wrong: Para 19 lets the government cap margins on non-scheduled drugs in public interest. The cap takes effect from 9 November 2026; a 2019 pilot covered 42 drugs. NPPA is an attached office of the Department of Pharmaceuticals.
**Q4. (b)** — 1 and 3 are correct. Statement 2 is wrong: the two plans run separate NAVs because their expense ratios differ, so Direct gives higher returns over time. SEBI was set up administratively in 1988 and given statutory status by the SEBI Act, 1992.
**Q5. (c)** — 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: the four sites (Prayagraj, Haridwar, Ujjain, Nashik) rotate, so a Kumbh falls at some site roughly every three years, but each site gets its Kumbh once in 12 years (Nashik's last was in 2015).
**Q6. (d)** — Pairs 1 and 2 are correct. Haridwar's Kumbh is on the Ganga (Har ki Pauri), not the Yamuna. Prayagraj is at the Ganga–Yamuna–(mythical) Saraswati confluence.
**Q7. (b)** — 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is wrong: decisions need at least THREE-FOURTHS of the weighted votes of members present and voting (Centre has one-third weight, all States together two-thirds).

**Mains skeleton:** see Part B3 above.
