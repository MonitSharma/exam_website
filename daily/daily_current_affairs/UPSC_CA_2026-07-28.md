## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Supreme Court stays coercive action in anti-NEET protest FIRs; signals a nationwide protest-policing protocol — Polity & Governance

**Headline.** A Bench led by CJI Surya Kant on 28 July ordered that no coercive action be taken against protesters named in FIRs arising from the anti-NEET-paper-leak demonstrations, directed the immediate release of all detainees below 18 with no criminal antecedents, and indicated it may frame **stronger nationwide guidelines** on police response to protests — remarking that its 2018 directions must now "be stronger."

**Exam angle.**

- This is the cleanest live vehicle in years for the **Right to Protest** — not an enumerated right, but read into **Art. 19(1)(a)** (speech and expression) and **Art. 19(1)(b)** (assemble peaceably and without arms), subject to reasonable restrictions under Art. 19(2) and 19(3).
- Doubles as a **juvenile justice** and **procedural-safeguards** question: detention of 16–18-year-olds beyond 24 hours without production before a magistrate touches Art. 22(2) and the JJ Act, 2015.
- The evidence-preservation order (CCTV, drone, body-cam, wireless-camera footage) and the plea against using **facial recognition technology** to identify protesters open a privacy/surveillance sub-thread — a Mains-ready overlap with *Puttaswamy*.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Bench: **CJI Surya Kant**. Interim orders: (i) no coercive action on the FIRs; (ii) release of minors without criminal antecedents; (iii) preservation of all surveillance material; (iv) an independent, court-supervised inquiry into **both** police excesses and attacks on police.
- Notices issued to the **Union** + **Delhi, Maharashtra, Bihar, Assam, West Bengal, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh**; Advocates General to appear virtually.
- Centre's submission (SG Tushar Mehta): **200+ police personnel injured**; no objection to an independent probe.
- Petitioners' claim: **140+ still detained in Bihar**, most aged 16–18, including one 13-year-old.
- The "2018 directions" the CJI referred to = ***Mazdoor Kisan Shakti Sangathan v. Union of India* (2018)** — designated protest sites, buffer zones, no weapons/lathis at demonstrations.

**Notebook.** GS2 — Polity (Fundamental Rights) / Governance (police accountability).

**Static anchor.** *Laxmikanth*, Ch. 7 "Fundamental Rights" — under Art. 19(1)(a)/(b). **Update:** build a one-page case ladder — *Ramlila Maidan Incident v. Home Secretary* (2012) → *Anita Thakur v. State of J&K* (2016, excessive force) → *MKSS v. UoI* (2018) → *Amit Sahni v. Commissioner of Police* (2020, Shaheen Bagh: no indefinite occupation of public ways) → this 2026 order. Cross-link to Ch. on Judiciary (PIL/continuing mandamus) and to the JJ Act notes in GS2 Vulnerable Sections.

### 2. NSE launches India's first natural gas futures linked to a **domestic** benchmark — Economy

**Headline.** Trading in **'Indian Natural Gas Futures'** (symbol **NATGASIND**) began on the National Stock Exchange on **27 July 2026** — India's first exchange-traded energy derivative priced off an Indian benchmark rather than Henry Hub or NBP.

**Exam angle.**

- Textbook **price-discovery / hedging** case study: Indian gas buyers have so far had to hedge against US or European benchmarks, importing basis risk that has nothing to do with Indian supply-demand. A domestic benchmark reduces that mismatch.
- Ties directly to India's stated goal of raising gas to **15% of the primary energy mix by 2030** (from roughly 6–7%) — a target that needs a liquid, transparent domestic gas market to work.
- Also a clean **regulatory-architecture** question: SEBI regulates commodity derivatives only because the **Forward Markets Commission was merged into SEBI on 28 September 2015**, the FCRA 1952 was repealed, and commodity derivatives moved under the **SCRA, 1956**.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Symbol **NATGASIND**; approved by **SEBI**; traded in NSE's **Commodity Derivatives Segment**; cleared by **NSE Clearing**.
- Underlying: **Indian Gas Exchange (IGX) Gujarat (Dahej) hub** price, quoted in **₹ per mmBtu**.
- **Cash-settled** (no physical delivery). Trading unit **250 mmBtu**. Final settlement = **monthly weighted-average price of actual IGX deliveries**.
- IGX is India's first automated national gas exchange, regulated by **PNGRB**; NSE founded 1992, HQ Mumbai.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Economy (financial markets) with an Infrastructure/Energy tag.

**Static anchor.** *Mrunal* Pillar 1 — Capital Markets → Derivatives & Commodity Exchanges. **Update:** add a three-line box "Who regulates what": SEBI (securities + commodity derivatives, post-2015), PNGRB (gas pipelines & marketing/IGX), CERC (power exchanges). Cross-link to the Energy chapter for the 15%-gas-share target.

### 3. "Made in Bharat chips for our rockets" — indigenous semiconductors headed into Indian launch vehicles — Science & Technology

**Headline.** IT & Electronics Minister **Ashwini Vaishnaw** announced on **27 July 2026** that India will soon fly **domestically fabricated semiconductors** on its space launch vehicles — the announcement following his meeting with the **Skyroot Aerospace** leadership after **Vikram-1 Test Flight-1 reached orbit**, the maiden success of India's first privately developed orbital-class rocket.

*What is new since 14 July:* Vikram-1 has now actually **reached orbit** (it was only on the pad then), and the space-grade chip supply chain is the new policy hook.

**Exam angle.**

- The space-grade chip story is not a press-release item: the **Vikram 3201**, India's first fully indigenous **32-bit** space-grade microprocessor, was designed by ISRO's **Semi-Conductor Laboratory (SCL), Chandigarh** with **VSSC**, and is the successor to the **16-bit Vikram 1601** that has flown ISRO rockets since 2009.
- Strategic-autonomy framing for Mains: rad-hardened, space-qualified chips are export-controlled globally; indigenising them removes a chokepoint that no amount of launch capability can compensate for.
- Links the **India Semiconductor Mission** to the **private space sector** (IN-SPACe / NSIL demand aggregation) — a good "policy convergence" line.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **Vikram 3201** — 32-bit, fully indigenous, space-grade; predecessor **Vikram 1601** (16-bit, in service since 2009). Designers: **SCL Chandigarh + VSSC**.
- **India Semiconductor Mission**: originally a **₹76,000 crore** outlay; as of mid-July 2026, **12 projects approved** with cumulative committed investment of **over ₹1.60 lakh crore**. (Semicon 2.0, ₹1,27,500 crore, was cleared separately in July 2026.)
- **Skyroot Aerospace** — Hyderabad; **Vikram-1** is India's first privately built orbital-class launch vehicle; its 2022 suborbital demonstrator was **Vikram-S** (Mission *Prarambh*).

**Notebook.** GS3 — Science & Technology (Space + Electronics/Semiconductors).

**Static anchor.** Sci-Tech notes → "Space Sector Reforms" and "Semiconductors" modules. **Update:** append a Vikram-family line (Vikram-S 2022 suborbital → Vikram-1 2026 orbital → Vikram 1601/3201 processors — same name, different things; UPSC loves that kind of trap) and cross-link to IN-SPACe / Indian Space Policy 2023.

### 4. *Caridina sharmai* — a new freshwater shrimp described from the Gomti at Lucknow — Environment

**Headline.** Researchers have described **_Caridina sharmai_**, a new species of atyid freshwater shrimp, from the **upper reaches of the River Gomti** in the Lucknow region of Uttar Pradesh — the first record of this genus from the stretch.

**Exam angle.**

- Prelims value is in the **habitat**, not the animal: the Gomti is an **alluvial, wholly rain- and groundwater-fed tributary of the Ganga** — no glacial source. A new endemic-looking invertebrate here is evidence that heavily urbanised Indo-Gangetic rivers still hold undescribed biodiversity, which strengthens the case against writing them off as "dead."
- *Caridina* species are **detritivores/grazers** low in the food web and highly sensitive to sediment, oxygen and pollutant load — i.e., classic **bioindicators** of freshwater health. That is the Mains line.
- Feeds the recurring "new species discovered in India" Prelims set — pair it with the Western Ghats *Allographa* lichens (23 July) and the Assam subterranean fish *Gitchak nakana* (19 July) in one revision box.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **_Caridina sharmai_** — family **Atyidae**, order Decapoda. Size **15–24 mm**. Short, knife-shaped **rostrum** with **15–22 dorsal teeth and 4–9 ventral teeth**; **no median spine on the telson**; deep excavation on the joints of the front legs.
- Wild colouration dark brown/black with yellowish-grey stripes; **turns nearly transparent in aquarium conditions**.
- Closest relatives: *Caridina jalihali*, *Caridina mathiassi*.
- **River Gomti** — rises at **Gomat Taal (Fulhaar Jheel), Pilibhit district, UP**; ~900 km; flows past Lucknow and Jaunpur; joins the **Ganga near Kaithi/Saidpur, Ghazipur district**. Entirely non-glacial.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Environment (Biodiversity) with a GS1 Geography (river systems) tag.

**Static anchor.** *Shankar IAS Environment*, Ch. "Biodiversity" → freshwater/inland-water ecosystems; and the Zoological Survey of India entry under Environmental Organisations. **Update:** start a running "New Species 2026" table (name — group — state — river/forest — what it indicates) and cross-link the Gomti entry to the NCERT/GC Leong Ganga-tributaries map work.

### 5. Coast Guard gets a gender-neutral officer-induction framework and a 'presumed dead' ex-gratia route — Internal Security

**Headline.** Raksha Mantri **Rajnath Singh** on **28 July 2026** unveiled two policy reforms for the **Indian Coast Guard** at Kartavya Bhavan-2, New Delhi: a **gender-neutral induction framework** opening both Short Service and Permanent Appointments to women, and a streamlined **ex-gratia** procedure for families of personnel **reported missing at sea on duty**, enabled by a 'presumed dead' declaration.

**Exam angle.**

- Continues the judicially driven arc on **women in the armed forces** — *Babita Puniya* (2020, Army PC for SSC women officers), *Annie Nagaraja* (2020, Navy), the NDA admission order (2021) — but here the change is **executive and pre-emptive**, not a response to litigation. That contrast is the essay/Mains point.
- Equality of opportunity from the **ab-initio training stage** is the operative phrase: it targets the pipeline, not just the promotion board, which is where earlier reforms stalled.
- The ex-gratia reform is an under-appreciated **administrative-law** angle: absent a body, families previously waited out the seven-year civil-law presumption of death before benefits crystallised. A service-specific 'presumed dead' declaration collapses that delay.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Announced at **Kartavya Bhavan-2**; present: Defence Secretary **Rajesh Kumar Singh**, DG ICG **Paramesh Sivamani**. Framed as a run-up to the ICG's **Golden Jubilee in 2027**.
- Reform 1: **gender-neutral** induction into **Short Service Appointment (SSA)** and **Permanent Appointment (PA)**; existing PA women officers to be considered for higher promotions subject to eligibility.
- Reform 2: **'presumed dead'** declaration to unlock ex-gratia for kin of those missing at sea on duty.
- **Indian Coast Guard** — the fourth armed force of the Union; formally constituted under the **Coast Guard Act, 1978** (18 August 1978), after an interim raising on **1 February 1977**; functions under the **Ministry of Defence**; motto ***Vayam Rakshamah*** ("We Protect"); it is the **lead agency for coastal security in territorial waters** (including areas under State jurisdiction) post-26/11.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Internal Security (coastal security, forces) with a GS1 Society (gender) tag.

**Static anchor.** Internal Security notes → "Coastal Security Architecture" (post-26/11 three-tier: Marine Police – ICG – Navy; NC3I/IMAC, Sagar Prahari Bal). **Update:** add a one-line "Women in Uniform" timeline card (Babita Puniya 2020 → Annie Nagaraja 2020 → NDA 2021 → Agnipath women intake → ICG gender-neutral induction 2026) usable in GS1 Society, GS2 Governance and Essay alike.

### C. Mains-Style Question

**GS2 — Polity (250 words, 15 marks)**

> *"The right to protest is not a licence for disorder, nor is public order a licence for coercion."* **Examine** the constitutional basis of the right to peaceful protest in India and **evaluate** whether uniform national guidelines on police conduct during demonstrations would strengthen or dilute this right.

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points).**

1. **Locate the right.** Not enumerated; read into Art. 19(1)(a) + 19(1)(b), subject to reasonable restrictions under Art. 19(2)/(3) for sovereignty, public order and incitement to offence. Add Art. 21 (dignity, bodily integrity of protesters) and Art. 22(2) (production within 24 hours) as the procedural floor.
2. **Trace the judicial arc.** *Ramlila Maidan* (2012) — midnight police action on sleeping protesters held unconstitutional; *Anita Thakur* (2016) — excessive force attracts compensation; *MKSS v. UoI* (2018) — designated sites, buffer zones, balancing test; *Amit Sahni* (2020) — no indefinite occupation of public thoroughfares. The 2026 order's contribution: an evidence-preservation regime and a court-supervised, **two-sided** inquiry.
3. **Case FOR uniform guidelines.** Ends the arbitrariness of state-varying SOPs; makes the graduated-force ladder (warning → water cannon → tear gas → lathi-charge) legally enforceable rather than departmental; body-cam and drone-footage retention creates accountability that survives the news cycle; protects police too, by documenting attacks on personnel (200+ injured here).
4. **Case AGAINST / caveats.** 'Police' and 'public order' are **State List** entries (Entries 1 and 2, List II) — a national protocol risks federal friction and must come as guidelines under Art. 141/142 rather than as a template statute; over-codification may legitimise a *minimum* level of force; facial-recognition and biometric collection at protests can chill assembly even where force is not used. **Conclusion:** guidelines strengthen the right only if paired with independent oversight and a bar on surveillance-based post-facto targeting — i.e. accountability, not merely procedure.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (a).** Statements 1 and 2 match the Court's interim directions. Statement 3 is wrong: the right to protest is **not expressly enumerated**; it is judicially read into Art. 19(1)(a) and 19(1)(b).

**Q2 — (b).** The contract is India's first energy derivative on a domestic benchmark (1) and references the **IGX Gujarat/Dahej hub** price (3). Statement 2 is wrong — it is **cash-settled**, with no physical delivery.

**Q3 — (d).** All three are correct: Vikram 3201 is a 32-bit space-grade processor from **SCL Chandigarh + VSSC**, succeeding the 16-bit **Vikram 1601** used in ISRO rockets since 2009.

**Q4 — (b).** *Caridina sharmai* is an atyid **freshwater shrimp** (15–24 mm) described from the upper reaches of the **River Gomti** near Lucknow, UP.

**Q5 — (a).** The two reforms are the gender-neutral induction framework (1) and the 'presumed dead' ex-gratia route (2). Statement 3 is wrong — the ICG continues to function under the **Ministry of Defence**.

**Q6 — (a).** FMC merged into SEBI on **28 September 2015** (1); FCRA 1952 was repealed and commodity derivatives moved under the **SCRA, 1956** (2). Statement 3 is wrong — the FMC was set up in **1953** under the Ministry of **Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution**, and moved to the Ministry of Finance only in 2013, shortly before the merger.

**Q7 — (b).** The Gomti rises at **Gomat Taal (Fulhaar Jheel) in Pilibhit district** — a marsh, **not a glacier** — so statement 1 is wrong. It is a **left-bank tributary** joining the Ganga in **Ghazipur district**, and both **Lucknow and Jaunpur** stand on it.
