## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Supreme Court tells Karnataka to comply with CWMA directions on Cauvery releases — Polity & Governance

**Headline:** On 17 August 2026 a Bench of Justices Vikram Nath and Sandeep Mehta directed Karnataka to ensure compliance with the Cauvery Water Management Authority's (CWMA) directions on releasing water to Tamil Nadu, called for a status report from the Authority, and posted Tamil Nadu's application to 24 August.

**Exam angle:**

- This is the cleanest live illustration of the *gap between adjudication and enforcement* in inter-State river water disputes — the exact fault line UPSC has asked about in Mains. The tribunal award exists, the implementing body exists, and yet the Court is still the enforcement backstop.
- Note the constitutional oddity: Article 262(2) permits Parliament to bar the jurisdiction of *all* courts including the Supreme Court in water disputes, yet the SC continues to hear these matters — via Article 136 special leave against tribunal awards, and via petitions on *implementation* rather than on the merits of the award.
- Also a Federalism/Centre-State relations hook: CWMA is a Central Government creation acting on States, which States resist as an encroachment on a State List subject (Entry 17, List II) qualified by Entry 56, List I.

**Key facts to memorise:**

- **Article 262** — Parliament may provide for adjudication of inter-State river/river-valley water disputes and may bar court jurisdiction. Enacted through the **Inter-State River Water Disputes (ISRWD) Act, 1956** (and the River Boards Act, 1956).
- **Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal** — constituted **1990**, final award **5 February 2007**.
- **Supreme Court judgment, 16 February 2018** — modified the award (Karnataka's share raised to 284.75 TMC, Tamil Nadu's reduced to 404.25 TMC) and directed the Centre to frame a scheme.
- **Cauvery Water Management Scheme, 2018** — framed under **Section 6A of the ISRWD Act, 1956**; created the **CWMA** (the authority) and the **Cauvery Water Regulation Committee (CWRC)**, which functions *under* the CWMA.
- **Biligundlu** — the inter-State measuring point at which releases from Karnataka to Tamil Nadu are gauged.
- Sequence in this dispute: **CWRC's 139th meeting (28 July 2026)** directed 3,500 cusecs at Biligundlu for 29 July–12 August; CWRC then directed **12,000 cusecs/day for 15 days from 12 August**, upheld by CWMA. Karnataka told the Court flows had crossed 12,000 cusecs on the morning of 17 August after catchment rainfall.
- ISRWD **Amendment Act, 2002**: tribunal to be constituted within **1 year** of receipt of request; award within **3 years** (extendable by 2). The **2019 Amendment Bill** (single standing tribunal + Dispute Resolution Committee) has *not* become law — do not assume it has.

**Notebook:** GS2 — Polity (Inter-State Relations) / Federalism.

**Static anchor:** **Laxmikanth, Ch. 16 "Inter-State Relations"** — the section on inter-State water disputes. *Update:* add a margin box distinguishing **CWMA vs CWRC vs Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal**, and cross-link to the **Krishna** and **Mahadayi** tribunals plus **Article 262 vs Article 131 vs Article 136** — UPSC's favourite confusion set. Also cross-link to your Geography note on the **Cauvery basin** (Talakaveri, Krishnarajasagar, Mettur, Kabini/Bhavani/Amaravati tributaries).

### 2. FAST-DS comes into force — a one-time window to disclose small foreign assets — Economy

**Headline:** The **Foreign Assets of Small Taxpayers – Disclosure Scheme (FAST-DS)**, contained in **Chapter IV of the Finance Act, 2026**, came into force on **16 August 2026**; the CBDT has notified the FAST-DS Rules, 2026 and the window closes on **31 December 2026**.

**Exam angle:**

- A textbook "voluntary disclosure scheme" — the same policy family as VDIS 1997, the Income Declaration Scheme 2016 and PMGKY 2016. Expect a Prelims question on *what it is* and a Mains angle on the **moral hazard of amnesty schemes** (they raise revenue and widen the base, but arguably penalise the honest taxpayer and invite expectations of the next amnesty).
- The framing matters: this is aimed at *inadvertent* non-reporting — Schedule FA omissions by people whose residential status changed, ESOPs in foreign parents, small overseas bank accounts — not at large-scale evasion. It is a compliance-facilitation measure riding on the back of **CRS/FATCA automatic exchange of information**, which is what makes such assets visible to the department in the first place.

**Key facts to memorise:**

- **Category 1** — undisclosed foreign asset/income *never offered to tax*: aggregate value must **not exceed ₹1 crore**. Levy = **30% tax + 30% additional amount in lieu of penalty = 60%** effective.
- **Category 2** — foreign assets already offered to tax, or acquired while the taxpayer was a **non-resident**, but not reported in the relevant return schedule: threshold **₹5 crore**; flat **fee of ₹1 lakh**.
- Valid declarants get **immunity from penalty and prosecution under the Black Money (Undisclosed Foreign Income and Assets) and Imposition of Tax Act, 2015**.
- "Asset" is broad: immovable property, jewellery, artistic work, shares, securities, any other asset or income.
- Baseline for contrast: the **Black Money Act, 2015** ordinarily charges **30% flat tax + 90% penalty (i.e. 3x)** on undisclosed foreign income/assets, with rigorous imprisonment up to 7 years (10 for wilful attempt to evade).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Public Finance / Black Money & Tax Administration).

**Static anchor:** **Mrunal Pillar 2 — Budget & Taxation**, sub-topic *Black Money & Tax Evasion*. *Update:* add FAST-DS to your amnesty-scheme timeline (VDIS 1997 → IDS 2016 → PMGKY 2016 → **FAST-DS 2026**), and cross-link to **FATCA/CRS**, **DTAA & Article 26 exchange of information**, **GAAR**, and the **PMLA/FEMA distinction** (FEMA = civil, PMLA = criminal).

### 3. CAG performance audit: Green India Mission missed its forest-cover target by 97.57% — Environment

**Headline:** A CAG performance audit tabled this month found the **National Mission for a Green India (GIM)** achieved **0.03409 million hectares** of forest/tree cover against a target of **1.4 million hectares** for the period audited — a **97.57% shortfall** — citing inadequate planning, weak inter-agency coordination and chronic under-funding.

**Exam angle:**

- This is the sharpest available critique of India's **carbon sink** commitment. India's NDC promises an additional sink of **2.5–3 billion tonnes CO₂-equivalent by 2030** through forest and tree cover; GIM is the principal instrument. An audit finding of a 97.57% shortfall converts a scheme-fact into a *Mains argument*.
- Second-order point worth quoting: the CAG used **GIS-based verification** and found roughly **70% of sampled locations showed no discernible change** attributable to GIM, and that **KML files** used for validation were unverified. Remote-sensing verification of afforestation claims is itself an emerging governance theme (relevant to the "tree cover vs forest cover vs plantation" debate in the ISFR).
- Also a Polity crossover: this is a **performance audit** by the CAG (Articles 148–151), and audit paragraphs of this kind go to the **Public Accounts Committee** — a good example of parliamentary financial control over the executive.
- *One line for the radar:* India is hosting the **12th BRICS Environment Ministers' Meeting at Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi on 18 August 2026** (senior officials' Environment Working Group and Contact Group on Climate Change met on 17 August), chaired by Bhupender Yadav under India's 2026 chairship theme *"Building for Resilience, Innovation, Cooperation and Sustainability"* — afforestation and sustainable lifestyles are among the priority areas, which sits awkwardly beside this audit.

**Key facts to memorise:**

- **GIM**: launched **2014** as a **Centrally Sponsored Scheme**; one of the **eight missions of the National Action Plan on Climate Change (NAPCC, 2008)**; nodal ministry **MoEFCC**.
- Original targets: increase forest/tree cover on **5 million ha** and improve quality of cover on another **5 million ha**; enhanced annual CO₂ sequestration of **50–60 million tonnes**.
- Audit findings: only **₹1,149.14 crore (47.88%)** received as budgetary support over 2015-16 to 2024-25 against a **₹2,000 crore** CCEA outlay approved for the initial four years; **8 States/UTs** maintained no annual accounts; audit covered **16 States/UTs**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Climate Change & Institutions); GS2 crossover — CAG/PAC.

**Static anchor:** **Shankar IAS Environment, "Climate Change" chapter — NAPCC and the eight missions**. *Update:* write the CAG numbers directly against the GIM entry so the mission never gets recalled as a success story. Cross-link to **Laxmikanth Ch. 60 (CAG)** and **Ch. 22 (Parliamentary Committees — PAC)**, and to the **India State of Forest Report** definitional debate (forest cover ≥ 1 ha with ≥10% canopy density, *irrespective of ownership or legal status* — which is why plantations inflate the number).

### 4. SraVaani — India's first speech-recognition model built for 65 Indian languages and dialects — Science & Technology

**Headline:** IISc's **SPIRE Lab**, with **ARTPARK** and **Google**, has released **SraVaani-1.0**, a multilingual automatic speech recognition (ASR) model covering **20 scheduled languages and 45 regional languages and dialects**, released openly on Hugging Face under an MIT licence.

**Exam angle:**

- Sci-Tech items about AI are usually un-askable; this one is askable because it is *Indian, named, and countable*. Prelims loves "what is X" for indigenous models and platforms (cf. BharatGen, Bhashini, AIKosh).
- The deeper theme is **digital inclusion and linguistic equity**: most ASR systems support only a handful of high-resource Indian languages, which silently excludes speakers of non-scheduled languages from voice-based e-governance, banking and health services. This is your Mains line on technology and the **Article 350 / Eighth Schedule** question of official recognition versus actual service delivery.
- Pairs naturally with the **IndiaAI Mission** (AIKosh datasets, IndiaAI Compute) and **Bhashini/National Language Translation Mission** in a GS3 answer on India's AI stack.

**Key facts to memorise:**

- Developed by **SPIRE Lab (Speech Processing, Interpreting and Recognition Engineering), IISc Bengaluru** + **ARTPARK** + **Google**; released **13 August 2026**, reported 16 August.
- Coverage: **65 Indian languages and dialects** (20 scheduled + 45 regional), **10 scripts**, with **automatic language identification** — the user does not have to select a language first.
- Languages newly served include **Garo, Angika, Chakma, Kokborok, Tulu, Bundeli, Bajjika**.
- Architecture: **FastConformer**-based ASR, trained through a three-stage pipeline on roughly **31,270 hours** of transcribed speech.
- Built on **Project Vaani** — a four-year IISc–Google effort with **31,000+ hours** of speech from **1,56,000+ speakers** across **165 districts** in 31 States/UTs.
- Remember the constitutional baseline: the **Eighth Schedule** lists **22 languages**; Articles **344(1)** and **351**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (AI / Digital India); GS1 crossover — Society (linguistic diversity).

**Static anchor:** Your **Sci-Tech "AI & Digital Public Infrastructure" note**. *Update:* create a single comparison line — **Bhashini** (translation DPI, MeitY) vs **BharatGen** (indigenous LLM) vs **AIKosh** (dataset repository under IndiaAI Mission) vs **SraVaani** (open ASR model). Cross-link to **Laxmikanth, Ch. on Official Language (Articles 343–351) and the Eighth Schedule**.

### 5. Places in News: the Subansiri rises without rain — Geography

**Headline:** The **Subansiri** in Arunachal Pradesh's **Upper Subansiri district** swelled to an unusually high and forceful level in mid-August 2026 **despite no rainfall being reported locally**, with timber seen in the flow — a pattern that points to something happening in the upstream, trans-boundary catchment.

**Exam angle:**

- "Rise without local rain" is the signature of an **upstream event**: a landslide-dammed lake breaching, a **GLOF**, or a sudden release/impoundment in the Tibetan reach. It is the standard hook for the **trans-boundary river data-sharing** problem with China — the same anxiety that attaches to the Yarlung Tsangpo/Siang.
- Ideal Prelims map-and-fact item, and a ready GS1 Geography + GS2 IR crossover: **hydrological data sharing MoUs**, absence of a comprehensive India–China water treaty, and the **Brahmaputra north-bank tributary** system.
- Also connects to the **Lower Subansiri HEP** and the long-running NGT/expert-committee litigation over downstream Assam's dam-safety and flood concerns.

**Key facts to memorise:**

- **Largest tributary of the Brahmaputra**; a **right-bank (north-bank)** tributary.
- **Trans-Himalayan and antecedent** — it predates the rise of the Himalayas and cut through as they rose (same class as the Indus, Sutlej, Brahmaputra/Siang).
- Rises on the **Tibetan Plateau** from the confluence of **Lokong Chu, Chayal Chu and Tsari Chu**; flows ~170 km east in Tibet, turns south and enters India near **Taksing**, Arunachal Pradesh; carves gorges through the **Miri Hills**; descends to the Assam valley at **Dulangmukh (Dhemaji district)**; passes near **Majuli** and joins the Brahmaputra at **Jamurighat, Lakhimpur district, Assam**.
- Length **~442 km**; basin **~32,000 sq km**; called the **"Gold River"** for gold dust in its waters.
- Tributaries: **Kamla, Ranganadi, Dikrong, Jiyadhol, Tsari, Yume, Nye, Laro**.
- **Lower Subansiri Hydroelectric Project** — **2,000 MW**, run-of-the-river, at **Gerukamukh** on the Arunachal–Assam border; will be **India's largest hydroelectric plant** on completion; developed by **NHPC**.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Geography (Drainage Systems / Places in News).

**Static anchor:** **NCERT Class XI, *India: Physical Environment*, Ch. 3 "Drainage System"** — the Brahmaputra system. *Update:* mark **Subansiri, Kameng, Manas, Sankosh** (north-bank) versus **Burhi Dihing, Dhansiri, Kopili** (south-bank) on your Brahmaputra sketch, and cross-link to your GS2 IR note on **India–China trans-boundary rivers** and the **Siang/Yarlung Tsangpo mega-dam** file.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Environment, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **"India's commitment to create an additional carbon sink of 2.5–3 billion tonnes of CO₂-equivalent by 2030 rests on institutional foundations that the CAG has found to be weak. Critically examine, with reference to the National Mission for a Green India."**

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the commitment precisely.** The additional-sink target is one of the quantified elements of India's NDC under the Paris Agreement, alongside the emissions-intensity and non-fossil-capacity targets. GIM, one of the eight NAPCC missions, is its principal delivery vehicle — so mission performance *is* NDC performance on this parameter.

2. **Establish the audit finding, with numbers.** 0.03409 million ha achieved against 1.4 million ha (a 97.57% shortfall); only ₹1,149.14 crore (47.88%) of the approved initial outlay actually released; eight States/UTs without annual accounts; GIS verification showing ~70% of sampled sites with no discernible attributable change. Diagnose the causes the audit identifies — planning, coordination, funding — rather than merely listing the numbers.

3. **Argue the structural, not merely administrative, reasons.** Afforestation is a Concurrent List subject executed by State forest departments with weak convergence (MGNREGS/VB-G RAM G, CAMPA, Nagar Van); survival-rate monitoring is thin; "tree cover" accounting under the ISFR definition allows plantations and orchards to substitute for ecologically functional forest; and community institutions under the Forest Rights Act (Gram Sabha/CFR rights) are under-used as implementers despite being the most durable protectors of standing forest.

4. **Balance and conclude with the reform path.** Acknowledge genuine gains — CAMPA corpus, expanding non-fossil capacity, LiFE/Ek Ped Maa Ke Naam mobilisation — then argue for: outcome-based release of funds tied to third-party-verified survival rates; mandatory geo-tagged, publicly auditable plantation data (fixing the unverified-KML problem); convergence of GIM with CAMPA and rural employment programmes; and a shift of metric from *area planted* to *tonnes sequestered and canopy retained*. Close on the credibility argument: a sink target that cannot be independently verified weakens India's negotiating position on climate finance and transparency at the UNFCCC.

### D. PYQ Mirror

**Theme 1 — Inter-State water disputes (Item 1).**

*UPSC Mains 2013, GS Paper II:* "Constitutional mechanisms to resolve the inter-state water disputes have failed to address and solve the problems. Is the failure due to structural or process inadequacy or both? Discuss." **(Verified.)**

The Cauvery order of 17 August is a live example for exactly this answer: adjudication is complete (1990 tribunal, 2007 award, 2018 SC modification) and an implementation body exists (CWMA/CWRC, 2018) — yet compliance still requires repeated judicial intervention. That is *process* inadequacy layered on *structural* inadequacy. Note also that the 2019 Bill proposing a single standing tribunal remains unenacted.

**Theme 2 — Black money and cross-border financial opacity (Item 2).**

*UPSC Mains 2021, GS Paper III (Q9, 150 words, 10 marks):* "Discuss how emerging technologies and globalisation contribute to money laundering. Elaborate measures to tackle the problem of money laundering both at national and international levels." **(Verified.)**

FAST-DS supplies fresh material for the "national measures" half: the Black Money Act 2015 as the deterrent leg, CRS/FATCA automatic exchange as the detection leg, and a calibrated disclosure window as the compliance leg — plus the counter-argument that amnesties dilute deterrence.
