## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Nepal confers the honorary rank of General on Indian Army Chief Gen Dhiraj Seth — International Relations

**Headline:** During a three-day visit to Nepal (17–19 August 2026), COAS Gen Dhiraj Seth was conferred the honorary rank of General of the Nepali Army by President Ramchandra Paudel at Sheetal Niwas, Kathmandu, on the invitation of Nepal Army chief Gen Ashok Raj Sigdel.

**Exam angle:** This is the single cleanest illustration of "civilisational + institutional" ties in the Neighbourhood First policy — a reciprocal ceremonial practice with no operational command attached, running unbroken since 1950 even through the Kalapani/Lipulekh map dispute, the 2015 blockade and Nepal's China tilt. Use it as the "continuity anchor" line in any India–Nepal answer: military-to-military ties survive political turbulence. Pairs naturally with the Gorkha regiments and the Agnipath-related recruitment pause.

**Key facts to memorise:**

- Tradition began in **1950**, when Gen **K.M. Cariappa** was made an Honorary General of the Nepal Army; reciprocated the same year when Nepal's Gen **Kiran Shumsher Jung Bahadur Rana** was made Honorary General of the Indian Army.
- Its political frame is the **India–Nepal Treaty of Peace and Friendship, July 1950**.
- The rank is **purely ceremonial** — it confers no command or authority over the other country's army.
- Gen Seth laid a wreath at the **Army Pavilion, Tundikhel**, Kathmandu, and was briefed by Nepal's DGMO.

**Notebook:** GS2 — International Relations (India & its Neighbourhood).

**Static anchor:** Your India–Nepal file under Neighbourhood First. **Add a cross-link** from the 1950 Treaty entry to this honorary-rank tradition, and tag the two irritants beside it (Kalapani–Lipulekh–Limpiyadhura in Nepal's 2020 map; the stalled Eminent Persons Group report) so the "warm ties / open disputes" contrast sits on one page.

### 2. Reliance Industries and Rolls-Royce declare intent to build a sovereign engine for the AMCA — Science & Technology

**Headline:** On 14 August 2026 RIL and Rolls-Royce announced a strategic intent to partner on the design, development, manufacture and delivery of an indigenous combat engine for India's Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft programme, including a dedicated **Aerospace Gas Turbine Complex** in India as a centre of excellence for power and propulsion.

**Exam angle:** The jet engine is India's most durable technology-denial story — from the failure of the **GTRE Kaveri** to power the Tejas, to today's dependence on GE F404/F414. What is new here is the entry of a *private* Indian prime into the hot-section end of aero-propulsion, and the framing of "sovereign" rather than merely "Make in India" (i.e., full IP and design authority, not licensed assembly). Note the caveat: this is **not** an award — the Government has not yet chosen the foreign technology partner, and a rival **Safran–GTRE** proposal remains under consideration.

**Key facts to memorise:**

- AMCA = India's **fifth-generation** stealth multi-role fighter for the IAF; managed by the **Aeronautical Development Agency (ADA)** under the Department of Defence R&D, Ministry of Defence.
- Required engine: a **clean-sheet ~120 kN** class design, more powerful than the GE engines on Tejas variants, intended to enable **supercruise** (supersonic cruise without afterburner).
- AMCA design features: stealth shaping, internal weapons bay, supercruise, AI-assisted avionics.
- Rolls-Royce is a **UK** firm; the competing bid is from **Safran (France)** with DRDO's **GTRE**.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Science & Technology (indigenisation of technology); cross-file to GS3 Internal Security (defence preparedness).

**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS Sci-Tech, **Defence Technology** chapter. **Update** the "aero-engine" entry with the Kaveri → GE F414 ToT → AMCA sovereign-engine sequence, and cross-link to the DAP-2020 categories (Buy Indian–IDDM) and iDEX in your GS3 defence-production notes.

### 3. Social Justice Ministry details the DNT welfare architecture — the SEED scheme in focus — Society

**Headline:** In a PIB statement this week the Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment set out its measures for the economic empowerment of **De-notified, Nomadic and Semi-Nomadic (DNT/NT/SNT) communities**, delivered through the **Development and Welfare Board for DNCs (DWBDNC)** and its four-component **SEED** scheme.

**Exam angle:** DNTs are the classic "invisible" category — historically criminalised, administratively un-enumerated, and constitutionally homeless (many are scattered across SC, ST and OBC lists, and some appear in none). This is prime GS1 Society and Ethics-case-study material: colonial stigma persisting as contemporary policing practice. The exam-friendly tension: a **non-statutory, non-constitutional Board** (DWBDNC) is asked to serve a population the Renke Commission put at over 10 crore.

**Key facts to memorise:**

- **Criminal Tribes Act, 1871** notified whole communities as "criminal by birth"; repealed in **1952**, when the communities were "de-notified" — hence the name.
- **Renke Commission**: National Commission for DNTs constituted **2005**, chaired by **Balkrishna Sidram Renke**, reported **2008**; estimated the population at ~**10.74 crore** (Census 2001 basis).
- **Idate Commission**: constituted **January 2015** under **Bhiku Ramji Idate**, report submitted **December 2017**; listed **1,200+** DNT/NT/SNT communities.
- **DWBDNC** constituted **February 2019** under the Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment — an administrative body, **not** a constitutional commission (contrast NCST under **Article 338A**).
- **SEED** (Scheme for Economic Empowerment of DNTs) launched **15 February 2022**; outlay **₹200 crore** over five years from 2021-22; four components — **free competitive-exam coaching, health insurance, livelihood support, housing**.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Indian Society (vulnerable sections, social exclusion); GS2 — Governance (welfare schemes for vulnerable sections).

**Static anchor:** NCERT Class XII Sociology, *Social Inequality and Exclusion*, plus your Laxmikanth chapters on the **NCSC/NCST/NCBC** commissions. **Add a cross-link** in the commissions chapter: a "constitutional vs statutory vs executive body" ladder with NCST (Art. 338A) → NCBC (Art. 338B, 102nd Amendment) → **DWBDNC (executive, 2019)** at the bottom. That distinction is exactly the kind of thing Prelims monetises.

### 4. CCEA clears ₹13,041 crore of rail and highway projects — including a four-lane run to the Nepal border — Economy

**Headline:** The Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs approved four railway multi-tracking projects worth about **₹9,450 crore** (adding ~**410 km** across 8 districts of West Bengal, Odisha, Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh) and the four-laning of the **82.578-km Muzaffarpur–Sitamarhi–Sonbarsa stretch of NH-22 in Bihar** at about **₹3,590.73 crore** under the **Hybrid Annuity Mode**.

**Exam angle:** Two exam hooks in one approval. First, the **PPP model question** — HAM is the model UPSC keeps circling because it splits risk: government funds part of construction, the developer takes construction and O&M risk, but **not** traffic/toll risk. Second, the **connectivity-as-diplomacy** hook — NH-22 terminates at **Sonbarsa on the India–Nepal border**, linking to NH-27 (East–West Corridor). Pair this item with Item 1 in the same answer on India–Nepal economic connectivity.

**Key facts to memorise:**

- Total package **₹13,041 crore**; rail ~₹9,450 crore + NH ~₹3,590.73 crore.
- Rail projects: Kharagpur–Bhadrak (Ranital) 4th line **173 km** (~₹3,352 cr); Bhadrak–Haridaspur 4th line **75 km** (~₹1,583 cr); Gummidipundi–Gudur 3rd & 4th lines **90 km** (~₹2,229 cr); Cuttack–Paradeep (Badabandha) 3rd & 4th lines **72 km** (~₹2,286 cr). Target completion **2030-31**.
- **HAM**: the Government typically funds **40%** of project cost during construction in instalments; the remaining **60%** is paid as **annuity** with interest over the concession period; the authority (NHAI) retains toll collection and hence traffic risk.
- Context figure for the macro paragraph: **India Ratings (Ind-Ra)** on 21 August raised its FY27 GDP growth forecast by 10 bps to **6.8%**, projected nominal GDP growth at **10.4%**, CPI ~**4.9%**, WPI **8.5%**, and expects the **CAD to widen to 1.5% of GDP** in FY27 from 0.6% in FY26.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure: roads, railways, PPP).

**Static anchor:** **Mrunal Pillar 5 (Infrastructure)** — the PPP models table. **Update** the HAM row with this NH-22 example and cross-link to **PM GatiShakti National Master Plan** and the **National Infrastructure Pipeline**; also add the BOT-Toll / BOT-Annuity / EPC / HAM / TOT comparison to one page if it is still scattered.

### 5. An indigenous medium agri-drone, NIKA2 Kisan, is unveiled — where India's drone-farming policy stack now stands — Agriculture

**Headline:** Hyderabad-based women-led deep-tech startup **AkinAnalytics** launched the indigenous **NIKA2 Kisan Medium Drone**, unveiled by Union Agriculture and Rural Development Minister **Shivraj Singh Chouhan** in New Delhi.

**Exam angle:** Treat the product as a peg and revise the **policy stack** behind it — this is how UPSC actually asks agri-mechanisation. India's drone-farming push runs on three legs: liberalised **Drone Rules, 2021** (Digital Sky, fewer approvals), demand creation through **Kisan Drone** subsidies and **Namo Drone Didi** (drones to women SHGs, i.e., mechanisation routed through the SHG-federation architecture rather than individual ownership), and supply-side **PLI for drones**. The Mains line: drones convert a lumpy capital good into a *rented service*, which is the only way small and marginal holdings (~86% of Indian farms) can access precision agriculture.

**Key facts to memorise:**

- NIKA2 Kisan: **16-litre** spraying payload, **12-litre** spreading payload; terrain-following, fail-safe systems, **night operation**.
- **Drone Rules, 2021** — Ministry of **Civil Aviation**; **Digital Sky** platform; airspace zoned green/yellow/red.
- **Namo Drone Didi** — Central Sector Scheme, outlay **₹1,261 crore**, **14,500** women SHGs under **DAY-NRLM**, drones at **80% subsidy up to ₹8 lakh**, for rental services (liquid fertiliser and pesticide application).
- **Kisan Drone / SMAM** subsidy: **50% up to ₹5 lakh** for SC/ST, small & marginal, women and NE farmers; **40% up to ₹4 lakh** for others.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Agriculture (mechanisation, technology missions).

**Static anchor:** **Mrunal Pillar 4 (Agriculture)** — farm mechanisation. **Add a cross-link** from the SMAM/Custom Hiring Centre entry to Namo Drone Didi and to your GS2 SHG/DAY-NRLM notes, so the "self-help groups as service-delivery vehicles" theme is retrievable from either side.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS1 — Indian Society, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **"For De-notified, Nomadic and Semi-Nomadic communities, colonial-era criminalisation has outlived the statute that created it." Examine this statement and evaluate the adequacy of India's current institutional response.**

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Frame the legacy.** Criminal Tribes Act, 1871 notified communities as criminal by birth; repealed 1952 but replaced in several states by Habitual Offenders legislation — the label migrated rather than disappeared. Persisting outcomes: routine police suspicion, absence of settled domicile, exclusion from documentation-dependent welfare.
2. **Establish the scale and the classification problem.** Renke Commission (2008) estimated over 10 crore persons; Idate Commission (2017) listed 1,200+ communities. Many are distributed unevenly across SC, ST and OBC lists and some fall outside all three — so entitlement depends on the state one is standing in, not on deprivation.
3. **Assess the institutional response.** DWBDNC (2019) plus SEED (2022, ₹200 crore over five years) covering coaching, health insurance, livelihood and housing. Strengths: a single nodal channel and a documentation drive. Limits: an executive board without the constitutional status of NCST (Art. 338A) or NCBC (Art. 338B); modest outlay against a 10-crore-plus population; nomadism itself makes residence-anchored delivery fail.
4. **Way forward.** Complete a caste-and-community enumeration to end the classification limbo; portable, Aadhaar-linked entitlements that travel with mobile households; review Habitual Offenders Acts; statutory backing for the Board; and school-hostel provision built for mobile families. Close on rights-based framing: the goal is de-stigmatisation, not merely subsidy.

## PART C — ANSWERS & EXPLANATIONS

**Q1 — (b) 1 and 3 only.** The tradition began in 1950 with Gen K.M. Cariappa and was reciprocated for Nepal's Gen Kiran Shumsher Jung Bahadur Rana; it continues for serving chiefs of both armies. Statement 2 is wrong — the rank is purely ceremonial and confers no command or authority.

**Q2 — (b).** AMCA is India's fifth-generation stealth multirole fighter for the IAF, managed by the Aeronautical Development Agency (ADA) under the Department of Defence R&D, Ministry of Defence.

**Q3 — (b).** RIL and Rolls-Royce announced a *strategic intent* to partner; no award has been made. The Government has yet to select a foreign technology partner, and a Safran–GTRE proposal remains under consideration.

**Q4 — (c) 1 and 3 only.** Under HAM the authority funds part of construction (commonly 40%) and pays the balance as annuities with interest. Statement 2 is wrong — HAM's defining feature is that **traffic/toll risk stays with the authority (NHAI)**, not the concessionaire; that is what distinguishes it from BOT-Toll. The NH-22 Muzaffarpur–Sitamarhi–Sonbarsa four-laning was approved under HAM.

**Q5 — (b) 2 only.** SEED's four components are coaching, health insurance, livelihood and housing. Statement 1 is wrong on the ministry: DWBDNC and SEED sit under the **Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment**, not the Ministry of Tribal Affairs.

**Q6 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The Criminal Tribes Act was repealed in 1952; the Idate Commission reported in 2017. Statement 3 is wrong — DWBDNC (2019) is an **executive/administrative body**, unlike the NCST, which is constitutional under Article 338A.

**Q7 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Drone Rules, 2021 are a Ministry of Civil Aviation instrument with the Digital Sky platform; Namo Drone Didi supplies drones to women SHGs under DAY-NRLM for rental services; and SMAM/Kisan Drone subsidy is higher (50%, up to ₹5 lakh) for small and marginal, SC/ST, women and NE farmers than for others (40%, up to ₹4 lakh).
