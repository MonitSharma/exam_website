### 1. BIMCO–ICS Seafarer Workforce Report 2026: India becomes the world's 2nd-largest supplier of seafarers — Reports & Indices

**Headline:** India has climbed to No. 2 globally in seafarer supply with 311,936 seafarers (12.16% of the world workforce), overtaking China and Russia, and now trails only the Philippines.

**Exam angle:**
- This is the classic UPSC "report + rank + ministry + why it matters" package. The report is the maritime sector's benchmark workforce study, jointly produced by **BIMCO** (Baltic and International Maritime Council) and the **ICS** (International Chamber of Shipping) — both *industry* bodies, **not** UN agencies. That distinction is exactly the kind of trap UPSC sets (cf. the recurring "which organisation released X report" question type).
- Substantively, it converts a demographic-dividend argument into a hard number: India's share **more than doubled** from 5.2% (5th place) in 2015 to 12.16% (2nd place) in 2026. Useful as a *service-export / remittance* case study, not just a shipping fact — seafarer wages are a significant invisible earning in India's current account.
- The global picture flips the usual "surplus labour" framing: supply is **2.57 million** against demand of **2.55 million**, i.e. a nominal surplus overall but a **shortfall of qualified officers**. India's officer share (13.41%) exceeds its ratings share (11.29%) — India is moving *up* the skill ladder, not just supplying deck labour.

**Facts/figures to memorise:**
- Total Indian seafarers: **311,936** | global share **12.16%** | rank **2nd** (after the Philippines; ahead of China, Russian Federation, Indonesia)
- Officers: **140,718** (13.41% of global officers) | Ratings: **171,218** (11.29% of global ratings)
- 2015 baseline: **5th** place, **5.2%** share → share more than doubled in a decade
- Global supply **2.57 mn** vs demand **2.55 mn** → officer-category shortage
- Publisher: **BIMCO + ICS** (industry associations). Domestic nodal ministry: **Ministry of Ports, Shipping and Waterways (MoPSW)**; regulator for seafarer training/certification: **Directorate General of Shipping (DG Shipping)**.

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure: Ports & Shipping; Services exports & human capital)

**Static anchor:** **Mrunal Pillar 4 — Infrastructure (Ports, Shipping & Inland Waterways)**, plus the Services/BoP sub-topic under Pillar 3.
*Nudge:* Add a one-line "India's maritime manpower" entry to your Ports & Shipping page and cross-link it to (a) **Maritime Amrit Kaal Vision 2047 / Maritime India Vision 2030** and (b) the **invisibles/services receipts** line in your BoP notes — so a Prelims "seafarer" question and a Mains "services export diversification" question both resolve to the same page.

### 2. ADB approves a USD 850 million loan (Subprogram 2) to scale PM Surya Ghar rooftop solar — Environment (Energy & Climate Finance)

**Headline:** The Asian Development Bank has approved an **$850 million** policy-based loan under Subprogram 2 of its *Accelerating Affordable and Inclusive Rooftop Solar Systems Development Program*, backing **PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana (PMSG-MBY)**.

**Exam angle:**
- The high-value angle is **climate finance architecture**, not the scheme itself. This is a *programme/policy-based* multilateral loan — money released against policy and institutional reforms (grid readiness, financing norms, skilling, manufacturing and recycling) rather than against a single physical asset. Subprogram 1 (November 2025) built the policy-institutional-digital framework; Subprogram 2 funds the scale-up. That two-stage design is a good illustration of how MDBs sequence reform lending.
- It also makes the **stacking of concessional finance** visible: PMSG-MBY is now drawing on both ADB and a separate **World Bank package of about $890 million** (IBRD loan + Clean Technology Fund concessional loan + a small IBRD grant). Excellent Mains material for "how should India finance its energy transition" — domestic budgetary support plus MDB reform lending plus climate funds.
- Quotable emissions/target line: the programme targets **30 GW of cumulative residential rooftop solar by FY2026-27** and an avoided **28.8 million tonnes of CO₂-equivalent** — links directly to India's NDC (50% non-fossil installed capacity by 2030) and to the *Panchamrit* pledges.

**Facts/figures to memorise:**
- Loan: **USD 850 million**, ADB, **Subprogram 2**; Subprogram 1 approved **November 2025**
- Scheme: **PMSG-MBY** — Central Sector Scheme of **MNRE**, launched **February 2024**; targets **1 crore households**; up to **300 units** of free electricity/month; outlay **₹75,021 crore**
- Programme targets: **30 GW** residential rooftop capacity by **FY27**; **28.8 MT CO₂e** avoided; certification/training of at least **6,000** people
- Parallel World Bank support to PMSG-MBY: about **USD 890 million** (IBRD loan + Clean Technology Fund + IBRD grant)
- ADB: established **1966**, HQ **Manila**; India a **founding member**; Japan and the USA are its **largest shareholders, with equal shareholding**

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Environment (Climate change & climate finance) + GS3 Economy (Energy infrastructure)

**Static anchor:** **Shankar IAS — Climate Change chapter (Climate Finance section)**; secondary anchor: **Mrunal Pillar 4 — Energy Infrastructure (Renewables)**.
*Nudge:* On your Climate Finance page, start a small table titled "Who funds India's energy transition" with columns *Institution | Instrument | Amount | Scheme* and enter ADB/World Bank/CTF against PMSG-MBY. Cross-link it to your NDC-Panchamrit page so the 30 GW and 28.8 MT numbers sit next to the 2030 targets.

### 3. Chhattisgarh launches DWEEPTI Yojana — India's first women-led distributed renewable energy policy — Society

**Headline:** Chhattisgarh has launched **DWEEPTI** (*Decentralised Women Empowerment on Energy and Policy for Transformative Inclusion*), the first state policy in India to place women's Self-Help Groups at the centre of rural rooftop-solar rollout as **vendors, technicians and entrepreneurs** — not merely as beneficiaries.

**Exam angle:**
- The conceptual pivot is worth a Mains paragraph on its own: it converts women from *recipients of a welfare transfer* into *participants in an energy value chain*. SHG federations (Cluster Level Federations) have been registered as **authorised solar vendors under PMSG-MBY** in partnership with the state discom — i.e. a livelihood policy plugged into a national energy scheme rather than run parallel to it. That is convergence, and convergence is a standing UPSC theme.
- It sits at the junction of three syllabus areas simultaneously: **women's empowerment (GS1 Society)**, **SHGs/NRLM institutional architecture (GS2 Governance)** and **distributed renewable energy (GS3)**. Very few current-affairs items give you a single case study usable in all three papers — flag it.
- Note the deliberate branding lineage: **'Solar Didis'** extends the **Lakhpati Didi**, *Bank Sakhi*, *Krishi Sakhi*, *Pashu Sakhi*, *Drone Didi* family of SHG-cadre roles. Expect UPSC to test the *type* of cadre, not the name.

**Facts/figures to memorise:**
- Full form: **D**ecentralised **W**omen **E**mpowerment on **E**nergy and **P**olicy for **T**ransformative **I**nclusion
- Developed by the **Panchayat & Rural Development Department, Chhattisgarh** with **Transform Rural India (TRI)** under the state's **Green Economy Transition Mission**
- Targets: **5,000** women-led energy enterprises over 5 years | **50,000** green jobs | clean-energy access for **5 lakh families** and **10,000 public institutions**
- Implementation: **13 Cluster Level Federations across 12 districts** registered as authorised PMSG-MBY vendors with **CSPDCL** (state discom); **Kabirdham** the first district (Vande Mataram CLF); ~**35** SHG women trained in rooftop installation as the first **'Solar Didis'** (Tata technical support)
- India's **first** women-led *distributed renewable energy* policy

**Destination notebook:** GS1 — Society (Role of women & women's organisations) | cross-file to GS2 (SHGs, NRLM/DAY-NRLM) and GS3 (Energy)

**Static anchor:** **Laxmikanth-adjacent governance notes / Mrunal Pillar 6 — Rural Development (DAY-NRLM & SHGs)**; secondary: your GS1 Society "Women's Empowerment" page.
*Nudge:* Add DWEEPTI as the newest row in your "SHG cadre roles" list (Bank Sakhi → Krishi Sakhi → Pashu Sakhi → Drone Didi → Lakhpati Didi → **Solar Didi**), and add a one-line cross-link from your PMSG-MBY page noting that SHG federations can be registered vendors — that is the detail most aspirants miss.

### 4. MHA data: over 12,000 e-Zero FIRs registered against cyber financial fraud; Delhi tops — Internal Security

**Headline:** The MoS for Home told the **Lok Sabha** that more than **12,000 e-Zero FIRs** have been registered against cyber financial fraud across **18 States/UTs**, with **Delhi** (6,115) and **Chandigarh** (4,174) far ahead of the rest.

**Exam angle:**
- This is the best available vehicle for the **e-Zero FIR** mechanism, which is a genuinely testable piece of criminal-procedure-meets-cybercrime plumbing. Launched by **I4C** (Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre, MHA) on **19 May 2025**, it *auto-converts* a cyber financial fraud complaint **above ₹10 lakh** filed on the **NCRP portal or the 1930 helpline** into a Zero FIR — removing the "wrong jurisdiction" excuse that used to kill the crucial first hours of a money-trail investigation.
- The systems-integration detail is high-yield: it stitches together **NCRP (I4C)** + **Delhi Police's e-FIR system** + **NCRB's CCTNS**. A Prelims question can be built purely on "which platforms are integrated".
- The statutory hook is **Section 173 of the BNSS, 2023**, which permits information about a cognizable offence to be given **electronically**, subject to the informant **signing it within three days**. That is why the complainant must appear at the cybercrime police station **within 3 days** to convert the Zero FIR into a regular FIR — the 3-day window is not administrative whim, it tracks the statute.
- The skew in the data (Delhi + Chandigarh ≈ 85% of all e-Zero FIRs) is a *rollout-timing* artefact, not a crime-geography finding — Delhi has run it since May 2025, Chandigarh only since November 2025, most others from 2026. Say this explicitly in an answer; it shows analytical care.

**Facts/figures to memorise:**
- **12,000+** e-Zero FIRs across **18** States/UTs | **Delhi 6,115** (since May 2025) | **Chandigarh 4,174** (since Nov 2025) | **Uttarakhand 552** (since Mar 2026) | Goa 446 | Rajasthan 251 | Assam 223 | Haryana 165 | Maharashtra 131
- Launched **19 May 2025** by **I4C** (MHA); threshold **₹10 lakh and above**
- Entry points: **NCRP (cybercrime.gov.in)** and helpline **1930**
- Platforms integrated: **NCRP + Delhi Police e-FIR + NCRB's CCTNS**
- Statutory basis: **Section 173, BNSS 2023** (electronic FIR; signature within 3 days) → **3-day** conversion window

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Internal Security (Cyber security, money laundering & basics of cyber crime) | cross-file to GS2 (e-Governance)

**Static anchor:** **Cyber Security chapter of your Internal Security notes (Ashok Kumar / Vivek Sharma-style)**; secondary anchor: your **BNSS 2023 vs CrPC 1973 comparison table**.
*Nudge:* On your BNSS comparison table, add a row for **Section 173 — electronic FIR** and write "e-Zero FIR (I4C, 2025) is its operational child" beside it. Then, on your I4C page, add the platform triad **NCRP–e-FIR–CCTNS** and the **₹10 lakh / 3 days** pair. Also cross-link to CFCFRMS (Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting & Management System) if you have it.

### 5. Delhi approves the 'Delhi Lakshmi Yojana' — ₹2,500 a month, part-paid in programmable CBDC — Economy

**Headline:** The Delhi Cabinet has approved the **Delhi Lakshmi Yojana**, giving eligible women **₹2,500 per month** with a budget of **₹5,110 crore** for 2026-27 — and, notably, routing **₹1,000** of it into a **CBDC (Digital Rupee) wallet** spendable only on approved goods and services, with **₹1,500** locked into a recurring/fixed deposit.

**Exam angle:**
- Ignore the transfer amount; the examinable innovation is the **use of programmable retail CBDC as a welfare-delivery rail**. UPSC has *already* asked (Prelims 2023) whether a digital currency can carry programmed conditions such as a spending time-frame. This scheme is the first prominent Indian instance of a state using that property to shape *how* a cash transfer is spent — a live illustration of "programmable money" and of the DBT-to-CBDC evolution.
- Second angle: **forced-savings design.** Splitting the transfer into a consumption tranche and a mandatory RD/FD tranche is a behavioural-economics intervention (nudge / commitment device) inside a welfare scheme. Pair this with the standard "cash transfer vs in-kind transfer vs conditional cash transfer" debate for a strong Mains paragraph.
- Third angle, and the one to write honestly: **state fiscal sustainability.** ₹5,110 crore of recurring revenue expenditure on one transfer scheme feeds directly into the "competitive welfarism / freebies vs merit subsidies" debate, the RBI's *State Finances: A Study of Budgets* warnings on committed revenue expenditure, and the FRBM discipline argument. Note the exclusions (>3 children, high electricity consumption, taxpayers, government employees, other-pension recipients) — these are attempts at targeting, and you can evaluate whether proxy means-testing of this kind actually reduces inclusion/exclusion error.

**Facts/figures to memorise:**
- **₹2,500/month**; outlay **₹5,110 crore** (2026-27); expected beneficiaries **17 lakh+**; scheme duration **3 years**
- Disbursal options: (i) **₹1,500 in RD/FD + ₹1,000 in a CBDC Digital Rupee wallet**, or (ii) the **entire ₹2,500 in RD/FD**
- Eligibility: women aged **21–60**, annual family income up to **₹2.5 lakh**, Delhi resident for at least **10 years**
- Exclusions: more than **three** children; annual electricity use above **2,400 units**; income taxpayers; government employees and their families; recipients of other government pensions/assistance
- Portal opens **1 August 2026**; first instalment **28 August 2026** (Raksha Bandhan), via DBT
- CBDC static: the **Digital Rupee (e₹)** is issued by the **RBI**; legal recognition came via the amendment to the **RBI Act, 1934** effected by the **Finance Act, 2022** (bringing CBDC within "bank note"); **wholesale pilot 1 Nov 2022**, **retail pilot 1 Dec 2022**; it is **legal tender** and a **direct liability of the RBI**

**Destination notebook:** GS3 — Economy (Money & Banking; Government budgeting) | cross-file to GS2 (Welfare schemes, DBT)

**Static anchor:** **Mrunal Pillar 1D — Money & Banking (CBDC / Digital Rupee)**; secondary anchors: **Pillar 2 — Budget & Subsidies (DBT)** and **Pillar 2C — State Finances**.
*Nudge:* On your CBDC page, add a line under "features of programmable CBDC" reading "**use case: Delhi Lakshmi Yojana, 2026 — purpose-restricted welfare wallet**", and cross-link it to the 2023 Prelims CBDC question already in your PYQ log. On your State Finances page, add this scheme to your running list of large state cash-transfer commitments.

> **A caution on naming:** press coverage around this decision also carries the older label *Mahila Samriddhi Yojana* for Delhi's ₹2,500 women's transfer commitment. Treat **Delhi Lakshmi Yojana** as the name of the scheme as approved in the July 2026 Cabinet decision, and do not assume the two are separate schemes in an answer.

### C. Mains-Style Question

**GS1 — Society (with GS3 overlap) | 250 words | 15 marks**

> **Examine** how placing women's Self-Help Groups at the centre of India's distributed renewable energy transition — as vendors, technicians and entrepreneurs rather than as beneficiaries — can simultaneously advance energy access and gender equity. What institutional conditions must be met for such models to scale?

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points):**

1. **Frame the shift (intro + 1st body point).** Define distributed renewable energy (DRE) and contrast two policy postures: SHG women as *recipients* of subsidised rooftop solar, versus SHG federations as *market actors* in the DRE value chain. Anchor with Chhattisgarh's DWEEPTI Yojana (5,000 enterprises, 50,000 green jobs, 13 CLFs registered as PMSG-MBY vendors) and the 'Solar Didi' cadre as the newest addition to the Bank Sakhi / Krishi Sakhi / Drone Didi lineage.

2. **The double dividend — argue both halves.** *Energy access:* DRE's binding constraint in rural India is last-mile trust, installation capacity and after-sales service, precisely where SHG federations have distributional depth that private vendors lack. *Gender equity:* movement from unpaid or low-skill work into technical, income-generating and asset-owning roles; enhanced bargaining power within the household; visibility of women in a non-traditional technical occupation, which shifts norms beyond the individual. Cite the SHG-microfinance evidence base (NRLM/DAY-NRLM, Lakhpati Didi) as the mechanism by which credit access translates into agency.

3. **Be honest about the constraints.** Working-capital and collateral gaps for SHG enterprises; technical certification depth (a few weeks of training does not produce a licensed electrician); after-sales and warranty liability sitting on thin-balance-sheet federations; discom-side friction in net-metering and vendor empanelment; time-poverty and mobility restrictions on rural women; risk that "women-led" becomes nominal with male family members operating the enterprise in practice; and the danger of over-reliance on a single external technical partner or NGO.

4. **Institutional conditions for scale + forward-looking conclusion.** (i) Formal empanelment pathways so SHG federations qualify as vendors under national schemes — the DWEEPTI–PMSG-MBY–discom linkage as template; (ii) dedicated, appropriately priced credit lines and a partial risk-guarantee for SHG energy enterprises; (iii) certification aligned to national skill standards with continuing upskilling, not one-off training; (iv) convergence with climate finance — since PMSG-MBY itself is now co-financed by ADB and the World Bank, reform-linked lending should carry gender-disaggregated outcome indicators; (v) transparent monitoring of *who actually operates* the enterprise. Conclude that India's energy transition and its women's-workforce-participation problem can be treated as one policy problem rather than two, and that DRE is the most natural place to attempt it.
