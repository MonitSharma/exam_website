## Part A — Briefing

### 1. EPFO's wage ceiling moves from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 — the first revision in twelve years — Economy

**Headline.** The **Union Cabinet on 16 September 2026** approved the Ministry of Labour & Employment's proposal to raise the **wage ceiling for mandatory EPFO coverage from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 per month**, effective **17 September 2026** (Vishwakarma Jayanti). Over **51 lakh additional employees** — those joining at wages in the ₹15,000–₹25,000 band, who until now fell outside automatic coverage — come within the statutory net for **EPF, EPS and EDLI**.

**Exam angle.** This is the cleanest available illustration of the **"missing middle" in Indian social security**: a worker formally employed but statutorily uncovered, because the coverage threshold had frozen while wages moved. Note the structure the examiner likes — a **wage ceiling is not a benefit cap, it is a coverage trigger and a contribution base**; raising it simultaneously (a) widens who must be enrolled, (b) raises the pensionable-wage base on which the 8.33% EPS diversion is computed, and (c) increases the Centre's 1.16% EPS subsidy outgo. Second hook: the **fiscal arithmetic of universalising social protection** — ₹11,339 crore a year against an existing ₹10,250 crore, i.e. the entire expansion costs about ₹1,100 crore extra annually, a useful counter-example to the assumption that coverage expansion is always fiscally expensive. Third: the **timing** — the ceiling was last revised in **September 2014**, and in several States statutory minimum wages had already crept up to the old threshold, which is the substantive argument for indexation rather than discretionary revision.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **Old ceiling ₹15,000** (set September 2014, unchanged 2004–2014 before that) → **new ceiling ₹25,000**, effective **17 September 2026**.
- **Coverage gain:** **51 lakh+** additional employees.
- **Fiscal:** annual Government outgo **~₹11,339 crore** (against existing budgetary support of **~₹10,250 crore**); **~₹56,696 crore over five years**. Recommended by the **Expenditure Finance Committee on 16 June 2026**.
- **EPFO scale:** about **7.98 crore contributing members** across **~7.68 lakh contributing establishments**; **EPS pays ~82 lakh pensioners**.
- **The three schemes:** **EPF** (provident fund), **EPS, 1995** (pension), **EDLI, 1976** (insurance) — all under the **Employees' Provident Funds & Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952**, which applies to scheduled establishments employing **20 or more** persons.
- **Contribution structure:** employee **12%**, employer **12%**, of which **8.33% is diverted to EPS** (computed on the wage ceiling) and **3.67%** to EPF; the Centre contributes **1.16%** of wages to EPS up to the ceiling.
- **Governance:** the **Central Board of Trustees (CBT)** is **tripartite** — Government, employers, employees — chaired by the **Union Minister for Labour & Employment**.
- **Context:** the Supreme Court had in **January 2026** directed the Centre and EPFO to decide on revising the EPF wage ceiling within four months.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Economy (Employment, social security, formalisation); spills into GS2 — Social Justice (welfare schemes for vulnerable sections).

**Static anchor.** Mrunal's **Pillar 6 — Human Resource Development / Social Security**, plus the labour-codes note. *Update:* add a one-line "EPFO ceiling timeline" (₹6,500 → ₹15,000 in 2014 → ₹25,000 in 2026) to the social-security page, and cross-link it to the **Code on Social Security, 2020** note — the Code's gig-and-platform-worker chapter is the *other* half of the coverage-expansion story and the two are almost always asked together.

### 2. "Guidelines that exist only on paper" — the Supreme Court orders RBI to make its loan-recovery rules bite — Polity & Governance

**Headline.** In **Hari Dutta Sharma v. State of U.P. & Ors.** (**2026 INSC 998**, judgment reported **16–17 September 2026**), a Bench of **Justices P. S. Narasimha and Alok Aradhe** held that a truck driver whose hypothecated vehicle was seized at about **1 a.m.** by breaking the steering lock, without the contractually required seven-day notice and without a signed possession memorandum, had been **arbitrarily deprived of his livelihood in violation of Articles 14 and 21**. The Court awarded **₹10 lakh compensation** plus refund of the ₹4.5 lakh sale price with 6% interest and ₹50,000 costs — and **directed the RBI to take effective steps to secure genuine compliance** by NBFCs and scheduled commercial banks with its recovery guidelines, master circulars and clarifications, which it said "have existed only on paper".

**Exam angle.** Three separable hooks, and this is the kind of judgment UPSC likes precisely because it is not about striking anything down. First, **Article 21 and the right to livelihood** — the Court applies *Olga Tellis* to a private financier's self-help repossession, which is an instructive extension of horizontal application through a regulated entity. Second, **the regulator's duty to enforce, not merely to issue** — RBI directions under **Section 35A of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949** have statutory force (*Internet and Mobile Association of India v. RBI*, 2020), so non-enforcement is itself a regulatory failure; this is a ready-made line on the gap between **rule-making and rule-enforcement capacity** in Indian regulation. Third, the Court's careful **refusal to condemn self-help repossession as such** — it said such clauses are "what make it commercially feasible" to lend against the financed asset to borrowers with no conventional collateral. That balance (financial inclusion vs. borrower dignity) is the quotable Mains line.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **Case:** *Hari Dutta Sharma v. State of U.P. & Ors.*, **Neutral Citation 2026 INSC 998**; Bench — **Justices P. S. Narasimha and Alok Aradhe**; appeal from the **Allahabad High Court**, which had dismissed the writ petition on delay.
- **Financier:** Cholamandalam Investment and Finance Company Ltd. (an **NBFC**); loan repayable in **75 monthly instalments**, secured by **hypothecation**.
- **Relief:** loan accounts closed; **₹4.50 lakh** sale price refunded with **6% p.a.** interest; **₹10 lakh** compensation for mental agony and loss of livelihood; **₹50,000** costs. The completed sale was **not** undone.
- **Constitutional holding:** arbitrary, unnotified night-time seizure depriving a person of livelihood violates **Articles 14 and 21**.
- **Regulatory hook:** **Section 35A, Banking Regulation Act, 1949** — RBI directions have statutory force; **RBI Fair Practices Code** and master circulars (2008–2015) on recovery agents, debt-collection hours, privacy, grievance redressal and the **BCSBI Code**.
- **Precedents relied on:** ***ICICI Bank Ltd. v. Prakash Kaur* (2007)** — banks cannot employ "goondas" to seize vehicles; ***Orix Auto Finance (India) Ltd. v. Jagmander Singh* (2006)** and ***Sundaram Finance Ltd. v. T. Thankam* (2015)** — repossession is a contractual right unless unconscionable or against public policy.
- **Operative direction:** RBI to secure genuine compliance; the Registry was directed to send RBI a copy of the judgment.

**Notebook.** GS2 — Polity & Governance (Fundamental Rights, judicial review, regulatory bodies); usable in GS3 — Economy (NBFC regulation, financial inclusion).

**Static anchor.** **Laxmikanth — Chapter 7, Fundamental Rights**, the Article 21 sub-section. *Update:* append this case to your **Article 21 expansion ladder** (*Maneka Gandhi* 1978 → *Olga Tellis* 1985 → *Francis Coralie Mullin* → *Puttaswamy* 2017 → this) with the one-line tag "livelihood vs. self-help repossession", and cross-link to the **RBI/regulators page** in Mrunal Pillar 1 under "Fair Practices Code / recovery agents".

### 3. Jhiram Valley: an NIA court awards the death penalty to all ten convicts, thirteen years on — Internal Security

**Headline.** On **16 September 2026** the **NIA Special Court at Jagdalpur, Chhattisgarh** sentenced **all ten convicts to death** in the **Jhiram Ghati (Jhiram Valley) attack of 25 May 2013**, in which **CPI (Maoist)** cadres ambushed the convoy of the Congress party's *Parivartan Yatra* in **Darbha, Bastar district** — an IED blast followed by indiscriminate firing and grenades. **Twenty-four people died on the spot** and the toll rose further as the injured succumbed, among them former Union Minister **V. C. Shukla**; **Mahendra Karma** and State Congress chief **Nand Kumar Patel** were killed in the attack. The ten had been **convicted on 5 September 2026** under the **IPC, Arms Act, Explosive Substances Act and UAPA**, after evidence from **101 prosecution witnesses**.

**Exam angle.** Do not memorise this as a news item; memorise it as a **procedure-and-capacity case study**. First, the **death sentence is not final** — a sessions-level death sentence must be **confirmed by the High Court** before it can be executed, and the convicts have been given thirty days to appeal; this is the single most commonly missed point in answers about capital punishment. Second, the **thirteen-year gap** between incident and verdict is the concrete number to deploy in any Mains answer on **delays in terror prosecutions** — witness protection, the difficulty of evidence-gathering in LWE-affected districts, and the attrition of accused (one died in custody; two senior leaders surrendered only recently). Third, the verdict lands amid the official narrative of **LWE's operational decline**, which makes it a good pivot between the security and the development halves of the standard Naxalism answer.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **Attack:** **25 May 2013**, **Jhiram Ghati, Darbha valley, Bastar district, Chhattisgarh**; target — the Congress *Parivartan Yatra* convoy; **24 killed on the spot**, **35+ injured**; weapons looted included **AK-47s, INSAS rifles, SLRs**.
- **Verdict:** convicted **5 September 2026**; sentenced **16 September 2026** by the **NIA Special Court, Jagdalpur**; **death penalty for all 10**; sentences require **High Court confirmation**; **30 days** to appeal. One accused died in judicial custody during trial; the NIA had chargesheeted **39** persons.
- **Statutes invoked:** **IPC**, **Arms Act, 1959**, **Explosive Substances Act, 1908**, **UAPA, 1967**.
- **NIA:** created by the **National Investigation Agency Act, 2008** after the **26/11 Mumbai attacks**; functions under the **Ministry of Home Affairs**; the **2019 amendment** widened its remit (including human trafficking, cyber-terrorism, arms offences) and empowered it to investigate scheduled offences committed **outside India**; it may designate **Special Courts**.
- **Doctrinal term:** the Maoists' **"Tactical Counter Offensive Campaign" (TCOC)** — the annual pre-monsoon (roughly February–June) offensive phase, which is exactly when this attack occurred.
- **Supporting architecture to recall:** **SAMADHAN** doctrine, the **Security Related Expenditure (SRE) Scheme**, **Special Infrastructure Scheme**, **Aspirational Districts Programme** in LWE districts, and the **Greyhounds / CoBRA** specialist forces.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Internal Security (Left-Wing Extremism; linkages of extremism with organised violence); GS2 — Polity (criminal justice, capital punishment procedure).

**Static anchor.** Your **GS3 Internal Security notes — the LWE chapter** (Ashok Kumar/PIB-based). *Update:* open a short "LWE landmark incidents" strip — **Dantewada 2010 (76 CRPF personnel)**, **Jhiram Ghati 2013**, **Sukma 2017** — with the case status against each, and cross-link to the **capital-punishment page** in your Polity notes for the confirmation-appeal-mercy (Articles 72 and 161) chain.

### 4. The monsoon starts to leave — withdrawal from West Rajasthan around 19 September, with the season 15% short — Geography

**Headline.** The **India Meteorological Department** said on **14 September 2026** that conditions are becoming favourable for the **withdrawal of the southwest monsoon from parts of West Rajasthan around 19 September 2026** — the start of the retreat from the Indian mainland, **about two days later than the normal date of 17 September**. Cumulative rainfall from **1 June to 13 September** stands at **15% below normal**, with the **South Peninsula worst hit at −28%**.

**Exam angle.** Two things to extract. First, the **withdrawal sequence itself is standard Prelims material** and it is the mirror image of onset: the monsoon **arrives** at Kerala around **1 June** and moves north-west, and it **withdraws** from **extreme north-west India (West Rajasthan) first**, around 17 September, retreating south-east and clearing the mainland by around **15 October** — a **gradual** retreat, unlike the abrupt "burst" of onset. Second, this year is a textbook case of **why seasonal totals mislead**: June was **−36.5%**, July was **+1%**, and the season still ends near −15% with huge regional spread (−28% in the South Peninsula, −6% in Central India). Pair that with the **reservoir number** — 129.87 BCM against a ten-year average of 134.23 BCM and last year's 159.18 BCM — and you have a complete rabi-sowing and drinking-water argument without needing any additional source.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **Withdrawal onset:** around **19 September 2026** from parts of **West Rajasthan**; IMD's **normal date for the start of withdrawal is 17 September** (revised in 2020 from the earlier 1 September); complete withdrawal from the country normally by around **15 October**.
- **Season so far (1 June – 13 September 2026):** all-India **−15%**; **South Peninsula −28%**, **East & North-East India −25%**, **North-West India −10%**, **Central India −6%**.
- **Month-by-month:** **June −36.5%**, **July +1%**, recovery in July–August narrowing the deficit to about 15%.
- **Agriculture:** kharif acreage had lagged about **21%** by end-June; by **11 September** total acreage was about **1.45% below last year** and **0.72% below the five-year normal**. Laggards: **urad, moong, soybean, cotton**.
- **Reservoirs (as on 3 September 2026):** **129.87 BCM** across the **178** monitored reservoirs, against **159.18 BCM** last year and a ten-year average of **134.23 BCM**.
- **Static frame:** IMD defines withdrawal by **cessation of rain for about five days, establishment of anticyclonic circulation and a marked drop in moisture**; the retreat is accompanied by **"October heat"** (high temperature with high humidity) and the onset of the **north-east / retreating monsoon**, which brings the main rains to the **Coromandel Coast**, Rayalaseema, south interior Karnataka and Kerala in **October–December**.

**Notebook.** GS1 — Geography (Indian climate, monsoon mechanism); GS3 — Agriculture and Disaster Management (drought, reservoir storage).

**Static anchor.** **NCERT Class XI, *India: Physical Environment*, Chapter 4 — Climate** (and Goh Cheng Leong's monsoon chapter). *Update:* on your monsoon page, write the **onset and withdrawal dates side by side as two arrows** (onset: Kerala 1 June → NW India 8 July, south-east to north-west; withdrawal: West Rajasthan 17 September → mainland clear by 15 October, north-west to south-east), and cross-link it to the **north-east monsoon** note so the "which coast gets rain in which season" question never costs you a mark.

### 5. PM Vishwakarma turns three and hits its 30-lakh registration target — Society

**Headline.** On its **third anniversary on 17 September 2026**, **PM Vishwakarma** — the ₹13,000-crore Central Sector Scheme for traditional artisans launched on **17 September 2023** — reported that it has **met its target of 30 lakh registered artisans and craftspeople**. Over **24.37 lakh** have completed basic training, **18.16 lakh** have received toolkits, and **6.19 lakh loans worth about ₹5,316 crore** have been sanctioned. The anniversary event was held in **Kolkata**.

**Exam angle.** The value here is as a **design case study, not a scheme to rote-learn**. Three features are worth arguing about in a Mains answer. First, **recognition before credit**: the scheme's operative innovation is the **PM Vishwakarma certificate and ID**, which gives an informal artisan a *verifiable identity* — the precondition for collateral-free institutional lending to someone with no conventional collateral and no balance sheet. Second, the **sequencing of benefits** — registration → training with stipend → toolkit → first loan tranche → second tranche — makes each benefit conditional on the previous step, a deliberately **graduated** design that you can contrast with one-shot transfer schemes. Third, the **gap between registration and credit** (30 lakh registered but 6.19 lakh loans) is the honest critique: enrolment is not the same as uptake, and this is exactly the kind of number an examiner rewards you for noticing. Note also the trade list — **barber, washerman, garland maker, cobbler** — which makes this a scheme with a visible **caste-occupation overlap**, and therefore usable in social-justice answers as well as MSME ones.

**Facts to memorise.**

- **Launched 17 September 2023**; **Central Sector Scheme** (fully Centre-funded); outlay **₹13,000 crore**; nodal ministry **MSME**, jointly implemented with the **Ministry of Skill Development & Entrepreneurship** and the **Department of Financial Services**.
- **18 trades**, including carpenter, boat maker, armourer, blacksmith, hammer-and-toolkit maker, locksmith, goldsmith, potter, sculptor/stone carver, stone breaker, cobbler, mason, basket/mat/broom maker and coir weaver, traditional doll-and-toy maker, **barber, garland maker, washerman, tailor** and fishing-net maker.
- **Benefits:** **recognition** (PM Vishwakarma certificate and ID card); **basic training** 5–7 days / 40 hours and **advanced training** 15 days, with a stipend of **₹500 per day**; **toolkit incentive up to ₹15,000** via **e-RUPI / e-vouchers**; **collateral-free "Enterprise Development Loan"** — **₹1 lakh** (first tranche, 18 months) and **₹2 lakh** (second tranche, 30 months) at a concessional **5%** interest with Government interest subvention of up to **8%**; **digital-transaction incentive of ₹1 per eligible transaction** (up to 100 a month); marketing support through the **National Committee for Marketing**.
- **Progress at three years:** **30 lakh** registered (target met); **24.37 lakh** basic training completed; **18.16 lakh** toolkits; **6.19 lakh** loans worth **~₹5,316 crore**; **12,000+** artisans trained in **AI tools** for branding, design, packaging and marketing.
- **Related schemes to cross-tag:** **PM SVANidhi** (street vendors), **PMEGP**, **Mudra (Shishu/Kishor/Tarun)**, **SFURTI** (traditional-industry clusters), **ODOP**.

**Notebook.** GS2 — Governance (welfare schemes, their design, implementation and performance) and Social Justice; GS3 — Economy (MSME, skilling, informal-sector credit).

**Static anchor.** Your **government-schemes register under the MSME ministry**, and Mrunal's **Pillar 4B — Sectors of Economy / MSME & skilling**. *Update:* record the three-year performance line (30 lakh registered / 24.37 lakh trained / 18.16 lakh toolkits / 6.19 lakh loans) next to the scheme's design features, and add a one-line cross-link to the **Code on Social Security, 2020** page — a PM Vishwakarma registrant is precisely the "unorganised worker" the Code contemplates, and pairing the two makes a much stronger answer than either alone.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Governance / Social Justice, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *"Regulations that exist only on paper are worse than no regulation at all, because they create an illusion of protection."* **Examine** this proposition in the light of recent judicial observations on the enforcement of loan-recovery norms by financial regulators in India.

**Model-answer skeleton.**

1. **Set the frame.** Distinguish **rule-making capacity** from **enforcement capacity**. Note that RBI directions under **Section 35A of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949** carry statutory force (*IAMAI v. RBI*, 2020), so an unenforced direction is not merely a lapse of practice but a failure of a statutory mandate — which is precisely why unenforced rules can be worse than none: the borrower believes she is protected and does not seek alternative remedies.
2. **Establish the evidence.** The Supreme Court's observation that RBI's guidelines, master circulars and clarifications on recovery practices "have existed only on paper"; the specific facts of night-time seizure without notice; the **Articles 14 and 21** holding; the continuity with ***ICICI Bank v. Prakash Kaur* (2007)**, where the Court had already said banks cannot employ "goondas" — i.e. the same problem recurring across nearly two decades despite repeated circulars.
3. **Diagnose the causes, do not merely list them.** Outsourcing of recovery to agents who fall outside the regulated entity's direct discipline; supervisory capacity concentrated on prudential and systemic risk rather than **conduct regulation**; grievance redressal that requires the weakest party to initiate; deterrence that is reputational rather than financial; and the regulatory dilemma that self-help repossession genuinely does enable unsecured-collateral lending to small borrowers, so a blanket prohibition would shrink credit access.
4. **Balance and conclude.** Argue for **proportionate conduct regulation** rather than prohibition: mandatory pre-repossession notice with a cure period, digital time-stamped possession memoranda, registration and accountability of recovery agents, supervisory action against the **regulated entity** for its agent's conduct, and suo motu compliance audits rather than complaint-driven action. Conclude that the test of a regulator is not the volume of circulars issued but the observable change in conduct on the ground — and that constitutional courts stepping in to enforce a regulator's own norms is itself a signal of a supervisory gap.

*(Note the directive: **Examine** — investigate the proposition's validity in detail and present both its force and its limits. Do not merely describe the judgment.)*

### D. PYQ Mirror — how UPSC has asked these themes before

**Theme 1 — Left-Wing Extremism (Item 3).** UPSC has returned to this in Mains repeatedly, and the framing has shifted over time from *security* to *development*:

- **GS3, 2018 (10 marks):** *"Left Wing Extremism (LWE) is showing a downward trend, but still affects many parts of the country. Briefly explain the Government of India's approach to counter the challenges posed by LWE."*
- **GS3, 2020 (15 marks):** *"What are the determinants of left-wing extremism in the Eastern part of India? What strategy should the Government of India, civil administration and security forces adopt to counter the threat in the affected areas?"*

**Takeaway:** the examiner consistently wants the **socio-economic diagnosis first, the security response second**. Today's item supplies the missing third leg — **prosecutorial and judicial capacity** — which almost nobody writes about and which will differentiate your answer.

**Theme 2 — IMD and the monsoon (Item 4).** The Mains hook is institutional rather than descriptive:

- **GS1, 2022 (10 marks):** *"Discuss the meaning of colour-coded weather warnings for cyclone prone areas given by India Meteorological Department."*

**Takeaway:** UPSC asks about **IMD as an institution and its products** — warnings, onset and withdrawal declarations, forecast categories — not just about monsoon physics. Know how IMD *defines* withdrawal (five-day rain cessation, anticyclonic circulation, moisture drop), not merely when it happens.

*On Prelims, the monsoon's basic mechanism is a recurring favourite — the classic single-line question "the seasonal reversal of winds is the typical characteristic of…" (answer: monsoon climate) is widely cited as a mid-2010s CSE Prelims question, **though the exact year should be treated as approximate**; the dependable takeaway is that onset and withdrawal dates, the north-east monsoon, and the El Niño/IOD linkage are all live Prelims territory.*

## Part C — Answers

**1. (a)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. The ceiling went from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000, effective 17 September 2026, and was last revised in September 2014. Statement 3 is wrong: the enhancement expands access to **EPF, EPS and EDLI** alike, in accordance with the applicable scheme provisions.

**2. (b)** — The case concerned an NBFC's repossession and sale of a truck without the contractually required notice, seized at about 1 a.m. by breaking the steering lock. The Court held this violated **Articles 14 and 21**, awarded ₹10 lakh compensation, and directed the RBI to secure genuine compliance with its recovery guidelines, which it said "have existed only on paper".

**3. (b)** — Statements 1 and 3 are correct. Withdrawal normally begins from extreme north-west India (West Rajasthan, normal date 17 September) and proceeds south-eastward. The South Peninsula recorded the largest deficiency at **−28%**. Statement 2 is wrong: rainfall from 1 June to 13 September 2026 was **15% below** normal, not above it.

**4. (c)** — Both are correct. The **National Investigation Agency Act, 2008** was enacted after the 26/11 Mumbai attacks and constitutes the NIA, which may have Special Courts designated for scheduled offences. A death sentence passed at the sessions level must be **confirmed by the High Court** before execution; here the convicts were also given 30 days to appeal.

**5. (b)** — Statements 2 and 3 are correct. PM Vishwakarma provides collateral-free loans of ₹1 lakh and ₹2 lakh in two tranches at a concessional 5% rate, and barbers (*naai*), washermen (*dhobi*) and garland makers (*malakaar*) are among the 18 eligible trades. Statement 1 is wrong: it is a **Central Sector Scheme**, fully funded by the Centre, not a Centrally Sponsored Scheme.

**6. (a)** — Statements 1 and 2 are correct. *Olga Tellis* (1985) read the right to livelihood into Article 21, and Article 41 is the relevant Directive Principle on the right to work, education and public assistance. Statement 3 is wrong: **Article 21 is available to all persons**, citizens and non-citizens alike.

**7. (d)** — All three are correct. The retreat is gradual (roughly mid-September to mid-October across the mainland) unlike the abrupt onset "burst"; the north-east monsoon supplies the bulk of the Coromandel Coast's annual rainfall in October–December; and October–November is a peak season for Bay of Bengal tropical cyclones.
