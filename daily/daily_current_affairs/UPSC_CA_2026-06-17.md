### 1. PM Modi at the 52nd G7 Summit, Évian (France) — India as G7 outreach partner
**Headline:** India joins the G7 Summit in Évian-les-Bains (15–17 June 2026) as an invitee/partner country; PM Modi to hold a bilateral with the US President on 17 June.

**Exam angle:** Highest-priority CA theme (IR). The recurring Prelims trap is membership vs. participation — India is **not** a G7 member; it attends by invitation. Note France's framing: it deliberately involved India *because* India holds the **BRICS chairmanship in 2026**, the perfect illustration of India's "multi-alignment." For Mains, this is a ready GS2 hook on India straddling Western and non-Western groupings.

**Memorize:**
- G7 members (7): **Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, UK, USA** — no India, no China, no Russia. All 7 are also G20 members.
- This is the **52nd** G7 Summit; venue **Évian-les-Bains, Haute-Savoie, France**; France is host/chair in 2026.
- India's **7th consecutive** G7 appearance; **13th time** as a partner country.
- Working session theme: *"Forging New Partnerships and Rebuilding International Solidarity."*

### 2. DRDO twin milestone — multi-layered BMD interceptors + maiden NASM-MR flight
**Headline:** Between 10–13 June 2026 DRDO conducted successful flight tests of its **AD-1/AD-2 ballistic-missile-defence interceptors** (Phase-II BMD) and the **maiden flight of NASM-MR**, an indigenous naval anti-ship cruise missile, off Chandipur, Odisha.

**Exam angle:** Priority #2 (S&T) with Internal-Security crossover. Two distinct, MCQ-friendly platforms in one news cycle. Prelims loves "what is X?" — fix **NASM-MR = naval anti-ship cruise missile** (not an interceptor, not anti-tank). The BMD tests validate India's layered shield against longer-range/ballistic threats — useful for a Mains line on indigenous defence and *Atmanirbharta*.

**Memorize:**
- **NASM-MR** = Naval Anti-Ship Missile (Medium Range): all-weather, over-the-horizon, **sea-skimming**, ~Mach 0.9, range ~100–350 km; launchable from Tejas, MiG-29K, ships, submarines.
- **AD-1 / AD-2** = Phase-II Ballistic Missile Defence interceptors (high-altitude / long-range interception).
- Test site: **Integrated Test Range (ITR), Chandipur, Odisha**; developer **DRDO**.

**Notebook:** GS3 Sci-Tech (+ GS3 Internal Security)
**Static anchor:** Sci-Tech "Defence/Missiles" page — slot NASM-MR beside **BrahMos** (anti-ship), and AD-1/AD-2 under **BMD Phase-I (PAD/AAD) → Phase-II**. Cross-link the missile family map (Agni, Prithvi, Akash, Nag, Astra, BrahMos) and tag test ranges (Chandipur ITR, Wheeler/APJ Abdul Kalam Island).

### 3. Invasive alien species spreading fast — Lantana threatens India's tiger landscapes
**Headline:** Recent assessments in the news flag that invasive alien plants are transforming India's ecosystems at an accelerating pace — **Lantana camara** now occupies large tracts of forest/scrub, suppressing native grasses, cutting tiger prey and raising human–wildlife conflict.

**Exam angle:** Priority #3 (Environment) and a textbook **case study** for Mains (biodiversity loss drivers) plus a Prelims species ID. Examiners pair "invasive species" with named offenders — keep the list tight. Links cleanly to Project Tiger / protected-area framework (see static MCQ below).

**Memorize:**
- Key invasive alien plants in India: **Lantana camara, Prosopis juliflora (vilayati kikar), Chromolaena odorata, Parthenium hysterophorus, Eichhornia/water hyacinth, Mikania micrantha.**
- Reported scale: India's natural areas losing on the order of **~15,500 sq km/year** to fast-spreading invasives; worst-hit zones include the **Western Ghats (Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve), Shivalik Hills, Central Indian deciduous forests.**
- Mechanism for tigers: invasives → fewer native grasses → less herbivore prey → tigers shift to cattle → more conflict.

**Notebook:** GS3 Environment
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS "Biodiversity / Threats to Biodiversity" → update the **Invasive Alien Species** sub-topic with this data point; cross-link to **Project Tiger / NTCA / Tiger Reserves** and to **Western Ghats (biodiversity hotspot)**.

### 4. Retail inflation rises to 3.93% (May 2026); RBI holds repo at 5.25%
**Headline:** CPI retail inflation rose to **3.93% in May 2026** (from 3.48% in April), driven by food; this follows the **RBI MPC (3–5 June 2026) holding the repo rate at 5.25%** with a *neutral* stance.

**Exam angle:** Priority #4 (Economy), classic data-point-to-syllabus link. Prelims tests *who releases CPI* (MoSPI) and the *flexible inflation targeting* band; Mains tests RBI's effectiveness against food inflation. Note inflation is comfortably inside the 2–6% band and below the 4% midpoint — yet the MPC stayed on hold citing West Asia tensions, crude prices and rupee pressure (good "policy trade-off" line).

**Memorize:**
- **CPI (May 2026): 3.93%** (rural 4.25%, urban 3.53%); **food inflation 4.78%**; released by **MoSPI** (NSO).
- **Repo rate: 5.25%** (held, neutral stance); RBI revised **FY27 CPI projection up to ~5.1%** and trimmed **GDP growth to ~6.6%.**
- **Flexible Inflation Targeting:** CPI target **4% ± 2%** (i.e., 2–6%), under the Monetary Policy Framework Agreement; **MPC has 6 members.**

### 5. Southwest Monsoon 2026 — onset over Kerala on 4 June, now advancing east-central India
**Headline:** The southwest monsoon set in over **Kerala on 4 June 2026** (about **3 days later** than the normal 1 June date) and, by mid-June, advanced into West Bengal, Bihar and adjoining areas; further progress into Odisha, Jharkhand and Chhattisgarh expected around 23 June.

**Exam angle:** A map/diagram-worthy "Places in News" + Geography staple. Prelims repeatedly tests the **normal onset date (1 June, Kerala)** and the **mechanism** (pressure systems, jet streams). A minor delay ≠ a weak season — useful nuance for Mains on monsoon variability and agriculture dependence.

**Memorize:**
- **Normal onset over Kerala: 1 June**; 2026 onset **4 June** (~3 days late).
- Drivers to revise: **ITCZ shift, Mascarene High, Somali (Findlater) Jet, Tropical Easterly Jet, ENSO (El Niño/La Niña) and the Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD).**
- Withdrawal normally begins **mid-September** from NW India.

**Notebook:** GS1 Geography (Places in News / Physical Geography)
**Static anchor:** NCERT (Class XI *Indian Physical Environment*) + GC Leong "Monsoon mechanism" — pin the 2026 onset date against the climatology; cross-link the **monsoon onset isochrone map** and **El Niño/IOD** notes.

### Mains-Style Question

**Q (GS2 — IR).** *India today engages simultaneously with Western-led groupings such as the G7 and non-Western platforms such as BRICS, while chairing the latter in 2026. Critically examine the opportunities and challenges this strategy of "multi-alignment" poses for India's foreign policy.* (250 words)

**Model-answer skeleton:**
1. **Frame:** Define multi-alignment / strategic autonomy (vs. Cold-War non-alignment); anchor in the live example — Modi as a G7 invitee in Évian *while* India chairs BRICS in 2026.
2. **Opportunities:** access to Western capital/technology and supply chains (G7, QUAD) + voice of the Global South and de-risked energy/defence ties (BRICS, SCO); bargaining leverage; agenda-setting on AI, climate finance, reformed multilateralism.
3. **Challenges:** balancing US–Russia–China tensions; credibility/coherence risks; pressure on de-dollarisation and sanctions; expectation-management within each bloc.
4. **Way forward / verdict:** issue-based coalitions, institutional reform (UNSC, Bretton Woods), and consistency between rhetoric and action; conclude that multi-alignment is sustainable if backed by domestic economic and military capacity.

## ANSWERS

**Q1 — (b) 2 and 3 only.** India is an invitee/partner, not a G7 member (so 1 is wrong). France hosted the 52nd Summit at Évian, and all seven G7 economies are G20 members.

**Q2 — (b) Naval anti-ship cruise missile.** NASM-MR is DRDO's medium-range, sea-skimming anti-ship cruise missile (≈Mach 0.9); the AD-1/AD-2 tested in the same window are the *interceptors*, not NASM-MR.

**Q3 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** CPI is released by MoSPI and FIT targets 4% ±2%. Statement 3 is wrong — the repo rate is set by the six-member MPC by majority, not the Governor alone.

**Q4 — (c) Both 1 and 2.** Normal Kerala onset is ~1 June (2026 onset was 4 June, ~3 days late), and the Mascarene High plus the cross-equatorial Somali Jet are key monsoon drivers.

**Q5 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Lantana camara, Prosopis juliflora and Chromolaena odorata are all major invasive alien plants spreading across Indian forests and scrublands.

**Q6 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Project Tiger began in 1973; the NTCA is statutory (Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972, as amended in 2006); and tiger reserves are notified with a core (critical tiger habitat) and a buffer zone.

**Q7 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The MPC has six members (three RBI + three government-appointed); the Governor has a casting vote in a tie; it is constituted under the RBI Act, 1934 (amended via the Finance Act, 2016).
