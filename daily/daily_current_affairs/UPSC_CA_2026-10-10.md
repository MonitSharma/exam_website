## PART A — BRIEFING

### 1. Nobel Peace Prize 2026 goes to Navi Pillay — "peace and international law" — International Relations
**Exam angle:** The Norwegian Nobel Committee honoured South African jurist Navanethem (Navi) Pillay, 85, of Indian Tamil origin, for promoting peace and international law and for helping ensure war crimes, crimes against humanity and genocide are prosecuted. It reads as a statement on the rules-based order. Useful for Mains IR/ethics: accountability, universal jurisdiction, role of international courts.
**Memorise:** 125th Peace Prize; 12 million SEK; ceremony Oslo, 10 Dec. Career: first woman to start a law practice in Natal (1967); defended Mandela; first woman of colour on SA High Court; judge at ICTR (helped establish rape as an act of genocide/crime against humanity); judge at ICC; UN High Commissioner for Human Rights; chaired UN Commission of Inquiry on the Occupied Palestinian Territory; reported to currently sit on the ICJ.
**Notebook:** GS2 – IR (International institutions)

### 2. 57th GST Council (8 Oct): arrest powers to go, prosecution threshold up to ₹5 crore — Economy
**Exam angle:** Part of a decriminalisation / ease-of-compliance push. Recommended: scrapping GST officers' power of arrest (the Finance Minister said prosecution remains where criminality is established, via a prima facie case), raising the prosecution threshold ₹1 crore → ₹5 crore, cutting the general penalty ₹25,000 → ₹10,000. Reports also mention no demand notice for tax below ₹10,000, a 5% GST (no ITC) for delivery services via e-commerce, and an officers' committee on ITC under Sec 16(2)(c). These are *recommendations* — require legislative/rule changes; FM said process reforms apply from **1 April 2027**.
**Memorise:** 57th meeting, 8 Oct 2026; Art 279A; recommendations persuasive, not binding (Mohit Minerals, 2022); Secs 69 (arrest) and 132 (offences/punishment) of CGST Act.
**Notebook:** GS3 – Economy (Taxation) / GS2 Federalism
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth – Ch. on GST Council (Art 279A) ; Mrunal Economy pillar "Indirect taxes". *Nudge:* add "decriminalisation of economic offences" as a Mains example next to Jan Vishwas.

### 3. World Bank's World Development Report 2026: "The Promise of Artificial Intelligence" — Reports & Indices
**Exam angle:** Released in New Delhi on 8 Oct (presented by Chief Economist Michael Kremer; launched by IT Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw with the IndiaAI Mission). Core message: AI can widen access to expertise and lift productivity in medical screening, farming advice and education, but gains depend on electricity, connectivity, compute, data, skills and institutions — a "full-stack" approach. India's AI-driven monsoon forecasts to farmers were cited as a lesson (Ethiopia adopting it).
**Memorise:** WDR = annual flagship of the World Bank; IndiaAI Mission – 7 pillars (compute, foundation-model innovation centre, AIKosh datasets platform, application development, FutureSkills, startup financing, safe & trusted AI). Minister's claims (sugarcane yield +40% in Baramati, TB detection +35%) are not report findings.
**Notebook:** GS3 – Sci-Tech / GS2 Governance
**Static anchor:** Mrunal "Reports & Indices" sheet (add WDR 2026); Spectrum/IT chapter on AI. *Nudge:* link with digital public infrastructure and IndiaAI Mission notes.

### 4. Pre-COP31 in Nadi, Fiji: India presses balance of adaptation, mitigation and means of implementation — Environment
**Exam angle:** Pre-COP31 (5–8 Oct) was co-hosted by Australia, Fiji and Tuvalu (leaders' event in Tuvalu) to centre Pacific priorities. COP31 will be at **Antalya, Türkiye, 9–20 Nov 2026**, with **Australia presiding over negotiations** — an unusual split-hosting arrangement. Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav called for equal attention to adaptation, mitigation and means of implementation, upheld CBDR-RC "in letter and spirit", and argued against one-size-fits-all action.
**Memorise:** COP31 dates/venue/presidency above; India's three asks: adaptation parity, finance/tech/capacity-building, CBDR-RC.

### 5. Government moves to cap trade margins at 30% of MRP on 110 anti-cancer drugs — Society (Health)
**Exam angle:** Reportedly decided (not yet notified; expected to be implemented this month) after a Supreme Court hearing on 22 Sep where the bench noted a drug with MRP ₹27,000 supplied to retailers at ₹2,700. Covers non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs — branded or generic, domestic or imported, patented or not; a health-department committee is finalising the list. Aim: curb mis-selling and exorbitant margins; ties to Right to Health (Art 21) and affordability.
**Memorise:** 110 drugs (35 patented); 30% of MRP; "trade margin rationalisation"; NPPA under Dept of Pharmaceuticals; status – sources-based reports.
**Notebook:** GS2 – Social Justice (Health)
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth – Art 21 / DPSP Art 47; Mrunal Society pillar "Health policy". *Nudge:* cross-link NPPA/DPCO 2013 with Jan Aushadhi and price-control notes.

### Mains Question
**Critically examine the GST Council's recommendation to withdraw officers' arrest powers and raise the prosecution threshold to ₹5 crore. (150 words, 10 marks)**
*Model-answer skeleton:*
1. **Intro:** 57th Council (8 Oct 2026) – decriminalisation push; effect from 1 Apr 2027 per FM; still recommendations.
2. **Merits:** curbs harassment/arbitrary arrests, ease of doing business, focus on large evaders, aligns with Jan Vishwas-style decriminalisation.
3. **Concerns:** deterrence against fake-invoice/ITC fraud rings; risk of evasion below ₹5 crore; need for strong data analytics and judicial oversight; Centre-State consensus.
4. **Way forward:** retain prosecution for criminality with magistrate-supervised process, tech-driven risk scoring, graded penalties, review after a year.

## PART C — ANSWERS
- **Q1 – (b):** Statements 1, 2 correct; the Peace Prize is awarded by the Norwegian Nobel Committee (Swedish Academy gives Literature).
- **Q2 – (c):** 1 and 2 match the Council's recommendations; recommendations are persuasive, not binding (Mohit Minerals, 2022).
- **Q3 – (a):** 1, 2 correct; the report says gains depend on electricity, connectivity, skills and institutions.
- **Q4 – (d):** Antalya venue and Australian presidency are right; Pre-COP31 was in Nadi, Fiji (leaders' event in Tuvalu).
- **Q5 – (b):** 30% cap on 110 non-scheduled anti-cancer drugs; NPPA is under Dept of Pharmaceuticals; cap not limited to patented drugs.
- **Q6 – (c):** CBDR-RC in UNFCCC Art 3; Paris adds national circumstances; Kyoto bound only Annex I.
- **Q7 – (c):** ICJ is the UN's principal judicial organ at The Hague; ICC is a separate treaty body, India not a party; ICJ doesn't try individuals.
