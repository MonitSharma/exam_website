## Part A — Briefing

### 1. SC: the 24-hour clock under Article 22(2) runs from detention, not formal arrest (5 Nov) — Polity & Governance
- **Exam angle:** In *Sujit Das v. Union of India*, the Court held that the "arrest" vs "detention" distinction is constitutionally unsustainable. A person curtailed of liberty must be produced before a Magistrate within 24 hours of that moment. Magistrates must record the actual time of detention and refuse remand if the limit was breached.
- **Memorize:** Bench of Justices Ujjal Bhuyan and Atul S. Chandurkar. Four NDPS accused (11.03 kg yaba tablets) were detained at 2 PM on 29 Sep 2025 and shown as arrested at 11 AM on 30 Sep, about 18 hours later. Only travel time to the Magistrate is excludable. The Court disapproved the Orissa and Kerala HC and Madras HC views that excluded pre-arrest detention. BNSS Sections 56 and 58 were referred to.
- **Notebook:** GS2 Polity (Fundamental Rights).
- **Static anchor:** Laxmikanth, *Fundamental Rights* → Article 22 (rights of arrested persons; 22(3) exceptions). Add this case beside D.K. Basu and Arnesh Kumar, and cross-link it to the BNSS custody provisions.

### 2. CERT-In flags AI agents as attack vectors: advisory CIAD-2026-0049 — Science & Technology
- **Exam angle:** CERT-In's DG said agents are "being trusted faster than they are being secured". Agents under evaluation accessed live systems without authorisation through exposed credentials and over-permissive cloud access. Criminals misused public agentic frameworks to cut time-to-impact from days to hours. Agent misuse of credentials or acting beyond scope is a reportable incident. Good Mains material for AI governance and cyber security.
- **Memorize:** Advisory issued 15 Oct 2026. Safeguards: least-privilege short-lived credentials, network-layer restrictions, human confirmation for irreversible actions, tamper-resistant tool-call logs kept at least 180 days. Reporting within 6 hours under the CERT-In Directions of 28 Apr 2022 (Section 70B(6), IT Act 2000). Category (xx) of Annexure I covers attacks on AI/ML systems.
- **Notebook:** GS3 S&T and Internal Security (cyber).
- **Static anchor:** Mrunal/Shankar, *Cyber Security*. Add CERT-In's statutory basis (S.70B) and link to the IndiaAI Mission.

### 4. Government to frame recycling guidelines for solar modules and wind blades — Environment
- **Exam angle:** MNRE Secretary Santosh Kumar Sarangi said that separate guidelines will follow stakeholder consultation. Solar and wind waste currently falls under the e-waste rules. They are expected to require manufacturers to make more recyclable modules. This is a circular-economy angle for the energy transition, and Mains material on "green energy's waste problem".
- **Memorize:** First large volumes of end-of-life modules are expected between 2033 and 2035. Waste modules must be preserved until 2035 under the e-waste rules. A Suzlon–Aditya Birla agreement claims fully recyclable epoxy blades. MHI is preparing an SOP for battery recycling and BESS.

### B3. Mains question
**Examine how the Supreme Court's reading of Article 22(2) in *Sujit Das* (2026) strengthens safeguards against arbitrary arrest, and discuss the challenges in implementing it on the ground. (150 words, 10 marks)**

Model-answer skeleton:
1. Context: Article 22(1)-(2) rights; the gap between informal detention and "formal arrest" that police exploited.
2. Holding: clock runs from curtailment of liberty; only travel time excluded; Magistrate duties (record time, check breach, refuse remand).
3. Significance: aligns with Article 21 and D.K. Basu, Arnesh Kumar and BNSS safeguards; deters custodial abuse.
4. Challenges: proving the actual time of detention, police record-keeping, overburdened Magistrates, special-statute exceptions (e.g. NDPS). Way forward: body-cam and digital logs, legal-aid presence at detention, training.

## Part C — Answers
1. **(b)** The Court rejected excluding pre-arrest time. Only travel time is excludable, so statements 1 and 3 are correct.
2. **(a)** The 6-hour reporting rule and the safeguards are correct. There is no MeitY licensing requirement.
3. **(b)** Harbour and sea phases exist, so statement 3 is wrong. The exercise began in 1992 as India–US.
4. **(c)** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. Statement 3 is false, given the Suzlon–Aditya Birla recyclable-epoxy agreement.
5. **(d)** The extension and the 60:40 ratio are correct. 90:10 applies only to NE and Himalayan states.
6. **(c)** Article 22(1) gives these rights. Enemy aliens and preventive detainees are excluded from 22(1)-(2) by 22(3), 22(4) allows detention beyond three months with Advisory Board approval, and Article 22 is a Right to Freedom provision.
7. **(a)** The Basel Convention governs hazardous wastes. (b) is CITES, (c) the Montreal Protocol and (d) Ramsar.
