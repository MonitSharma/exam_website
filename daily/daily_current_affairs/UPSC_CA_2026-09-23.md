### 1. Exercise 'Veer Guardian 2026' concludes at Jodhpur — and Japanese F-2s fly from Indian soil for the first time — International Relations

**Headline:** The India–Japan bilateral air exercise **Veer Guardian 2026** concluded at **Air Force Station Jodhpur** on **22 September 2026** — the **second edition** of the series, and the first held in India.

**Exam angle:**
- The direction of travel is what matters. The maiden edition (January 2023) was hosted at **Hyakuri Air Base, Japan**; hosting the second in India means the JASDF deployed fighters abroad to an Indian base — a reciprocity that only a handful of India's partners have. For a country whose Article 9 constitution constrained overseas military activity for decades, Japanese fighter deployments abroad are themselves a data point about Japan's security normalisation.
- Note what the exercise practised: **within-visual-range (WVR) air combat** and coordinated multi-aircraft operations — i.e. tactical interoperability, not a symbolic flypast. The **LCA Tejas** flying alongside the F-2 is the indigenisation subplot: the two air chiefs flew together in a Tejas on Distinguished Visitors' Day.
- Place it in the architecture, because UPSC tests the framework, not the exercise name. India–Japan is a **"Special Strategic and Global Partnership"**, serviced by an **Annual Summit**, a **2+2 Ministerial Dialogue**, and the **Acquisition and Cross-Servicing Agreement (ACSA, 2020)** — a logistics-sharing pact of the same family as LEMOA (US), and the enabler that makes deployments like this practical.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **Veer Guardian**: India–Japan **air** exercise. 1st edition **January 2023, Hyakuri Air Base, Japan**; 2nd edition **September 2026, AFS Jodhpur**, concluded **22 September 2026**.
- Assets: **Japan — Mitsubishi F-2** (3 aircraft, 8th Wing, ~110 personnel); **India — Su-30MKI, Rafale, LCA Tejas**.
- Full India–Japan exercise set for the table: **Veer Guardian** (air), **Dharma Guardian** (army — recent edition at Chaubatia, Uttarakhand), **JIMEX** (navy, bilateral), **Malabar** (navy, with the US and Australia — Quad navies), **MILAN** (multilateral, Indian-hosted).
- Framework: Special Strategic and Global Partnership; **2+2 Ministerial**; **ACSA (2020)**; cooperation threads on **UNICORN** integrated mast (via BEL), DRDO–ATLA R&D, maritime domain awareness.

### 2. Green nod for ports now lasts 20 years — and can stretch to 30 — Environment

**Headline:** On **22 September 2026**, the Environment Ministry amended the **EIA Notification, 2006** to raise the validity of prior environmental clearance for **ports, harbours, breakwaters and dredging** projects from **10 years (+1)** to **20 years**, extendable by **two further blocks of 5 years** — a possible **30-year** total.

**Exam angle:**
- This is the cleanest available illustration of the standing Mains tension: **regulatory certainty versus regulatory dilution**. The Ministry's stated ground is that port and harbour projects have "long gestation periods and phased development"; the Sectoral Expert Appraisal Committee held that 10+1 years was insufficient. The counter-argument writes itself — a clearance is an appraisal of *predicted* impacts against a *baseline*, and coastal baselines (sediment transport, shoreline, turtle nesting, mangrove cover, fisher livelihoods) move faster than 20 years. Longer validity means a project can be built against science two decades old, without re-appraisal.
- Watch the drafting detail, which is exactly the kind of specificity that earns marks: the final amendment inserts a **new paragraph 9(iva)** modelled on the **mining** provisions, rather than amending paras 9(ii)(b), 9(ii)(c) and the proviso to 9(iii) as the **May 2026 draft** had proposed. And the final term — **20 years** — is *longer* than the draft's 15, i.e. post-consultation the period went up, not down. That is a process point worth a line in an answer.
- The political overlay: **Jairam Ramesh** alleged the amendment was tailored to the **Great Nicobar Island** transshipment port. You do not need to adjudicate that; you need to note that project-specific criticism of a general notification is a recurring feature of Indian environmental regulation, and that **Great Nicobar** (Galathea Bay, leatherback turtles, Shompen and Nicobarese tribal reserve land) is a live case study you should already have on file.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Amendment to the **EIA Notification, 2006** (framed under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**), notified **22 September 2026**.
- Categories covered: **ports, harbours, breakwaters, dredging**. Old: **10 years + 1-year extension**. New: **20 years**, plus a **5-year** extension on EAC examination, plus a further **5 years** in deserving cases with **additional environmental safeguards** — **maximum 30 years**.
- Mechanism: **new para 9(iva)**, modelled on the mining-sector template. Draft (May 2026) had proposed **15 years**.
- Baseline for comparison: EIA 2006 sets validity by sector — e.g. **river valley** projects 10 years, **mining** up to 30 years (project life as assessed by the EAC).

**Notebook:** GS3 — Environment (environmental impact assessment; conservation and infrastructure trade-offs).

**Static anchor:** **Shankar IAS — Environmental Impact Assessment** chapter. Update the EIA-validity table with a fresh "ports/harbours/dredging: 20 (+5+5)" row and date-stamp it. Add a cross-link to the **CRZ Notification, 2019** (ports are a *coastal* clearance story as much as an EIA one) and to the **Great Nicobar** case-study card. In the Polity notebook, cross-link to **NGT** as the appellate forum against clearances under the NGT Act, 2010.

### 3. "Squatting" on mineral blocks — the Mines Ministry names the bottleneck, and promises a lithium–nickel processing scheme — Economy

**Headline:** Mines Secretary **Keshav Chandra** said on **22 September 2026** that an **incentive scheme for domestic lithium and nickel processing** will be launched "very soon", and flagged **"squatting"** — auctioned, cleared, leased blocks that are never operationalised — as a core problem.

**Exam angle:**
- The insight worth carrying into an answer: India's critical-minerals gap is **midstream**, not upstream. Auctions allocate *deposits*; batteries need *battery-grade chemicals*. Between the two sits refining and processing capacity that is overwhelmingly concentrated in one country. A block auctioned in Chhattisgarh and a cathode plant in India are separated by a processing step India currently imports — which is why "processing parks" and a lithium–nickel processing incentive are the policy response, not more auctions.
- **Squatting** is a genuinely new term for your notes and a clean example of a **policy-implementation gap**: the auction cleared, the clearances came, the lease was signed, and nothing was mined. Causes to name — price volatility making a marginal deposit uneconomic, speculative bidding to deny a rival the block, and financing that never closed. Remedies to name — work-programme milestones, performance-security forfeiture, and lease revocation for non-commencement.
- Mains framing: critical minerals sit at the intersection of **energy transition, supply-chain security and foreign policy** (MSP, the Khanij Bidesh India Ltd route to overseas assets). Treat them as a strategic-autonomy question, not a mining question.

**Facts to memorise:**
- Statement date **22 September 2026**; **Keshav Chandra**, Secretary, Ministry of Mines. Scheme for **lithium and nickel** processing "to be launched soon" (no outlay announced).
- **Critical mineral processing parks** approved in **Gujarat, Maharashtra, Odisha and Andhra Pradesh**.
- **"Squatting"** = block auctioned, clearances obtained, lease executed — but operations never commenced.
- Standing framework to revise alongside: **National Critical Mineral Mission (2025)**; **MMDR (Amendment) Act, 2023** — empowered the **Central Government to exclusively auction** mining leases and composite licences for specified critical minerals and opened atomic minerals like lithium and beryllium to private exploration; **Offshore Areas Mineral (Development and Regulation) Act, 2002** as amended in 2023; **Minerals Security Partnership (MSP)**, which India joined in 2023; **KABIL (Khanij Bidesh India Ltd)** for overseas assets.

**Notebook:** GS3 — Economy (mineral resources, industrial policy) / overlaps GS2 — IR (resource diplomacy).

**Static anchor:** **Mrunal — Infrastructure & Industrial Policy** pillar, minerals section; plus the **Indian Geography (NCERT Class 12, *India: People and Economy*)** mineral-belts chapter. Add a two-column page: *upstream* (reserves, auctions, MMDR) versus *midstream* (processing, parks, incentives) — and note which of India's 30 identified critical minerals India actually has domestically. Cross-link to **PLI for ACC battery storage** and to the **Minerals Security Partnership** card in the IR notebook.

### 4. Ayurveda Day is now a fixed calendar date — and the story is mainstreaming, not the festival — Society

**Headline:** **23 September 2026** is the **11th Ayurveda Day**, themed **"Ayurveda for a Healthier Tomorrow"** — the second observance since a **Gazette Notification of 23 March 2025** moved the day off the lunar calendar (it had been tied to **Dhanteras / Dhanvantari Jayanti**) and fixed it to 23 September.

**Exam angle:**
- The shift from a movable religious date to a fixed civil date is itself the exam point: it converts a festival observance into an **institutional health-system marker**, which is what lets it be aligned with international health calendars and WHO engagement.
- The substance is **integration**, and it comes with numbers you can quote: Ayush has been folded into primary care through **Ayush Ayushman Arogya Mandirs**, and into the mainstream financing and classification systems — insurance empanelment and the **WHO ICD-11 Traditional Medicine Module 2** (launched January 2024), which for the first time gives traditional-medicine diagnoses a standard international code. Coding is the unglamorous precondition for evidence, reimbursement and research.
- The honest critique, which is what separates a 10-mark answer from a 6: integration is currently measured in **inputs** (facilities co-located, doctors funded) rather than **outcomes**; the evidence base for many formulations remains thin; and standardisation, heavy-metal contamination in some products, and regulatory capacity at the State drug-controller level are unresolved. Say this plainly and then say what would fix it.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **11th Ayurveda Day, 23 September 2026**; theme **"Ayurveda for a Healthier Tomorrow"**. Date fixed to 23 September by **Gazette Notification dated 23 March 2025**; previously observed on **Dhanteras (Dhanvantari Jayanti)**, first observed **2016**.
- **12,260** Ayush Ayushman Arogya Mandirs operational against a target of **12,500**; Ayush services co-located in **13,249** public health facilities; **25,322** Ayush doctors and **5,666** paramedics supported.
- **Ministry of Ayush** budget **₹4,408 crore** for **2026–27**. **All India Institute of Ayurveda (AIIA), New Delhi** empanelled with **all 32 general insurers** (10 February 2026).
- **WHO ICD-11 Traditional Medicine Module 2**, launched **January 2024**. Sector valued at about **US$43 billion (2024)** with a **US$200 billion by 2030** ambition.
- Institutions for the table: **Ministry of Ayush** (carved out 2014), **National Ayush Mission (NAM, 2014)**, **CCRAS** (research council), **NCISM** (National Commission for Indian System of Medicine Act, 2020 — replaced the CCIM), **WHO Global Centre for Traditional Medicine, Jamnagar**.

### 5. Vande Mataram, six stanzas and the conscience question — the Supreme Court signals that *Bijoe Emmanuel* still governs — Modern History

**Headline:** On **22 September 2026**, a three-judge Bench of **CJI Surya Kant**, **Justice Joymalya Bagchi** and **Justice V. Mohana**, hearing Carnatic musician **T.M. Krishna's** petition, orally observed that it expects **no criminal consequences** for a person who declines to sing *Vande Mataram* on religious grounds, and that ***Bijoe Emmanuel* will govern the Act**. Notice was issued to the Union. Separately, Karnataka **Governor Thaawarchand Gehlot** wrote to the Chief Minister asking the State to reconsider its **8 September 2026** order limiting the rendition to two stanzas.

**Exam angle:**
- This is a **Modern History** item wearing a legal coat, and that is how you should file it. The two-stanza convention is not a 2026 invention: the **Indian National Congress in 1937** confined public singing to the **first two stanzas** after objections that the later stanzas' imagery of the motherland as goddess was devotionally specific. Tagore, Nehru, Subhas Chandra Bose and Rajendra Prasad were associated with that accommodation. The song's career runs from **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay's *Anandamath* (1882)** — the words were composed earlier, in the 1870s — through its singing at the **1896 Calcutta session** of the Congress and its adoption as the rallying cry of the **Swadeshi movement (1905)**.
- Know the constitutional status precisely, because this is where candidates lose the mark: on **24 January 1950**, Dr Rajendra Prasad's statement in the Constituent Assembly adopted ***Jana Gana Mana*** as the **National Anthem** and said *Vande Mataram* "shall be honoured equally with" it. The anthem is named in a Constituent Assembly statement; **neither song is designated in the text of the Constitution**, and *Vande Mataram* has no separate statutory designation as "National Song".
- The doctrine is **conscientious objection** under **Article 25**. ***Bijoe Emmanuel v. State of Kerala* (1986)** — three Jehovah's Witness children expelled for standing respectfully silent during the anthem — held that standing in respectful silence does not amount to disrespect, and that compelling the singing violates Article 25. The present challenge is to **Section 3** of the **Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971**, as amended in **2026**, read with MHA orders of **28 January** and **9 July 2026**. Also relevant: **Article 51A(a)** makes respect for the National Anthem a **Fundamental Duty** — non-justiciable, and a duty to respect is not a duty to sing.

**Facts to memorise:**
- **22 September 2026**: interim oral observations, notice to Union. Bench: **CJI Surya Kant, Joymalya Bagchi, V. Mohana**. Petitioner: **T.M. Krishna**. Challenge: **Section 3, Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971**, as amended 2026; MHA orders **28 Jan** and **9 July 2026**. *Not a final judgment.*
- ***Bijoe Emmanuel v. State of Kerala* (1986)** — respectful silence protected; Article 25.
- *Vande Mataram*: **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay**, published in ***Anandamath* (1882)**; sung at the **1896 Calcutta Congress session** (Rabindranath Tagore); **six stanzas**, Congress resolution of **1937** confined public rendition to the **first two**.
- **24 January 1950** — Constituent Assembly / Dr Rajendra Prasad's statement: *Jana Gana Mana* the **National Anthem**; *Vande Mataram* to be **honoured equally**.
- **Article 51A(a)** — Fundamental Duty to respect the National Flag and National Anthem. Karnataka GO of **8 September 2026** (two stanzas); Governor **Thaawarchand Gehlot**'s letter, **22 September 2026**.

**Notebook:** GS1 — Modern History (national movement, cultural nationalism) / cross-files to GS2 — Polity (Article 25, Fundamental Duties).

**Static anchor:** **Spectrum — *A Brief History of Modern India*, the Swadeshi/Anti-Partition chapter**. Add a "national symbols" box: anthem, song, flag, emblem — with dates of adoption and the exact instrument of adoption for each (this is a perennial Prelims trap). In **Laxmikanth**, cross-link **Chapter on Fundamental Duties (Art. 51A)** and the **Freedom of Religion articles (25–28)**; add *Bijoe Emmanuel* to your case-law list beside *Shyam Narayan Chouksey* (the 2016 cinema-hall anthem order, modified in 2018).

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Environment, 250 words, 15 marks)

**Q.** *"Longer validity for environmental clearances buys regulatory certainty at the cost of regulatory currency."* **Critically examine** this proposition in the light of the September 2026 amendment extending the validity of prior environmental clearance for port and harbour projects.

**Model-answer skeleton:**

1. **Set the frame in two lines.** Prior environmental clearance under the EIA Notification, 2006 is a *conditional, time-bound* permission granted on a predicted-impact assessment measured against an environmental baseline. Validity periods exist precisely because predictions age. State the change: ports/harbours/dredging move from 10(+1) to 20 years, extendable twice by five years — up to 30.

2. **The case for.** Port projects are capital-intensive, phased and dependent on trade volumes and concession timelines; re-appraisal mid-build imposes cost and financing risk without necessarily improving environmental outcomes. Repeat appraisals also consume scarce EAC capacity that could be spent on new proposals. Aligns ports with the mining template already in the notification, i.e. sectoral consistency rather than ad hocism. Supports Sagarmala/Maritime Amrit Kaal objectives and reduces the regulatory-uncertainty premium that deters long-horizon infrastructure investment.

3. **The case against.** A twenty-year-old baseline is not a baseline — coastal geomorphology, sediment cells, mangrove extent, turtle nesting and fisher dependence all shift materially within a decade, and climate-driven sea-level and storm-surge assumptions shift faster. Longer validity weakens the leverage that periodic renewal gives the regulator over compliance with clearance conditions, and it lengthens the gap before any fresh public consultation. Process concern: the final period (20 years) exceeded the May 2026 draft (15 years), and the mechanism shifted to a new para 9(iva), which invites the criticism that consultation loosened rather than tightened the proposal. Project-specific apprehensions (Great Nicobar) sharpen the objection that a general notification can be shaped around a particular case.

4. **Resolve with a design argument, not a verdict.** The choice is not "long versus short" but "long *with what conditions*". Propose: mandatory mid-term environmental audit and baseline revalidation at a fixed interval irrespective of validity; automatic re-appraisal triggers on material change in scope, capacity or coastal configuration; publication of compliance reports on PARIVESH with third-party verification; and tying each five-year extension to demonstrated compliance rather than to mere non-operationalisation. Conclude that regulatory certainty and ecological currency are reconcilable only if duration is decoupled from scrutiny.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**Q1 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Veer Guardian is the India–Japan bilateral **air** exercise; the maiden edition was held in January 2023 at Hyakuri Air Base, Japan, so the September 2026 edition at AFS Jodhpur was the first hosted in India. Japan deployed **Mitsubishi F-2** fighters; India flew Su-30MKI, Rafale and LCA Tejas.

**Q2 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The 22 September 2026 amendment raises validity for ports, harbours, breakwaters and dredging from 10 years (with a 1-year extension) to **20 years**, with a further 5-year extension on EAC examination and another 5 years in deserving cases with additional safeguards — a maximum of **30 years**. The EIA Notification, 2006 is issued under the **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**.

**Q3 — (b).** "Squatting", as used by the Mines Secretary on 22 September 2026, means holding an auctioned block on which all clearances have been obtained and the lease executed, without commencing operations — locking up mineral wealth unproductively.

**Q4 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Ayurveda Day was first observed in 2016 on **Dhanteras (Dhanvantari Jayanti)**, a lunar-calendar date; a **Gazette Notification of 23 March 2025** fixed it to **23 September**. The 11th observance, on 23 September 2026, carried the theme **"Ayurveda for a Healthier Tomorrow"**.

**Q5 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** *Vande Mataram* appeared in **Bankim Chandra Chattopadhyay's *Anandamath* (1882)**, and the Congress in **1937** confined public rendition to the first two stanzas. Statement 3 is the classic trap: the **Constitution does not designate either the National Anthem or the National Song**; *Jana Gana Mana* was adopted as the anthem by **Dr Rajendra Prasad's statement in the Constituent Assembly on 24 January 1950**, which also said *Vande Mataram* would be **honoured equally** with it.

**Q6 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Category 'A' is appraised centrally by the MoEFCC on the recommendation of the Expert Appraisal Committee; Category 'B' goes to the **SEIAA** advised by the SEAC. Category 'A' projects skip **screening** because an EIA is mandatory for them — screening exists only to sort Category 'B' into B1 (EIA required) and B2 (exempt). Statement 3 is wrong: **public consultation is not universal** — B2 projects and certain listed categories are exempt from it.

**Q7 — (c) All three.** The ***Sushruta Samhita*** is the foundational text of **shalya tantra (surgery)**; the ***Charaka Samhita*** is the principal text of **kayachikitsa (internal medicine)**; the ***Ashtanga Hridaya*** (with the *Ashtanga Sangraha*) is attributed to **Vagbhata**. Together these three constitute the ***Brihat-trayi***, the "great triad" of classical Ayurveda.
