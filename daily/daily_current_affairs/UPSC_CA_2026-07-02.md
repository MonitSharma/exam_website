## PART A — BRIEFING (5 items)

### 1. Nine years of GST — collections cross ₹22 lakh crore, "GST 2.0" two-slab structure beds in
**Headline:** India's Goods and Services Tax completed nine years on 1 July 2026; gross annual GST collections have grown from ~₹7.4 lakh crore (2017-18) to ~₹22.27 lakh crore (2025-26), with Q1 FY2026-27 (Apr-Jun 2026) collections at ₹5.14 lakh crore and the taxpayer base up from 66.5 lakh (2017) to 1.65 crore (May 2026).
- **Exam angle:** A guaranteed "anniversary" Economy story that also tests constitutional architecture. Prelims loves the Article 246A/269A/279A trio and GST Council voting weights; Mains loves the fiscal-federalism tension (compensation cess, revenue growth guarantees, states' loss of taxation autonomy) — this is the *same theme* as a genuine PYQ (see Part B).
- **Key facts to memorise:** 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016 introduced GST; "GST 2.0" rate rationalisation (effective 22 Sept 2025) cut the five-slab structure (0/5/12/18/28%) to mainly two slabs — 5% and 18% — with a 40% rate for luxury/sin goods; compensation cess wound down for most goods; collections ₹7.4 lakh cr (2017-18) → ₹22.27 lakh cr (2025-26); taxpayers 66.5 lakh → 1.65 crore.
- **Notebook:** GS3 (Economy — indirect taxes/fiscal federalism) with a GS2 (Polity — Centre-State financial relations) cross-tag.
- **Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth — "Centre-State Financial Relations"* (Art 246A, 269A, 279A) + *Ramesh Singh/Mrunal Economy — GST chapter*. **Nudge:** update your GST timeline note with the "GST 2.0" two-slab reform (Sept 2025) and this 9-year data table; cross-link to the GST (Compensation to States) Act, 2017.

### 2. e-OCI digital card + FCRA 2.0 Portal launched
**Headline:** Union Home Minister Amit Shah launched the **e-OCI card** (a QR-code based digital replacement for the physical OCI booklet, for 50+ lakh OCI holders) together with the **FCRA 2.0 Portal** (API-based, AI-chatbot-enabled compliance system for foreign-funded NGOs) on 30 June–2 July 2026.
- **Exam angle:** Classic "governance + tech" Polity item and a reliable trap-setter: OCI is repeatedly confused with dual citizenship in Prelims. Distinguish clearly from the FCRA (Regulation) Amendment Rules, 2026 covered earlier this month — today's news is the *digital portal roll-out*, not new FCRA substantive rules.
- **Key facts to memorise:** e-OCI = digital, QR-verifiable credential replacing the blue booklet; processing time cut to ~15 working days (from 6-8 weeks); OCI is NOT dual citizenship — no voting rights, no constitutional/most government posts, no agricultural-land purchase; FCRA 2.0 Portal reduces paperwork via API-based bank/NGO verification.
- **Notebook:** GS2 (Polity & Governance — citizenship, e-governance, NGO regulation).
- **Static anchor:** *Laxmikanth — "Citizenship"* chapter (Art 5-11, Citizenship Act 1955, OCI provisions). **Nudge:** add e-OCI + FCRA 2.0 Portal to your "e-Governance initiatives" list; refresh the OCI "rights denied" table (voting, constitutional posts, agri-land) alongside the June FCRA Amendment Rules entry.

### 3. ZSI: 709 new faunal records in 2025; India's first complete "Fauna Checklist Portal" launched
**Headline:** At the Animal Taxonomy Summit 2026 (marking the Zoological Survey of India's 111th Foundation Day, 1 July 2026), the Environment Minister released *Animal Discoveries–2025* and the **Fauna of India Checklist Portal** — making India the first country with a complete taxonomic checklist of its own fauna (1,04,561 species catalogued across 121 checklists spanning 36 phyla).
- **Exam angle:** High-yield "institution + data" Environment/Society item. Tests ZSI's parent ministry and founding year (a classic Laxmikanth-adjacent "body in news" trap against BSI), plus a quotable Mains statistic on India's megadiverse status.
- **Key facts to memorise:** ZSI founded 1916, HQ Kolkata, under MoEFCC; 2025 = 709 new faunal records (483 new to science + 226 new-to-India); India's total documented fauna now 1,05,953 species; Kerala topped states with 98 new discoveries, then West Bengal (76), Karnataka (67), Arunachal Pradesh (65).
- **Notebook:** GS3 (Environment — biodiversity institutions) with a GS1 (Society — India's megadiverse status) cross-tag.
- **Static anchor:** *Shankar IAS Environment — "Biodiversity: Institutions"*. **Nudge:** build a ZSI (1916, Kolkata, fauna) vs BSI (1890, Kolkata, flora) comparison row; add the 2026 Fauna Checklist Portal as a "first of its kind" fact.

### 4. Rajasthan-Haryana operationalise the 32-year-old Yamuna Water Project via a new SPV
**Headline:** On 29 June 2026, Rajasthan and Haryana signed an MoU (with the Centre) to finally implement the 1994 Upper Yamuna River Board (UYRB) agreement — a ₹34,102 crore project to pipe ~580 million cubic metres of Yamuna water from the Hathnikund Barrage via the Western Yamuna Canal to the drought-prone Shekhawati region (Churu, Sikar, Jhunjhunu), through a new **Rajasthan-Haryana Yamuna Water SPV**.
- **Exam angle:** A textbook "Places in News" + cooperative-federalism case study — a three-decade-old interstate water-sharing deal finally moving to execution. Directly mirrors a real GS2 PYQ on inter-state water disputes (see Part B).
- **Key facts to memorise:** UYRB constituted under the 12 May 1994 agreement among UP, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Rajasthan and NCT of Delhi; 2026 project cost ₹34,102 crore; ~580 MCM water via 3 underground pipelines (monsoon months, July-October); source = Hathnikund Barrage → Western Yamuna Canal; beneficiary = Shekhawati region.
- **Notebook:** GS1 (Geography — drainage/interstate rivers) + GS2 (Polity — cooperative federalism, Art 262, ISRWD Act 1956).
- **Static anchor:** *NCERT XI Physical Geography — Drainage System* + *Laxmikanth — "Inter-State Water Disputes"* chapter. **Nudge:** add UYRB (1994) and the 2026 SPV as a "resolved dispute" case study beside the still-unresolved Cauvery/Mahadayi/Satluj-Yamuna Link entries.

### Mains-Style Question (GS2 — Polity/Governance, 250 words, 15 marks)

**"The 2026 Rajasthan-Haryana agreement to operationalise the three-decade-old Yamuna Water Project illustrates both the promise and the difficulty of inter-state river-water cooperation in India." Discuss the structural and political-economy factors that stall such agreements, and suggest measures to strengthen the framework for inter-state water sharing.**

*Model-answer skeleton:*
1. **Legal-institutional context:** Article 262 bars ordinary courts from most inter-state water disputes; Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956 and River Boards Act, 1956 provide the tribunal/board route; the 1994 UYRB agreement took 32 years to reach implementation via the 2026 SPV.
2. **Structural/political-economy factors:** multiplicity of riparian states with competing claims and contested hydrological data; absence of binding timelines (partly addressed by the 2019 amendment creating a single tribunal); politicisation around state elections; climate-variability-driven flow uncertainty; historically weak implementation architecture (no dedicated execution body until the SPV model).
3. **Way forward:** basin-level authorities/SPVs with ring-fenced funding and timelines; real-time hydrological data sharing (National Water Informatics Centre); time-bound tribunal awards; NITI Aayog/Centre as a neutral mediator; linking settlements to Jal Shakti Abhiyan and climate-resilient water budgeting.
4. **Conclusion:** Durable inter-state water sharing needs cooperative federalism backed by implementation bodies (SPVs) — not just paper agreements; the Yamuna SPV can be a template for stalled disputes (Cauvery, Mahadayi, Satluj-Yamuna Link).
