## Part A — Briefing

### 1. Vultures are gone from 72% of their recorded nesting sites — the Centre asks for more Vulture Safe Zones — Environment

**Headline.** On International Vulture Awareness Day (first Saturday of September, 5 September in 2026; India's national workshop was held 6 September), Union Environment Minister Bhupender Yadav inaugurated a national-level workshop themed **"From Crisis to Recovery — Conserving India's Vultures"** at Pinjore, Panchkula district, Haryana, and called for the creation and expansion of **Vulture Safe Zones** built on vulture-safe veterinary practices and assured safe food availability.

**Exam angle.** This is the classic UPSC species story — an anthropogenic, drug-driven collapse (not habitat loss, not poaching) that produced a textbook cascade of ecosystem-service failure: feral dog population explosion, rabies burden, carcass disposal costs, and pressure on Parsi dokhma practices. It is also an *institutional* story: MoEFCC + Central Zoo Authority + Bombay Natural History Society + a State forest department co-running a conservation-breeding programme is exactly the multi-agency architecture the exam likes to test. The 72% figure gives you a hard number to deploy in a Mains answer on the limits of *ex-situ* conservation.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Workshop organisers: MoEFCC, **Central Zoo Authority (CZA)**, Haryana Forest & Wildlife Department, **Bombay Natural History Society (BNHS)**, and the **Jatayu Conservation Breeding Centre (JCBC), Pinjore** — India's first and largest vulture conservation-breeding centre.
- Vultures have disappeared from roughly **72% of their historically recorded nesting sites** in India.
- JCBC Pinjore in **2025-26**: housed **356 vultures**, bred **35 chicks**, released/contributed **87 birds** to reintroduction programmes, transferred **90 birds** to other conservation-breeding institutions, and trained **1,222 participants** from **22 institutions**.
- Mechanism: **diclofenac**, an NSAID given to cattle, causes **renal failure and visceral gout** in vultures that scavenge treated carcasses. **Gyps** species were worst hit — *Gyps indicus* and *Gyps tenuirostris* populations fell ~**97.4%**; the overall Indian vulture decline exceeded **99%**.
- India banned the **veterinary use of diclofenac in 2006**; safe alternative is **meloxicam**. Other nephrotoxic NSAIDs still in circulation: aceclofenac, ketoprofen, nimesulide.
- **Vulture Action Plan 2020-25** (MoEFCC); the **SAVE** consortium (Saving Asia's Vultures from Extinction).

**Notebook.** GS3 — Environment (Conservation).

**Static anchor.** *Shankar IAS Environment* — Chapter on **Animal Diversity of India / Species in News** and the **Conservation Efforts** chapter (conservation breeding, CZA). Add a cross-link from the **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972** section: note which vultures sit in **Schedule I** post-2022 amendment, and cross-link to **CITES Appendix II** for *Gyps* species. Also add a one-line link into your *keystone/scavenger species → ecosystem services* note.

### 2. Round 1 of the ₹37,500-crore coal gasification scheme closes with seven bids — Economy

**Headline.** The Ministry of Coal announced on 8 September that **Round 1** of the **Scheme for Promotion of Surface Coal/Lignite Gasification Projects** drew **seven applications** from major private industry and PSUs, covering urea, direct-reduced iron, syngas and synthetic natural gas.

**Exam angle.** This is the sharpest current illustration of the "coal as chemical feedstock, not just boiler fuel" argument — and therefore the cleanest way into the GS3 energy-security-versus-decarbonisation trade-off. Note the *import-substitution* framing: the products targeted (urea, ammonia, methanol, SNG) are exactly India's large import bills. Also note the incentive design (capital subsidy on plant and machinery, with per-project, per-product and per-group caps) — a recurring PLI-style template UPSC has begun to test.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Scheme approved by the **Union Cabinet on 13 May 2026**; outlay **₹37,500 crore**; administered by the **Ministry of Coal**.
- It **replaced** the earlier **₹8,500-crore** incentive scheme approved in **January 2024**.
- Target: gasification of about **75 million tonnes** of coal/lignite.
- Incentive: up to **20% of the cost of plant and machinery**. Caps — **₹5,000 crore** per single project; **₹9,000 crore** per single product (except Synthetic Natural Gas and Urea); **₹12,000 crore** per single entity group across all projects.
- Round 1 applicants: **Adani Enterprises (three applications, all urea)**, **Gallantt Ispat** (DRI + syngas), **NTPC** (synthetic natural gas), **Shyam Sel & Power** (syngas), **Talcher Fertilisers** (urea).
- Background target: the Ministry of Coal's **National Mission — 100 MT coal gasification by 2030**.
- Chemistry to be able to state in one line: coal + limited O₂/steam → **syngas (CO + H₂)** → methanol, ammonia, urea, SNG, hydrogen.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Economy (Infrastructure/Energy) with a cross-entry in GS3 Environment.

**Static anchor.** *Mrunal Economy* — **Infrastructure pillar (Energy)**, sub-head "Coal & Clean Coal Technologies". Add a cross-link to the **fertiliser subsidy** note (urea import substitution) and to the **National Green Hydrogen Mission** note, flagging the grey-versus-green hydrogen distinction. In *Shankar IAS Environment*, cross-link the **carbon capture, utilisation and storage (CCUS)** paragraph — coal gasification's climate case rests entirely on it.

### 3. Kharif acreage slips to 1,086.31 lakh hectares — but pulses and jute buck the trend — Agriculture

**Headline.** The Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers' Welfare reported total kharif sown area at **1,086.31 lakh hectares as on 4 September 2026**, against **1,104 lakh hectares** in the corresponding period last year, with the shortfall attributed to a deficient monsoon following El Niño conditions.

**Exam angle.** Acreage data is the single most quotable input for any Mains answer on food security, cropping-pattern rigidity, or monsoon dependence. The internal composition matters more than the headline: **rice down, pulses up** is precisely the diversification direction policy has been pushing for a decade (Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses), so this is a rare year where the data supports the policy narrative. Cotton and oilseeds being nearly flat is the counterpoint — edible-oil import dependence is untouched.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Total kharif area, as on **4 September 2026**: **1,086.31 lakh ha** (previous year, same date: **1,104 lakh ha**).
- **Rice: 421.82 lakh ha** (previous year 438.19) — the main drag.
- **Pulses: 117.13 lakh ha**, up **1.83 lakh ha** — driven by *urad* and *moong*.
- **Oilseeds: 184.46 lakh ha** (previous year 185.36) — marginally down.
- **Cotton: 109.17 lakh ha** (previous year 109.87).
- **Jute** also recorded a rise.
- Cause cited: **deficient monsoon following El Niño**.

**Notebook.** GS3 — Agriculture.

**Static anchor.** *Mrunal Economy* — **Agriculture pillar**, sub-head "Cropping seasons & cropping pattern". Update your kharif/rabi acreage table with the 2026 figures and add a one-line cross-link to the **El Niño / ENSO** entry in *G.C. Leong* (Chapter on climate) and to the **Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses** entry in your schemes register.

### 4. Bhupen Hazarika's birth centenary opens — the 'Bard of the Brahmaputra' at 100 — Art & Culture

**Headline.** **8 September 2026** marked the **100th birth anniversary** of **Dr Bhupen Hazarika**, opening a year-long centenary programme across Assam and nationally; the celebrations are to culminate at **Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, on 29 November 2026**, with a **₹100 commemorative coin** to be released.

**Exam angle.** Art & Culture personalities are a reliable Prelims slot, and Hazarika is the highest-yield Assamese figure because he sits at the intersection of *cinema, folk-music revival, and cultural nationalism in the Northeast*. Learn him as a cluster with the other Assamese cultural icons (Jyoti Prasad Agarwala, Bishnu Prasad Rabha, Srimanta Sankardeva) — UPSC's preferred trick is to swap names within such a cluster.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Born **8 September 1926 at Sadiya**, in present-day **Tinsukia district, Assam**; died **5 November 2011**, Mumbai.
- Epithets: **Sudhakantha** ("nectar-throated") and the **Bard of the Brahmaputra**.
- Awards: **Padma Shri (1977)**, **Padma Bhushan (2001)**, **Padma Vibhushan (2012, posthumous)**, **Bharat Ratna (2019, posthumous)**.
- **Dadasaheb Phalke Award, 1992** — the **first Assamese** to receive it.
- **National Film Award for Best Music Direction, 1975** (for *Chameli Memsaab*).
- **Sangeet Natak Akademi Award (1987)** and **Sangeet Natak Akademi Fellowship (2008)**; **Asom Ratna (2009)**, conferred by the Government of Assam.
- Composed over a thousand songs across **Assamese, Bengali and Hindi**; worked as singer, composer, lyricist, director, producer and writer.

**Notebook.** GS1 — Art & Culture (with an Indian Society cross-entry on cultural integration in the Northeast).

**Static anchor.** *Nitin Singhania / NCERT Fine Arts* — **Modern Indian cultural personalities** and the **Music** chapter. Create (or extend) a one-page "Assam cultural cluster" note: **Srimanta Sankardeva → Bhakti/Ekasarana Dharma, Borgeet, Ankiya Naat, Bhaona, Sattra institution → Sattriya (recognised classical, 2000)**, then the modern layer — **Jyoti Prasad Agarwala** (father of Assamese cinema, *Joymati* 1935), **Bishnu Prasad Rabha**, **Bhupen Hazarika**. Cross-link to your **Bharat Ratna list** and **Dadasaheb Phalke Award** note.

### 5. PM-SETU clears ₹735.70 crore for three more ITI clusters — Society

**Headline.** The **5th National Steering Committee** under **PM-SETU** (Pradhan Mantri Skilling and Employability Transformation through Upgraded ITIs), chaired by MSDE Secretary Debashree Mukherjee, approved **Strategic Investment Plans worth ₹735.70 crore** for ITI clusters in **Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh and Telangana**, taking total sanctioned investment to **₹2,171 crore across nine ITI clusters**.

**Exam angle.** The demographic-dividend argument is only as good as the delivery vehicle you can name, and PM-SETU is now that vehicle — replacing the vaguer "skill India" framing with a specific, financeable design. Two design features are worth carrying into an answer: the **hub-and-spoke** model (a lead ITI upgraded to anchor a cluster of feeder ITIs) and the **"government-owned, industry-managed"** formulation, which is a distinct governance idea — neither pure PPP nor pure state provision. The multilateral co-financing (ADB + World Bank) is also a testable detail.

**Facts to memorise.**

- Scheme outlay **₹60,000 crore**; approved by the Union Cabinet in **2025**; implemented by the **Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE)**.
- Financing split: **Centre ₹30,000 crore + States ₹20,000 crore + Industry ₹10,000 crore**. The **Asian Development Bank** and the **World Bank** co-finance up to **50% of the central share**, equally between them.
- Coverage: upgradation of **1,000 government ITIs** on a **hub-and-spoke** model, plus capacity augmentation of **five National Skill Training Institutes (NSTIs)** including **five National Centres of Excellence for Skilling**.
- Target: **20 lakh youth** skilled over **five years**.
- Pan-India rollout approved across **200 ITI clusters**.
- 8 September approvals: **Bhiwadi (Rajasthan) ₹241 crore**; **Medchal (Telangana) ₹254.30 crore**; **Meerut (Uttar Pradesh) ₹240.40 crore**.

**Notebook.** GS2 — Governance / Social Justice (with a GS3 Economy cross-entry on human capital and employment).

**Static anchor.** *Mrunal Economy* — **Human Resource Development / Skilling** pillar; and the **Education & Skilling** section of your GS2 Governance notes. Add a cross-link to the **National Education Policy 2020** target of **50% vocational exposure by 2025** and to the **Economic Survey** chapter on employment and the skills mismatch, so the scheme sits inside an argument rather than standing alone as a fact.

### C. Mains-Style Question (GS3 — Energy / Environment, 250 words, 15 marks)

> **"Coal gasification is being positioned as the bridge between India's coal endowment and its import-substitution goals, but it sits uneasily with the net-zero commitment. Examine."**

**Model-answer skeleton (4 points):**

1. **Frame the opportunity precisely.** Coal gasification converts coal/lignite into syngas (CO + H₂) and thence into urea, ammonia, methanol, synthetic natural gas and hydrogen — the exact basket where India runs large import bills. Anchor with the ₹37,500-crore scheme (Cabinet, May 2026), the ~75 MT gasification target, the National Mission target of 100 MT by 2030, and the Round 1 profile (urea-heavy applications from Adani Enterprises, NTPC's SNG project, Talcher Fertilisers). Add the resource logic: India's coal is high-ash and low-calorific-value, which suits gasification better than combustion.

2. **State the strategic case.** Energy and fertiliser security (reduced LNG, urea, ammonia, methanol imports); monetisation of domestic coal reserves as the power sector's coal demand plateaus; industrial feedstock for steel (DRI via syngas) that avoids coking-coal imports; forward linkage into hydrogen.

3. **State the climate and resource costs honestly.** Gasification is carbon- and water-intensive; lifecycle emissions of coal-derived ("grey"/"brown") hydrogen far exceed those of the green route; the entire climate case rests on CCUS, which is unproven at Indian scale and cost. Add land, ash-handling and just-transition concerns in the coal belt, and the risk of stranded assets if carbon pricing or CBAM-type border measures tighten.

4. **Resolve, don't hedge.** Argue for a *bounded* role: prioritise gasification where no clean substitute yet exists (fertiliser feedstock, DRI) rather than for power; make CCUS-readiness a condition of the capital subsidy; set a sunset date aligned with the 2070 net-zero pathway; and keep the incentive design product-capped, as the scheme already does, so it does not crowd out the National Green Hydrogen Mission. Close on the transition-fuel framing: a bridge is justified only if the far bank is specified.

### D. PYQ Mirror — how UPSC has asked these themes before

**Theme: the vulture collapse.** *UPSC CSE Prelims 2012* — "Vultures which used to be very common in Indian countryside some years ago are rarely seen nowadays. This is attributed to:" with options including destruction of nesting sites, a drug used by cattle owners for treating their diseased cattle, scarcity of food, and a widespread fatal disease. **Answer: the drug used by cattle owners (diclofenac).** *Takeaway:* UPSC tested the causal mechanism, not the species list — so learn the *why* before the *which*.

**Theme: coal and its environmental externalities.** *UPSC CSE Prelims 2020* — "Consider the following statements: 1. Coal ash contains arsenic, lead and mercury. 2. Coal-fired power plants release sulphur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen into the environment. 3. High ash content is observed in Indian coal." **Answer: all three are correct.** *Takeaway:* the high-ash characteristic of Indian coal is examinable in both directions — as a pollution problem and, as the gasification scheme shows, as a feedstock argument.

## Part C — Answers & Explanations

**Q1 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Diclofenac causes fatal renal failure and visceral gout in scavenging vultures; India banned its veterinary use in 2006. Statement 3 is wrong: the Egyptian Vulture is *Neophron percnopterus*, not a *Gyps* species; the White-rumped (*G. bengalensis*) and Indian (*G. indicus*) vultures are.

**Q2 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The scheme was cleared by the Cabinet on 13 May 2026 with a ₹37,500-crore outlay, replacing the ₹8,500-crore scheme of January 2024, and offers up to 20% of plant-and-machinery cost. Statement 3 is wrong: it is administered by the **Ministry of Coal**.

**Q3 — (c) Pulses.** Pulses rose to 117.13 lakh ha, up 1.83 lakh ha year-on-year (urad and moong). Rice (421.82 vs 438.19), cotton (109.17 vs 109.87) and oilseeds (184.46 vs 185.36) all declined.

**Q4 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.** Hazarika received the Dadasaheb Phalke Award in 1992 as the first Assamese recipient, the Bharat Ratna posthumously in 2019, and the Sangeet Natak Akademi Fellowship in 2008.

**Q5 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** PM-SETU upgrades 1,000 government ITIs on a hub-and-spoke model and is financed ₹30,000 cr (Centre) + ₹20,000 cr (States) + ₹10,000 cr (industry). Statement 3 is wrong: it is implemented by the **Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship**, not the Ministry of Education.

**Q6 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** The White-rumped Vulture and the Red-headed Vulture are Critically Endangered (as are the Indian and Slender-billed Vultures). The Himalayan Griffon is **Near Threatened**; the Egyptian Vulture is Endangered.

**Q7 — (a) 1 and 2 only.** Sattriya originates in the Sattras of the Ekasarana tradition, and Borgeet are the devotional songs of Sankardeva and Madhavdeva. Statement 3 is wrong: the Sangeet Natak Akademi recognised Sattriya as a classical dance form in **2000**, not 1958.
