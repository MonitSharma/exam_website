## Briefing

### 1. Great Nicobar: SC won't stay Calcutta HC hearings; "preservation of culture is a fundamental right" — Polity & Governance
**Exam angle:** On 5 Oct a bench of CJI Surya Kant, Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V. Mohana declined to interfere with the Calcutta HC order upholding maintainability of a PIL alleging Forest Rights Act violations (consent of tribal communities) in the project. Justice Bagchi's oral remark: protecting the Shompen's identity, not forcing integration, is a constitutionally protected right. A live test of development vs tribal rights vs ecology.
**Memorize:** Shompen = PVTG; HC next hearing 25–26 Nov; two earlier NGT proceedings exist; FRA 2006 requires gram sabha consent.
**Notebook:** GS2 Polity/Governance (also GS1 Society, GS3 Environment).
**Static anchor:** Laxmikanth — Fundamental Rights (Arts 21, 29) + Scheduled & Tribal Areas; *Update:* add "Great Nicobar 2026 – culture as a right" under Art 29(1) and link to PVTG list.

### 2. First international cargo from Bogibeel: Brahmaputra trade route reopens — Economy
**Exam angle:** On 5 Oct Sarbananda Sonowal flagged off barge DLB Patkai (tug Kushal Konwar) carrying 540 MT methanol from APCL, Namrup to Dhaka region via NW-2 and the Indo-Bangladesh Protocol Route. Reported as the first cargo from Upper Assam to a foreign port since 1947; ties to Act East, NE connectivity and Bangladesh relations.
**Memorize:** ~768 km on NW-2, ~1,300 km total; NW-2 = Brahmaputra, Sadiya–Dhubri; NW-1 = Ganga, Prayagraj–Haldia.
**Notebook:** GS3 Economy (Infrastructure/Logistics); GS2 IR (Bangladesh).
**Static anchor:** Ramesh Singh/Shankar — Transport: Inland Waterways; Spectrum Geography — Brahmaputra; *Update:* add 2026 Bogibeel cross-link to the National Waterways list.

### 3. India–Japan Joint Crediting Mechanism gets its Operational Manual — Environment
**Exam angle:** Launched 30 Sep in New Delhi by Japan's Ambassador and the MoEFCC Secretary. Covers the cycle from Project Idea Note to issuance and authorisation of credits under Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement. Eligible: green hydrogen, offshore wind, storage-backed renewables, CCUS. Relates to India's Carbon Credit Trading Scheme.
**Memorize:** MoC 7 Aug 2025 → Rules 8 Jun 2026 → Manual 30 Sep 2026; India NDC: 45% intensity cut by 2030, net zero 2070.
**Notebook:** GS3 Environment (also GS2 IR – Japan).
**Static anchor:** Shankar IAS — Climate Change: Paris Agreement & carbon markets; *Update:* add JCM as a worked Article 6.2 example next to CCTS.

### 4. India's supercomputing ecosystem: 40 indigenous systems, ~68 PF — Science & Technology
**Exam angle:** Under the National Supercomputing Mission (2015; ~₹4,500 cr; DST + MeitY; C-DAC and IISc), 40 systems with ~68 petaflops are deployed, using indigenous Rudra servers and Trinetra interconnects (100/200 Gbps). Mains lens: imported CPUs/GPUs remain the self-reliance gap.
**Memorize:** 40 systems / 68 PF; PARAM Rudra; Trinetra-A/B; uses: weather, drug screening, genomics, seismic imaging.
**Notebook:** GS3 Science & Technology.
**Static anchor:** Spectrum/Shankar S&T — Computing & Supercomputers (PARAM Siddhi, Pratyush); *Update:* add NSM progress figures; cross-link to semiconductor mission.

### 5. First National Household Travel Survey (NSO) — Reports & Indices
**Exam angle:** NSO (MoSPI) surveyed ~4.92 lakh households across 14,274 villages and 10,377 urban blocks (Jul 2025–Jun 2026). About two-thirds of students travel ≥1 km daily; walking leads (~37%), then buses (~29.5%) and cycles (~15.8%). Two-wheelers lead worker commutes (~42.6%); monthly commute spend ~₹785 for workers. Useful for urban transport and gender-mobility answers.
**Memorize:** Women workers commute less (41.9% vs 61.9%) and spend less (₹533 vs ₹835).
**Notebook:** GS1 Society / GS3 Economy (Infrastructure).
**Static anchor:** Mrunal — Urbanisation & Transport; *Update:* add NHTS as a data source in urban mobility and women's workforce notes.

### C. Mains Question
**Q8. Examine how infrastructure and development projects in ecologically and culturally sensitive regions can be reconciled with the constitutional protection of tribal communities, with reference to the Great Nicobar project. (15 marks, 250 words)**

Model-answer skeleton:
1. Context: strategic and economic case vs presence of the Shompen (PVTG), biodiversity and seismic risk.
2. Constitutional/legal basis: Art 21 and Art 29(1) (culture), Forest Rights Act 2006 (gram sabha consent), Andaman and Nicobar Protection of Aboriginal Tribes Regulation, NGT/EIA norms.
3. Challenges: consent and "forced integration" concerns, compensatory afforestation, the contested nature of procedural compliance.
4. Way forward: independent social-impact and ecological audit, binding safeguards for PVTG, phased development, transparent monitoring; judiciary as a check, as in the 5 Oct hearing.

## Answers
- **Q1: (b)** — Statements 1 and 2 match the reported hearing (bench of CJI Surya Kant, Justices Bagchi and Mohana; next High Court hearing 25-26 Nov). The Shompen are a Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group (PVTG) among Scheduled Tribes, not a Scheduled Caste, so 3 is wrong.
- **Q2: (c)** — The barge DLB Patkai carried ~540 MT methanol from APCL (Namrup) down ~768 km of NW-2 and on via the Indo-Bangladesh Protocol Route to the Dhaka region (~1,300 km in all). NW-1 is the Ganga (Prayagraj-Haldia), so 3 is wrong.
- **Q3: (d)** — All three are correct: MoC 7 Aug 2025, Rules of Implementation 8 Jun 2026, Operational Manual launched 30 Sep 2026 in New Delhi under Article 6.2 (bilateral ITMO-type cooperation).
- **Q4: (a)** — NSM (2015, about Rs 4,500 crore) is steered by DST and MeitY, not ISRO/DRDO, so 1 is wrong. C-DAC and IISc implement it, and processors/accelerators are still imported, though indigenous Rudra servers and Trinetra interconnects exist (40 systems, ~68 PF reported).
- **Q5: (c)** — NSO (MoSPI) ran the survey July 2025-June 2026 over about 4.92 lakh households. Walking is the leading student mode (~37%), and two-wheelers lead worker commutes (~42.6%).
- **Q6: (b)** — Art 29(1) protects a distinct language, script or culture of any section of citizens (not limited to minorities). Art 29(2) bars denial of admission on grounds of religion, race, caste or language but applies to citizens; Art 30 covers minorities' institutions.
- **Q7: (a)** — Art 6.2 = decentralised bilateral/plurilateral cooperation (ITMOs); Art 6.4 = Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism supervised by a body under the CMA. Article 6 is open to all Parties, so 3 is wrong.
