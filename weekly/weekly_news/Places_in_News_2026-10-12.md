## Indian places

**1. Ganga at Malda — Bhutni diara and Manikchak, Malda district, West Bengal.** Malda lies on the Ganga in northern West Bengal, with Bangladesh's Chapainawabganj across the river to the east; the Farakka barrage lies downstream to the south-east. *Why in news:* on **Oct 8** the state government announced ₹1.8 lakh per fully damaged home in flood-hit Malda, and IIT Kanpur was tasked with a permanent flood-solution report. The floods followed the Ganga breaching the ring embankment on Bhutni island in early September.

**2. Western Ghats — the draft Ecologically Sensitive Area (ESA), six states.** The Ghats run parallel to the west coast from the Tapi valley to Kanyakumari, with the Deccan plateau to the east. The July 2026 draft covers about 56,826 sq km across Karnataka (largest share), Kerala (including Wayanad villages), Goa, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu and Gujarat. *Why in news:* on **Sep 30** the Karnataka legislature unanimously opposed the ESA recommendations in their present form, after the Centre reissued the draft on **Jul 27**.

**3. ⚠ Srinagar airport — Jhelum valley, Jammu & Kashmir.** Srinagar sits on the Jhelum in the Kashmir valley, between the Pir Panjal (south) and the Zabarwan hills near Dal Lake. *Why in news:* flight operations were reported suspended from **Oct 1 to Oct 16** for essential work. This rests on a single regional aggregator; confirm with the airport notice before using.

## World places

**4. Gulf Coast — Mobile Bay (Alabama) to Choctawhatchee Bay (Florida Panhandle), USA.** Pensacola and Dauphin Island lie nearby on the northern Gulf of Mexico, with the Mississippi coast to the west. *Why in news:* **Hurricane Isaias**, the first Atlantic hurricane of 2026 (formed Oct 8, the latest first hurricane in 121 years), strengthened to Category 3 (120 mph) on Oct 9 and was forecast to make landfall on Friday night, Oct 9, between Mobile Bay and the Florida Panhandle. ⚠ I could not confirm the actual landfall or damage.

**5. Mount Ibu — West Halmahera, North Maluku, Indonesia.** On the Halmahera island in the Molucca Sea region of eastern Indonesia, with Ternate and Tidore lying to the west. *Why in news:* per a Databoks report, it erupted repeatedly on **Oct 8** (ash to about 400 m above the summit) and logged 27 eruptions in the week to that date. It remains at **Level II (Alert)**, with a 2 km exclusion zone and an extended 3.5 km zone to the north. ⚠ AI-translated source; verify with PVMBG.

**6. Mount Semeru — Lumajang, East Java, Indonesia.** The highest peak of Java, lying south of the Tengger caldera (Bromo is to its north-east), draining south towards the Indian Ocean through the Besuk Kobokan river. *Why in news:* it is at **Level III (Alert)** after repeated eruptions through **Oct 1–10**. Authorities bar all activity within 13 km in the south-east sector, and pyroclastic surges and lahars may reach 17 km from the summit. ⚠ AI-translated source.

**7. ⚠ Ili Lewotolok — Lembata Island, East Nusa Tenggara, Indonesia.** In the Lesser Sundas, east of Flores, with Adonara and Solor lying between them. *Why in news:* ash eruptions were reported on **Oct 2**, with a 2 km exclusion zone (3 km to the south and south-east). The latest source I could verify with dated detail is from Sep 6, and reports disagree on the alert label.

**8. Tambolaka, Sumba Island — East Nusa Tenggara, Indonesia.** Sumba lies south of Flores across the Sumba Strait, facing the Indian Ocean to the south. *Why in news:* a **magnitude 5.9** earthquake struck about 39 km WSW of Tambolaka on **Oct 3** at 46 km depth; USGS PAGER gave a green alert, meaning limited damage was expected.

**9. Campi Flegrei — Phlegraean Fields, west of Naples, Italy.** A restless caldera around the Gulf of Pozzuoli, with Vesuvius to the south-east and Ischia and Procida to the west. *Why in news:* a **Md 4.7** quake on **Jul 31** damaged buildings and disrupted transport; INGV counted **428 earthquakes in August** (largest Md 3.4, on Aug 10), with uplift of about 10 mm per month. ⚠ Latest verified figures are from August; check INGV for current status.

**10. Mount Olympus (Greece) — Pieria–Thessaly border, northern Greece.** Mytikas, the highest peak, rises above the Aegean, south of the Thermaic Gulf, near Litochoro. *Why in news:* the **wider area of Mount Olympus** was inscribed as a mixed natural–cultural World Heritage Site at the **48th World Heritage Committee, Busan (July 2026)**. This is a July listing rather than last-week news, included because it is a new UNESCO inscription.

**11. Boma–Badingilo Migratory Landscape — south-eastern South Sudan.** Boma lies in the east, close to the Ethiopian border between the White Nile and the Ethiopian highlands; Badingilo lies to the west, joined by a savannah corridor. *Why in news:* inscribed in **July 2026** as South Sudan's first World Heritage Site (the same session also added Comoros and São Tomé and Príncipe for the first time). A July listing, included for the same reason as item 10.

## Map / location MCQs

**Q1.** The Gulf Coast landfall forecast for Hurricane Isaias centred on Mobile Bay, which lies on the coast of which US state?
a) Texas  b) Alabama  c) Louisiana  d) Mississippi

**Q2.** **Mount Semeru**, at Level III in early October, lies immediately south of which caldera complex?
a) Toba  b) Tengger (Bromo)  c) Batur  d) Krakatau

**Q3.** **Mount Ibu**, erupting repeatedly on Oct 8, lies on which island of North Maluku?
a) Sulawesi  b) Halmahera  c) Seram  d) Flores

**Q4.** **Boma–Badingilo**, South Sudan's first World Heritage Site, lies close to the border of which country?
a) Uganda  b) Kenya  c) Ethiopia  d) Central African Republic

### Answer key

- **Q1 — b** (Alabama; Mobile Bay lies on its Gulf coast, with Choctawhatchee Bay in Florida to the east)
- **Q2 — b** (Tengger caldera, which contains Bromo; Semeru lies to its south)
- **Q3 — b** (Halmahera, the largest island of North Maluku)
- **Q4 — c** (Ethiopia; Boma sits between the White Nile and the Ethiopian highlands)
