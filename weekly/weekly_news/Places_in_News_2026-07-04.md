## Indian places

**1. Dhemaji & Lakhimpur districts — Assam.** On the north bank of the **Brahmaputra**, in Upper Assam near the Arunachal Pradesh border; the **Simen river** is a local Brahmaputra tributary here. *Why:* the **first major flood wave of the 2026 monsoon** hit six districts of Assam and Arunachal Pradesh, affecting 22,000+ people, submerging ~100 villages and 1,600+ hectares, and partially collapsing a railway bridge over the Simen at Simen Chapari.

**2. Dzongu — North Sikkim (Mangan district).** A triangular Lepcha reserve region bounded by the **Teesta river** to the southeast and the **Kanchenjunga** massif to the west; lies on the road corridor to **Lachen and Lachung** (both at the confluence of Teesta tributaries). *Why:* a **Bailey bridge over the Phee Khola washed away** at Phidang in Dzongu in the current monsoon spell, cutting road connectivity to Lachen and Lachung.

**3. Jaintia Hills — Meghalaya.** Eastern Meghalaya on the **Shillong plateau**, drained by the **Myntdu river**, which flows south into Bangladesh (joining the Surma–Meghna system). *Why:* the **Myntdu-Leshka Stage II (210 MW) hydro project** — one of several Meghalaya dams planned on rivers shared with Bangladesh — is nearing financial closure, reviving **transboundary river-sharing concerns** downstream.

**4. Kangpokpi — Manipur (Sadar Hills).** North of Imphal in the hill districts, straddling **National Highway-2** (Imphal–Dimapur, via Nagaland) and **NH-37** (Imphal–Silchar). *Why:* the Kuki-Zo body **CoTU issued a 48-hour ultimatum (from June 27)** demanding removal of alleged unauthorised checkpoints on these highways, escalating Manipur's ongoing ethnic stand-off after the deadline lapsed.

**5. Malappuram & Palakkad districts — Kerala.** Central Kerala, inland of the Malabar Coast, between the **Nilgiri** spur of the Western Ghats and the Arabian Sea lowlands. *Why:* Kerala's recurring **Nipah virus** spillover claimed a fresh case-death in Malappuram (July 1) and produced **Palakkad's first-ever Nipah case** — widening the outbreak beyond the usual Kozhikode hot spot.

**6. Sriharikota — Andhra Pradesh.** A barrier island on the Andhra coast between the **Bay of Bengal** and **Pulicat Lake** (India's second-largest brackish-water lagoon), just north of Chennai. *Why:* the **Satish Dhawan Space Centre** is gearing up for its **July 2026 GSLV Mk II launch**, keeping India's spaceport in the news.

## World places

**7. Medog (Motuo) / Yarlung Tsangpo gorge — Tibet, China.** The world's deepest canyon, where the **Yarlung Tsangpo** loops around **Namcha Barwa** before entering India as the **Brahmaputra** (Arunachal Pradesh/Assam) and, further downstream, Bangladesh's **Jamuna**. *Why:* construction advanced on China's ~60 GW mega-dam cascade here — the "project of the century" — deepening India's and Bangladesh's concerns over unilateral control of a 130-million-strong river basin.

**8. Indus basin — Pakistan (Punjab/Sindh; Chenab & Jhelum).** The Indus and its western tributaries, the **Chenab** and **Jhelum**, flow from J&K/Ladakh through Pakistani Punjab to the Arabian Sea near **Karachi**. *Why:* Islamabad **hosted an international conference on the Indus Waters Treaty** this week to contest India's continued suspension (in abeyance since the 2025 Pahalgam attack), with Pakistan's defence minister renewing war rhetoric.

**9. Gaza Strip — Gaza City / "Yellow Line."** The Mediterranean coastal enclave between Israel and Egypt's Sinai; Gaza City sits in the north. *Why:* marking **1,000 days since the war began**, Israel pressed to expand military control to over 70% of the Strip beyond the so-called Yellow Line, even as a nominal ceasefire remains in place.

**10. Tobelo — North Halmahera, North Maluku, Indonesia.** On Halmahera's northeastern coast in the **Molucca Sea**, part of Indonesia's volcanic-seismic arc (Ibu volcano, covered here last week, lies on the same island). *Why:* a **magnitude-6.2 earthquake struck near Tobelo on July 3** — a fresh seismic event distinct from Ibu's ongoing ash eruptions.

**11. Iwate Prefecture — Japan.** On the **Sanriku coast** of northeastern Honshu, facing the Pacific and the offshore **Japan Trench**. *Why:* a **magnitude-6.0 earthquake struck off Iwate's coast on July 1**, in one of the world's most closely monitored subduction zones.

## Quick map MCQs

**Q1.** The Yarlung Tsangpo becomes which river after entering India?
a) Indus  b) Sutlej  c) Brahmaputra  d) Ganga

**Q2.** The Myntdu river, at the centre of a new Meghalaya hydro project, ultimately drains into which transboundary river system?
a) Padma  b) Surma–Meghna  c) Teesta  d) Barak

**Q3.** Which two rivers are the main western tributaries of the Indus flowing through Pakistani Punjab?
a) Ravi & Beas  b) Chenab & Jhelum  c) Sutlej & Beas  d) Kabul & Swat

**Q4.** Dzongu, cut off after a bridge washout this week, lies in which Indian state?
a) Arunachal Pradesh  b) Sikkim  c) West Bengal  d) Uttarakhand

**Q5.** Iwate Prefecture, hit by a fresh earthquake, is on which coast of Japan?
a) Sanriku (Pacific) coast  b) Sea of Japan coast  c) Seto Inland Sea  d) Ryukyu coast

### Answer key
1 — **c** (Brahmaputra — the Yarlung Tsangpo's name after crossing into Arunachal Pradesh)
2 — **b** (Surma–Meghna system, via Bangladesh)
3 — **b** (Chenab and Jhelum; the Ravi, Beas and Sutlej are the eastern tributaries)
4 — **b** (Sikkim — Dzongu lies in North Sikkim's Mangan district)
5 — **a** (the Sanriku/Pacific coast of NE Honshu)
