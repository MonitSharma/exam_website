## Indian places

**1. Nagpur — Maharashtra.**
Central India, on the **Deccan plateau** in the Vidarbha region, on the **Nag river**; the **Zero Mile Stone** marks it as roughly the geographic centre of India, and it is the gateway to **Tadoba-Andhari Tiger Reserve**. *Why in news:* host of the main **International Tiger Day (July 29)** event — an International Tiger Conference at **IIM Nagpur** that released the **"Nagpur Tiger Declaration,"** as India runs its **6th All-India Tiger Estimation (2026 census)**.

**2. Sarnath — Uttar Pradesh.**
On the **Ganga plains** just north of **Varanasi**, near the **Ganga–Varuna** confluence; where the Buddha gave his first sermon. *Why in news:* the **Ancient Buddhist Site of Sarnath was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List** at the **48th World Heritage Committee session in Busan (July 2026)** — India's **45th** World Heritage Site.

**3. Bandipora & Wular Lake — Jammu & Kashmir.**
North Kashmir, in the **Jhelum valley** at the foot of the **Harmukh** peak; **Wular Lake** is **India's largest freshwater lake** (a Ramsar site), fed and drained by the **Jhelum**. *Why in news:* a spell of **15–16 cloudbursts in the first fortnight of July** (a cloudburst hit the **Nagmarg** area of Bandipora); a swelling **Jhelum and Wular near the flood mark** put lakeside villages on evacuation alert (28+ deaths across J&K in July).

**4. Chamba — Himachal Pradesh.**
Western Himalaya, on the **Ravi river**, cradled between the **Dhauladhar** and **Pir Panjal** ranges in the state's northwest. *Why in news:* **flash floods in streams around Chamba** during the July monsoon spell washed away a road, killing five members of a family — part of the wider July 2026 Himalayan flood-and-landslide disaster (50+ dead nationwide).

**5. Amrabad Tiger Reserve — Telangana.**
On the **Nallamala Hills** of the Eastern Ghats, in the Deccan south of the **Krishna river** (below the **Nagarjunasagar** reservoir), spanning Nagarkurnool district. *Why in news:* **preliminary 2026 census data showed a 16.6% rise in tigers** (36 → 42) — cited around **International Tiger Day** as evidence of recovery in a southern reserve.

## World places

**6. Kumamoto / Kyushu — Japan.**
On **Kyushu**, the southernmost of Japan's four main islands, near **Mount Aso** (one of the world's largest calderas) and facing the **Ariake Sea**. *Why in news:* a **magnitude-6.8 (JMA 7.1) earthquake struck Kumamoto on July 28**, killing 30+, collapsing part of a shopping mall and briefly triggering a tsunami warning — a fresh event distinct from the earlier off-Iwate quake.

**7. Chiapas coast / Gulf of Tehuantepec — Mexico.**
On the **Pacific coast** of southern Mexico near the **Guatemala border**; the **Gulf of Tehuantepec** lies just west, above the boundary where the **Cocos plate subducts under the North American plate**. *Why in news:* a **Mw 7.3 earthquake struck offshore on July 17**, prompting a Pacific tsunami alert for Mexico and Guatemala (waves stayed small) and a busy aftershock sequence.

**8. El Obeid & North Kordofan — Sudan.**
Capital of **North Kordofan** in **west-central Sudan**, on the **Sahel** belt west of the White Nile and Khartoum (well east of Darfur's El Fasher). *Why in news:* North Kordofan became a **hotspot of the RSF–army war**, with 50+ drone strikes recorded in the contest to control the state capital **El Obeid**.

**9. Western Niger (Tillabéri / Liptako-Gourma) — Niger.**
The **Tillabéri** region of far-western Niger, in the **Liptako-Gourma "three-borders" zone** where **Niger, Mali and Burkina Faso** meet along the **Niger river** in the Sahel. *Why in news:* western Niger became the **main battleground between JNIM (al-Qaeda-linked) and Islamic State Sahel Province** as both intensified attacks in July.

**10. Comoros · São Tomé and Príncipe · South Sudan — first-time World Heritage countries.**
Two Indian/Atlantic island states and one landlocked African country: **Comoros** in the **Mozambique Channel** (between Madagascar and the East African mainland), **São Tomé and Príncipe** in the **Gulf of Guinea** (off West-Central Africa, near the Equator), and **South Sudan** on the **White Nile / Sudd** wetlands. *Why in news:* at the **Busan session**, all three received their **first-ever UNESCO World Heritage inscriptions** (25 new sites added in total).

**11. Loyalty Islands — New Caledonia (France).**
A chain (Lifou, Maré, Ouvéa) in the **southwest Pacific / Coral Sea**, east of New Caledonia's main island, near the seismically active **New Hebrides–Vanuatu trench**. *Why in news:* a **magnitude-6.7 earthquake struck southeast of the Loyalty Islands on July 13**, a reminder of the southwest Pacific's place on the **Ring of Fire**.

## Quick map MCQs

**Q1.** Wular Lake, near the flood mark this July, is India's largest freshwater lake and is fed by which river?
a) Chenab   b) Jhelum   c) Ravi   d) Indus

**Q2.** Sarnath, newly inscribed as India's 45th UNESCO World Heritage Site, lies just north of which city?
a) Gaya   b) Prayagraj   c) Varanasi   d) Kushinagar

**Q3.** The Kumamoto earthquake of July 28 struck which of Japan's four main islands, home to the vast Mount Aso caldera?
a) Honshu   b) Hokkaido   c) Shikoku   d) Kyushu

**Q4.** The July 17 Mw 7.3 quake off Chiapas occurred where which oceanic plate subducts beneath the North American plate?
a) Nazca   b) Cocos   c) Juan de Fuca   d) Caribbean

**Q5.** Amrabad Tiger Reserve, which reported a 16.6% rise in tigers, lies on which hill range?
a) Satpura   b) Aravalli   c) Nallamala   d) Nilgiri

**Q6.** The Loyalty Islands, hit by a July 13 earthquake, are part of which territory?
a) Fiji   b) Vanuatu   c) New Caledonia   d) Solomon Islands

### Answer key
**Q1 — b** (Jhelum; Wular lies in the Jhelum valley of north Kashmir)
**Q2 — c** (Varanasi; Sarnath is just to its north, near the Ganga–Varuna)
**Q3 — d** (Kyushu, the southernmost main island; Mount Aso is in Kumamoto)
**Q4 — b** (Cocos plate, subducting off the Gulf of Tehuantepec)
**Q5 — c** (Nallamala Hills, Telangana, south of the Krishna)
**Q6 — c** (New Caledonia, a French territory in the SW Pacific)
