## GS Mains — answer skeletons

### M1 — Electoral rolls (GS2)
- **Intro:** the vote is only as good as the roll. Universal franchise (Art 326) depends on the roll existing in practice.
- **Framework:** Art 324 (superintendence), Art 325 (one general roll, no exclusion by religion, race, caste or sex), Art 326; RPA 1950 (preparation, revision, s.21 special revision, s.22–23 correction and inclusion); Registration of Electors Rules 1960 (Forms 6/7/8); ERO/AERO/BLO hierarchy; appeal to the DEO and the CEO.
- **Challenges:** (i) migrant and seasonal workers marked "absent" → exclusion errors; (ii) data-driven flagging ("unmapped") without field verification; (iii) burden of proof moved onto the voter (documents, hearings); (iv) capacity, i.e. BLO shortages and tight timelines; (v) partisan pressure and loss of trust; (vi) inclusion errors (deaths, duplicates).
- **Recent:** the EC dropped mandatory hearings for unmapped voters, moved to BLO home visits, restored the old Form 6, and set up an ECINET compliance review (Sept 2026). This shows course-correction under scrutiny.
- **Way forward:** door-to-door verification before deletion; notice and a hearing before removal (natural justice); publish deletions ward-wise; Aadhaar linkage only on a voluntary basis; a multi-member EC that decides collectively and transparently.
- **Conclusion:** deletion errors silence a citizen and inclusion errors dilute the vote, but the two are not symmetric harms. The design should therefore default to inclusion.

### M2 — Green Energy Corridor-III (GS3)
- **Intro:** India's 500 GW non-fossil target for 2030 — capacity additions now outrun the grid.
- **Why evacuation binds:** RE sites (Rajasthan, Gujarat, Ladakh) are far from load centres; transmission takes 4–5 years against 18 months for solar; curtailment and stranded capacity follow; intra-state networks (InSTS) are the weak link.
- **Why storage binds:** solar peaks at midday while demand peaks in the evening; RE is variable; a grid needs inertia and frequency support; batteries and pumped storage provide firming.
- **GEC-III:** ₹1.86 lakh cr in total, of which ₹1.36 lakh cr is for InSTS and ₹50,000 cr for **50 GWh BESS** (the first GEC phase with storage built in); CFA ₹54,082 cr; 135 GW evacuation by FY 2032-33; greenfield lines under TBCB.
- **Remaining gaps:** DISCOM finances, right-of-way, battery supply chains (dependence on China for cells), market design (ancillary services, time-of-day tariffs), skills.
- **Conclusion:** the transition is moving from a megawatt race to a systems problem, and GEC-III is the first budget that is built that way.

## GS4 case — model approach

**(a) Ethical issues.** Universal franchise against roll purity; political pressure against impartiality; expediency ("nobody will blame you") against duty; procedural fairness (notice and hearing before deletion); the vulnerability of migrant workers; accountability for errors in both directions. **Stakeholders:** the 9,000 electors (especially the ~85% who are genuine), the MLA, the opposition party, the CEO/ECI, the BLOs, the courts, and the public's trust in elections.

**(b) Options.**
1. **Delete the MLA's list.** This is fast and avoids conflict. But it is partisan, unverified, illegal without notice under the RPA 1950 and the 1960 Rules, and likely to be struck down in court. Reject.
2. **Delete nothing and keep all 9,000.** This is safe on exclusion, but it ignores the ~15% genuine deaths and duplicates and breaches the duty to keep the roll accurate. Weak.
3. **Delete all unmapped names to "clean" the roll.** This mass-disenfranchises genuine voters. Reject.
4. **Verify, then decide on evidence.** Prioritise field checks; give notice before any deletion; use the record from the 200-name sample; reach migrants through employers, labour departments and construction-site camps; ask the CEO for more time and staff. Slower, but lawful, fair and defensible. **Choose.**

**(c) What I will do.** (i) Acknowledge the MLA's list **in writing** and treat it like any other Form-7 objection: it gets notice and verification, not automatic deletion. (ii) Report the pressure and the sample findings to the CEO, and ask for extra BLOs and, if needed, an extension of the deadline. (iii) Run **door-to-door verification plus outreach at work sites**, with special camps on Sundays. (iv) Delete only names that are verified as dead, duplicate or shifted, each with a recorded reason. Publish the deletion list ward-wise and leave a window for re-inclusion. (v) Brief all parties together, so that no party gets privileged access.
**Justification:** neutrality is a matter of using the same procedure for every party, which is different from keeping equal distance from all of them. When in doubt, the error should fall on the side of keeping the citizen on the roll. Gandhi's talisman applies here: the migrant labourer away on a site is "the poorest and weakest" person in this case.

## Essay — model approach: "The means are the ends in the making."

- **Hook:** Gandhi in *Hind Swaraj*: you cannot get a rose by planting a noxious weed. The relation between means and ends is the same as that between a seed and a tree.
- **Thesis:** ends are not destinations reached after the means stop. The means *become* the end, in institutions, in habits and in character.
- **Body 1 — Individual:** character is formed by repeated means. A student who cheats to get a rank becomes a cheat with a rank. (Bridge to integrity and civil-service ethics.)
- **Body 2 — Polity:** a democracy won through rigged rolls or defections teaches its citizens that power is taken, not consented to. Anti-defection and electoral integrity are means that shape the kind of democracy that results. Ambedkar's warning against the "grammar of anarchy" in the Constituent Assembly makes the same point.
- **Body 3 — Economy and development:** growth driven by environmental damage produces a degraded future (the Western Ghats, Delhi's air). Inclusive means such as skilling and social sectors produce inclusive ends.
- **Body 4 — Nations and the world:** India's freedom struggle relied on non-violence and mass participation, and so produced a mass democracy. Revolutions won through terror have often produced terror states.
- **Counter-view:** sometimes ends justify hard means, as with emergency powers or deterrence. Answer: what matters is proportion and the possibility of reversal. Hard means that are bounded by law (Art 22 safeguards, judicial review) protect the end; unbounded ones replace it.
- **Conclusion:** return to the rose and the weed. Choosing means is choosing which ends you will actually reach.
