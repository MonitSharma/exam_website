### Mains skeletons

**M1 — Directive: CRITICALLY EXAMINE** (weigh both sides, then take a reasoned position).
1. *Intro:* The 1857 revolt resists a single label — between a mere mutiny and a planned national war.
2. **More than a sepoy mutiny:** spread beyond barracks to peasants, taluqdars, artisans; civil rebellion in Awadh, Bundelkhand, Bihar; grievances economic (drain, dispossession) + political (annexations, Doctrine of Lapse) + socio-religious (cartridge, missionary fear).
3. **Yet short of national independence:** no unified leadership/ideology, limited geography (south & most of Punjab/Bengal aloof), feudal not modern-nationalist aims, restoring old order (Bahadur Shah) not building a nation.
4. **Significance (watershed):** end of Company rule → Crown (GoI Act 1858); reorganised army; policy of divide; seedbed of later nationalism — the "first war" in memory if not in fact.
5. *Conclusion:* Best read as a **transitional, popular-yet-pre-national upheaval** — its failure of unity is exactly what later nationalists learned from. *(Mirrors UPSC 2016 & 2019.)*

**M2 — Directive: CRITICALLY EXAMINE.**
1. *Intro:* GDP captures the *volume* of market output — vital, but a partial mirror of progress.
2. **What GDP gets right:** comparability, growth tracking, fiscal/monetary anchor, investor signal.
3. **What it misses:** distribution/inequality, non-market work, environmental depletion, well-being/health/education, informal economy — "jobless" and "polluting" growth still raise GDP.
4. **Beyond-GDP measures:** HDI, MPI, Green/Inclusive GDP, GNH, SDG dashboards.
5. *Conclusion:* GDP is necessary but not sufficient — pair it with **distribution + sustainability + human-development** metrics to measure *direction*, not just speed.

### Ethics case — model approach

- **Stakeholders:** you (SDM); the ~5,000 distressed worker-families (vulnerable, owed payouts); the private insurer (contractual + reputational interest); your senior/the State (continuity, fiscal prudence, image); the NGO (advocacy, independent data); the courts; and **public trust in welfare delivery**.
- **Core ethical issues:** integrity & truth-telling vs loyalty to a superior; **duty of care to the vulnerable** (Art. 21 livelihood) vs institutional convenience; transparency vs reputational management; evidence-based administration vs political expedience.
- **Options:** (i) **Endorse the insurer & deflect to courts** — *rejected*: betrays the suffering, abdicates duty, suppresses truth. (ii) **Leak the NGO data anonymously** — *rejected*: deceptive, undermines due process and your own authority. (iii) **Act on the record:** convene insurer + IMD + NGO, get an authoritative reading, invoke the contract's trigger, escalate in writing, and bridge with disaster-relief funds while the dispute is resolved — *preferred*.
- **Recommended action:** treat the payout as a **time-critical relief obligation**: (1) immediately release interim support from SDRF/contingency to the worst-hit; (2) convene a **joint verification** (IMD + insurer + independent data) to settle the temperature reading on record; (3) enforce the contractual auto-trigger and **put your objection to any stalling in writing** up the chain; (4) keep communication factual and humane — no media games, no scapegoating the clerk-level staff; (5) protect the NGO's data by *using* it officially rather than leaking it.
