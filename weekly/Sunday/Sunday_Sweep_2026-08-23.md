### Prelims quiz — key and explanations

**Q1 — (a) 1, 2 and 3 only.**
Article 262(1) lets Parliament provide for adjudication of inter-State river water disputes and 262(2) lets it bar the jurisdiction of *all* courts including the Supreme Court — statement 1 is correct as a statement of the *power*. The CWMA was created by the **Cauvery Water Management Scheme, 2018**, framed under **Section 6A of the ISRWD Act, 1956** (2 correct), and the **CWRC functions under the CWMA** (3 correct). Statement 4 is the trap: **"Water" is Entry 17 of the State List**, subject to Entry 56 of the Union List (regulation and development of inter-State rivers to the extent Parliament declares it expedient in the public interest). It is *not* in the Concurrent List.

**Q2 — (b) 2 and 3 only.**
SAFTA is a **free trade area**, not a customs union — members retain their own external tariffs, which is exactly why Rules of Origin are needed to stop **trade deflection** (goods from a non-member being routed through a member to capture the preference). Statement 1 is therefore wrong and statement 2 right. **CAROTAR 2020**, resting on **Section 28DA** of the Customs Act, 1962, shifts the burden onto the *importer* to possess origin information rather than merely produce a certificate — 3 is correct.

**Q3 — (d) 1, 2 and 3.**
COP17 to the UNCCD was held at **Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia** — the first UNCCD COP hosted by Mongolia. **Q4 — (a) 1, 2 and 3 only.**

Armenia is landlocked in the South Caucasus, bordered by **Turkey (west), Georgia (north), Azerbaijan (east, and Nakhchivan to the south-west) and Iran (south)**; **Lake Sevan** is within Armenia. **Mount Ararat**, though a national symbol of Armenia, lies **across the border in Turkey** — statement 4 is false. ### M1 — GS2 skeleton (Judicial review vs judicial activism, via PIL)

**Frame (2–3 lines).** Judicial review is a *defensive* power — testing State action against the Constitution. Judicial activism is a *constructive* posture — supplying content and remedies the political branches have not. PIL is the procedural bridge across which the first became the second.

**Body 1 — the doctrinal base.** Judicial review is nowhere named but is textually anchored (Art 13, 32, 131–136, 143, 226, 246, 251, 254, 372) and was made irremovable by **Kesavananda (1973)**, reaffirmed in **Indira Nehru Gandhi (1975)**, **Minerva Mills (1980)** and **I.R. Coelho (2007)**. So far the Court is a check, not an actor.

**Body 2 — how PIL changed the posture.** The enabling move was the **relaxation of *locus standi*** (*S.P. Gupta*, 1981) and epistolary jurisdiction; the enabling remedies were **Art 32/226 read with Art 142** and **continuing mandamus**. Trace the arc with four cases and one line each: *Hussainara Khatoon* (1979, speedy trial as part of Art 21), *Olga Tellis* (1985), *Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum* (1996, precautionary principle and polluter pays imported into domestic law), *Vishaka* (1997, guidelines with the force of law in a legislative vacuum).

**Body 3 — the case that the line has blurred.** Guideline-making that is legislative in substance; monitoring that is executive in substance (environment, police reform, mid-day meals); the Art 142 problem — a power to do "complete justice" that has no articulated limits; the institutional cost — a docket crisis and delayed ordinary justice; the democratic-accountability objection.

**Body 4 — the defence.** Legislative and executive default; the vindication test — Vishaka became the 2013 Act, *Hussainara* became CrPC 436A; the Court's own filters (*Balwant Singh Chaufal*, 2010; costs for frivolous PILs); Art 39A as constitutional authorisation for access-to-justice activism.

**Conclusion.** The distinction is not to be restored by retreat but by **self-limitation with reasons** — activism justified where a right is enforceable and the political branch has defaulted; restraint where the question is polycentric and budgetary. Suggest one concrete measure: reasoned articulation of the limits of Art 142.

### M2 — GS3 skeleton (Price support vs income support)

**Frame.** MSP is announced for 23 crops and effectively realised for two or three, in two or three states. That gap is the whole question.

**Body 1 — the narrowness.** Procurement concentration in paddy and wheat, Punjab–Haryana–MP; NSSO/NABARD-type evidence that only a small minority of farmers sell at MSP; CACP's advisory, non-statutory character; A2+FL vs C2 and why the choice of cost concept changes the number materially.

**Body 2 — the costs.** *Fiscal* — food subsidy, FCI carrying cost, buffer stocks far above norms, fertiliser and power subsidies that ride alongside. *Ecological* — groundwater depletion in the north-west, stubble burning and the NCR air-quality externality, soil nutrient imbalance from skewed urea pricing, monoculture and lost pulse/oilseed acreage. *Allocative* — price signals that no longer reflect scarcity or demand. *External* — WTO Amber Box, the AoP de minimis limits, and the public-stockholding peace clause dispute.

**Body 3 — the case for price support.** Risk aversion in the absence of insurance; the thin, fragmented output market; MSP as a floor that anchors private-trade expectations even where procurement does not occur; political economy — the 2020–21 farm-law episode showed that the perceived security value of MSP exceeds its measured incidence.

**Body 4 — can income support substitute?** PM-KISAN and the Telangana Rythu Bandhu / Odisha KALIA experience; the design questions that decide the answer — **tenancy and land-record exclusion**, per-hectare vs per-household, adequacy of the transfer, and whether decoupled support actually shifts cropping without a parallel procurement guarantee for alternatives. Note the WTO advantage of decoupled (Green Box) support.

**Conclusion.** Not substitution but **sequencing** — a decoupled income floor, procurement diversification into pulses/oilseeds/millets, deficiency-price-payment pilots (Bhavantar-type) with honest reporting of their failures, and marketing reform that is negotiated rather than imposed.

### GS4 case study — model approach

**(a) Ethical issues.**
Truthfulness and the integrity of a submission to a court, against loyalty to the State's litigation position. Objectivity and impartiality in administration, against local political capture. Compassion for a farming population facing crop failure, against the duty to maintain public order and the proportionate use of force. Transparency and the citizen's right to know, against the propriety of a serving officer commenting publicly on inter-State litigation. Beneath all four: the **conflict between accountability upward (to the State government) and accountability outward (to the Constitution and the court)**.

**(b) The confidential request — options and choice.**
*Option 1: comply and omit internal distribution failures.* Attractive — protects the State's case, protects your posting, avoids embarrassing powerful local interests. Fatal — a statement filed in the Supreme Court that omits a material fact is not diplomacy, it is a misleading of the court, and it corrupts the very adjudication the district depends on. It also fails on consequence: if the omission surfaces later, it damages the State's credibility in the same litigation far more than candour would have.
*Option 2: refuse outright and escalate publicly.* Honest but disproportionate; it converts a correctable request into a confrontation and forfeits your ability to influence the outcome.
*Option 3 (adopted): file a complete and accurate statement, and manage the relationship professionally.* Submit the water-availability statement with **full factual accuracy**, including the tail-end tank holdings and the internal distribution failure. Separate **fact from characterisation** — report quantities, dates and gauged flows; do not editorialise about the upper riparian State. In an accompanying note, place the internal failure in its proper frame: it is a distribution problem you have already begun to correct, and correcting it strengthens rather than weakens the State's standing as a good-faith litigant. Speak to the senior official **before** filing, on record in writing, so that the request is not repeated and no one is ambushed.

*On the other three threads, briefly:* release the tail-end tank water immediately under your own authority, with a written order and a public announcement — it is lawful, it is roughly a quarter of the shortfall, and it also removes the moral inconsistency in your position. On the blockade, meet the farmers' representatives before Monday with the release order in hand and a published day-by-day water schedule; instruct the SP to plan for **graded, minimum-force containment with videography** and to keep the arterial route partially open rather than attempt an early dispersal that injures protestors. Decline the on-record television interview as a serving officer in a matter *sub judice*; offer instead a **factual written press note** from the district administration, which serves the public's information interest without prejudicing the case.

**(c) Long-term institutional measures.**
Publish a **real-time district water dashboard** — reservoir and tank levels, releases, gauged inflows — so that internal distribution cannot quietly diverge from the official position. Audit and cancel informal upstream allocation arrangements within the district, with a written, rule-based rotational schedule (*warabandi*-type) placed in the public domain. Institutionalise a **standing farmers' consultative committee** that meets at the start of every season so that the first contact in a crisis is not a blockade. Push, through the State, for **automated telemetric gauging** at inter-State measuring points, which removes the factual dispute from the political dispute. And build crop-diversification and micro-irrigation incentives into the district plan, because a district whose entire kharif hinges on fifteen days of releases has a structural problem that no amount of good administration will solve in-season.

### Essay — model approach

> **"Justice delayed is justice denied; justice hurried is justice buried."**

**What the topic actually asks.** It is a **paradox essay**, not a law essay. The two halves are both true, they pull in opposite directions, and the examiner is watching for whether you notice that the tension is *productive* rather than merely clever. A candidate who writes 1200 words on pendency has answered half the topic. A candidate who writes on the value of due process has answered the other half. The mark is in the synthesis.

**Suggested structure.**

*Opening (150 words).* Concrete, not abstract. An undertrial who has served longer awaiting trial than the maximum sentence for the offence charged; or a land dispute resolved in the fourth generation of the family that began it. Then name the paradox in one line: the same clock that starves justice of meaning also protects it from error.

*Section 1 — Delay as denial (250 words).* Pendency across the pyramid; undertrials as a share of the prison population; the economic cost of contract-enforcement delay and its effect on investment and on the weak litigant, who cannot outlast a rich adversary; **Art 21 and speedy trial as a fundamental right** (*Hussainara Khatoon*); **CrPC 436A**. The deeper point: delay redistributes justice toward whoever can afford to wait, so it is not merely inefficiency, it is inequality.

*Section 2 — Haste as burial (250 words).* Due process exists because certainty is expensive and error is irreversible. Speedy-trial courts that convict without adequate defence; the record of wrongful convictions and acquittals after decades; "encounter" killings applauded as fast justice; the mob and the media trial as the purest forms of hurried justice. Executions and the irreversibility argument. Move beyond the courtroom: hurried legislation without committee scrutiny, hurried demolitions, hurried administrative penalties.

*Section 3 — The synthesis (250 words).* The maxims are not opposites; they are two failure modes of the same variable. What both demand is not speed or slowness but **proportionate, predictable time**: fast where liberty is at stake and facts are simple (bail, remand, small causes), deliberate where consequences are irreversible (capital sentencing, constitutional questions). Introduce the distinction between **delay** (unplanned, systemic, arbitrary) and **deliberation** (planned, purposive, reasoned). Delay is the enemy; deliberation is the point.

*Section 4 — Widening the aperture (200 words).* The maxim applies beyond courts. In administration — a pension file, a compensation claim, an FIR not registered. In transitional justice — commissions that report a generation late. In personal and social life — an apology offered too late, a reform conceded only after the crisis. Draw one or two examples from outside law; the essay lifts here or it doesn't.

*Conclusion (150 words).* Do not resolve the paradox falsely. Argue that a just system is one that has **decided in advance how much time each kind of decision deserves**, and then keeps to it — arrears reduction, judicial vacancies filled, case-flow management, ADR and Lok Adalats, plea bargaining used honestly, but all of it inside a commitment to procedure rather than instead of it. Close on the human scale you opened with.

**Two things to avoid.** Do not turn it into a listicle of judicial reforms — the examiner has read a hundred of those. And do not spend more than a fifth of the essay on statistics; the topic is philosophical and the marks are in the reasoning.
