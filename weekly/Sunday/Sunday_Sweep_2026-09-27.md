## GS Mains — answer skeletons

### M1 — Tribunalisation (GS2)
- **Intro:** Tribunals — 42nd Amendment (Swaran Singh Committee) created Part XIVA to cut arrears and add expertise.
- **Rationale:** specialisation (tax, service, environment, telecom); speed; decongesting HCs; technical members.
- **Judicial rebuke:** *S.P. Sampath Kumar* (1987) accepted tribunals as substitutes; ***L. Chandra Kumar* (1997)** — review by HC/SC is basic structure, so tribunals supplement rather than replace courts; the *Madras Bar Association* line (2010, 2014, 2020, 2021) — independence, tenure, selection committees dominated by the executive; Tribunals Reforms Act 2021 re-enacted provisions the SC had struck down.
- **Problems:** vacancies, parent-ministry control (funding, infrastructure), short tenures inviting executive favour, appeals direct to SC bypassing HCs.
- **Way forward:** National Tribunals Commission (SC-recommended), uniform service conditions, placement under Law Ministry, 5-year tenure minimum, performance audit.
- **Conclusion:** specialisation is compatible with independence only when the executive that litigates before the tribunal does not also control it.

### M2 — Critical minerals: midstream (GS3)
- **Intro:** energy transition runs on lithium, nickel, cobalt, graphite and REEs; India's gap lies in processing.
- **Upstream progress:** MMDR Amendment 2023 (Centre auctions specified critical minerals; lithium opened to private exploration), National Critical Mineral Mission 2025, KABIL overseas assets, Minerals Security Partnership (2023).
- **Midstream gap:** refining concentrated in one country; auctions allocate deposits, not battery-grade chemicals; **"squatting"** — auctioned, cleared, leased blocks never worked (price volatility, speculative bidding, finance not closing).
- **Remedies:** lithium–nickel processing incentive (announced 22 Sep), processing parks (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Odisha, AP), milestone-linked leases with performance-security forfeiture, recycling / urban mining, offtake partnerships, R&D in alternative chemistries (sodium-ion).
- **Conclusion:** critical minerals are a strategic-autonomy question — ownership of ore without refining capacity replaces one dependency with another.

## GS4 case — model approach

**(a) Issues & stakeholders:** candidates' right to a fair exam (equity, trust in merit); probity — possible conflict of interest (member's relative as vendor, disclosed but unmanaged); institutional reputation vs integrity; whistle-blower protection (junior officer's identity); due process — acting on unverified information; the Chairperson's instruction to "handle it quietly" (obedience vs conscience). Stakeholders: 3.2 lakh candidates, honest vendor staff, the Commission, the junior officer, the public.

**(b) Options:**
1. *Ignore as rumour* — avoids disruption; but if true, the exam is void later at much greater cost, and you would be complicit.
2. *Postpone immediately* — safest for integrity; but disproportionate on zero evidence, costly to candidates, and rewards rumour-mongering.
3. *Verify fast and proportionately, keep contingency ready (preferred)* — in the 36 hours: preserve the message and metadata; inform the Chairperson **in writing** (not "quietly"); ask the cyber cell/police for a rapid check of the WhatsApp source; audit the press's chain of custody (sealed packets, CCTV, packet counts); **activate the reserve question-paper set** printed at a different press, which most Commissions keep; recuse the related member from the decision.
4. *Go to the media* — breaches confidentiality and could cause panic; wrong channel.

**(c) Course of action:** Option 3. If verification shows a breach → switch to the reserve set or postpone, with a transparent public notice. If nothing is found → hold the exam with reinforced security (paper opened at centres under video, sets randomised). Protect the junior officer's identity; record every decision in writing. Duty is owed to the candidates' fair chance, not to the Commission's image. Gandhi's talisman applies directly: the candidate who has sat three years for this exam is the person to think of.

**(d) Systemic:** mandatory recusal and rotation of vendors (disclosure alone is not management); multiple paper sets from different presses; digital encryption with print-at-centre; a whistle-blower channel with statutory protection (reference: the Public Examinations (Prevention of Unfair Means) Act, 2024 for central exams — States to adopt similar laws); a pre-agreed postponement protocol so the decision is not improvised under pressure.

## Essay — model approach: "Strength that must be displayed every day is not yet strength."

- **Opening:** an image — Alauddin Khalji's Delhi, with fixed prices, spies in the market and a vast standing army, and how fast it all fell apart after 1316. Performed power needs constant maintenance; real power can rest.
- **Define:** *displayed* strength (coercion, spectacle, posture) vs *secure* strength (legitimacy, capability in reserve, institutions that work without being watched).
- **Individual:** confidence vs bravado; people with integrity don't need to announce it; Gandhi's non-violence was strength that didn't need to be displayed.
- **State and institutions:** deterrence works best when credible and rarely demonstrated (India's no-first-use doctrine); institutions (the Election Commission, the courts) earn authority through consistency rather than headlines; the Sultanate's garrison model against Chola village assemblies that ran themselves.
- **International:** great powers that need daily demonstrations are often insecure; India's strategic autonomy is quiet multi-alignment rather than posture.
- **Counter-view (essential):** sometimes strength *must* be shown — deterrence depends on signalling, and a new institution has to prove itself (a regulator's first penalty). The fix: display should be occasional and credible, not constant.
- **Society & economy:** a currency defended every day by reserves vs one backed by fundamentals; brands built on advertising vs trust.
- **Conclusion:** strength eventually shows itself in how little it needs to be displayed. Link back to the opening image.
