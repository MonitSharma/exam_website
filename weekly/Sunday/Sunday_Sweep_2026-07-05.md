### Mains skeletons

**M1 — Directive: DISCUSS** (present the dimensions of the issue in a balanced, exploratory way).
1. *Intro:* The Preamble is not enforceable per *In Re Berubari (1960)*, yet has grown into the Constitution's most-cited interpretative compass.
2. **Non-justiciable but interpretatively central:** *Kesavananda Bharati (1973)* used the Preamble's core ideals (justice, liberty, equality, fraternity, sovereignty, secularism) to help identify the **basic structure**; *S.R. Bommai (1994)* leaned on "secularism" from the Preamble to strike down arbitrary President's Rule.
3. **Amendability but not erasability:** the 42nd Amendment (1976) added Socialist/Secular/Integrity — showing the Preamble *can* be amended (post-Kesavananda), but not so as to alter the basic structure it helped define.
4. **Practical utility:** courts invoke it to resolve ambiguity in enacting provisions, and legislatures invoke it rhetorically to justify social/economic legislation (e.g., reservations under "socialism/justice").
5. *Conclusion:* The Preamble's power lies precisely in this paradox — unenforceable in itself, yet foundational to how every enforceable provision is read.

**M2 — Directive: CRITICALLY EXAMINE** (weigh both framings, then take a position).
1. *Intro:* 19th-century reform (Brahmo, Arya, Prarthana, Young Bengal) is often read as either colonial-modernity's mirror or a home-grown response — both readings hold partial truth.
2. **Response to colonial modernity:** English education, missionary critique of "backward" customs (sati, child marriage, caste) and Western liberal ideas of individual rights shaped Ram Mohan Roy and Young Bengal's Derozio-ians directly.
3. **Assertion of civilizational confidence:** Arya Samaj's "Back to the Vedas" and the Ramakrishna Mission explicitly rejected wholesale Westernisation, arguing reform *from within* Hindu textual tradition — a proto-nationalist, not merely imitative, project.
4. **Synthesis:** most reformers straddled both — Ram Mohan Roy invoked the Upanishads *and* Enlightenment rationalism; the movements collectively built the intellectual scaffold (women's education, press, associations) that fed directly into the Congress-era nationalism.
5. *Conclusion:* the movements are best read as a **dialectic**, not a binary — colonial modernity supplied the provocation and idiom, indigenous tradition supplied the legitimacy and content.
