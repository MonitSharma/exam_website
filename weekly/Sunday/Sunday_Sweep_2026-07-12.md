1. *Intro:* FRs (Part III) are enforceable guarantees under Art 32, but each operates within constitutional limits and public-interest balancing — they are foundational precisely because they are principled, not unlimited.
2. **Why not absolute:** Art 19's six freedoms carry **reasonable restrictions** (19(2)–(6)); Art 25 is subject to public order, morality, health; national-security, public-order and social-reform limits recur throughout Part III.
3. **Why the moral centre:** they protect dignity, equality, liberty, minority rights and due process; **Art 32 (Ambedkar's "heart and soul")** makes them justiciable; the **basic-structure doctrine** shields their core from amendment.
4. **Judicial balancing:** proportionality (*Puttaswamy*), free-speech vs public-order calibration, and protection against majoritarian overreach show limits and centrality coexisting.
5. *Conclusion:* FRs are meaningful *because* they combine enforceability with reasoned limits — the balance, not the absoluteness, is what anchors constitutional democracy.

**M2 — Directive: CRITICALLY EXAMINE** (weigh both claims, then take a position).
1. *Intro:* The Moderates (1885–1905) — Naoroji, Gokhale, Ranade, Pherozeshah Mehta — are often dismissed as timid; yet they built the scaffolding nationalism later stood on.
2. **What they created:** a **pan-Indian platform (INC itself)**; the **vocabulary** of constitutionalism and rights; the **economic critique** of colonialism (Naoroji's Drain Theory, *Poverty and Un-British Rule*); methods of petition, resolution and public opinion; and early wins (Public Service Commission demand, expansion of Councils → 1892 Act).
3. **What they failed at:** they **did not reach the masses** — elite, English-educated, urban; the "3 P's" (petition-prayer-protest) yielded thin results; faith in British justice looked naïve after the 1905 Partition of Bengal, which discredited them and birthed the Extremists.
4. **Synthesis:** methods and institutions were necessary preconditions — the Extremists and Gandhi supplied mass politics *on top of* the Moderates' foundation, not instead of it.
5. *Conclusion:* the Moderates are best judged as **institution-builders of a first phase**, not as the finished article — the critique of their timidity is fair, but the debt nationalism owes them is real.

### Ethics case — model approach

- **Stakeholders:** you (DM, custodian of law + public trust); the wetland-dependent fisher community and the notified Schedule-I species (voiceless, ecologically vulnerable); local unemployed youth (livelihood aspiration); the defence manufacturer (investment, jobs, national-security value); the state government (development/political incentives); the ecologist/petitioners (accountability); future citizens (intergenerational equity).
- **Core ethical issues:** administrative honesty vs institutional/political loyalty; the letter of a granted clearance vs the *validity* of the evidence behind it; development vs ecological due process; short-term livelihoods vs long-term sustainability; procedural integrity vs expediency.
- **Options:** (i) **Sign now on the stale survey** — *rejected*: knowingly relying on false ecological data is a dereliction that invites future litigation, ecological harm and a bigger scandal. (ii) **Blanket-halt the project indefinitely** — *rejected*: disproportionate; punishes legitimate development and the youth's livelihood claim. (iii) **Pause, order a fresh time-bound ecological survey, and seek a supplementary EIA/mitigation plan while ring-fencing the wetland** — *preferred*: honest, proportionate, and keeps the project alive if it can be made compliant.
- **Recommended action:** (1) do **not** sign on the flawed file — record in writing why the survey is inadequate (the notified wetland + Schedule-I presence); (2) commission a **fast-tracked fresh survey** with a fixed deadline and independent ecologists + the community; (3) explore **project redesign/mitigation** (buffer zone, alternate layout, wetland exclusion) so jobs and ecology can coexist; (4) communicate transparently with youth, community and media — frame it as "getting it right once" not "blocking development"; (5) resist the informal pressure on record, escalating through proper channels if directed to act unlawfully.
- **Systemic fix:** mandate a **validity window for ecological surveys** in EIA appraisals (e.g., surveys older than 2–3 years, or predating a wetland/species notification, auto-trigger re-survey), plus a **living GIS layer** of notified wetlands and Schedule-I habitats that clearance files must be checked against — so a stale survey cannot silently pass appraisal.

### Essay — model approach (philosophical)

- **Thesis:** Development and environment are not rival goods but two accounts of the same balance-sheet; the "choice" between them is usually a failure of imagination, sequencing or institutions — not a real trade-off.
- **Multidimensional arc:** (1) *Conceptual* — the false binary vs **sustainable development** (Brundtland: meeting present needs without compromising the future); Sen's capabilities (a degraded environment shrinks real freedoms). (2) *Economic* — natural capital as the base of GDP; the cost of "grow now, clean later" (health, disaster, lost productivity) vs green-growth, circular economy, ecosystem services. (3) *Social/justice* — the poor bear environmental costs first (fishers, forest-dwellers, the wetland community of the case above) — so the binary is also a distributional question. (4) *Institutional* — EIA integrity, environmental courts (NGT), the difference between environment-as-obstacle and environment-as-design-constraint. (5) *Civilizational/philosophical* — Gandhi ("the Earth provides enough for every man's need but not every man's greed"); Indian traditions of *prakriti* and stewardship. (6) *Way forward* — internalise externalities, price natural capital, treat ecological due process as part of good development, not an enemy of it.
- **Intro (sample):** *"A river that is dammed for power and dead for fish has not been developed — it has merely been spent."*
- **Conclusion (sample):** *"The measure of development is not how much of nature we consume to grow, but how much of both we manage to hand on — for a prosperity that outlives its own ecology has only borrowed against a debt it cannot repay."*
- **Fodder (quotes/data):** Gandhi — "enough for need, not for greed"; Brundtland Report (1987) definition of sustainable development; Amartya Sen — development as expansion of freedoms; *State of India's Environment 2026* — 7/9 planetary boundaries breached (this week's live data-point); NGT/EIA as institutional bridges; India's LiFE (Lifestyle for Environment) mission.
