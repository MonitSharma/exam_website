**Q4 — (b).** MDR is the **fee a merchant pays**, shared among the acquiring bank, the issuing bank (interchange) and the network/payment operator, for processing a digital transaction. ### Mains skeletons

**M1 — President's discretionary space (GS2, 250 words)**

*Intro (2 lines):* Article 53 vests executive power in the President; Article 74 makes him bound by ministerial advice. The constitutional question is not whether he is nominal, but where the nominality ends.

*Body — three heads:*
1. **Situational discretion (real, and unavoidable).** Appointing a PM when no party has a majority; dismissing a government that has lost confidence but will not resign; dissolving the Lok Sabha on the advice of a PM who has lost the House. None of these is written down; all have occurred (1979, 1989, 1996, 1998).
2. **Textual discretion (narrow but genuine).** **Art 74(1) proviso** — the power to *require reconsideration once* (44th Amendment); **Art 111** — the pocket veto, which is a power of indefinite delay because no time limit is prescribed (1986 Indian Post Office Bill); **Art 201** — withholding assent to State bills, where the President is not bound even on re-passage; the power to seek information under **Art 78**; **Art 143** advisory reference.
3. **What the 42nd and 44th Amendments actually settled — and did not.** The 42nd made advice **binding**, ending the Sarkaria-era ambiguity; the 44th added the **single reconsideration** and disciplined emergency proclamations (written Cabinet advice, armed rebellion, the revocation clock). Together they settled the *ordinary* case and left the *pathological* case — hung House, defecting majority, a Cabinet acting outside its mandate — exactly where it was: with the President's judgment.

*Counter-current (1 line):* the more the President's discretion is exercised, the more it becomes political — which is why restraint, not power, has been the office's actual contribution.

*Conclusion:* the office is nominal in operation and consequential at the margin; the amendments narrowed the margin without abolishing it, and the margin is where constitutional continuity has repeatedly been preserved. **Cite: S.R. Bommai, Shamsher Singh v. State of Punjab (1974), Rameshwar Prasad (2006).**

**M2 — Quit India: failure as insurrection, success as signal (GS1, 250 words)**

*Intro:* 8 Aug 1942, Bombay AICC; the entire leadership arrested within hours; the movement that followed was leaderless by design and by accident.

*Body:*
1. **Why it failed as an insurrection.** Decapitated leadership; no coordinated plan beyond "Do or Die"; overwhelming repression (troops, machine-gunning from the air, ~90,000 arrests); the Muslim League, Communists, Hindu Mahasabha and the Depressed Classes leadership all stood aside; the parallel governments at **Ballia, Tamluk and Satara** were locally impressive and strategically isolated; suppressed within months in the towns, by 1943 in the countryside.
2. **Why it succeeded as a signal.** It established that British rule now required **permanent coercion** rather than consent; it produced a generation of local cadre (Lohia, Aruna Asaf Ali, JP) who ran the post-war mobilisation; it made the *scale* of the withdrawal question unavoidable in London; combined with the **INA trials (1945–46)** and the **RIN mutiny (1946)** it broke the reliability of the two instruments — the army and the police — on which the Raj rested.
3. **The counter-argument, given fairly.** The decisive variables were arguably external: Britain's post-war economic exhaustion, the **1945 Labour government**, and American pressure. On this reading Quit India accelerated a timetable it did not set.

*Conclusion:* it did not seize power, and it was not meant to survive contact with repression; what it did was convert Indian independence from a negotiable date into an administrative certainty. Its success is measurable not in 1942 but in the tone of the **Cabinet Mission of 1946**.

### Ethics case — model approach

**(a) Ethical issues.**
Public interest vs institutional convenience; the integrity of a statutory calculation vs the political cost of correcting it; **conflict of interest** created by an implied career inducement; the **duty of care to a subordinate** who is being asked to record something under pressure and whose ACR you write; environmental justice for a population that is not in the room; and — the quiet one — **four months of "under examination"**, which is a failure of process that predates the phone call and is partly yours to own.

**(b) Options.**
1. *Record nothing, let the file remain pending until after the inauguration.* Costless today, indefensible later. It converts a procedural delay into a deliberate one, and it teaches the probationer that files can be timed. **Reject.**
2. *Direct the probationer to record the existing calculation as final.* This makes her the author of a defective record and shifts personal risk to the most junior person in the chain. Ethically the worst option available. **Reject absolutely.**
3. *Reopen the calculation immediately and unilaterally, with a public notice.* Correct in substance, poor in judgment. Bypassing the Principal Secretary invites a jurisdictional fight that the compensation claim may not survive, and grandstanding is not integrity.
4. *Put the correct position on the file, in writing, and route it up properly — while separating the compensation question from the commissioning question.* Record the High Court order, the revised computation, and a clear recommendation. Send it to the Principal Secretary and the competent authority with a copy to the pollution control board. Simultaneously note that **the shortfall is a recoverable liability, not a bar to commissioning** — so the inauguration need not be stopped, and no one is asked to choose between 4,000 jobs and the law.

**(c) Chosen course: option 4, plus two additions.**
Reduce the Principal Secretary's call to a **file note of what was discussed** — not as a weapon, but because an unrecorded conversation is what makes later pressure possible. And issue a **demand notice with a payment schedule** rather than a stop-work order, so that the remedy is proportionate. Say plainly to the Principal Secretary, on the phone, what I am doing and why; no one should learn it from the file. On the posting: I neither raise it nor let it enter the calculation, and I would record my recommendation identically if it had never been mentioned. *Foundational values demonstrated: objectivity, courage of conviction, non-partisanship, and proportionality.*

**(d) To the probationer.**
Tell her exactly what to record — the facts, the order, the computation, the recommendation — and then **sign the covering note myself**. She should record the truth; the risk of having recorded it should sit with me, because that is what seniority is for. Then tell her the general rule she will need for thirty years: *you are never obliged to record a conclusion you do not hold, and you are always obliged to record the facts.* The lesson is not that integrity is costless. It is that integrity is survivable when it is **documented, routed correctly, and proportionate** — and that a senior officer's job is to make it survivable for the people below them.

### Essay — model approach

> **"A nation is not built by what it announces, but by what it maintains."**

**Framing.** The tension is between the **founding act** and the **sustaining act** — between the moment of creation, which is visible and dateable, and the work of maintenance, which is invisible precisely because it succeeds. The essay should not become an anti-announcement polemic; announcements are how a democracy states intent, and intent matters.

**Suggested arc (five movements):**

1. **Open with a concrete image, not a thesis.** A dam inaugurated with a ceremony and desilted by no one; a school built and unstaffed; a statute passed and unenforced. One paragraph, no abstraction.
2. **Concede the case for announcement.** Announcement is how a state signals priority, coordinates a federal system, and creates accountability by putting a promise on the record. The Constitution itself was an announcement. **Purna Swaraj, 26 Jan 1930**, was nothing but an announcement — and it set the date the Republic later kept.
3. **The turn: maintenance is the harder virtue because it is unwitnessed.** Institutions decay by neglect, not by attack. Draw on: the maintenance backlog in physical infrastructure; the difference between passing an Act and staffing the tribunal that enforces it; the way conventions — the Speaker's neutrality, the audit function, the census cycle — survive only by being repeated. A short comparative note (Rome's roads, Japan's *Ise Jingu* rebuilt every twenty years) earns its place if it is one sentence, not a paragraph.
4. **Widen beyond the state.** Maintenance applies to language, ecology, and relationships. A river is not restored by a mission but by a thousand undramatic decisions upstream. A family is not built on a wedding.
5. **Resolve without moralising.** Announcement and maintenance are not opposites but a sequence with a fatal gap in the middle — the gap where attention goes elsewhere. A mature polity is one that has learned to make the second half of the sequence as legible as the first: outcome budgeting, sunset review, published maintenance data. End on the aphorism inverted: *what a nation maintains eventually becomes what it need not announce.*

**Fodder to have ready:** Ambedkar's warning that a constitution's working depends on those who work it; the difference between capital and revenue expenditure as a moral distinction and not merely an accounting one; Amartya Sen on institutions vs entitlements; the maintenance of the census and the survey system as the substrate of every other claim about India.
