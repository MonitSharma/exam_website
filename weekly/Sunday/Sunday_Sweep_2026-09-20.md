## Prelims quiz — key and explanations

**1. (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The India–MERCOSUR PTA was signed at New Delhi on 25 January 2004 and entered into force on 1 June 2009, following the Framework Agreement at Asunción in 2003. MERCOSUR is a customs union with a Common External Tariff (formalised by the Ouro Preto Protocol, 1994), which is precisely why individual member states cannot negotiate tariff concessions separately. Bolivia became a full member in July 2024; Venezuela has been suspended since December 2016. The existing PTA is narrow — concessions on roughly 450 tariff lines by India and 452 by MERCOSUR — which is why the expansion talks launched on 14 September 2026 matter.

**2. (b) 1 and 3 only.** Statement 2 is the error. The **Reserve Tranche Position is an asset**, not a liability: it is the portion of a member's IMF quota paid in reserve assets, which the member can draw upon **without conditionality**. The four components are foreign currency assets (much the largest), gold, SDRs and the RTP. Statement 3 is the examinable point behind the record $785.7 billion figure — reserves built on FCNR(B)-window inflows are **borrowed, redeemable and interest-bearing**, so the reserve number and the external-liability number move together. Read reserve adequacy against the Net International Investment Position and the short-term-debt-to-reserves ratio.

**3. (c) 2 and 3 only.** Statement 1 is wrong: **the WPI covers only goods** — it has no services component, which is one of the standard arguments for the Producer Price Index that has been under consideration. The WPI is compiled by the Office of the Economic Adviser, DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce & Industry (current base 2022-23), while the CPI is compiled by the NSO under MoSPI. The monetary policy framework agreement fixes the CPI target at 4% with a ±2 percentage point tolerance band — note that **the RBI targets CPI, not WPI**, which is why an unusually wide WPI–CPI divergence is an analytical puzzle rather than a policy trigger.

**4. (d) 1, 2 and 3.** The EPF & MP Act, 1952 applies to scheduled establishments employing twenty or more persons and houses three schemes — EPF, EPS (1995) and EDLI (1976). Employee and employer each contribute 12 per cent; of the employer's share, **8.33 per cent goes to EPS** (computed on the wage ceiling) and 3.67 per cent to EPF, with the Centre adding 1.16 per cent of wages to EPS up to the ceiling. The Central Board of Trustees is tripartite — government, employers and employees — chaired by the Union Minister for Labour & Employment. The ceiling moved from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 with effect from 17 September 2026, the first revision since 2014.

Project Cheetah translocated animals from **Namibia (September 2022) and South Africa** to Kuno National Park, Madhya Pradesh; the Asiatic cheetah was declared extinct in India in 1952.

## GS Mains — answer skeletons

### M1 — India–Pakistan: territorial, treaty and trade instruments (GS2, 250 words)

**Introduction (3 lines).** Frame the shift, don't narrate it: since 2016, and decisively since 2019 and 2025, India has replaced a policy of episodic engagement with one of **simultaneous pressure across three domains that were previously kept separate** — territory, water and trade. The question is whether that simultaneity is design or accumulation.

**Body — three instruments, one paragraph each, each ending with its cost.**

1. **Territorial and treaty-legal.** Rejection of the Pakistan–China Boundary Joint Commission (16 Sep 2026) on the ground that the 1963 cession of the Shaksgam Valley was *ultra vires* Pakistan's own title and is, by **Article 6 of that agreement**, provisional. India's position rests on **legal consistency maintained over six decades** — which is its strength. **Cost:** legal consistency does not alter facts on the ground, and CPEC continues through Gilgit-Baltistan regardless.
2. **Water.** The Indus Waters Treaty, 1960, survived three wars precisely because it was insulated from politics; placing it in abeyance ends that insulation. **Argument for:** treaty benefits cannot be unilateral when the other party's conduct is not. **Cost:** India's reputation as a treaty-abiding upper riparian is an asset in its own disputes with China over the Brahmaputra, and the storage and diversion infrastructure to actually use the western rivers does not yet exist — so the instrument is currently more signal than substance.
3. **Trade and connectivity.** MFN withdrawal (2019) and the DGFT prohibition on **direct or indirect** imports (2 May 2025), with **DRI's 'Operation Deep Manifest'** as the enforcement proof that third-country transshipment through Jebel Ali is the real leakage. **Cost:** enforcement burden falls on Indian customs, and near-zero formal trade removes the leverage that trade itself provides.

**The coherence question — the paragraph that earns the marks.** The three instruments share a single logic — **raising the cost of the status quo while refusing to legitimise it** — but they differ in reversibility: trade measures are switchable, treaty abeyance is not, and territorial positions are permanent. A policy composed of instruments with different exit costs is **coherent in intent and asymmetric in risk**.

**Conclusion.** The strategy is real, but it is a strategy of **cost imposition without a defined end state**. Add the one line that shows judgement: a pressure policy needs a stated condition under which pressure is relieved, or it becomes indistinguishable from the absence of policy.

### M2 — Semiconductors: financial or upstream constraints? (GS3, 250 words)

**Introduction.** ISM 1.0 (2021, ₹76,000 crore) was a **capital-subsidy** instrument; what it bought was overwhelmingly **ATMP/OSAT** capacity — the back end. Semicon 2.0 (₹1,27,500 crore, approved July 2026) is organised around **six pillars — design, machines and materials, fabs, ATMP/OSAT, R&D and talent** — which is an implicit admission of where the constraints actually sit.

**Body.**

- **Why money was never the binding constraint.** Subsidy closes a cost gap; it does not create lithography equipment access, ultra-pure materials supply chains, EDA tool licences or process engineers. A fab is a **systems integration problem**, not a capital goods purchase.
- **The four upstream constraints, one line each.** *Equipment* — extreme concentration in a handful of suppliers, and export-control exposure. *Materials* — gallium, germanium, rare earths, high-purity chemicals and gases; connect explicitly to critical-minerals policy. *Design tools* — EDA is a duopoly-plus-one; note the extension of tool access to 400+ institutions and 100+ start-ups as the policy response. *Talent* — the stated target of ~1 lakh engineers is a decade-scale problem, not a scheme-cycle one.
- **What is genuinely working, stated fairly.** Twelve approved units and over ₹1.64 lakh crore of committed investment, with three operational; a design-linked incentive track; and **States competing to host units** (Gujarat, Assam, UP, Punjab, Odisha, Andhra) — a good example of competitive federalism in industrial location.
- **The strategic frame.** Semiconductors are the clearest live case of industrial policy inside a **globally concentrated, export-control-shadowed** supply chain; "China+1" creates the opening, but friend-shoring also means accepting dependence on a different set of chokepoints.

**Conclusion.** The constraints are upstream, not financial. An adequate industrial policy therefore looks less like a larger subsidy and more like **long-horizon public R&D, materials and critical-minerals security, standing access to design tools, and university-scale talent pipelines** — instruments whose payoff periods exceed any single scheme cycle, which is precisely why they are the hard part.

- **Possible forgery**, which is a criminal question and not merely a regulatory one.
- **Conflict of interest and the subtlest pressure in the case.** Mr. Verma's scholarship is genuine good. The mention of its renewal date, made "without emphasis", converts philanthropy into leverage. **Recognising that this is a conflict of interest even though no bribe was offered is the core competency being tested.**
- **Utilitarian framing offered as a threat.** "Fifty families will suffer to help one" is an argument, and it must be answered rather than dismissed — but note that it is **an argument for better lending, not for lawless recovery**, and that accepting it would make the protection of any individual contingent on their usefulness to an aggregate.
- **Institutional void.** The absence of a grievance mechanism converts a rights question into a question of the officer's personal character — which is itself an ethical failure of design.

**(b) Options.**

1. **Do nothing; advise a civil suit.** *Merit:* strictly within jurisdictional limits; preserves lending relationships. *Demerit:* a man with no income cannot litigate; it ratifies "this is how it is done"; and it makes the SDM complicit by omission.
2. **Informal pressure on Verma for a private settlement.** *Merit:* fast relief for Ramesh. *Demerit:* off-the-record use of office; no record, no precedent, no deterrence; and it leaves the SDM personally indebted to a private party's goodwill — the very entanglement the scholarship remark was inviting.
3. **Formal reference on the record — the recommended core.** Register the complaint; refer the alleged forgery to the police for investigation; refer the recovery conduct to the regulator's ombudsman scheme and the financier's grievance officer; inform Ramesh in writing of his remedies and the limitation period; and assist with access to legal aid. *Merit:* lawful, documented, replicable. *Demerit:* slow; Ramesh's immediate distress is unaddressed.
4. **Punitive action against the financier's local operations.** *Merit:* visible deterrence. *Demerit:* disproportionate, outside the SDM's remit, likely to be reversed, and it would validate the credit-withdrawal fear.

**(c) Course of action.** Take **Option 3 as the spine**, with two additions. First, **address the immediate distress separately and lawfully** — link the household to existing entitlements and legal aid, so that relief does not depend on the financier's cooperation. Second, **deal with the scholarship explicitly rather than pretending not to have heard it**: record the remark in the file, and state plainly to Mr. Verma that the scholarship will be assessed on its own merits and that its renewal has no bearing on the complaint. Saying this aloud, once, and recording it, is what removes the leverage — silence is what preserves it. Answer his utilitarian argument on its own terms: responsible lending at scale depends on borrowers trusting that the process is bounded, so lawless recovery raises the cost of credit rather than lowering it.

**(d) Systemic measure.** Institute a **standing sub-divisional grievance desk for financial-service complaints** — a single registration point with a published escalation path to the regulator's ombudsman, mandatory logging of every repossession in the sub-division (date, time, notice served, acknowledgement), and a quarterly review published in the district's public-grievance report. Add a **standing register of officers' interests and of local philanthropic contributions**, so that the next SDM does not have to rely on personal restraint to notice a conflict. The test of the measure is precisely the one the case poses: **the next Ramesh should get the same outcome from a different officer.**

**Values to name explicitly in the answer:** objectivity, non-partisanship, integrity, empathy toward the weaker section, courage of conviction, and accountability — and note that **the case tests empathy and objectivity together**, because sympathy for Ramesh cannot be the reason for the decision; the law must be.

## Essay — model approach

> **"A state that measures everything may still miss what matters."**

**What the topic is asking.** Not "statistics are bad". The topic sets up a gap between **measurement and mattering**, and the best essays live in that gap rather than picking a side. The failure modes are (i) a listicle of indices, and (ii) an anti-data rant. Aim instead at: *measurement is indispensable, and its indispensability is exactly why its blind spots are dangerous.*

**Possible structure — four movements.**

1. **Open with the case *for* measurement, honestly and at length.** A state that cannot count cannot govern: famine relief, vaccination, poverty targeting, fiscal planning and the administration of any entitlement all presuppose enumeration. Use concrete Indian instances — the Census as the backbone of delimitation and reservation; the PLFS replacing a five-yearly survey with monthly estimates; the Multidimensional Poverty Index deliberately widening "poverty" beyond income; DBT and Aadhaar seeding reducing leakage because they made transfers *countable*. **Establish that you are not anti-measurement, or nothing that follows will be credible.**
2. **Then the turn: the three distinct ways measurement misses.** *(a) What is not counted.* Unpaid care work, informal-sector incomes, ecosystem services, the value of a grassland that produces no timber — the last is especially usable, because it is this week's material. *(b) What counting does to the counted.* Goodhart's law: once a measure becomes a target it stops being a good measure — enrolment rates rising while learning outcomes stall; open-defecation-free declarations outrunning usage; hospitals optimising for reportable metrics. *(c) What aggregation conceals.* A national average can rise while a region, a caste group or a gender falls — GDP growth alongside a female labour-force participation rate in the mid-thirties; a rising HDI with a widening Palma ratio.
3. **The deeper claim — and this is where a good essay separates itself.** Some things are **constitutively unmeasurable**, not merely unmeasured: dignity, trust in institutions, the felt security of belonging, the quality of a relationship between a citizen and a clerk. A state can measure the time taken to process an application; it cannot measure whether the applicant was treated as a supplicant. **The danger is not that these go unmeasured but that they become invisible in a system that acts only on what it sees.** Bring in the Indian constitutional vocabulary here — fraternity and dignity are in the Preamble, and neither has an index.
4. **Resolution — refuse the false choice.** The answer is not less measurement but **better epistemic humility about it**: triangulating administrative data with survey data with qualitative evidence; publishing disaggregated data by default; building in deliberate feedback from the governed (social audits, Gram Sabha verification, citizens' charters with teeth); and treating every indicator as a proxy that must periodically be re-justified against the thing it stands for. **Close on the line the essay has been earning:** the purpose of measurement is to make the state see; the risk is that the state comes to believe that what it sees is all there is.

**Quotable anchors to use sparingly (one or two, no more):** Goodhart's law; Amartya Sen's capability framing — development as the expansion of substantive freedoms rather than the growth of income; the Preamble's pairing of justice, liberty, equality and **fraternity**, of which only the first three have ever been indexed.

**Two cautions.** Keep the Indian material dominant — an essay on this topic can drift into abstraction very quickly, and examiners reward the paragraph that names a scheme, a survey or a court judgment. And **write the counter-argument properly**: the strongest objection to your thesis is that unmeasured priorities are how patronage and discretion survive, and that insisting on what "cannot be counted" has historically been an argument used to avoid accountability. Answer it, don't avoid it.
