### M1 skeleton - Directive: Discuss

- **Intro:** FRs are enforceable guarantees, but each operates within constitutional limits and public-interest balancing.
- **Why not absolute:** Art 19 reasonable restrictions; Art 25 subject to public order, morality, health; national security and social reform limits.
- **Why moral centre:** protect dignity, equality, liberty, minority rights, due process and judicial remedies through Art 32/226.
- **Judicial role:** proportionality, basic structure, privacy, free-speech balancing; courts prevent majoritarian overreach.
- **Conclusion:** FRs are meaningful because they combine enforceability with principled limits, not because they are unlimited.

### M2 skeleton - Directive: Examine

- **Intro:** Inclusion is not just opening accounts; it is regular, safe and trusted use of finance.
- **Access:** PMJDY, UPI, RuPay, AePS, BC network, SHG-bank linkage.
- **Trust gap:** dormant accounts, fraud, mis-selling, digital illiteracy, grievance delays.
- **Consumer protection:** transparent charges, consent architecture, ombudsman, cyber hygiene, data protection.
- **Way forward:** local language interfaces, assisted digital finance, strong fraud liability rules, financial literacy tied to welfare delivery.
- **Conclusion:** inclusion becomes developmental only when access converts into confident usage.

### Ethics case model approach

- **Stakeholders:** adult learners, teachers, block officials, district administration, Ministry/State education department, NGO, taxpayers, future employers/certifiers.
- **Values:** honesty, accountability, compassion for frontline pressure, public trust, outcome integrity, fairness to real learners.
- **Options:** hide data (quick but unethical); punish immediately (signals seriousness but may create fear and cover-up); verify and correct with remedial support (slower but durable).
- **Action:** freeze new "completed" entries for suspect blocks; order sample verification with NGO + neutral educators; protect whistleblowers; separate deliberate fraud from pressure-driven error; publish corrected dashboard; run evening/weekend assessments for wage workers; retrain staff.
- **System fixes:** third-party audits, learner OTP/biometric-lite checks where appropriate, random oral assessments, realistic targets, dashboard flags for sudden spikes, grievance channel.

### Essay model approach

- **Thesis:** Rights are promises on paper until institutions protect those least able to enforce them.
- **Arc:** Constitution -> courts/commissions/media/civil service -> vulnerable groups -> risks of institutional capture -> reforms.
- **Examples:** Article 32 as a remedy; legal aid; Right to Education; privacy and dignity; social audits; welfare delivery for migrants and women.
- **Counterpoint:** Rights culture also needs citizens, civil society and political accountability, not courts alone.
- **Quotes/data:** Ambedkar: "Constitutional morality is not a natural sentiment"; Gandhi: "The true measure of any society can be found in how it treats its most vulnerable members" (often attributed); NFHS/education dashboards can be used as data examples if verified.
- **Conclusion:** Institutional courage is the bridge between formal liberty and lived justice.
