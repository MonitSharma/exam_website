### GS Mains — answer skeletons

**M1 — Monetary tightening vs supply-side inflation (GS3)**
- **Intro:** the 7 Oct 2026 hike (5.25 → 5.50%, unanimous, "calibrated tightening"). The drivers were mostly on the supply side: monsoon at 87% LPA, crude near $100, food and fuel.
- **Why rates look like the wrong tool:** rates cannot grow pulses or cut oil prices; tightening hurts output and MSME credit; transmission lags of 2–4 quarters; the risk of squeezing growth during a supply shock.
- **Why the RBI still acts:** **second-round effects** (wages, core inflation), **de-anchoring of expectations** (the FIT credibility of 4% ±2), rupee and imported inflation when global yields are high, and the statutory mandate under the RBI Act (2016 amendment).
- **Better mix:** supply-side fiscal tools (buffer-stock release, import-duty cuts, OMSS), agricultural resilience (irrigation, crop diversification), fuel-tax adjustment, and monetary policy as the **anchor**, not the cure.
- **Conclusion:** tighten to stop a shock turning into a spiral, and let fiscal and agricultural policy do the curing.

**M2 — Culture as a fundamental right and PVTGs (GS2)**
- **Intro:** the 5 Oct 2026 SC remark while declining to stay the Calcutta HC hearings on Great Nicobar. The Shompen are a PVTG, about a few hundred people, hunter-gatherers.
- **Constitutional toolkit:** Art 29(1) (distinct culture); Art 21 (life with dignity); Arts 46, 244 and the Fifth/Sixth Schedules (*Samatha* 1997 on tribal land); Art 338A (NCST); **FRA 2006** (gram sabha consent; *Niyamgiri/Orissa Mining Corp* 2013); PESA 1996 where applicable; the Andaman and Nicobar (Protection of Aboriginal Tribes) Regulation, 1956.
- **The development side:** the strategic trans-shipment port, IOR security, the national interest.
- **Tensions:** consent vs consultation; forced integration vs isolation (the Shompen policy of 2015); ecological loss (Galathea Bay, leatherback turtles, mangroves).
- **Balance:** prior informed consent, independent social and ecological audit, a buffer that protects ranging areas, and a "no-contact unless chosen" principle.
- **Conclusion:** constitutional morality treats the right to remain different as part of the right to life.

### GS4 case — model approach

- **Issues:** corruption that turns victims into bribe-payers; **unequal access to justice** (the poor and women pay more); refusal to register FIRs (*Lalita Kumari* 2013 makes FIRs mandatory for cognisable offences); institutional pressure to protect performance figures over integrity; conflict between the officer's usefulness and his misconduct.
- **Stakeholders:** victims (especially elderly pensioners), the public's trust in police, the head constable, the cyber-cell team, banks, the journalist, and you as SP (accountability).
- **Next 48 hours:** register FIRs on all three pensioner complaints and issue freeze requests at once through the **1930 helpline / CFCFRMS (I4C) portal**; move the head constable **out of the complaint-handling chain** (not a public shaming) pending a **preliminary inquiry by an officer outside the cell**; preserve records; tell the journalist an inquiry is under way, without prejudging it.
- **Three months:** a written SOP — every cyber complaint becomes an FIR or a CSR within 24 hours, with an SMS acknowledgement; **bank-nodal turnaround norms** agreed with the LDM and bank heads, escalated through the SLBC; a public dashboard of complaints → FIR → freeze → recovery; staffing and skill training so the cell does not rely on one person; a regular victims' grievance day; a complaint box outside the chain of command.
- **The principle in (c):** make recovery **procedural, time-bound and visible**, so discretion and therefore "speed money" disappear. Integrity is not just personal honesty but **designing systems that leave no room to sell what is owed for free**.
- **Values to name:** integrity, impartiality, compassion toward the weaker sections, accountability, courage to refuse to "handle it quietly".

### Essay — model approach: "The quality of a state is measured by how it treats those who cannot complain."

- **Opening (anecdote):** an elderly pensioner who has lost her savings to online fraud, has no smartphone literacy, and cannot pay "speed money". Her experience *is* the state, for her.
- **Thesis:** states are judged not by how they serve the loud, but by whether their systems work **without being pushed**. Silence is a test of design.
- **Who cannot complain:** children, the disabled (air-travel accessibility), PVTGs (Shompen), leprosy-affected people facing stigma, animals and ecosystems (Living Planet Index), future generations (climate).
- **Philosophical frame:** Rawls's difference principle; Gandhi's talisman; Ambedkar's constitutional morality; Amartya Sen's capabilities (voice as a capability).
- **Indian institutional answers:** Directive Principles; PIL and epistolary jurisdiction (the court *listening* for those who cannot complain); NALSA legal aid; the RTI Act; social audits (MGNREGA); FIR-registration duties.
- **Counter-view:** complaint mechanisms are essential, and a state cannot know what it is not told. Answer: that is why proactive design (default entitlements, DBT, auto-inclusion) beats grievance redress alone.
- **Way forward:** "rights by default", time-bound services, independent audits, empathy in public service (link to GS4).
- **Conclusion:** return to the pensioner. A good state reaches her before she has to ask.
