### Mains skeletons

**M1 — Article 22(2) and magistrates (GS2)**
- **Intro:** Liberty is measured in hours. Art 22(2) makes production before a Magistrate within 24 hours a **constitutional** command; ***Sujit Das* (Nov 2026)** closed the gap between "detention" and "arrest".
- **Why the ruling matters:** ends paper arrests that hide hours of custody (the case: ~18 hours unrecorded); reinforces *D.K. Basu* (1997) safeguards and *Arnesh Kumar* (2014) on mechanical arrests; aligns with BNSS ss. 56, 58.
- **Magistrate as guardian:** record actual time of detention; scrutinise remand, not rubber-stamp it; refuse remand on breach; check for custodial violence; legal aid (Art 39A).
- **Challenges:** caseload and night-time production; police records; under-trial share of prisons (~3/4 of inmates, NCRB; **flag the latest figure**); weak legal aid.
- **Way forward:** CCTV and electronic arrest memos; time-stamped detention logs; duty magistrates; audits by district legal services authorities; training.
- **Conclusion:** a right delayed is a right denied; the magistrate turns Art 22 from text into practice.

**M2 — Forex reserves (GS3)**
- **Intro:** reserves at **$719.75 bn** (wk to 23 Oct), a seventh weekly fall, ~$66 bn below the **4 Sept record of $785.71 bn**.
- **Valuation vs intervention:** gold and non-USD currency moves change dollar value without transactions (gold −$2.65 bn while FCA +$1.36 bn that week); intervention = RBI spot/forward dollar sales to smooth the rupee; other drivers: OMC dollar facility, FCNR-type repayments.
- **Adequacy metrics:** import cover (months), reserves vs short-term external debt (Greenspan–Guidotti), reserves vs broad money, IMF ARA metric; **forward book** as a hidden claim. **Fill the latest import-cover number from your CA card.**
- **Assessment:** comfortable on most metrics, but costs of carry and limits to defending the rupee; Impossible Trinity trade-offs; FPI flows (KYC easing as a signal).
- **Conclusion:** reserves are insurance, not a target; let valuation pass through, use intervention only against disorderly moves.

### Ethics case — model approach

- **Issues:** **public interest** (access to life-saving drugs) vs private profit; **artificial scarcity** as a form of hoarding; **conflict of interest and political pressure** (the trustee link); **impartiality** in enforcement; **transparency** with the press without causing panic; compassion for patients.
- **Stakeholders:** patients and families; chemists and distributors; manufacturers; the hospital chain; the pricing authority; the minister; the public's trust in regulation; you and your team.
- **Next 48 hours:** (1) **stock audit** at distributors and C&F agents in the three towns; invoke the DPCO 2013 and the Essential Commodities Act provisions on hoarding and non-compliance, and report to the national pricing authority; (2) **set up a helpline and a public list** of chemists with stock, and coordinate with government hospitals for buffer supply; (3) **inspect the hospital pharmacy like any other** and record the informal request in writing to your superior: an even-handed, documented decision protects you and the office; (4) **brief the journalist with facts** (what the cap is, where stock is, the helpline) rather than blame; (5) meet patient groups and invite two representatives to the monitoring meeting.
- **Longer term:** monitor supply on a dashboard (availability, not only price); require manufacturers to give advance notice of supply discontinuation; staggered transition for old stock; trade-margin rationalisation paired with procurement through Jan Aushadhi and State medical corporations; a whistle-blower channel for distributors.
- **Values to name:** integrity, impartiality, courage of conviction, compassion, accountability, **"price control must not become supply denial"**.

### Essay — model approach: *"Rights that cannot be enforced in time are rights only in name."*

- **Opening:** a hook from real life, e.g. an under-trial who has spent longer in jail than the maximum sentence for the offence; or a Magistrate refusing remand because the 24-hour clock was breached.
- **Thesis:** a right has three parts, *recognition, remedy and timeliness*. Without the third, the first two become symbolic. Time is itself a constitutional value.
- **Dimension 1, civil liberty:** Art 21/22, *Hussainara Khatoon* (1979, speedy trial), *Sujit Das* (2026); under-trial prisoners; bail delays.
- **Dimension 2, socio-economic rights:** MGNREGA wage delays and compensation; RTI's 30-day clock; RTE admissions; pensions; delayed justice in consumer and labour forums.
- **Dimension 3, institutions and technology:** e-Courts, virtual hearings, fast-track courts (POCSO/rape FTSCs to 2031), time-bound service delivery (Right to Services laws); counter-risk: speed without fairness.
- **Counter-view:** some rights need deliberation; haste can trample due process. Timeliness must not become mechanical disposal.
- **Way forward:** judge strength, case management, digital records, citizen charters with penalties, legal aid; measure "time to remedy" as a governance KPI.
- **Conclusion:** return to the opening image; a Constitution is judged by how quickly it reaches the person who needs it.
