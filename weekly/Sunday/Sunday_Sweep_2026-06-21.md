### Mains skeletons

**M1 — Directive: EXAMINE** (inspect components, weigh how far the claim holds).
1. *Intro:* Europeans came as **traders, not conquerors** — but commercial footholds became political levers.
2. **Commercial → territorial creep:** fortified factories, trade monopolies, revenue farming (Bengal).
3. **Exploiting Indian disunity:** Plassey (1757) & Buxar (1764) — alliances with disaffected nobles; **Diwani 1765** = revenue + army.
4. **Tools of dominance:** Subsidiary Alliance (Wellesley), Doctrine of Lapse (Dalhousie), drain of revenue funding further conquest.
5. **Counter-weight:** rivalry among European powers (Anglo-French Carnatic wars) and Indian resistance shaped, not just enabled, the outcome.
6. *Conclusion:* The "seeds" metaphor holds — **economic penetration furnished the means and motive** for political control, consolidated by superior military-fiscal organisation.

**M2 — Directive: DISCUSS** (examine multiple sides, give a reasoned view).
1. *Intro:* The Assembly (Cabinet Mission, 1946) had to forge **one charter for a plural, partitioned, unequal society**.
2. **Diversity of the house:** regions, religions, castes, princely states, ideological spread (Nehru–Patel–Ambedkar–conservatives).
3. **Consensus machinery:** committee system, the *Objectives Resolution* as moral compass, near-unanimous adoption by accommodation not majority steamrolling.
4. **Hard compromises:** language, federal balance, separate-electorate rejection, reservations, property rights.
5. **Ambedkar's role + borrowed-yet-adapted features** → a document both indigenous and cosmopolitan.
6. *Conclusion:* Its durability proves consensus-building, not imposition, was the method — a **"living document"** designed to absorb dissent.

### Ethics case — model approach

- **Stakeholders:** you (SDM) · genuine eligible families (denied) · the clerk (coerced, vulnerable) · DM (superior, complicit-by-silence) · MLA/contractor (beneficiaries of fraud) · the public exchequer / rule of law.
- **Values in tension:** integrity & probity in governance **vs** loyalty to a superior; compassion for the clerk **vs** accountability; courage **vs** career safety.
- **Options:** (i) comply/regularise — *rejected*: abets corruption, betrays the poor; (ii) blind escalation that crushes the clerk — *partly unjust*; (iii) **document evidence, give the clerk a chance to retract & cooperate, correct the list, and report through proper channel (written, to DM then higher/vigilance if stonewalled)** — *preferred*.
- **Recommended action:** Verify and **preserve evidence**; remove ghost names and admit waitlisted eligibles; protect the coerced clerk as a witness while making clear future complicity won't be shielded; put your objection to the DM **in writing**; escalate to the Collector/vigilance if pressured; stay within rules, no media leaks.
- **Systemic safeguards:** Aadhaar/DBT-seeded beneficiary lists, geo-tagged + third-party social audits, biometric verification, public display of lists (transparency), and whistle-blower protection.

### Essay — model approach

- **Thesis:** Growth and ecology are **not a trade-off but a sequence** — an economy that liquidates its natural capital is borrowing prosperity from a future it is simultaneously destroying.
- **Multidimensional points:** (1) *Economic* — climate-hit GDP losses (e.g., World Bank's ~2.8%-by-2050 warning), agrarian shocks, stranded assets; (2) *Ecological* — breaching planetary boundaries (India 7/9), biodiversity & freshwater stress; (3) *Social-equity* — the poor pay first via heat, floods, lost livelihoods; (4) *Ethical/inter-generational* — Gandhian "enough for need, not greed"; (5) *Governance/tech* — green transition, circular economy, renewables as growth engines, LiFE; (6) *Global* — CBDR, COP commitments, just transition.
- **Intro (sample):** *"A felled forest looks like income on a balance sheet and a debt on the planet's — and the bill, unlike the profit, is never written off."*
- **Conclusion (sample):** Sustainability is **not the brake on growth but its insurance policy**; India's path lies in *green growth* — prosperity that keeps the foundations it stands on intact.
- **Fodder (quotes/data):** "We do not inherit the Earth from our ancestors; we borrow it from our children." · Gandhi: "The Earth provides enough for every man's need, but not every man's greed." · India breached **7 of 9** planetary boundaries (State of Environment 2026); **CRI 2026 rank 9th**; World Bank ~**2.8%** GDP risk by 2050.
