1. The tomb of Shaikh Salim Chishti there is of white marble with fine jali screens.  
2. It was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1986.  
3. The Diwan-i-Khas has a central pillar supporting a circular platform.  
4. It remained Akbar's capital throughout his reign.  
How many of the statements given above are correct?

- (a) Only one
- (b) Only two
- (c) Only three
- (d) All four


## Answer key with explanations

**Q1. (b) 1 and 2 only** *[Easy - Pressure groups vs political parties]*

A pressure group seeks to influence policy from outside government; it does not contest elections, and so it has no electoral accountability. Laxmikanth notes that they are also called interest or vested groups. Statement 3 is wrong.

**Q2. (d) All four** *[Moderate - Almond-Powell classification of interest groups]*

All four follow the Almond-Powell typology that Laxmikanth uses. Institutional groups are formal bodies with other functions. Associational groups are specialised to articulate interests. Anomic groups are spontaneous. Non-associational groups rest on kinship, ethnicity, region or status.

**Q3. (c) Only three** *[Moderate - Trade unions and their party links]*

BMS (1955) is linked to the Sangh Parivar and the BJP, not to the CPI(M). The other three are correct: INTUC (1947) with the Congress, CITU (1970) with the CPI(M), and AITUC (1920, India's oldest central trade union) with the CPI.

**Q4. (c) Being recognised as a State party in at least four States** *[Easy - Symbols Order 1968 - State party vs national party conditions]*

Recognition as a State party in four or more States is a route to national-party status, not State-party status. Options (a), (b) and (d) are State-party conditions. The 8% vote test was added in 2011.

**Q5. (a) 1 and 2 only** *[Hard - National party recognition and the 2016 review change]*

Statement 1 is one of the three national-party routes. Statement 2: in August 2016 the ECI amended the Order so that status is reviewed every ten years, i.e. across two elections. Statement 3 is wrong because the threshold is four States, not three.

**Q6. (b) 1-3-2-4** *[Moderate - Coalition era - Prime Ministers in sequence]*

V. P. Singh led the National Front (1989-90). Chandra Shekhar followed with Congress outside support (1990-91). After 1996 came the United Front governments, first Deve Gowda (1996-97) and then Gujral (1997-98).

**Q7. (c) 1 and 3 only** *[Moderate - Features of coalition government]*

Statement 2 is wrong. Pre-poll alliances such as the NDA (1999) and the UPA (2009) have formed governments, and the 1977 Janata Party was itself a pre-poll merger. The CMP and the 91st Amendment's 15% cap, which is often read as a coalition-era reform, are correct.

**Q8. (a) 1 and 3 only** *[Moderate - National Integration Council]*

Nehru convened the National Integration Conference (Sept-Oct 1961), which led to the NIC. The NIC is a non-statutory, non-constitutional forum chaired by the PM. Article 263 is the basis for the Inter-State Council, not the NIC.

**Q9. (d) 1, 2 and 3** *[Moderate - Gujral Doctrine]*

All three are correct. The doctrine's five principles also include non-interference, respect for territorial integrity and sovereignty, and settling disputes through peaceful bilateral negotiations. Gujral set it out in 1996 as EAM in the Deve Gowda government.

**Q10. (c) Only three** *[Hard - Non-Aligned Movement - origins]*

Statement 3 is wrong. The five founders usually named are Nehru, Nasser, Tito, Sukarno and Nkrumah. Cuba joined later and hosted summits in 1979 and 2006. The 7th summit was held in New Delhi in 1983.

**Q11. (b) Abolition of intermediaries such as zamindars** *[Easy - Land reforms - most successful component]*

Zamindari abolition in the early 1950s brought about 20 million cultivators into direct contact with the State. Ceiling and tenancy laws were weakly implemented, and consolidation succeeded mainly in Punjab, Haryana and western UP.

**Q12. (a) 1 and 2 only** *[Moderate - Operation Barga]*

Operation Barga recorded bargadars to give them heritable tenancy and a fixed share of the crop. It did not confer ownership, which is the trap in statement 3.

**Q13. (d) 1 and 2 only** *[Easy - Bhoodan movement]*

The first gift came at Pochampalli on 18 April 1951. Bhoodan land was managed under State Bhoodan Acts, since land is a State subject, not under a central law.

**Q14. (b) 1 and 3 only** *[Hard - Model Agricultural Land Leasing Act 2016]*

The Model Act was drafted by NITI Aayog's expert committee, chaired by T. Haque. It legalises leasing while protecting the owner: a lease creates no claim to ownership or adverse possession. Land is a State subject, so the Act is only a model for States to adopt.

**Q15. (a) All three** *[Moderate - WTO Agreement on Agriculture - subsidy boxes]*

All three are correct. India's MSP procurement for public stockholding falls under the amber-box de minimis test, which is why the Bali 2013 'peace clause' matters to it.

**Q16. (c) Only three** *[Moderate - Poverty estimation committees]*

Pair 2 is wrong. Lakdawala recommended State-specific poverty lines, updated with State price indices (CPI-AL rural, CPI-IW urban).

**Q17. (b) 1 and 3 only** *[Moderate - PPP models - HAM and TOT]*

Under HAM (2016) NHAI pays 40% in instalments during construction and the developer recovers the rest as annuities. NHAI keeps the toll revenue, so traffic risk stays with the authority. TOT is an asset-monetisation model.

**Q18. (c) 1 and 2 only** *[Hard - New Industrial Policy 1991 - what it changed]*

The 1991 policy cut the reserved list from 17 (IPR 1956) to 8, and later notifications left only atomic energy and railway operations. Compulsory licensing still applies to a short list of hazardous or strategic industries, so statement 3 is wrong.

**Q19. (d) 1-2-3-4** *[Easy - Battles - chronology]*

Khanwa 1527 (Babur over Rana Sanga), Chausa 1539 (Sher Khan over Humayun), Panipat II 1556 (Akbar's forces under Bairam Khan over Hemu), Haldighati 1576 (Man Singh against Maharana Pratap).

**Q20. (c) Only three** *[Moderate - Sher Shah's reforms]*

Statements 1-3 are correct. Mansabdari is Akbar's system, formalised around 1573-74.

**Q21. (b) 1 and 2 only** *[Easy - Jagirdar vs zamindar]*

Jagirs were rotated so that jagirdars would not take root locally. Zamindars held hereditary local rights to a share of the produce. Statement 3 describes jagirdars, not zamindars. The jagirdar/zamindar contrast follows the pattern of UPSC Prelims 2019 (local PYQ data); this is not that question.

**Q22. (d) 1, 2 and 3** *[Moderate - Mansabdari - zat, sawar, du-aspa sih-aspa]*

All three are correct. Du-aspa sih-aspa (two- and three-horse troopers) raised a trusted noble's sawar obligation beyond his zat. Most mansabdars were paid through jagirs.

**Q23. (a) 2-4-1-3** *[Moderate - Akbar's religious policy - chronology]*

Jizya was abolished in 1564, the Ibadat Khana was built in 1575, the Mahzar was issued in 1579 and Din-i-Ilahi was proclaimed in 1582.

**Q24. (b) Only two** *[Hard - Mughal land-revenue methods]*

Pair 2 is wrong. Kankut means estimating the yield by inspection (kan = grain, kut = estimate). Dividing the harvested crop is batai or ghallabakhshi. Zabt with dahsala averages was Todar Mal's 1580 system.

**Q25. (c) Only two** *[Moderate - Central officials]*

The Mir Bakhshi was head of the military pay and intelligence department and recommended appointments to mansabs. The imperial household and karkhanas came under the Mir Saman (Khan-i-Saman).

**Q26. (a) 1 and 2 only** *[Easy - Europeans at the Mughal court]*

Hawkins reached Agra in 1609 and Roe arrived in 1615. Manucci was a Venetian.

**Q27. (c) Only three** *[Hard - Mughal painting]*

Statement 4 is wrong, because Aurangzeb withdrew court patronage and painters dispersed to provincial courts. UPSC Prelims 2019 (local PYQ data) asked which emperor shifted from manuscripts to albums and portraits (Jahangir); this question only follows that theme.

**Q28. (b) Only three** *[Hard - Persian translations and chronicles]*

The Akbarnama is by Abul Fazl. Badauni wrote the critical Muntakhab-ut-Tawarikh. Pattern note: UPSC 2022 (local PYQ data) asked about the Yogavasistha's Persian translation under Akbar.

**Q29. (d) 1, 2 and 3** *[Easy - Restriction endonucleases]*

All three are correct. Sticky ends let DNA ligase join fragments cut by the same enzyme.

**Q30. (a) 1 and 3 only** *[Moderate - Cloning vector pBR322 and insertional inactivation]*

Recombinants have an inactivated beta-galactosidase gene, so they give white colonies, while non-recombinants turn blue. Statement 2 inverts this.

**Q31. (b) a thermostable DNA polymerase from Thermus aquaticus that survives the repeated high-temperature denaturation steps** *[Easy - PCR and Taq polymerase]*

PCR cycles through denaturation (about 94 degrees C), primer annealing and extension. Taq polymerase stays active through the heating, so it does not have to be added again in every cycle.

**Q32. (a) 1 and 2 only** *[Moderate - Bt toxin - mechanism]*

The bacterium is not harmed because the toxin stays as an inactive crystal protoxin inside it. That makes statement 3 wrong. Activated toxin binds midgut epithelial cells and creates pores that kill the insect.

**Q33. (d) 1 and 2 only** *[Easy - RNA interference against nematodes]*

Nematode-specific genes were introduced into the host using Agrobacterium vectors. The dsRNA they produced silenced the parasite's mRNA, so the nematode could not survive. No cry genes were involved.

**Q34. (b) 1 and 2 only** *[Hard - Recombinant human insulin]*

NCERT describes the A and B chains being produced separately, then extracted and joined by disulphide bonds. Statement 3 is wrong.

**Q35. (c) All three** *[Moderate - Gene therapy - ADA deficiency]*

All three are correct as given in NCERT. Lymphocyte therapy has to be repeated because the engineered cells are not immortal.

**Q36. (b) Denmark** *[Easy - EFTA membership]*

EFTA has four members: Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland. Denmark left EFTA when it joined the EEC in 1973 and is an EU member. Pattern note: UPSC 2026 (local PYQ data) tested EU membership in a similar format.

**Q37. (d) 1, 2 and 3** *[Moderate - India-EFTA TEPA]*

All three are correct (EFTA joint communique, 1 Oct 2025). TEPA is the first Indian FTA with a target-based investment commitment.

**Q38. (a) 1 and 2 only** *[Moderate - India-Africa Forum Summits]*

The second IAFS was held in Addis Ababa in 2011, not Nairobi.

**Q39. (c) 1 and 2 only** *[Easy - African Union and the G20]*

The OAU was founded in 1963. The AU replaced it in 2002, launched at Durban.

**Q40. (a) All three** *[Hard - Kampala principles, AAGC and tele-education]*

All three are correct. The AAGC was conceived with Japan, and its vision document was prepared by RIS, ERIA and IDE-JETRO. The e-VBAB networks launched in 2019.

**Q41. (b) 1 and 2 only** *[Moderate - India-EU - TTC, IMEC, CBAM]*

CBAM covers carbon-intensive goods: iron and steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, electricity and hydrogen. It does not cover rice or tea. UPSC 2023 (local PYQ data) asked about the US-EU TTC.

**Q42. (c) Only three** *[Moderate - Major ports and their locations]*

Mormugao is in Goa. New Mangalore is Karnataka's major port. The format follows UPSC 2023's port pairs (local PYQ data).

**Q43. (d) Kolkata** *[Easy - Riverine port]*

Kolkata, with its subsidiary port at Haldia, lies on the Hugli about 128 km inland. It needs constant dredging. Visakhapatnam is a landlocked harbour, Kochi is a natural harbour at the head of a lagoon, and Paradip is a deep-water port on the coast.

**Q44. (b) 1 and 3 only** *[Moderate - Jhabua watershed case study]*

NCERT presents Jhabua as a government watershed programme (Ministries of Rural Development and Agriculture) that worked because of Bhil participation, for example in the pasture regeneration by the Petlawad block's Bhils. It was not a CSR project.

**Q45. (a) 1 and 4 only** *[Hard - Wastelands - natural vs human-induced]*

NCERT groups waterlogged/marshy land and saline/alkaline land under causes that are both natural and human-induced, largely from over-irrigation. Gullies, desertic or coastal sands, barren rock, steep slopes and glacial areas are natural.

**Q46. (c) Akbar's conquest of Gujarat** *[Easy - Buland Darwaza]*

Akbar built the Buland Darwaza after his Gujarat campaign (1573). It is the grand southern gateway to the Jama Masjid of Fatehpur Sikri.

**Q47. (b) Only two** *[Moderate - Later Mughal monuments - patrons]*

Aurangzeb built the Delhi Red Fort's Moti Masjid. Shah Jahan's Moti Masjid is the one in the Agra Fort. Itmad-ud-Daulah's tomb is noted for early large-scale pietra dura in white marble.

**Q48. (a) 1 and 2 only** *[Easy - Humayun's Tomb]*

The tomb is red sandstone with white-marble inlay and a double dome. It became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1993 and anticipates the Taj Mahal.

**Q49. (d) Only two** *[Moderate - Mughal gardens]*

The Pinjore (now Yadavindra) Gardens were laid out by Nawab Fidai Khan in Aurangzeb's reign, not under Akbar.

**Q50. (c) Only three** *[Moderate - Fatehpur Sikri]*

Fatehpur Sikri was the capital only from about 1571 to 1585. Akbar then moved to Lahore and later to Agra.
