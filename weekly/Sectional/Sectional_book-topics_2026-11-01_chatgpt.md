## Answer key

| Q | Key | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **(b)** | Regulating Act 1773 -> Pitt's India Act 1784 -> Charter Act 1853 (open competition) -> Indian Councils Act 1861 (legislative powers restored to Bombay and Madras). |
| 2 | **(c)** | Pairs 1-3 are correct. Provincial dyarchy came with the 1919 Act; the 1935 Act abolished it, introduced provincial autonomy and provided for dyarchy at the Centre (which never came into operation). |
| 3 | **(b)** | The Act provided for a Federal PSC, Provincial PSCs and Joint PSCs for two or more provinces, so 3 is wrong. The Federation never came into being because the princely states did not join, but the Act did provide for it. |
| 4 | **(c)** | Under the Cabinet Mission scheme, provincial members were elected indirectly by the provincial assemblies. Sinha was temporary President at the first sitting (9 Dec 1946). The Objectives Resolution was moved on 13 Dec 1946; the Drafting Committee was set up on 29 Aug 1947. |
| 5 | **(a)** | Citizenship (Arts 5-9), elections (Art 324), the provisional Parliament (Art 379), temporary and transitional provisions and the short title came into force on 26 Nov 1949. Part III came into force with the rest on 26 Jan 1950. |
| 6 | **(b)** | 1 and 4 are correct. The rule of law comes from the British Constitution. A federation with a strong Centre and residuary powers vested in the Centre is borrowed from Canada (in the USA, residuary powers lie with the states). |
| 7 | **(c)** | Berubari (1960): not a part; Kesavananda (1973) reversed this; LIC (1995) reaffirmed it. The Preamble is neither a source of power nor a prohibition on the legislature's powers, and it is non-justiciable. It was amended once, by the 42nd Amendment (1976). |
| 8 | **(d)** | Article 4(2) says laws under Articles 2 and 3 are not amendments under Article 368, so a simple majority is enough. After Berubari, the 9th Amendment (1960) was needed to transfer Berubari; the 100th Amendment (2015) implemented the 1974 LBA and its 2011 Protocol. |
| 9 | **(d)** | The Act gives five modes of acquisition: birth, descent, registration, naturalisation and incorporation of territory. Deprivation is one of the three modes of LOSS (with renunciation and termination). |
| 10 | **(a)** | Termination (1) and deprivation (2) are correct. OCI cardholders have no voting rights. Part II covers only citizenship at the commencement; Article 11 leaves the rest to Parliament, which enacted the Citizenship Act, 1955. |
| 11 | **(c)** | National Income = NNP at factor cost, i.e. GDP at market prices - depreciation + net factor income from abroad - net indirect taxes. |
| 12 | **(a)** | Imputed rent and own-account production are counted. Pensions are transfer payments (no current production). The sale of a second-hand good was counted when it was first produced; only a dealer's commission would add to GDP. |
| 13 | **(d)** | GNP = GDP + NFIA. With negative NFIA, GNP < GDP (and likewise NNP < NDP). India's NFIA is usually negative. |
