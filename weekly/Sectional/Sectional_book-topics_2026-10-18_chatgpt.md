## Answer key

| Q | Ans | Level | Explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **(d)** | M | All three are correct. The 24th Amendment (1971) made assent to amendment bills obligatory. Article 111 sets no time limit for ordinary bills, which is what allows the pocket veto. The absolute veto is used for private members' bills and for bills of an outgoing cabinet. |
| 2 | **(c)** | E | Zail Singh kept the Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986 pending without a decision. The other three are the subjects of the three joint sittings (1961, 1978, 2002), a common distractor. |
| 3 | **(c)** | M | Article 123 bars an ordinance only when both Houses are in session, so statement 1 is correct. An ordinance lapses six weeks after Parliament reassembles, so the maximum life is about six months and six weeks; it is not counted from promulgation. Statement 3 is the 7-judge ruling in Krishna Kumar Singh. |
| 4 | **(b)** | H | A bill pending in the Lok Sabha, or passed by it and pending in the Rajya Sabha, lapses. ## Revision checklist

1. Veto: absolute, suspensive, pocket (Zail Singh, Post Office Bill 1986). No veto over amendment bills since the 24th Amendment.
2. Art 123: barred only when **both** Houses are in session (one House sitting is enough); lapses **6 weeks after reassembly**. *Krishna Kumar Singh* (2017): re-promulgation is a fraud on the Constitution.
3. Lapse on dissolution: the Lok Sabha side lapses. A Rajya Sabha-origin bill pending in the RS, a bill awaiting assent, and a notified joint sitting all survive.
4. VP removal: **Rajya Sabha only**, effective majority, then a simple majority in the LS, 14 days' notice.
5. Judges cases 1981 → 1993 (CJI+2) → 1998 (CJI+4) → 2015 (NJAC struck down). Removal motion: 100 LS / 50 RS signatures; committee of CJI/SC judge + HC CJ + jurist.
6. Arts 127 (ad hoc) and 128 (retired judges, only with consent). Curative petition = *Rupa Ashok Hurra* 2002.
7. Commissions: Rajamannar 1969 (TN) · Sarkaria 1983 · Punchhi 2007 · NCRWC = Venkatachaliah. Constitutional bodies: NCSC 338, NCST 338A, NCBC 338B. NCW and NCM are statutory.
8. Companies 1600-1602-1616-1664. Swally 1612 · Colachel 1741 (Dutch) · Bedara 1759 · Wandiwash 1760 (Coote vs Lally).
9. Mysore treaties: **M**adras 1769 → **M**angalore 1784 → **S**eringapatam 1792. Subsidiary Alliance: Hyderabad 1798, Bassein 1802. Awadh 1856 was annexed for misrule, not lapse.
10. Pre-INC: Landholders' 1838 → BIA 1851 → EIA 1866 (London) → Indian Association 1876. INC 1885 Bombay, Bonnerjee, 72 delegates. Ilbert Bill 1883.
11. Revolutionaries: Chapekars 1897 · Muzaffarpur 1908 · Dhingra 1909 · Hardinge bomb 1912. Abhinav Bharat (Savarkars), India House and the *Indian Sociologist* (Shyamji Krishna Varma).
12. Wetlands Rules 2017: EP Act; State Wetlands Authority; exclude river channels and paddy fields. First Ramsar sites: Chilika + Keoladeo (1981). Largest: Sundarban.
13. ESZs come under the **EP Act**, not the WPA. NBWL is chaired by the PM. CTH is defined in **WPA s.38V**, CWH in **FRA s.2(b)**. The Dugong reserve (Palk Bay, 2022) protects a sirenian.
14. GCC has six members (no Jordan). India-UAE CEPA in force May 2022, rupee-dirham MoU July 2023. Evacuations: Rahat-Yemen, Kaveri-Sudan, Ajay-Israel, Devi Shakti-Afghanistan, Ganga-Ukraine. SAGAR 2015 → MAHASAGAR 2025 (both announced in Mauritius).
15. Rivers: Pindar at Karnaprayag. Chandra + Bhaga at Tandi. Jhelum: Verinag → Wular → Chenab. Ganga's right bank: Yamuna, Son. Brahmaputra's right bank: Subansiri, Kameng, Manas, Sankosh (Teesta in Bangladesh). Yamuna's left bank: Hindan. Kabul joins the Indus on the right at Attock.
