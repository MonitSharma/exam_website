## Answer key with explanations

**1. (b)** [Easy] Article 79 provides that Parliament consists of the President and the two Houses - the Council of States (Rajya Sabha) and the House of the People (Lok Sabha). Though the President is not a member of either House, he is an integral part of Parliament.

**2. (c)** [Easy] The maximum strength of the Rajya Sabha is 250 - up to 238 representatives of States and Union Territories (elected) plus 12 members nominated by the President. Its present actual strength is 245.

**3. (b)** [Moderate] Representatives of each State are elected by the elected members of its Legislative Assembly through proportional representation by means of a single transferable vote, by open ballot. Members of Legislative Councils do not participate.

**4. (c)** [Easy] The maximum strength of the Lok Sabha is 550 (530 from States + 20 from UTs). The 104th Amendment Act, 2019 ended the provision (Article 331) for nomination of two Anglo-Indian members, which had lapsed in January 2020.

**5. (a)** [Moderate] A Money Bill (Article 110) is introduced only in the Lok Sabha on the President's recommendation, and the Speaker's certification of it as a Money Bill is final. The Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations within 14 days, which the Lok Sabha may accept or reject; it cannot amend or reject the Bill. Hence statement 3 is wrong.

**6. (b)** [Hard] A joint sitting (Article 108) is a mechanism only for ordinary bills. It cannot be used for Money Bills (the Rajya Sabha has no effective power over them) or for Constitution Amendment Bills (each House must pass them separately by the special majority required under Article 368).

**7. (a)** [Moderate] A Policy Cut expresses disapproval of the policy underlying a demand and reduces the demand to Re 1. An Economy Cut seeks a reduction by a specified amount to effect economy. A Token Cut reduces the demand by Rs 100 to air a specific grievance.

**8. (b)** [Easy] Zero Hour is an Indian innovation, in existence since 1962. It is not mentioned in the Rules of Procedure and is thus an informal device. It starts immediately after the Question Hour (around noon) and allows members to raise matters without prior notice.

**9. (c)** [Moderate] Under Article 85, six months should not intervene between the last sitting of one session and the first sitting of the next. Consequently Parliament must meet at least twice a year.

**10. (b)** [Easy] Under Article 75, the Prime Minister is appointed by the President. By convention the President appoints the leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha; where no party has a clear majority, the President may exercise personal discretion.

**11. (b)** [Moderate] Article 75(1A), inserted by the 91st Amendment, caps the Council of Ministers (including the PM) at 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha. For States, the corresponding cap is 15% of the Assembly strength, subject to a minimum of 12 ministers.

**12. (a)** [Moderate] Cabinet Committees are extra-constitutional - not mentioned in the Constitution but set up by the Prime Minister under the Government of India (Transaction of Business) Rules. Their membership varies from three to eight and may include non-Cabinet (Ministers of State) members, so statement 2 is wrong.

**13. (a)** [Hard] The 42nd Amendment made ministerial advice binding on the President; the 44th Amendment allowed him to return it for reconsideration once, after which he must accept it. Article 74(2) expressly bars courts from inquiring into the advice tendered, so statement 3 is incorrect.

**14. (b)** [Easy] The First Plan was based on the Harrod-Domar model, emphasising a higher savings-investment rate, and gave top priority to agriculture in the wake of Partition and rising food prices. The Mahalanobis heavy-industry model shaped the Second Plan.

**15. (a)** [Moderate] The Second Plan (1956-61) was based on the Mahalanobis model, prioritising rapid industrialisation with a focus on heavy and basic industries in the public sector. It underpinned the Industrial Policy Resolution of 1956.

**16. (d)** [Moderate] After the Third Plan (1961-66), the combined shock of the 1965 war, two consecutive droughts, the 1966 rupee devaluation and general inflation led to a 'Plan Holiday' in which three annual plans were formulated for 1966-69.

**17. (a)** [Moderate] IPR 1956 divided industries into three categories: Schedule A (17 industries) - exclusive responsibility of the State; Schedule B (12 industries) - State to increasingly establish new units while the private sector could supplement; Schedule C - left to the private sector, subject to licensing.

**18. (c)** [Easy] NITI Aayog is an advisory think-tank set up by an executive resolution (not a constitutional or statutory body) on 1 January 2015. Unlike the erstwhile Planning Commission, it does not allocate funds to States; fund devolution is now the domain of the Finance Commission and the Union Budget.

**19. (c)** [Moderate] Make in India carried forward the National Manufacturing Policy (2011) goal of raising manufacturing's share of GDP to 25% (originally with a 2022 horizon). The share has, however, remained around the mid-teens, and the target has repeatedly been pushed back.

**20. (c)** [Moderate] The 2020 reform replaced the earlier investment-only test with a composite criterion combining investment in plant and machinery/equipment and annual turnover, and made the definition uniform for manufacturing and services. Enterprises register on the Udyam portal.

**21. (c)** [Hard] The eight core industries (coal, crude oil, natural gas, refinery products, fertilisers, steel, cement, electricity) comprise about 40% of the weight of the IIP. Among the eight, refinery products carry the largest weight (roughly 28%). Both statements are correct.

**22. (d)** [Hard] The 1991 reforms abolished industrial licensing for most industries, diluted the MRTP Act (later replaced by the Competition Act, 2002), and replaced the restrictive FERA, 1973 with the liberal FEMA, 1999. All three were pillars of the pre-1991 'command' architecture.

**23. (c)** [Easy] The 1937 elections were held under the Government of India Act, 1935, and the Congress formed ministries in a majority of provinces. These ministries resigned in October-November 1939 after the Viceroy unilaterally declared India a belligerent in the Second World War.

**24. (a)** [Moderate] The August Offer (1940) proposed expansion of the Viceroy's Executive Council, a War Advisory Council, and a post-war body to frame the constitution; it also gave minorities a veto. It did not offer immediate independence, and dominion status remained the eventual goal, so statement 2 is wrong.

**25. (b)** [Moderate] In the Individual (or symbolic) Satyagraha of 1940, Vinoba Bhave was selected as the first Satyagrahi and Jawaharlal Nehru as the second. The aim was to affirm the right to free speech against the war effort without launching a mass struggle.

**26. (b)** [Moderate] Gandhi's famous description referred to the Cripps Mission (1942), which offered dominion status and a constituent assembly only after the war, with the right of provinces to stay out. The offer's post-war, conditional nature prompted the 'post-dated cheque' remark.

**27. (a)** [Easy] The AICC passed the Quit India Resolution at Gowalia Tank, Bombay, on 8 August 1942, where Gandhi gave the mantra 'Do or Die'. Most leaders were arrested in the early hours of 9 August, triggering the mass phase of the movement.

**28. (a)** [Moderate] The Jatiya Sarkar was set up at Tamluk (Midnapore, Bengal); the Prati Sarkar at Satara (Maharashtra) under Nana Patil; and Ballia (UP), under Chittu Pandey, formed one of the earliest, short-lived parallel governments of 1942.

## Revision checklist

1. **Parliament composition (Art 79):** President + Rajya Sabha + Lok Sabha; the President is part of Parliament though not a member of either House.
2. **House strengths:** Rajya Sabha max 250 (238 elected + 12 nominated), Lok Sabha max 550; 104th Amendment ended Anglo-Indian nomination.
3. **Rajya Sabha election:** by elected MLAs via PR with single transferable vote, open ballot; 1/3 retire every two years (permanent House).
4. **Money Bill (Art 110):** introduced only in Lok Sabha on President's recommendation; Speaker's certification is final; RS can only recommend within 14 days.
5. **Joint sitting (Art 108):** for ordinary bills only - NOT Money Bills or Constitution Amendment Bills.
6. **Cut motions:** Policy cut (to Re 1), Economy cut (specified sum), Token cut (Rs 100). Zero Hour = Indian innovation, not in the Rules.
7. **PM & CoM:** Art 75 appointment, Art 74 aid-and-advise binding (42nd) with one reconsideration (44th), Art 75(1A) 15%-of-Lok-Sabha cap (91st). Cabinet Committees are extra-constitutional (Rules of Business).
8. **Planning strategy:** 1st Plan Harrod-Domar (agriculture), 2nd Plan Mahalanobis (heavy industry), Plan Holiday 1966-69, IPR 1956 Schedules A/B/C.
9. **Pre-1991 controls dismantled:** industrial licensing, MRTP 1969, FERA 1973 (-> FEMA 1999). NITI Aayog (1 Jan 2015) is advisory, allocates no funds.
10. **Post-2014:** Make in India 25%-of-GDP manufacturing target (from NMP 2011); MSME 2020 composite (investment + turnover) definition; eight core industries ~40% of IIP, refinery products the largest weight.
11. **History 1937-45 arc:** Congress ministries 1937 (GoI Act 1935) -> resign 1939; August Offer 1940; Individual Satyagraha (Vinoba first, Nehru second); Cripps 1942 ('post-dated cheque').
12. **Quit India + INA:** Gowalia Tank 8 Aug 1942 'Do or Die'; parallel govts - Tamluk (Jatiya Sarkar), Satara (Prati Sarkar), Ballia; INA - Mohan Singh/Rash Behari Bose (1942) -> Subhas Bose Azad Hind (Oct 1943), Rani of Jhansi Regiment.
13. **Space:** LVM3 = Gaganyaan vehicle (cryogenic); SSLV for small sats; Aditya-L1 halo orbit at L1; NavIC 7 sats (3 GEO + 4 GSO); NISAR = dual-frequency SAR (NASA L-band + ISRO S-band); IN-SPACe = regulator, NSIL = commercial arm; BAS by 2035.
14. **India-US:** Quad (India/US/Japan/Australia) + Malabar; LEMOA (2016, logistics), COMCASA (2018, comms), BECA (2020, geospatial); 2+2 = Defence + External Affairs; iCET (2022) -> TRUST (2025) + COMPACT; Artemis Accords signed 2023; Comprehensive Global Strategic Partnership.
15. **Common traps:** NITI Aayog allocates no funds; joint sitting excludes money + amendment bills; NavIC is regional (not global like GPS); Artemis Accords are non-binding; LEMOA is not a basing or mutual-defence treaty.
