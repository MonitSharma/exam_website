## Answer key and explanations

**1. (a)** An amendment under Article 368 can be initiated only by introducing a Bill in either House of Parliament; a State Legislature cannot initiate it.
**2. (c)** Article 75(3) makes the Council of Ministers collectively responsible to the House of the People, that is, the Lok Sabha.
**3. (b)** Under Article 3, the President refers the proposal to the affected State Legislature for its views. Parliament is not bound by those views.
**4. (d)** All three pairs capture the successive positions: Shankari Prasad upheld the power, Golaknath denied it as to Fundamental Rights, and Kesavananda restored a broad but limited amending power.
**5. (a)** In a parliamentary system, the real executive is drawn from the legislature and remains collectively responsible to the popular House.
**6. (c)** Prior presidential recommendation is not required. Each House must pass the Bill separately, and the President is constitutionally bound to assent once it is duly passed.
**7. (b)** The proviso to Article 74(1) permits one reconsideration, but requires the President to follow the advice tendered after reconsideration.
**8. (d)** The Bill needs a majority of the total membership, which is at least 121, and two-thirds of members present and voting. With 180 voting, two-thirds is 120; the higher threshold is 121.
**9. (b)** Such grants are booked as revenue expenditure because the Union does not own the resulting asset, but they are added to capital expenditure to derive effective capital expenditure.
**10. (a)** Public Account transactions include money such as provident funds and small savings that the government holds in trust; tax receipts and government borrowings enter the Consolidated Fund.
**11. (d)** Pairs 1 and 2 are correct. An exceptional grant is for a purpose that forms no part of the current service of any financial year.
**12. (c)** Interest, subsidies and salary grants are revenue expenditure. A loan creates a financial asset for the lender and is capital expenditure.
**13. (b)** An excess grant regularises expenditure already incurred above the amount granted; the Public Accounts Committee examines it before the demand is presented for voting.
**14. (a)** Fiscal deficit is 100 minus 60 minus 10, or 30. Primary deficit is fiscal deficit minus interest payments, or 22.
**15. (d)** All three are correctly matched. They respectively express disapproval of policy, seek economy in expenditure, and ventilate a specific grievance.
**16. (c)** The Fund permits urgent advances, but Parliament subsequently authorises the expenditure and the Fund is recouped. It does not permanently bypass legislative control.
**17. (a)** Annie Besant used New India and Commonweal to popularise the demand for Home Rule.
**18. (d)** Under tinkathia, tenants were compelled to grow indigo on three kathas out of every twenty kathas of land, a fixed portion of the holding.
**19. (b)** The dispute arose when mill owners sought to withdraw the plague bonus while workers demanded a wage increase amid rising prices.
**20. (c)** Champaran occurred in 1917; Ahmedabad and then Kheda in 1918; the Rowlatt Satyagraha followed in 1919.
**21. (a)** The movement grew among Indian emigrants on the Pacific coast and published Ghadar. It advocated revolutionary overthrow of British rule, not petition-based dominion status.
**22. (b)** Statements 1 and 2 are correct. The return and clash at Budge Budge became intertwined with wartime anti-colonial mobilisation, so statement 3 is false.
**23. (d)** The declaration accepted progressive realisation of responsible government but left its stages and pace to British judgment, without a definite timetable.
**24. (c)** All three were features of the Lucknow Pact: separate electorates, calibrated provincial weightage, and a communal legislative safeguard.
**25. (a)** The Rowlatt Act came first, followed by the 6 April hartal, the massacre on 13 April, and Tagore's renunciation in protest afterward.
**26. (b)** A food web links many food chains and shows alternative feeding pathways. Energy still flows one way rather than cycling back.
**27. (c)** Estuaries occur where freshwater meets seawater, producing variable salinity and often high biological productivity.
**28. (d)** Energy declines at successive trophic levels, but standing biomass may be inverted in aquatic systems and numbers may be inverted where one large producer supports many consumers.
**29. (a)** Secondary productivity is the rate of formation of new organic matter by consumers; primary productivity concerns producers.
**30. (b)** The littoral zone is the shallow shore region with sufficient light for rooted vegetation; the profundal zone is deeper and poorly lit.
**31. (c)** All three pairs correctly identify characteristic features of these terrestrial biomes.
**32. (d)** Phosphorus is largely sedimentary and enters ecosystems through rock weathering. Nitrogen fixation primarily produces ammonia or related reduced forms, not nitrate only.
**33. (a)** Mangroves use specialised roots, reproductive strategies and salt-management mechanisms to tolerate anoxic, unstable and saline intertidal conditions.
**34. (c)** Bipolarity described the organisation of world politics around two rival superpower centres led by the United States and the Soviet Union.
**35. (b)** The discovery of Soviet nuclear-capable missiles in Cuba triggered a confrontation with the United States and brought the superpowers close to nuclear war.
**36. (d)** All three are correctly matched; these alliances formed part of the Cold War's bloc and containment architecture.
**37. (a)** Non-alignment sought policy autonomy and issue-based judgment. It was neither isolation, permanent neutrality nor a third military alliance.
**38. (c)** Bandung met in 1955 and the first NAM summit at Belgrade in 1961. India participated actively; Commonwealth membership did not preclude non-alignment.
**39. (b)** Shock therapy denoted a rapid package of privatisation, price liberalisation, trade openness and market transition in post-communist economies.
**40. (d)** Russia inherited the Soviet UN Security Council seat, and the CIS was created among former republics. Immediate universal NATO membership did not occur.
**41. (a)** All three descriptions are correct: LTBT left underground tests outside its ban, NPT used the 1 January 1967 threshold, and the ABM Treaty constrained strategic defences.
**42. (b)** The Peninsular Plateau is an ancient and stable block composed largely of crystalline igneous and metamorphic rocks.
**43. (c)** The east coast is generally wider and receives major delta-forming rivers such as the Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri.
**44. (d)** Both rivers flow westward through structural troughs or rift valleys and enter the Arabian Sea, mainly through estuaries.
**45. (a)** The regional sequence from west to east is Punjab or Kashmir-Himachal Himalaya, Kumaon Himalaya, Nepal Himalaya and Assam Himalaya.
**46. (c)** All three statements correctly describe the major subdivisions and margins of the Peninsular Plateau.
**47. (b)** In a radial pattern, streams diverge outward from a central high point. Centripetal drainage converges inward toward a basin or depression.
**48. (d)** Across the Punjab region, the broad north-west to south-east sequence is Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas and Sutlej.
**49. (a)** The Siang or Dihang is joined by the Dibang and Lohit in Assam; the combined river is then known as the Brahmaputra.
**50. (c)** NCERT identifies all three geological events as major controls on the evolution and present form of Peninsular drainage.
