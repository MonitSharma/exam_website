## Answer key with explanations

**1. (b)** — Article 52 merely declares that there shall be a President of India; Article 53 vests the executive power of the Union in the President, exercisable directly or through officers subordinate to him. *[Easy]*

**2. (b)** — Under Article 54, the electoral college comprises elected members of both Houses of Parliament and elected members of State Legislative Assemblies, including (by the 70th Amendment) the Assemblies of Delhi and Puducherry. Nominated members are excluded. *[Easy]*

**3. (c)** — Article 55 mandates proportional representation by single transferable vote with voting by secret ballot, and expressly seeks, as far as practicable, uniformity in the scale of representation of different States as well as parity between the States as a whole and the Union. *[Moderate]*

**4. (b)** — Article 58 requires that the candidate be qualified for election as a member of the Lok Sabha (House of the People) and be at least 35 years of age. *[Moderate]*

**5. (c)** — Article 58 fixes the minimum age for the office of President at 35 years, the same as for the Vice-President. *[Easy]*

**6. (a)** — Impeachment for violation of the Constitution can be initiated in either House, needs a 14-day notice signed by one-fourth of members, and must be passed by a two-thirds majority of the total membership. Nominated members, though barred from the President's election, DO participate in impeachment; elected members of State Assemblies do not. Hence statement 3 is wrong. *[Hard]*

**7. (c)** — Unlike the US (two-term limit under the 22nd Amendment), the Indian Constitution places no limit on the number of times a person may be re-elected as President. *[Moderate]*

**8. (a)** — The President's power under Article 72 extends to court-martial cases and to death sentences. The Governor's power under Article 161 does NOT extend to court-martial and does not include pardon of a death sentence (the Governor may suspend, remit or commute, but the power to pardon a death sentence rests with the President). Statement 3 is therefore wrong. *[Hard]*

**9. (b)** — Article 64 makes the Vice-President the ex-officio Chairman of the Rajya Sabha (Council of States); he is not a member of either House. *[Easy]*

**10. (b)** — Under Article 66, the Vice-President is elected by an electoral college consisting of the members of BOTH Houses of Parliament, including nominated members. State legislatures play no part. Hence statements 1 and 2 are correct and 3 is wrong. *[Moderate]*

**11. (b)** — Article 75(3) embodies collective responsibility of the Council of Ministers to the Lok Sabha. Article 74 deals with the Council aiding and advising the President, Article 77 with conduct of business, and Article 78 with the Prime Minister's duty to furnish information. *[Moderate]*

**12. (a)** — Under Article 76 the Attorney General is appointed by the President, holds office during pleasure, and must be qualified to be a Supreme Court judge. He enjoys the right to speak in both Houses and their committees but has NO right to vote. Statement 3 is therefore wrong. *[Hard]*

**13. (b)** — The National Institution for Transforming India (NITI Aayog) was set up on 1 January 2015 by an executive resolution of the Union Cabinet, replacing the Planning Commission. *[Easy]*

**14. (a)** — NITI Aayog is neither a constitutional nor a statutory body; it was created by executive resolution and functions as a policy think-tank. The Prime Minister chairs it. Unlike the Planning Commission, it has no power to allocate funds. Only statement 2 is correct. *[Moderate]*

**15. (d)** — GST, introduced through the 101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016, came into force nationwide on 1 July 2017, subsuming a range of central and state indirect taxes. *[Easy]*

**16. (b)** — Following the 2016 amendment to the RBI Act, the Centre notified a CPI inflation target of 4 per cent with a tolerance band of plus or minus 2 per cent (i.e., 2 to 6 per cent), a framework retained for successive five-year periods. *[Moderate]*

**17. (b)** — The Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC) was enacted in 2016, consolidating the framework for insolvency resolution of companies and individuals and establishing the IBBI as the regulator. *[Moderate]*

**18. (d)** — The MPC is a six-member body: three from the RBI (Governor, a Deputy Governor and one RBI officer) and three external members appointed by the Central Government. Decisions are by majority, and in case of a tie the Governor exercises a casting vote. All three statements are correct. *[Hard]*

**19. (b)** — Demonetisation of the high-denomination notes was announced on 8 November 2016, with the stated aims of curbing black money, counterfeiting and corruption. *[Moderate]*

**20. (b)** — PMJDY is a financial-inclusion mission providing every household access to a basic bank account, along with RuPay debit cards, overdraft facilities and insurance cover, and it underpins Direct Benefit Transfer. *[Moderate]*

**21. (b)** — The Indian Statutory Commission, headed by Sir John Simon, contained no Indian member, which was seen as an insult to Indian self-respect and provoked nationwide protests under the slogan 'Simon Go Back'. *[Easy]*

**22. (b)** — The committee was chaired by Motilal Nehru (with Jawaharlal Nehru as secretary) and produced the Nehru Report, the first major attempt by Indians to draft a constitutional framework. *[Moderate]*

**23. (b)** — The Nehru Report favoured dominion status and, crucially, rejected separate electorates in favour of joint electorates (with reservation of seats in some cases), while incorporating a set of fundamental rights. Statement 2 is therefore wrong. *[Hard]*

**24. (b)** — The Civil Disobedience Movement began with the Salt March (Dandi March) starting 12 March 1930, when Gandhi set out from Sabarmati Ashram to break the salt law. *[Easy]*

**25. (b)** — After a march of about 240 miles over roughly 24 days, Gandhi reached Dandi and violated the salt law by making salt on 6 April 1930, formally inaugurating the Civil Disobedience Movement. *[Moderate]*

**26. (d)** — The Congress boycotted the First Conference (its leaders were in jail during Civil Disobedience); Gandhi attended the Second Conference as the sole Congress representative following the Gandhi-Irwin Pact; and the Congress again stayed away from the Third Conference. All three statements are correct. *[Moderate]*

**27. (b)** — Under the Gandhi-Irwin Pact the Congress agreed to suspend the Civil Disobedience Movement and to participate in the Second Round Table Conference, in return for the release of political prisoners and certain concessions. *[Moderate]*

**28. (b)** — The Communal Award had provided separate electorates for the Depressed Classes; Gandhi's fast-unto-death led to the Poona Pact, which replaced separate electorates with a larger number of reserved seats for the Depressed Classes within joint (general) electorates. *[Hard]*

**29. (c)** — The AQI covers eight pollutants: PM10, PM2.5, NO2, SO2, CO, ozone, ammonia and lead. Carbon dioxide, though a greenhouse gas, is not one of the criteria pollutants in the AQI. *[Easy]*

**30. (c)** — Excess nitrate in drinking water is converted to nitrite in the infant gut, impairing the blood's oxygen-carrying capacity and causing methaemoglobinaemia, commonly called blue baby syndrome. *[Moderate]*

**31. (c)** — PM2.5 denotes particles smaller than 2.5 micrometres; being finer, they bypass the upper airways and reach the alveoli, making them more hazardous to health than coarser PM10. Both statements are correct. *[Moderate]*

**32. (b)** — Eutrophication results from nutrient over-enrichment (chiefly nitrogen and phosphorus from fertiliser run-off and sewage), which triggers algal blooms, oxygen depletion and death of aquatic life. *[Easy]*

**33. (d)** — Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides form acids that cause acid rain; carbon monoxide binds haemoglobin more readily than oxygen, reducing oxygen transport; and lead is a neurotoxin affecting the nervous system. All three pairs are correctly matched. *[Hard]*

**34. (b)** — Biomagnification is the progressive increase in the concentration of a persistent, non-degradable substance (such as DDT or mercury) as it passes up the food chain from lower to higher trophic levels. *[Moderate]*

**35. (b)** — Tropical evergreen or rain forests occur in the heavy-rainfall belts (over 200 cm) such as the Western Ghats, north-eastern hills and the Andamans; they are dense, multi-layered and remain green throughout the year. *[Easy]*

**36. (a)** — Tropical deciduous forests are called monsoon forests; the trees shed their leaves for six to eight weeks in the dry season. They are the most widespread forest type in India. *[Moderate]*

**37. (b)** — The Sundarbans, in the delta of the Ganga-Brahmaputra, hosts the most extensive mangrove forests in India, dominated by the sundari tree from which the region takes its name. *[Moderate]*

**38. (c)** — Black soil, or regur, formed from the weathering of Deccan basalt, is fine-textured, moisture-retentive and rich in lime, iron and magnesia, making it ideal for cotton - hence 'black cotton soil'. *[Easy]*

**39. (a)** — Laterite soils form under high temperature and heavy rainfall with alternate wet and dry spells, where intense leaching removes silica and leaves iron and aluminium oxides. They are poor in humus, nitrogen, potash and lime, so statement 2 is wrong. *[Moderate]*

**40. (d)** — Nanda Devi Biosphere Reserve lies in Uttarakhand, Nokrek in the Garo Hills of Meghalaya, and Similipal in Odisha. All three pairs are correctly matched. *[Hard]*

**41. (b)** — The three-stage nuclear programme was formulated by Homi J. Bhabha, aiming to use India's limited uranium and vast thorium reserves through a closed fuel cycle. *[Easy]*

**42. (b)** — Stage 1 uses Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors fuelled by natural uranium; Stage 2 uses Fast Breeder Reactors that use plutonium (from Stage 1) and breed more fissile material; Stage 3 aims at thorium-U-233 based reactors. *[Moderate]*

**43. (c)** — India holds one of the world's largest thorium reserves (in monazite sands). The third stage aims to breed fissile uranium-233 from thorium, making thorium the long-term backbone of India's nuclear energy. *[Moderate]*

**44. (b)** — A moderator slows down fast neutrons to thermal energies at which they are more likely to cause further fission, thereby sustaining a controlled chain reaction. Control rods, by contrast, absorb neutrons to regulate the reaction. *[Easy]*

**45. (d)** — Fission splits heavy nuclei (e.g., U-235) releasing energy; fusion combines light nuclei (e.g., hydrogen isotopes) as in the Sun; and enrichment raises the U-235 content above the roughly 0.7 per cent found in natural uranium. All three statements are correct. *[Hard]*

**46. (a)** — SAARC was established in 1985 at its first summit in Dhaka, with seven founding members (Afghanistan joined later in 2007). *[Easy]*

## Revision checklist

1. **Art 52 vs 53** — Art 52 only *declares* the office; Art 53 *vests* the Union's executive power in the President.
2. **President's electoral college (Art 54)** — elected MPs + elected MLAs of States and of Delhi & Puducherry; nominated members excluded.
3. **Qualification vs age (Art 58)** — must be qualified for the *Lok Sabha* and be at least *35*; same age bar for the Vice-President.
4. **Impeachment (Art 61)** — either House, 1/4 notice, 2/3 of *total* membership; nominated members *participate*, State legislators do *not*.
5. **Pardon: Art 72 vs 161** — only the *President* can pardon a death sentence and act in court-martial cases; the Governor cannot.
6. **Vice-President (Art 63-66)** — ex-officio Rajya Sabha Chairman; electoral college = *both Houses only*, nominated members included, no States.
7. **Collective responsibility (Art 75)** — Council of Ministers answers to the *Lok Sabha*, not the President or Rajya Sabha.
8. **Attorney General (Art 76)** — SC-judge qualification; right to *speak* in both Houses but *no vote*.
9. **NITI Aayog** — executive body (non-constitutional, non-statutory), PM chairs, *does not allocate funds* — the key distinction from the Planning Commission.
10. **Post-2014 date anchors** — PMJDY Aug 2014; NITI Aayog 1 Jan 2015; demonetisation 8 Nov 2016; IBC 2016; GST 1 Jul 2017.
11. **Inflation targeting** — 4% CPI ±2% (2-6%); MPC = 6 members (3 RBI + 3 govt); Governor has the casting vote.
12. **Nehru Report** — dominion status + fundamental rights + *joint* electorates (rejected separate electorates); chaired by *Motilal* Nehru.
13. **Dandi/Civil Disobedience** — started 12 Mar 1930, salt broken at Dandi 6 Apr 1930.
14. **Round Table Conferences** — Congress absent from the 1st and 3rd; Gandhi alone at the 2nd (after the Gandhi-Irwin Pact).
15. **Poona Pact (1932)** — replaced separate electorates (Communal Award) with *reserved seats in joint electorates* for the Depressed Classes.
16. **Pollution quick-hits** — AQI = 8 pollutants (no CO2); nitrate = blue-baby syndrome; biomagnification climbs *trophic levels*.
17. **Soils** — black/regur from basalt (cotton); laterite = heavy leaching, *poor* in humus.
18. **Nuclear three stages** — PHWR (uranium) → FBR (plutonium) → thorium/U-233; moderator *slows* neutrons, control rods *absorb* them.
19. **SAARC/SAFTA** — SAARC 1985 (Dhaka); SAFTA in force 2006.
20. **UN** — P-5 = US, UK, France, Russia, China; 10 non-permanent members sit on the *Security Council*; G-4 = India, Brazil, Germany, Japan.
