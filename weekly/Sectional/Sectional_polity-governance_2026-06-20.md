## Explanations

**Q1. (c)** — Ambedkar called Article 32 (Right to Constitutional Remedies) the 'heart and soul of the Constitution' as it guarantees the right to move the Supreme Court for enforcement of Fundamental Rights. _(Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32) · Static · Easy)_

**Q2. (a)** — The 42nd Amendment (1976) added the words 'Socialist', 'Secular' and 'Integrity'. 'Sovereign' was part of the original Preamble, so only three of the four were added. _(42nd Amendment and the Preamble · Static · Moderate)_

**Q3. (b)** — A Money Bill (Article 110) can be introduced only in the Lok Sabha and only on the President's recommendation. The Rajya Sabha can only make recommendations (within 14 days), which the Lok Sabha may accept or reject; it cannot amend or reject a Money Bill, so statement 3 is wrong. _(Money Bill (Article 110) · Static · Moderate)_

**Q4. (d)** — Forests was shifted from the State List to the Concurrent List by the 42nd Amendment, so (d) is correct. Police and Public order are in the State List, while Citizenship is in the Union List. _(Seventh Schedule (distribution of legislative powers) · Static · Moderate)_

**Q5. (d)** — All three are correct. The 44th Amendment (1978) substituted 'armed rebellion' for 'internal disturbance' and reduced the approval period from two months to one month, requiring approval by both Houses by a special majority. _(National Emergency (Article 352) · Static · Moderate)_

**Q6. (b)** — Article 37 states both that the Directive Principles are not enforceable by any court and that they are nevertheless fundamental in the governance of the country. Both statements are true, but their non-enforceability is a deliberate constitutional design, not a consequence of being 'fundamental in governance'; hence R is not the correct explanation of A. _(Nature of DPSP (Article 37) · Static · Hard)_

**Q7. (c)** — A Constitution Amendment Bill can be introduced in either House and does not require the President's prior recommendation, so statement 2 is wrong. Amendments touching the federal provisions need ratification by not less than half of the state legislatures, making statements 1 and 3 correct. _(Amendment procedure (Article 368) · Static · Moderate)_

**Q8. (a)** — All three are correct. The 73rd Amendment (1992) inserted Part IX (The Panchayats) and the Eleventh Schedule listing 29 functional subjects, and mandates reservation of at least one-third of seats (including chairperson posts) for women. _(73rd Amendment and Panchayati Raj · Static · Moderate)_

**Q9. (b)** — The ECI is a permanent, independent constitutional body and decisions in the Commission are by majority, with the CEC and ECs having equal powers and salary. However, the Constitution does NOT lay down the qualifications of its members, so statement 2 is incorrect. _(Election Commission of India (Article 324) · Static · Moderate)_

**Q10. (d)** — All three are correct. Part II (Articles 5-11) deals with citizenship; India follows single citizenship; and Article 11 empowers Parliament to regulate citizenship by law, exercised through the Citizenship Act, 1955. _(Citizenship provisions (Part II) · Static · Easy)_

**Q11. (c)** — The Governor is appointed by the President (Article 155) and holds office during the President's pleasure (Article 156). The minimum age to be a Governor is 35 years, not 30 (Article 157), so statement 2 is wrong. _(Governor (Articles 153-157) · Static · Moderate)_

**Q12. (d)** — All three are correct. The 52nd Amendment (1985) inserted the Tenth Schedule; disqualification questions are decided by the presiding officer (subject to judicial review per Kihoto Hollohan); and the 91st Amendment (2003) removed the protection earlier given to a one-third 'split'. _(Anti-defection law (Tenth Schedule) · Static · Hard)_

**Q13. (b)** — All three are correct. The 42nd Amendment (1976) added Fundamental Duties (Article 51A) on the Swaran Singh Committee's recommendation, originally ten in number; the 86th Amendment (2002) added the eleventh duty relating to education of children aged 6-14. _(Fundamental Duties (Article 51A) · Static · Moderate)_

**Q14. (a)** — The Seventh Schedule contains the three legislative lists (Union, State and Concurrent). Forms of oaths and affirmations are in the Third Schedule, so (a) is incorrectly matched. The other pairings are correct. _(Schedules and their subject matter · Static · Moderate)_

**Q15. (d)** — All three are correct. Habeas Corpus lies against both the State and private persons; Mandamus cannot be issued against the President or Governors (Article 361); and Quo Warranto can be sought by any person, regardless of whether their own rights are affected. _(Writ jurisdiction (Articles 32 and 226) · Static · Hard)_

**Q16. (a)** — All three are correct. The CAG (Article 148) is appointed by the President, can be removed only in the manner of a Supreme Court judge, and audits the accounts of both the Union and the States. _(Comptroller and Auditor General (Article 148) · Static · Moderate)_

**Q17. (c)** — The President is elected by an electoral college of elected MPs and elected MLAs (including those of Delhi and Puducherry) through proportional representation by single transferable vote. Members of State Legislative Councils do not participate, so statement 2 is wrong. _(Election of the President (Article 54-55) · Static · Moderate)_

**Q18. (b)** — In its advisory opinion delivered on 20 November 2025, the five-judge bench held that courts cannot prescribe timelines for the Governor/President under Articles 200/201 and that there is NO concept of 'deemed assent' (so statement 2 is wrong). The questions had been referred by President Droupadi Murmu under Article 143. _(Presidential Reference on Governor's assent (Articles 200/201) · Current Affairs · Hard)_

**Q19. (a)** — Under Article 143(1) the President may refer a question of law or fact of public importance, but the Supreme Court 'may' tender its opinion and can decline to answer; it is therefore not bound to answer every reference, so statement 2 is wrong. The advisory opinion is also not binding on the President (only persuasive), so statement 3 is wrong. _(Advisory jurisdiction (Article 143) · Static · Hard)_

**Q20. (c)** — On 15 September 2025 the Court granted only an interim stay of certain provisions; it did NOT strike down the whole Act (statement 2 wrong). It stayed the five-year 'practising Islam' condition pending framing of rules and capped non-Muslim membership of the Central Waqf Council at four (and State Waqf Boards at three). _(Waqf (Amendment) Act, 2025 interim order · Current Affairs · Hard)_

**Q21. (b)** — The 129th Amendment Bill, 2024 (with the Union Territories Laws (Amendment) Bill) proposes simultaneous Lok Sabha and Assembly elections and was referred to a Joint Committee of Parliament headed by P.P. Chaudhary, whose tenure has been extended to the Monsoon Session 2026. The Bill has NOT been passed, so statement 3 is wrong. _(129th Amendment Bill / simultaneous elections · Current Affairs · Moderate)_

**Q22. (c)** — The 2023 Act's Selection Committee comprises the Prime Minister, a Union Cabinet Minister and the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha — the Chief Justice of India is NOT a member, so statement 1 is wrong. The Act followed the Anoop Baranwal (2023) verdict, and the President makes the appointment on the Committee's recommendation. _(CEC and ECs Appointment Act, 2023 · Current Affairs · Hard)_

**Q23. (a)** — Justice Surya Kant was sworn in as the 53rd CJI in November 2025, and the CJI is appointed by the President (Article 124). A Supreme Court judge retires at 65 years (62 is the age for High Court judges), so statement 3 is wrong. _(Appointment and tenure of the CJI · Current Affairs · Moderate)_

**Q24. (b)** — Article 142 enables only the Supreme Court (not all courts) to do 'complete justice', so statement 3 is wrong. In the April 2025 Tamil Nadu Governor case the Court used Article 142 to deem the pending Bills assented — a step the later November 2025 advisory opinion effectively disapproved. _(Complete justice power (Article 142) · Current Affairs · Hard)_

**Q25. (a)** — All three are correct. The Rajya Sabha is a continuing/permanent body not subject to dissolution; one-third of its members retire every second year; and its maximum strength is 250 (238 representatives of States and UTs plus 12 nominated by the President). _(Composition of the Rajya Sabha · Static · Easy)_
