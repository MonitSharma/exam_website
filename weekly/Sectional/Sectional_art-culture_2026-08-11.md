## Answer Key & Explanations

**Q1. (a)** — Inscribed at the 47th session (July 2025), it became India's 44th World Heritage Site under cultural criteria (iv) and (vi). Of the 12 forts, 11 are in Maharashtra and Gingee Fort is in Tamil Nadu, so statement 2 is wrong.

**Q2. (d)** — The five languages granted classical status in October 2024 were Marathi, Pali, Prakrit, Assamese and Bengali, raising the total to 11. Odia had already been declared a classical language earlier (2014).

**Q3. (b)** — Durga Puja in Kolkata was inscribed in 2021. Kumbh Mela (2017) and Garba of Gujarat (2023) are also on India's list of intangible cultural heritage elements. Both statements are correct.

**Q4. (c)** — The Triveni Sangam at Prayagraj marks the confluence of the Ganga, the Yamuna and the mythical (invisible) Saraswati. The Maha Kumbh 2025 was held there from 13 January to 26 February 2025.

**Q5. (b)** — Lanjia Saura painting (Odisha) and Wancho wooden craft (Arunachal Pradesh) are correct, but the Banaras Tabla GI belongs to Uttar Pradesh, not West Bengal. Hence only two pairs are correctly matched.

**Q6. (a)** — Sattriya developed in the 15th-16th century in the Vaishnavite satras (monasteries) founded by Srimanta Sankardeva in Assam and was recognised as a classical dance form by the Sangeet Natak Akademi in 2000.

**Q7. (b)** — The Natya Shastra, dealing with dramaturgy, dance, music and aesthetics (including the theory of rasa), is attributed to Bharata Muni. Nandikeshvara wrote the Abhinaya Darpana and Sharngadeva the Sangita Ratnakara.

**Q8. (d)** — Carnatic music is largely composition-based (kriti-centred), while Hindustani music allows greater scope for improvisation (alaap, taan). The tabla is characteristic of the Hindustani tradition; the mridangam is the main percussion in Carnatic music.

**Q9. (a)** — The Vesara style, developed notably under the Chalukyas and later the Hoysalas in the Deccan, is a hybrid that blends elements of the northern Nagara and southern Dravida styles.

**Q10. (c)** — Kangra and Basohli are both Pahari sub-schools; Basohli is famous for bold colours and the use of beetle-wing fragments, Kangra for its lyrical, delicate style. Tanjore (Thanjavur) paintings are known for gold foil and inlaid gems. All three are correct.

**Q11. (c)** — A chaitya is a prayer or worship hall usually enclosing a stupa, while a vihara is the monastery serving as the residence of monks. The relic mound is the stupa, and the gateway is the torana.

**Q12. (c)** — Guru Nanak (Punjab), Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (Bengal, Gaudiya Vaishnavism) and Andal (the only woman Alvar saint from Tamil Nadu) are all correctly matched.

**Q13. (b)** — The 'Dancing Girl' is a small bronze figurine made by the lost-wax (cire perdue) technique, found at Mohenjo-daro, belonging to the Indus (Harappan) Civilisation.

**Q14. (a)** — Mauryan (Ashokan) pillars are monolithic with a distinctive high polish (Mauryan/Chunar sandstone polish). The Lion Capital from Sarnath is India's State Emblem. All three statements are correct.

**Q15. (d)** — The Gandhara school (north-west) shows marked Greco-Roman influence and used grey schist, while the indigenous Mathura school used spotted red sandstone. Both statements are correct.

**Q16. (c)** — Cheraw (the bamboo dance) is from Mizoram, Bihu from Assam, and Ghoomar from Rajasthan. All three pairs are correctly matched.

**Q17. (a)** — The Sangam corpus in Tamil (the Ettuthogai/Eight Anthologies and Pattupattu/Ten Idylls) is the earliest surviving classical Tamil literature, a key reason for Tamil's classical status.

**Q18. (b)** — The finest Nataraja bronzes were produced under the Cholas using the lost-wax technique, representing the peak of South Indian metal sculpture.

**Q19. (d)** — Ajanta paintings were done in the fresco-secco (applied on a dry surface) technique and depict predominantly Buddhist themes, including the Jataka stories of the Buddha's previous births. Both statements are correct.

**Q20. (a)** — The classical four-fold classification is Tata (stringed), Sushira (wind, e.g., flute), Avanaddha (membrane/percussion, e.g., tabla) and Ghana (solid/non-membrane percussion, e.g., cymbals). Sushira denotes wind instruments.

**Q21. (c)** — The six orthodox (astika) schools are Nyaya, Vaisheshika, Samkhya, Yoga, Mimamsa and Vedanta. Charvaka (Lokayata) is a heterodox (nastika/materialist) school that rejects the authority of the Vedas.

**Q22. (b)** — Hornbill (Nagaland) and Losar (Ladakh/Arunachal Buddhist regions) are correct, but Wangala, the Garo '100 drums' harvest festival, belongs to Meghalaya, not Nagaland. Hence only two pairs are correctly matched.

**Q23. (b)** — Tholu Bommalata is the leather shadow-puppet tradition of Andhra Pradesh. (Kathputli is the string-puppet form of Rajasthan; Tholpavakoothu is Kerala's shadow puppetry.)

**Q24. (d)** — The Chishti order (silsila) was established in India by Khwaja Moinuddin Chishti, whose dargah is at Ajmer. The Naqshbandi order is linked to Bahauddin Naqshband and later Sirhindi; the Qadiri order to Abdul Qadir Jilani.

**Q25. (a)** — The Bengal School, a nationalist art movement reacting against academic Western realism, was pioneered by Abanindranath Tagore (e.g., 'Bharat Mata'). Raja Ravi Varma represented the earlier academic-realist style.
