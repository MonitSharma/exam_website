## Answer key with explanations

**1. (c)** Article 245 permits Parliament to legislate for all or part of India and protects parliamentary laws from invalidity solely on the ground of extra-territorial operation.

**2. (b)** A law made under Article 249 continues for six months after the Rajya Sabha resolution ceases to operate; past acts under the law remain unaffected.

**3. (d)** All three follow Article 252: Parliament legislates on consenting States' request, other States may adopt the law later, and only Parliament can amend or repeal it in those States.

**4. (c)** Article 256 authorises compliance directions, while Article 365 states the possible constitutional consequence of a State failing to give effect to Union directions.

**5. (a)** The Council is an advisory and coordination body. Article 263 does not make it a court or give it power to issue binding decrees.

**6. (b)** Article 261 provides for full faith and credit to public acts, records and judicial proceedings across India.

**7. (d)** Article 304(b) permits reasonable public-interest restrictions, but requires the President's previous sanction before the Bill or amendment is introduced.

**8. (c)** Approval and six-month continuations require the Article 368-type special majority: a majority of total membership plus two-thirds present and voting in each House.

**9. (d)** President's Rule is approved six months at a time, has a three-year constitutional maximum, and the two stated conditions are required for continuation beyond one year.

**10. (a)** Article 360 permits both salary-reduction directions, including for constitutional-court judges, and reservation of State Money or Financial Bills for the President.

**11. (c)** In a direct tax, impact and incidence ordinarily rest on the same person; indirect taxes are designed to be shifted through prices.

**12. (d)** The statements capture the standard meanings of progressive, proportional and regressive tax structures.

**13. (c)** Ad valorem duties vary with value, while specific duties are fixed with reference to a physical unit.

**14. (b)** Input tax credit allows eligible input taxes to be credited against output-tax liability, so tax is effectively imposed on value addition rather than repeatedly on gross value.

**15. (a)** The quorum and Union-State weightages are correct. Votes are weighted, so the decision rule is not one member, one equal vote.

**16. (c)** Tax expenditures are deviations from the benchmark tax structure that confer concessions and therefore represent revenue forgone.

**17. (d)** The crisis response combined exchange-rate adjustment with industrial delicensing and a phased opening of trade, among wider stabilisation and structural reforms.

**18. (b)** LPG is the standard shorthand for Liberalisation, Privatisation and Globalisation, the three broad directions of the post-1991 reform programme.

**19. (a)** The sequence is Calcutta (September 1920), Nagpur (December 1920), Chauri Chaura (5 February 1922), and Bardoli (12 February 1922).

**20. (d)** Breaking the salt law became central to the Civil Disobedience Movement in 1930; the Non-Cooperation programme used surrender, boycott and constructive work.

**21. (b)** All three changes helped transform the Congress from an annual deliberative body into a broad-based, continuously functioning mass organisation.

**22. (b)** The Ali brothers were among the principal leaders of the Khilafat agitation, which Gandhi linked with Non-Cooperation.

**23. (c)** The Pro-changers favoured entering councils to obstruct colonial governance from within; the No-changers prioritised Gandhi's constructive programme outside them.

**24. (a)** The Swarajists contested elections to enter the legislatures and pursue obstruction, while remaining within the wider national movement.

**25. (d)** All three are correct: Kakori belongs to the HRA phase, the socialist reorganisation occurred in 1928, and the low-intensity Assembly bombs were intended to publicise the cause.

**26. (b)** The Bardoli decision suspended mass action, and Gandhi redirected cadres toward constructive activity and preparation rooted in non-violence.

**27. (a)** Richness counts how many species occur, while evenness describes how uniformly individuals are distributed among those species.

**28. (c)** Beta diversity captures species turnover between habitats; alpha is within-habitat diversity and gamma covers the wider landscape.

**29. (b)** An endemic species has a naturally restricted geographical distribution, which can also make it vulnerable to habitat loss in that area.

**30. (c)** A hotspot must combine exceptional plant endemism with severe habitat loss: at least 1,500 endemic vascular plants and no more than 30 per cent original vegetation remaining.

**31. (a)** Seed banks conserve genetic material outside the natural habitat and are ex situ; national parks and sanctuaries conserve organisms in situ.

**32. (d)** Data Deficient and Not Evaluated describe assessment status, not a level within the threatened categories Vulnerable, Endangered and Critically Endangered.

**33. (a)** CITES is a trade-control treaty, whereas the Red List is a risk assessment. Their categories use different criteria and have different legal effects.

**34. (b)** The north-east monsoon gathers moisture over the Bay of Bengal and brings substantial October-December rainfall to the Coromandel coast, including Tamil Nadu.

**35. (c)** A northward shift of the monsoon trough suppresses rain over much of the northern plains while enhancing it along the Himalayan foothills.

**36. (a)** The positive IOD has a warm western and cool eastern pole, strengthening the westward moisture gradient; it can support the monsoon but does not determine rainfall by itself.

**37. (c)** These are extra-tropical systems originating around the Mediterranean-West Asian region and carried eastward by the westerlies.

**38. (d)** Khadar is younger, frequently renewed floodplain alluvium; bhangar is older alluvium occupying relatively elevated terraces.

**39. (b)** Black soil is closely linked to Deccan basalt and has shrink-swell clay that cracks when dry; it is generally deficient in nitrogen and phosphorus.

**40. (b)** All three are correct under the Disaster Management Act framework: NDMA and SDMA are chaired by the Prime Minister and Chief Minister, while the NDRF is operational.

**41. (d)** All four roles are correctly matched; Agni was the fifth system associated with the original IGMDP family.

**42. (a)** A sealed launch canister protects the missile, simplifies transport and handling, and permits faster launch preparation.

**43. (c)** Cruise missiles fly like unmanned aircraft using aerodynamic lift and sustained propulsion; ballistic missiles coast largely under gravity after their powered boost.

**44. (b)** The 2003 statement retains No First Use but reserves the option of nuclear retaliation in response to a major chemical or biological weapons attack.

**45. (d)** The Political Council chaired by the Prime Minister alone authorises nuclear use; the NSA-led Executive Council provides inputs and executes its directives.

**46. (b)** Soft power works through attraction and consent, unlike coercive military power or structural control over economic rules.

**47. (c)** Bandwagoning means working within and benefiting from the hegemonic order; hiding means reducing exposure to the hegemon's exercise of power.

**48. (a)** Desert Storm followed Iraq's invasion of Kuwait (1991), Enduring Freedom began in Afghanistan (2001), and Iraqi Freedom began in Iraq (2003).

**49. (d)** Both are standard features of ASEAN: an informal consensus-oriented diplomatic style and the ARF's wider security-dialogue role.

**50. (a)** China pursued gradual, state-guided sequencing, beginning with agricultural changes and using SEZs as controlled openings, rather than rapid wholesale privatisation.
