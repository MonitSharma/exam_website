## Section A — Reading Comprehension (Q1–Q6)

### Passage I — Q1–Q3 (systems science / analytical)

> Anyone who has crawled along a motorway only to find, miles later, no accident, no stalled lorry, nothing at all to explain the delay, has met one of the stranger products of collective behaviour: the phantom traffic jam. Such jams are not caused by any single obstacle. They emerge from the interaction of hundreds of drivers, each reacting a fraction of a second late to the car ahead. When traffic is light, a driver's small hesitation is absorbed harmlessly. But past a certain density, one driver's tap on the brakes forces the driver behind to brake a little harder, and the next harder still, until a wave of stopped cars travels backward through the flow — even as the road ahead empties. The jam is real, yet it has no cause you could point to; it is a property of the system, not of any part of it.
>
> What makes phantom jams instructive is that they cannot be cured by the usual instinct to find and remove a culprit. There is no culprit. Widening the road often merely raises the density at which the instability reappears. The more promising interventions work on the collective dynamics themselves: adaptive speed limits that smooth the flow, or automated cars that brake gently and in anticipation rather than reactively, damping the wave before it can build. The lesson reaches beyond the motorway. Wherever many agents respond locally, and with delay, to one another — in financial markets, in crowds, in supply chains — the most consequential disturbances may be those that arise from the pattern of interaction itself, owing nothing to any flaw in the individuals involved. To look for a villain in such cases is to misunderstand what kind of thing has gone wrong.

**Q1.** Which of the following best captures the central argument of the passage?

- (a) Traffic jams are usually caused by accidents or obstacles that, once removed, allow the flow to recover.
- (b) In systems of many interacting agents, some serious disruptions emerge from the pattern of interaction itself rather than from any individual fault, and so resist remedies aimed at finding a culprit.
- (c) Automated vehicles will, in time, eliminate congestion on all major roads.
- (d) Widening roads is the single most effective way to reduce the density that causes congestion.

**Q2.** It can be inferred that the author would regard the search for one person or part to blame for a phantom jam as:

- (a) justified, since every observable effect must have a definite, locatable cause.
- (b) misguided, because the phenomenon is a property of the collective system rather than of any component.
- (c) a useful first step, even if it ultimately proves incomplete.
- (d) impossible, because the individual causes are simply too numerous to identify.

**Q3.** The author's claim that widening the road often fails rests on the assumption that:

- (a) drivers always distribute themselves evenly across all available lanes.
- (b) added road capacity tends to be taken up, so the instability simply reappears at a higher density.
- (c) governments cannot afford the cost of widening motorways.
- (d) phantom jams occur only on motorways and not on ordinary roads.

### Passage II — Q4–Q6 (abstract / philosophical)

> To forgive is commonly imagined as a private softening of the heart: the injured party, by an inward effort, ceases to feel resentment and so releases the wrongdoer from a debt. On this picture forgiveness is something one does chiefly to oneself, a kind of self-administered cure for the poison of grievance. Yet this account, for all its consolations, misses what is most demanding in the act. Resentment is not merely an unpleasant feeling to be dissolved; it is a form of moral protest. To resent an injury is to insist that it mattered, that the person who inflicted it was answerable, that one's own standing was not nothing. To give up resentment too readily — before the wrong has been acknowledged, before anything has changed — is not always a triumph of grace. It may be a quiet abandonment of the claim that one deserved better.
>
> Forgiveness worthy of the name, then, is neither forgetfulness nor the mere subsiding of anger. It is the deliberate decision to no longer hold a wrong against someone whom one continues to regard as having done wrong. The paradox is essential: one must keep the judgment while releasing the grievance. This is why forgiveness cannot be exacted as a duty, nor dispensed as a favour to spare the wrongdoer discomfort. Offered too cheaply, it insults the one who offers it, for it treats the injury as though it had never counted. Offered rightly, it is among the hardest of moral achievements, precisely because it asks the injured to affirm their own worth and, in the same motion, to lay down the weapon that worth entitles them to carry.

**Q4.** Which of the following best captures the central idea of the passage?

- (a) Forgiveness is best understood as forgetting an injury so that resentment can gradually fade.
- (b) Genuine forgiveness releases the grievance while retaining the judgment that a wrong was done, and is therefore a demanding moral act rather than a mere easing of feeling.
- (c) Resentment is a poison that a wise person should learn to dissolve as quickly as possible.
- (d) Wrongdoers are under a duty to seek forgiveness from those they have injured.

**Q5.** It can be inferred that the author considers forgiving "too readily," before any acknowledgement of the wrong, problematic mainly because it:

- (a) allows the wrongdoer to escape any legal punishment they deserve.
- (b) can amount to surrendering the legitimate moral claim that one was wronged and deserved better.
- (c) makes the injured party feel morally superior to the wrongdoer.
- (d) is a psychological state that cannot in practice be sustained for long.

**Q6.** The author's tone toward the "private softening of the heart" view of forgiveness is best described as:

- (a) contemptuous and dismissive.
- (b) respectful of its appeal but ultimately critical of its adequacy.
- (c) wholly endorsing and admiring.
- (d) indifferent and detached.

## Answers & Worked Methods

### Reading Comprehension

**Q1 — (b).** *Main idea.* The passage moves from the concrete phantom jam to a general thesis: in systems of many delayed, interacting agents, some serious disturbances "arise from the pattern of interaction itself," so hunting for a culprit misfires. (a) and (d) are views the text rejects; (c) is an over-strong extrapolation from one illustration.

**Q2 — (b).** *Inference.* "To look for a villain in such cases is to misunderstand what kind of thing has gone wrong" — the fault is systemic, not local. (d) is the trap: the problem is not that causes are *too numerous* to find, but that there is no single culprit to find at all.

**Q3 — (b).** *Assumption.* The criticism that widening "merely raises the density at which the instability reappears" only bites if the extra capacity gets used up (induced demand). Negation test: if added capacity were *not* taken up, widening would in fact relieve the jam and the objection collapses — so the argument assumes (b). (a), (c) and (d) are irrelevant to the reasoning.

**Q4 — (b).** *Main idea.* The passage's thesis is the "essential paradox": keep the judgment that a wrong was done while releasing the grievance, which makes genuine forgiveness "among the hardest of moral achievements." (a) and (c) are the shallow view being criticised; (d) is never claimed.

**Q5 — (b).** *Inference.* The text states that giving up resentment too readily "may be a quiet abandonment of the claim that one deserved better." (a) imports legal punishment the passage never discusses; (c) and (d) are not supported.

**Q6 — (b).** *Tone.* The author grants the common view its "consolations" and appeal, then judges that it "misses what is most demanding" — appreciative but ultimately critical. Not contempt (a), not endorsement (c), not indifference (d).

### Logical Reasoning & Analytical Ability

**Q7 — (b) R.** Fix seats 1–6 clockwise; facing the centre means **right = counter-clockwise (decreasing), left = clockwise (increasing).** Put Q at 1. "P second to the right of Q" → 1 → 6 → 5, so **P = 5.** "R third to the left of P" → 5 → 6 → 1 → 2, so **R = 2.** "U immediately to the left of P" → seat 6, so **U = 6.** "S immediately to the right of T" fixes the last two seats (3, 4) as **T = 4, S = 3** (right of T(4) → seat 3), and (v) T not adjacent to Q holds. Final (clockwise): 1-Q, 2-R, 3-S, 4-T, 5-P, 6-U. Second to the **left** of U(6): 6 → 1 → 2 = seat 2 = **R.**

**Q8 — (b) Wednesday.** Count odd days. 12 Jul 2026 → 12 Jul 2027 spans a non-leap February (2027), = 365 days = 52 weeks + **1** odd day → Sunday + 1 = Monday. 12 Jul 2027 → 12 Jul 2028 spans Feb 2028 (leap), = 366 days = 52 weeks + **2** odd days → Monday + 2 = **Wednesday.** (Total 1 + 2 = 3 odd days from Sunday.)

**Q9 — (c) Only II and III.** All poets ⊂ dreamers; some dreamers = realists. **I** — the realist-dreamers need not be poets, so "some poets are realists" does not follow. **II** — "some dreamers are realists" converts to "some realists are dreamers": follows. **III** — a model where poets = dreamers (all dreamers are poets) with some of them realist is fully consistent with both statements, so it is a valid *possibility*: follows. Hence II and III only.

**Q10 — (b) 4158.** HEAD → H = 8, E = 5, A = 1, D = 4 → concatenate → 8514 → reverse the digits → **4158.** Option (a) 8514 is the trap for anyone who forgets the final reversal step.

**Q11 — (a) Chetan and Esha.** Chain the inequalities: Esha < Amar < Bina < Divya, and Chetan > Divya. Full order: **Chetan > Divya > Bina > Amar > Esha.** Highest = Chetan, lowest = Esha; the order is fully determined, so (d) is wrong.

**Q12 — (b) 24.** 64 = 4³, so each edge has n = 4 small cubes. Exactly-two-faces-painted cubes lie along the edges but not at corners: 12 edges × (n − 2) = 12 × 2 = **24.** (Check: corners 8 + two-face 24 + one-face 6×2² = 24 + interior 2³ = 8 → 8 + 24 + 24 + 8 = 64 ✓.) Trap: (a) 8 is the three-face count, (c) 12 is just the number of edges.

### Data Interpretation

**Q13 — (a) Alpha.** Totals: Alpha 120 + 200 + 160 = **480**; Beta 90 + 150 + 210 = 450; Gamma 160 + 140 + 100 = 400; Delta 130 + 180 + 160 = 470. Highest = Alpha (480).

**Q14 — (b) 46.7%.** Beta August = 210; Beta's own three-month total = 450. 210 / 450 = 0.4667 = **46.7%.** Trap (a) 33.3% comes from dividing by the *all-cities* August total (160 + 210 + 100 + 160 = 630): 210/630 = 33.3% — the wrong denominator.

**Q15 — (b) ₹12,000.** Housing = 30% of 80,000 = ₹24,000; Education = 15% of 80,000 = ₹12,000. Difference = 24,000 − 12,000 = **₹12,000.** (Equivalently, the 15-percentage-point gap × 80,000 = ₹12,000.)

**Q16 — (c) 32.7%.** New housing = 24,000 × 1.20 = ₹28,800. New income = 80,000 × 1.10 = ₹88,000. Share = 28,800 / 88,000 = 0.3272 = **32.7%.** Trap (a) 30.0% assumes the proportion is unchanged; it isn't, because housing grows faster than income.

### Decision-Making

**Q17 — (c).** Balances rule-adherence with empathy: understand the cause, regularise the leave through proper provisions, and reaffirm the process — without either ignoring the lapse (b) or over-punishing / going over the person's head (a, d).

**Q18 — (b).** Integrity and transparency: disclose promptly, correct the figure, own the mistake. Concealment (a), shifting blame (c) and passive waiting (d) all fail the accountability test.

**Q19 — (c).** A leader stays impartial, redirects the conflict from personal blame to shared work responsibilities, and sets clear conduct expectations — rather than taking sides (a), abdicating (b) or reaching for a disruptive quick fix (d).

### Quantitative Aptitude

**Q20 — (a) 15.2% profit.** Let CP = 100. MP = 160. After 20% off: 160 × 0.80 = 128. After a further 10% off: 128 × 0.90 = 115.2. SP = 115.2 → profit = **15.2%.** Trap (b) 30% comes from naïvely doing 60 − 20 − 10; successive percentages don't add.

**Q21 — (b) 4:21 and 9/11 minutes.** Let the hands meet m minutes past 4. Minute hand: 6m degrees. Hour hand: 120 + 0.5m degrees. Set equal: 6m = 120 + 0.5m → 5.5m = 120 → m = 120 / 5.5 = 240/11 = **21 9/11 minutes.** So 4:21 and 9/11 min (≈ 4:21:49).

**Q22 — (b) 127.** Subtract the remainders: 1657 − 6 = 1651 and 2037 − 5 = 2032; the required number is HCF(1651, 2032). 2032 − 1651 = 381; 1651 = 381 × 4 + 127; 381 = 127 × 3 + 0 → HCF = **127.** Check: 1657 ÷ 127 = 13 r 6 ✓ and 2037 ÷ 127 = 16 r 5 ✓.

## Timing & strategy tip
