## Section A — Reading Comprehension (Q1–Q6)

### Passage I — Q1–Q3 (economic / analytical)

> For two centuries the claim that machines would abolish work has been made confidently and refuted quietly. The refutation, however, has been misread. What history shows is not that technology creates as many jobs as it destroys by some law of nature, but that it has so far happened to do so, through a mechanism that can be described — and that can therefore fail.
>
> The useful unit of analysis is not the occupation but the task. A radiologist, a clerk and a machinist each perform a bundle of distinct activities, and a new technology rarely takes the bundle whole; it takes some tasks and leaves others. Two effects follow. The displacement effect removes tasks from labour and assigns them to capital, reducing the demand for workers at any given wage. The reinstatement effect works in the other direction: as production is reorganised, new tasks appear in which labour holds a comparative advantage, and these absorb the displaced. The historical record is the sum of the two, and the reassuring aggregate conceals how contingent the second term is.
>
> Nothing guarantees that reinstatement will keep pace. A technology may be adopted merely because it is marginally cheaper than the worker it replaces, while adding little to output — automation that is, in the strict sense, mediocre. Such innovation shifts income from labour to capital without expanding the pie enough to compensate anyone. Meanwhile the research subsidies, tax codes and procurement rules of most states are tilted, often unintentionally, towards replacing labour rather than augmenting it.
>
> The right question, then, is not whether there will be enough work. It is which tasks we choose to create, and who captures the gains from those we automate. That is a matter of policy and of design, not of destiny — which is at once the more hopeful answer and the more demanding one.

**Q1.** Which of the following best captures the central argument of the passage?

(a) History proves that technology has always created at least as many jobs as it has destroyed, so present anxieties are unfounded.
(b) The occupation, rather than the task, is the correct unit for analysing the labour-market effects of new technology.
(c) The pace of automation must be slowed until displaced workers can be retrained for the jobs that remain.
(d) The employment effect of automation turns on a contingent, policy-sensitive balance between the displacement and the creation of tasks, rather than on any inevitable law.

**Q2.** It can be inferred that the author would regard a technology that automates a task at slightly lower cost than the worker it replaces, without materially raising output, as:

(b) neutral, since the displaced workers will in due course be reinstated in new tasks.
(c) harmful on balance, since it redistributes income without enlarging the gains available to be shared.
(d) impossible, since firms adopt only those technologies that substantially raise productivity.

**Q3.** The passage's closing claim that the outcome is "a matter of policy and of design, not of destiny" rests most directly on the assumption that:

(a) workers can be retrained for whatever new tasks happen to emerge.
(b) the direction in which technology develops responds to incentives that states and firms are able to alter.

### Passage II — Q4–Q6 (abstract / philosophical)

> The most common defence of surveillance is also the most misleading: that a person with nothing to hide has nothing to fear. The reply usually offered — that everyone has something to hide — concedes the wrong point. It accepts that privacy is essentially concealment, a shelter for what would embarrass or incriminate us, and then bargains over how much shelter we need. But if privacy were only that, the innocent would indeed have little stake in it, and the argument would be over before it began.
>
> Consider instead what we actually do with the boundaries we keep. We tell our physician what we do not tell our employer, and our closest friend what we do not tell our physician. These are not evasions; they are the substance of the relationships themselves. A friendship is constituted in part by what is shared with the friend and not with others, and a world in which everything were available to everyone would not be a world of perfect honesty but one in which such distinctions had no material to work with. Intimacy is not a feeling added to information; it is a pattern of asymmetric disclosure.
>
> There is a second loss, less often noticed. Thinking requires a space in which one may entertain a view without yet owning it — where an idea can be tried on, found ridiculous, and abandoned before anyone has seen it. Under observation, the trying-on stops. One rehearses only what one is already prepared to defend, and the interior draft becomes a public statement long before it is ready.
>
> Neither loss registers as a harm in the way a theft does. Nothing is taken; a person is merely, and permanently, in view. That is precisely why the language of "nothing to hide" persists: it invites us to produce a victim and a stolen good, when what is being worn away is a condition rather than a possession.

**Q4.** Which of the following best captures the central idea of the passage?

(b) Since everyone has something to hide, surveillance harms the innocent and the guilty alike.
(c) Surveillance should be prohibited because it places dangerous powers in the hands of the state.
(d) Honesty is unattainable in any society whose members keep information from one another.

**Q5.** It can be inferred that the author regards the reply "everyone has something to hide" as:

(d) beside the point, since surveillance is a legal rather than a moral question.

## Answers & Worked Methods

### Reading Comprehension

**Q1 — (d).** *Main idea.* The passage's spine is that history's reassuring aggregate is the *sum* of two effects, and that "nothing guarantees that reinstatement will keep pace" — hence the closing shift to policy and design. (a) is the misreading the first paragraph corrects; (c) is never proposed; (b) is the opposite of the stated method ("the useful unit of analysis is not the occupation but the task").

**Q2 — (c).** *Inference.* This is the passage's own definition of "mediocre" automation: it "shifts income from labour to capital without expanding the pie enough to compensate anyone." (b) is the trap — the whole point is that reinstatement is contingent, not automatic.

**Q3 — (b).** *Assumption.* Negation test: if the direction of technological development were unresponsive to subsidies, tax codes and procurement rules, then the outcome *would* be destiny and the closing claim collapses. The mention of state incentives being "tilted… towards replacing labour rather than augmenting it" only bites if those tilts can be changed. (a) is a separate claim about workers, not about direction; (c) contradicts the task framework; (d) is historically irrelevant to the argument.

**Q4 — (a).** *Main idea.* Two positive claims carry the passage: differentiated relationships are *constituted* by asymmetric disclosure, and thought needs an unobserved draft stage. (b) is explicitly named as conceding the wrong point; (c) is an argument about state power the passage never makes; (d) inverts the friendship paragraph.

**Q5 — (c).** *Inference.* "It accepts that privacy is essentially concealment… and then bargains over how much shelter we need." The objection is not that the reply is false but that it fights on the wrong ground. (a) is the trap: the author never denies that people have things to hide.

**Q6 — (c).** *Tone.* The author reasons through the case calmly, conceding what the opposing slogan gets right before dismantling it, and closes with a measured formulation ("a condition rather than a possession") rather than an alarm. Not polemical (a); the passage clearly takes a side, so not non-committal (b); no appeal to a better past, so not (d).

### Logical Reasoning & Analytical Ability

**Q7 — (d) Pari — Cuttack — hockey.** Ojas plays tennis, so by (i) he is not from Delhi and by (v) not from Bhopal; Cuttack is Pari's by (iv); hence **Ojas = Erode**. Rahul is not from Erode (vi) and not Cuttack, so Neha and Rahul share Bhopal and Delhi; Neha is not from Delhi (ii), so **Neha = Bhopal, Rahul = Delhi**. Then Neha plays badminton (v) — consistent with (ii) — and Rahul plays chess (i), leaving **hockey for Pari**. Final: Neha–Bhopal–badminton, Ojas–Erode–tennis, Pari–Cuttack–hockey, Rahul–Delhi–chess. Only (d) matches.

**Q8 — (c) Either I or II follows.** From "all clerks are auditors" the clerks sit inside the auditor circle; "some auditors are engineers" says the engineer overlap touches auditors *somewhere*, but not necessarily inside the clerk sub-region. So **I does not follow** (the overlap may miss the clerks entirely) and **II does not follow** either (the overlap may fall wholly within the clerks). But "some clerks are engineers" and "no clerk is an engineer" are contradictories — exactly one of them must be true in every diagram. That is precisely the either-or case. Trap (d): "neither follows individually" is the *precondition* for either-or, not the answer.

**Q9 — (b) −2.** LOGIC: consonants L(12) + G(7) + C(3) = **22**; vowels O(15) + I(9) = **24**; code = 22 − 24 = **−2**. (Check the example: F(6) + C(3) = 9, A(1) + E(5) = 6, 9 − 6 = 3 ✓.) Trap (a) 2 is the answer for anyone who subtracts in the wrong order; (c) 46 is the total 22 + 24.

**Q10 — (d) Cousin.** "My father's mother" = Meera's paternal grandmother. Her **only daughter** cannot be Meera's mother (Meera's father is her *son*); it is Meera's paternal aunt. That aunt's only son is therefore Meera's **cousin**. Trap (a) Brother comes from misreading "only daughter of my father's mother" as Meera's own mother.

**Q11 — (b) South.** Shortly after sunrise the sun is in the east, so every shadow points **west**. Sunil's shadow fell to Vikram's right ⇒ west lies on Vikram's right hand ⇒ Vikram faces **south**. (Consistency check: facing south, the right hand points west ✓; since they face each other, Sunil faces north.)

**Q12 — (c) Referred to the Director.** Condition (i) is met (26 lies in 21–28) and condition (iii) is met (3 years ≥ 2). Condition (ii) fails — 58% is below 60%. Since (ii) **alone** fails and Ananya holds a doctoral degree, clause (iv) applies exactly: the case goes to the **Director**. (v) is irrelevant because (iii) is satisfied, and (b) is wrong because the rules provide a referral rather than a rejection.

### Data Interpretation

**Q13 — (d) Wheat.** Production = area × yield: Rice 30 × 2.6 = **78**; Wheat 24 × 3.5 = **84**; Maize 15 × 4.2 = **63**; Pulses 20 × 1.1 = **22**; Cotton 12 × 2.0 = **24** (lakh tonnes). Highest = **Wheat (84)**. Trap (a) Rice has the largest *area*; trap (c) Maize has the highest *yield* — neither wins on the product.

**Q14 — (a) 12.6%.** New pulses area = 20 × 1.5 = 30 lakh ha; new pulses production = 30 × 1.2 = **36** lakh tonnes. Old total = 78 + 84 + 63 + 22 + 24 = **271**; new total = 271 − 22 + 36 = **285**. Share = 36 / 285 = 0.1263 ≈ **12.6%**. Trap (b) 13.3% divides by the *old* total (36 / 271) and forgets that the denominator has grown too; (c) 8.1% is the original share (22/271).

**Q15 — (d) 538.** Applications: Revenue 40% × 12,000 = **4,800**; Health 35% × 12,000 = **4,200**; Education = 12,000 − 9,000 = **3,000**. Shortlisted: 15% × 4,800 = **720**; 20% × 4,200 = **840**; 25% × 3,000 = **750**. Selected: 720 ÷ 6 = **120**; 840 ÷ 5 = **168**; 750 ÷ 3 = **250**. Total = 120 + 168 + 250 = **538**.

**Q16 — (a) 4.5%.** 538 / 12,000 = 0.04483 ≈ **4.5%**. Trap (b) 4.9% is the plain average of the three departmental selection rates — Revenue 120/4,800 = 2.5%, Health 168/4,200 = 4%, Education 250/3,000 = 8.33%, mean ≈ 4.94% — which is invalid because the departments have very different application bases. Trap (c) 23.3% is selected ÷ shortlisted (538 / 2,310), the wrong denominator.

### Decision-Making

**Q17 — (d).** Firm, proportionate and documented: it stops the malpractice and preserves the evidence, punishes only those involved rather than 400 innocent candidates, uses the police for perimeter security rather than for a confrontation, and reports up the same day so that no one can later allege a cover-up. (a) abandons the duty of the moment; (b) is grossly disproportionate; (c) yields to political pressure and destroys the evidence trail.

**Q18 — (b).** Rule-abiding *and* empathetic: the false claim is refused, but the colleague's real difficulty is met with the entitlements that actually exist, and escalation is reserved for a pattern rather than a first request. (a) and (d) both certify a false claim — the amount does not change what it is; (c) is disproportionate for a first approach that caused no loss and forecloses the corrective conversation.

**Q19 — (c).** An officer has no discretion to disobey a court order, but does have a duty to explain and to seek a lawful remedy for genuine hardship. (c) complies, communicates in person rather than hiding, records the impact, and channels the relief through the department. (a) is contempt of a court order dressed up as compassion; (b) is technically correct but abandons the public at exactly the moment communication matters most; (d) is public buck-passing that neither helps the families nor serves institutional discipline.

### Quantitative Aptitude

**Q20 — (d) 3/11.** Total ways to choose 3 balls from 12 = C(12,3) = **220**. One of each colour = 5 × 4 × 3 = **60**. Probability = 60 / 220 = **3/11**. Trap (b) 1/22 is what you get from the sequential product 5/12 × 4/11 × 3/10 = 1/22 if you forget to multiply by the 3! = 6 possible orders (1/22 × 6 = 3/11 ✓).

**Q21 — (b) 60 minutes.** Without the leak, A + B fill at 1/20 + 1/30 = 5/60 = **1/12** per minute, i.e. 12 minutes. With the leak it takes 12 + 3 = **15** minutes, so the net rate is **1/15**. Leak rate = 1/12 − 1/15 = (5 − 4)/60 = **1/60** per minute ⇒ the leak alone empties a full tank in **60 minutes**.

**Q22 — (b) 58.32 litres.** Each replacement leaves the fraction (1 − 8/80) = 0.9 of the milk present. After three operations: 80 × (0.9)³ = 80 × 0.729 = **58.32 litres**. Trap (a) 56 litres is the naïve 80 − 3 × 8, which wrongly assumes every draw removes 8 litres of *pure milk*.

## Timing & strategy tip

**Open with Decision-Making, not with Passage I.** Q17–Q19 carry no negative marking, need no computation, and reward the same instinct every time — the option that follows the rule *and* treats the person decently is almost always the longest, most specific one. Three near-certain marks in about two minutes buys you the calm to attack the RC passages, which is where the real time sink is. Passage-first is the commonest self-inflicted wound in CSAT: candidates spend eleven minutes on two passages, panic, and then rush the sections that were free.
