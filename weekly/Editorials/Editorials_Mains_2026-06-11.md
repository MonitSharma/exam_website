## 1. India's road through Myanmar is one of engagement
**The Hindu (op-ed) · GS Paper 2 — IR (Neighbourhood First / Act East)**

**Core argument (2–3 lines):** India's decision to receive Myanmar's military chief U Min Aung Hlaing as President (visit 30 May–3 June 2026) is realpolitik, not endorsement. With the West isolating the junta and China filling the vacuum, India judged that engagement — not isolation — best protects its border, its Act East ambitions, and its strategic space.

**Key data / facts cited:**
- Myanmar shares a **1,643 km border** with four NE states (Arunachal, Nagaland, Manipur, Mizoram); it is India's **only land bridge to Southeast Asia**.
- Two stalled connectivity projects dominated the summit: the **Kaladan Multi-Modal Transit Transport Project** (Kolkata–Sittwe–Paletwa–Zorinpui; the critical 109 km Paletwa–Zorinpui road now targeted for **2027**) and the **India–Myanmar–Thailand Trilateral Highway** (Moreh–Mae Sot, ~1,360 km, originally due 2019).
- Bilateral trade **$1.95 billion (2025–26)**; both sides agreed to push a **rupee–kyat settlement** mechanism and explore critical-mineral/rare-earth cooperation.
- **2,400+ Indian nationals** rescued from Myanmar scam centres over 18 months; Mekong–Ganga ICCR scholarships raised from **36 to 100** annually.
- Quote attributed to Foreign Secretary Vikram Misri: India's policy is *"not intended to be a commentary on the internal political arrangements"* in Myanmar.

**The other side of the debate:** Engaging a junta that ousted an elected government (2021 coup) carries a normative cost — it dilutes India's credentials as a democracy and "Vishwaguru," and risks legitimising military rule in the neighbourhood. Critics also note connectivity gains are illusory while armed groups control corridor territory.

**Mains value-addition points:**
- Frame as the classic tension between **values (democracy promotion) and interests (security + connectivity)** in India's foreign policy — use as a counter-example to "ethical foreign policy."
- "**China factor**" template: strategic disengagement creates vacuums Beijing fills (cf. Sri Lanka, Maldives, Nepal) — engagement is risk-management, not approval.
- Connectivity-as-strategy: Kaladan + Trilateral Highway = operationalising **Act East** and NE integration; delays show how **internal conflict in a neighbour becomes India's infrastructure problem**.

## 2. The Supreme Court's SIR verdict — electoral rolls and the right to vote
**The Hindu (op-ed/analysis) · GS Paper 2 — Polity & Governance (ECI, electoral reforms)**

**Core argument (2–3 lines):** On 27 May 2026 a bench of CJI Surya Kant and Justice Joymalya Bagchi upheld the ECI's **Special Intensive Revision (SIR)** of electoral rolls as lawful, proportionate and procedurally sound. The op-eds welcome cleaner rolls in principle but question the legal basis, the timing, and the citizenship-verification overreach.

**Key data / facts cited:**
- Court grounded the power in **Section 21(3) of the RP Act, 1950**, reading "any constituency" expansively ("any" can mean "all" where context warrants) — rejecting petitioners' narrow, constituency-specific reading.
- Court held the ECI is **constitutionally duty-bound** to ensure only citizens are on the rolls, and may examine citizenship **only for inclusion/exclusion** (not as a general citizenship adjudication).
- Verdict framed as advancing the **constitutional imperative of free and fair elections** (Art. 324).

**The other side of the debate:** Critics argue the ECI devised a "novel mechanism with no rooting in written law," and that a **complete overhaul of rolls four months before the Bihar election** was unexplained — especially when routine summary revisions already occurred after the 2024 LS polls. Risk: a verification drive can become **de facto disenfranchisement** and shift the burden of proving eligibility onto poor/migrant voters.

**Mains value-addition points:**
- Use to discuss **ECI's autonomy vs. accountability** and the **"basic structure" anchor of free and fair elections** (Art. 324; *Mohinder Singh Gill*, *ADR* line of cases).
- Tension between **purity of the electoral roll** (one-person-one-vote integrity) and the **right to vote as a facet of democracy** — note vote is a statutory right but rooted in constitutional democracy.
- Governance lesson: **process legitimacy matters as much as outcome legitimacy** — timing and transparency determine whether a clean-up is seen as reform or exclusion.

## 3. Hormuz, chokepoints and the case for IMEC
**The Indian Express (op-ed) · GS Paper 2/3 — IR + Economy (energy security, connectivity)**

**Core argument (2–3 lines):** The 2026 Strait of Hormuz disruption exposed how dangerously exposed India is to a single chokepoint and to great-power conflict it does not control. The piece argues the crisis strengthens the case for diversification and for resilience corridors like **IMEC**, while honestly admitting IMEC's own war-zone vulnerabilities.

**Key data / facts cited:**
- Shipping through Hormuz largely blocked since **28 Feb 2026** after US–Israel strikes on Iran; ~**one-fifth of seaborne crude and LNG** transits the strait (IEA called it the largest supply disruption in oil-market history).
- India imports **~88% of its crude**; Hormuz carried ~**41% of crude, 55% of LNG, 88% of LPG** imports (first nine months FY2026).
- India's crude basket nearly doubled **$69/bbl (Feb) → $126 (Mar)**, peaking ~$157; March crude imports fell ~**23%**.
- Mitigation: imports now from **~40 countries**, ~**70% routed via alternative maritime routes**; **VLCC terminal at Mundra** (Jan 2026); resumed Iran imports after a 7-year gap; chose **bilateral safe-passage talks with Iran over joining the US-led naval coalition**.

**The other side of the debate:** IMEC is not a quick fix — as of mid-2026 it has **no firm funding or construction timelines**, and the Gaza/Iran conflicts directly threaten its corridor (Haifa port, Gulf infrastructure). Sceptics say diversified sourcing + strategic petroleum reserves deliver faster resilience than a still-paper megacorridor.

**Mains value-addition points:**
- Classic **chokepoint vulnerability** case study (pair with Malacca dilemma, Bab-el-Mandeb/Red Sea, Suez): argue for **route + source + storage diversification** as a three-layer energy-security doctrine.
- **Strategic autonomy in action:** India balancing US tilt, Iran ties and Gulf partners simultaneously — bilateral safe passage over bloc alignment.
- IMEC as **geoeconomics**: connectivity as a hedge against both Chinese BRI and chokepoint risk — but note "**resilience corridors are only as stable as the regions they cross.**"

## 4. India's new IIP series (base year 2022–23) — better data, sharper picture
**The Hindu (editorial/analysis) · GS Paper 3 — Economy (industrial growth, statistics & data quality)**

**Core argument (2–3 lines):** MoSPI's revised Index of Industrial Production rebases from 2011–12 to **2022–23** and widens coverage, giving a more accurate read of a structurally changed economy. Better statistics improve policy — but the rebasing also revealed a more sober industrial growth print.

**Key data / facts cited:**
- New base **2022–23**; basket up to **1,042 products / 463 item groups** (from 839 items / 407 groups); committee report released **25 May 2026**.
- For the first time IIP adds **gas supply, water supply, sewerage & waste management** beyond mining/manufacturing/electricity; tracks emerging items — **rare-earth minerals, aircraft/spacecraft parts, vaccines, medical devices, CCTV cameras**.
- **Manufacturing ≈ 76% weight**, grew **6.2%** in Apr 2026 (vs 6.3% Apr 2025); headline IIP grew **4.9% y-o-y** in April 2026 under the new series.

**The other side of the debate:** Rebasing improves accuracy but **breaks comparability** with the old series and can flatter or deflate trend growth; critics caution that a broader basket doesn't fix India's deeper issue — **uneven manufacturing momentum and weak consumption-driven sectors**. Frequent base revisions also fuel debates on **data credibility**.

**Mains value-addition points:**
- Use as evidence that **"statistical capacity is governance capacity"** — accurate, current data underpins evidence-based policy (link to NSO reforms, GDP base-year debates).
- Signals the economy's **structural shift** toward new-economy and services-linked industrial activity (rare earths, aerospace, medical devices) — supports "**make in India / value-addition up the chain**" arguments.
- Caveat for answers: a single month/print is noise; **judge industrial health on trend, capacity utilisation, and core-sector data**, not headline IIP alone.

## GS Mains practice question (this week's dominant theme)

> **"In an increasingly fragmented global order, India's foreign policy is shifting from value-based positioning to interest-based pragmatism." Critically examine in light of recent developments in India's neighbourhood and energy security. (15 marks, 250 words)**

*Directive word: **Critically examine** — present the claim, weigh evidence on both sides, and arrive at a reasoned judgement.*

**Model-answer skeleton (5 points):**
1. **Intro (frame):** Define the shift — strategic autonomy operating in a multipolar, conflict-prone order; one line that India is increasingly leading with interests (security, energy, connectivity) over normative posturing.
2. **Evidence FOR pragmatism:** Myanmar engagement despite the junta (geography + China factor + Act East); bilateral safe-passage talks with Iran over joining a US naval coalition; resumed Iran oil imports; diversified sourcing across ~40 countries.
3. **Evidence of continuity / values still matter:** India still frames choices in Neighbourhood First, multilateralism, Global South leadership; engagement is calibrated ("acceptance of reality, not endorsement"); IMEC and partnerships show rules-based connectivity ambitions.
4. **Costs & risks of pure pragmatism:** Reputational cost of dealing with authoritarian regimes; over-reliance on chokepoints/single partners; corridors (IMEC) exposed to the same instability they hedge against; need for credible capacity (data, infrastructure, reserves) to back the doctrine.
5. **Balanced conclusion:** India is best served by **"principled pragmatism"** — interests-led but anchored in long-term credibility, diversification (route + source + storage), and reliable partnerships; autonomy is sustained by capability, not slogans.
