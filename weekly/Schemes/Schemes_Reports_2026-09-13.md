### 1. DILRMP 3.0 — Digital India Land Records Modernisation Programme ⭐ *scheme of the week*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Nodal agency** | Department of Land Resources, **Ministry of Rural Development** |
| **Launched** | Operational Guidelines for DILRMP 3.0 released **10 September 2026**, Krishi Bhawan, New Delhi. Parent programme: began as NLRMP (2008), restructured and renamed **DILRMP in 2016** |
| **Type** | **Central Sector Scheme** |
| **Period / outlay** | 2026–2031 · **₹565.50 crore** |

**Objective.** Move land administration beyond stand-alone digitisation towards an integrated, **GIS-enabled "Land Stack"** that links cadastral maps, registration records and dispute data through secure APIs.

**Key features**

- **Universal Bhu-Aadhaar (ULPIN)** — nationwide rollout of the **14-digit Unique Land Parcel Identification Number** for every land parcel.
- **75 high-footfall Sub-Registrar Offices** to be upgraded into **Registration Seva Kendras** (dedicated budget **₹37.5 crore**).
- Paperless **Revenue Court Case Management System (RCCMS)** integrated with land records for dispute handling.
- Builds on the completed **NAKSHA** pilot for urban land-record modernisation.

**Coverage achieved so far.** Earlier phases digitised **99.90% of Records of Rights (RoRs)** and **97% of cadastral maps**.

**Significance.** Land disputes clog Indian courts and land-title opacity is a first-order constraint on credit, infrastructure acquisition and agricultural investment. An API-linked Land Stack is the land-sector analogue of India Stack. **Criticism / caution:** India follows a system of **presumptive** (not conclusive) title — digitisation alone does not confer guaranteed title; land is a **State subject (Entry 18, List II)**, so uptake depends entirely on State revenue departments; survey and map accuracy remains uneven outside pilot districts.

> **GS tag:** GS2 — Government policies & interventions; e-governance. **GS3** — land reforms, infrastructure.

### 2. National HPV Vaccination Campaign — crosses 80 lakh doses

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | Ministry of Health & Family Welfare |
| **Launched** | **28 February 2026**, Ajmer, Rajasthan |
| **Milestone** | **Over 80 lakh doses administered** (announced 11 September 2026) |

**Objective.** Population-level prevention of **cervical cancer**, India's second most common cancer among women.

**Key features**

- **Single-dose quadrivalent Gardasil-4** vaccine.
- Target group: **adolescent girls aged 14**; free, voluntary, with **parental consent**, at government health facilities.
- Annual cohort of 14-year-old girls ≈ **1.2 crore** (RGI 2021 estimates).

**Coverage.** **Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh and Mizoram** at 100% of identified targets; **Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, Assam** above 90%; **Karnataka** above 85%. **Uttar Pradesh** has the highest absolute number — over **22 lakh** girls.

**Significance.** WHO's global cervical-cancer elimination strategy targets 90% HPV vaccination of girls by age 15. Single-dose schedules cut cost and logistics sharply. **Criticism:** uptake is voluntary and consent-dependent, so vaccine hesitancy and messaging gaps remain the binding constraint; the campaign is not yet formally folded into the Universal Immunisation Programme schedule.

> **GS tag:** GS2 — Health; issues relating to development and management of Social Sector/Services.

### 5. PMMSY at six years — Pradhan Mantri Matsya Sampada Yojana *(milestone, 10 September 2026)*

### 6. Mission Rangeen Machhli 2031 — Strategic Action Plan for Ornamental Fisheries

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | Department of Fisheries, MoFAH&D |
| **Released** | **31 August 2026**, Agatti, **Lakshadweep**, by Vice-President **C. P. Radhakrishnan** |
| **Type** | Strategic action plan / roadmap (not a funded scheme in itself) |

**Objective.** Organise and scale India's **ornamental fisheries** (breeding, rearing and trade of decorative fish) into a globally competitive, regulated industry by 2031.

**Key features**

- Standardised protocols for **broodstock management** and hatchery operations.
- Uniform rules for **biosecurity, quarantine, quality assurance and health management**.
- Improved **traceability**, handling, transport and trade practices for export competitiveness.
- Special focus on **Lakshadweep's** marine ornamental resources as a sustainable livelihood and blue-economy avenue.

**Significance.** Ornamental fish is a high-value, low-volume niche where India's share of the global trade is tiny relative to its biodiversity endowment. Links directly to the **Blue Economy** pillar and island development. **Criticism:** wild collection of marine ornamentals carries reef-damage risk; enforcement of biosecurity norms across dispersed small units is difficult.

> **GS tag:** GS3 — Blue economy; agriculture & allied; conservation.

### 7. Comprehensive CSR Framework for Indian Coal Companies — first sector-wide CSR framework

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | **Ministry of Coal**, with Coal India Limited (CIL) |
| **Launched** | **8 September 2026**, New Delhi, by Union Minister for Coal & Mines **G. Kishan Reddy** |
| **Prepared by** | **Indian Institute of Corporate Affairs (IICA)** |
| **Distinction** | **First sector-specific CSR framework** since CSR became statutory under **Section 135, Companies Act, 2013** |

**Key features**

- Shifts coal-sector CSR from expenditure-reporting to **outcome-oriented, measurable** social impact.
- Tailored to development and environmental needs of **coal-bearing and mining-affected areas**.
- Emphasis on local community needs assessment, project planning and monitoring.
- Launched alongside CIL health milestones — **Thalassemia Bal Sewa Yojana (TBSY)** and **Nanha Sa Dil**: over **1,050 bone-marrow transplants** completed, assistance up to **₹10 lakh per patient**.

**Significance.** Coal PSUs are among India's largest CSR spenders, and mining districts overlap heavily with **Scheduled Areas** and aspirational districts. A sectoral framework addresses the mismatch between CSR spend and local displacement/environmental burden. **Criticism:** CSR is not compensation — it sits alongside, and can obscure, statutory obligations under the **DMF/PMKKKY** and rehabilitation law; frameworks without binding outcome audits risk remaining advisory.

> **GS tag:** GS2 — Government policies; GS3 — mineral resources, inclusive growth, corporate governance.

### 9. Nasha Mukt Bharat 2029 Roadmap *(new development on NMBA)*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | **Ministry of Home Affairs** (with Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment); NCB as enforcement arm |
| **Launched** | **6 September 2026**, Panaji, **Goa**, by Union Home Minister Amit Shah |
| **Period** | **July 2026 – July 2029** |

**Four pillars**

1. **Intelligence-led enforcement**
2. **Control of precursor and synthetic drugs**
3. **Demand reduction and harm reduction**
4. **Coordination and capacity building**

**Targets announced**

- Network of **one lakh "Nasha Mukt Bharat Mitras"** (volunteers).

### 10. OneTag — NETC FASTag portability

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Launched** | **11 September 2026**, **Global Fintech Fest 2026**, Mumbai, by Union Minister **Nitin Gadkari** |
| **Implementing bodies** | **IHMCL** (Indian Highways Management Company Ltd, promoted by NHAI) + **NPCI** |
| **Access** | NHAI's **Rajmargyatra** mobile app |

**What it does.** Allows a FASTag user to **switch issuer bank while retaining the same physical tag** on the vehicle — previously, changing banks required deactivating the old tag and obtaining a new one.

**Significance.** Extends the **portability principle** (already familiar from MNP and account-number portability) to tolling; increases competition among issuer banks and reduces consumer switching costs. Part of the broader NETC ecosystem alongside the "One Vehicle, One FASTag" rule.

## POLICY / REGULATORY CHANGE OF THE WEEK

### 11. Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) (Amendment) Rules, 2026

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Notified by** | **Department of Consumer Affairs**, Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution |
| **Parent law** | **Consumer Protection Act, 2019**; amends the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, **2020** |
| **Effective** | **1 January 2027** |

**Key provisions**

- Mandatory compliance with the **Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023**, plus an **annual self-audit** and display of a **certificate of compliance**.
- Tighter disclosure on **search algorithms and sponsored listings**.
- Curbs on **fake/inflated discount claims** — prior-price disclosure requirements.
- Stronger **seller disclosure** norms and consumer-data safeguards.

**Dark patterns covered.** Drip pricing, basket sneaking, confirm-shaming, subscription traps, misleading prompts.

**Why it matters.** Of **17.71 lakh** complaints on the **National Consumer Helpline in 2025**, over **5.11 lakh (~29%)** came from e-commerce. Enforcement sits with the **Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA)**, created under the 2019 Act.

**Criticism.** Self-audit and self-certification place the compliance burden on the same entity being regulated; the 1 January 2027 commencement gives a long runway; algorithmic-ranking transparency is hard to verify without regulatory access to code.

> **GS tag:** GS2 — Statutory/regulatory bodies; government policies. **GS3** — e-commerce, consumer protection, digital economy.

### A. *Unincorporated Sector at the District Level: Insights from ASUSE 2025* — **NSO / MoSPI** ⭐ *report of the week*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Publishing body** | **National Statistical Office (NSO)**, Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation |
| **Released** | **10 September 2026** |
| **Source survey** | **Annual Survey of Unincorporated Sector Enterprises (ASUSE) 2025** |
| **First of its kind** | India's **first-ever district-level estimates** of the unincorporated non-agricultural sector |

**What it measures.** Establishments and workers, ownership and employment structure, women's participation, registration status, emoluments per hired worker, and **GVA per establishment and per worker** — for the informal/unincorporated non-farm economy.

**Coverage.** **757 of 770 districts** in the sampling frame.

**Key findings**

1. The **top 50 districts account for nearly one-third** of the sector's establishments, workforce and GVA — extreme spatial concentration of informal economic activity.
2. All-India average **GVA per worker ≈ ₹1,56,539**.
3. In **Mizoram**, female-owned proprietary establishments constitute **at least 50%** of establishments in *every* district of the State.

**Significance.** The unincorporated sector employs the overwhelming majority of India's non-farm workforce. Until now, large enterprise surveys produced estimates only at State/national level — district granularity lets administrators identify laggard districts and design targeted credit, cluster and skilling interventions. Directly useful for **MSME policy, PM Vishwakarma, PMEGP and district-level planning**.

> **GS tag:** GS3 — Indian economy, employment, MSMEs, informal sector. **GS2** — statistical governance.
