## SCHEMES, PROGRAMMES & INITIATIVES

### 1. BIO-NIVESH ⭐ *initiative of the week*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | Department of Biotechnology (DBT), **Ministry of Science & Technology**, with **BIRAC** (Biotechnology Industry Research Assistance Council) |
| **Launched** | **3 September 2026**, New Delhi, by Dr Jitendra Singh (MoS, Independent Charge, Science & Technology) |
| **Type** | Platform / recurring investor-connect initiative (not a funded scheme with a separate outlay) |

**Objective:** Close the "valley of death" between laboratory-stage biotech research and commercial scale-up by channelling **private capital** into biotechnology and deep-tech startups.

**Key features (4):**

- **Two-way engagement design**, not a conventional startup pitch-fest — investors and innovators both present, so that regulatory and scale-up bottlenecks are discussed rather than only valuations.
- **Inaugural edition:** ~**20 startups** (selected on innovation, development stage, market potential, scalability and regulatory readiness) with ~**50 private investors**.
- **Recurring, multi-city format** — future editions planned in other cities to plug regional biotech ecosystems into national investment networks.
- Stated approach: **"Whole of Government plus Whole of Nation"**.

**Institutional anchor to memorise:** **BIRAC** — a not-for-profit **Section 8 company** under DBT, set up in **2012**; acts as a **Second-Level Fund Manager** under the **RDI (Research, Development and Innovation) Fund**.

**Significance:** India's BioE3 policy and the ₹1 lakh crore RDI Fund both assume private risk capital will follow public seed money; BIO-NIVESH is the plumbing for that assumption. Biotech is one of the few sectors where India has deep public R&D but shallow late-stage venture funding.

**Criticism / watch-points:** A convening platform is not a financing instrument — without a co-investment or first-loss guarantee window, it risks being an annual networking event. Biotech scale-up in India is constrained more by **regulatory clearance timelines (CDSCO, GEAC)** and manufacturing capacity than by investor awareness.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-III — Science & Technology (biotechnology, indigenisation); GS-III Economy (startup ecosystem, venture capital).

### 2. PM-AJAY — Pradhan Mantri Anusuchit Jaati Abhyuday Yojana

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | **Department of Social Justice & Empowerment**, Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment |
| **Launched** | **FY 2021–22** |
| **Type** | Centrally Sponsored umbrella scheme (Adarsh Gram and grants-in-aid components run on central assistance) |

**Objective:** Socio-economic upliftment of **Scheduled Castes** — poverty reduction, infrastructure and essential services in SC-dominated villages, livelihood creation, and educational access.

**Formation — a merger of three earlier schemes (high-yield Prelims point):**

1. **Pradhan Mantri Adarsh Gram Yojana (PMAGY)**
2. **Special Central Assistance to Scheduled Castes Sub Plan (SCA to SCSP)**
3. **Babu Jagjivan Ram Chhatrawas Yojana (BJRCY)**

**Three components:**

- **Adarsh Gram** — develop SC-majority villages into model villages via Village Development Plans.
- **Grants-in-aid** for district- and State-level socio-economic development projects.
- **Hostel construction** in educational institutions (successor to BJRCY).

**Coverage / progress (PM-AJAY portal, as of July 2026):** **47,324 villages** covered under Adarsh Gram; **47,59,399 beneficiaries**; **46,705 development works** completed; **24,133 Village Development Plans** generated.

**Significance:** The main non-scholarship vehicle for Article 46 (DPSP — promotion of educational and economic interests of SCs/STs and weaker sections). Digitalisation of the portal has made village-level fund flow publicly auditable.

**Criticism:** Persistent underutilisation of SCSP allocations by States; "Adarsh Gram" certification has been criticised as checklist-driven rather than outcome-driven; hostel construction lags demand.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-II — Welfare schemes for vulnerable sections, mechanisms and bodies for protection of SCs; Article 46, Article 338 (NCSC).

### 3. NAMASTE — National Action for Mechanised Sanitation Ecosystem *(new development)*

- **Nodal:** **Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment**, jointly with **MoHUA**; implementing agency **NSKFDC** (National Safai Karamcharis Finance and Development Corporation).
- **Replaced:** the earlier **SRMS** (Self Employment Scheme for Rehabilitation of Manual Scavengers).
- **This week's development:** NAMASTE's **Occupational Safety Training Programme** was inaugurated for sanitation workers of **Durgapur Municipal Corporation, West Bengal**.
- **Three pillars:** **Safety · Dignity · Mechanisation**.
- **Training design:** 5 days / 30 hours; certificate plus **₹1,000 stipend** on **70% attendance**. Registered workers are termed **"Safaimitras"**.
- **Convergence:** linked with **SVEP (Start-up Village Entrepreneurship Programme)** so that trained workers can become **sanitation entrepreneurs** owning mechanised equipment rather than remaining wage labour.
- **Legal backdrop:** *Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their Rehabilitation Act, **2013*** — bans manual scavenging and hazardous sewer entry.

**Criticism:** Deaths in sewer and septic-tank cleaning continue despite the 2013 Act; enumeration of hazardous-cleaning workers remains incomplete; mechanisation depends on ULB capital spending, which is weak in smaller municipalities.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-II — Welfare schemes, social justice, vulnerable sections; GS-I Indian Society (caste and occupation).

### 4. EPF Scheme, 2026 + EPFO Amnesty Scheme, 2026 *(labour & social security)*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | **Ministry of Labour & Employment**; administered by **EPFO** (CBT chaired by the Union Labour Minister) |
| **Notified** | **29 June 2026** |
| **Parent statute** | **Code on Social Security, 2020** |

**What was notified:** Three replacement schemes under the Social Security Code — the **Employees' Provident Funds Scheme, 2026**, **Employees' Pension Scheme, 2026** and **Employees' Deposit-Linked Insurance Scheme, 2026**.

**The Amnesty Scheme, 2026 (the operative news):**

- A **one-time, six-month window** — **29 June 2026 to 28 December 2026** — for establishments to **retrospectively regularise exempted PF trusts**.
- **Who it targets:** organisations running PF trusts **recognised under the Income-tax Act** but **without a formal exemption notification under Section 17 of the EPF & MP Act, 1952**.
- **Trigger:** the **Finance Act, 2026** now requires recognised PF trusts to hold formal exemption under EPF law to retain income-tax recognition.
- **Relief offered:** retrospective regularisation, relaxation of certain compliance requirements, and settlement of pending legal proceedings on trust status. Operational guidelines issued by **EPFO circular dated 11 July 2026**.

**Related, previously covered:** **VISHWAS, 2026** (Voluntary Initiative for Settlement of Historic Waivers and Amicable Solutions) — *covered in the 26 July tracker*. **New this week:** EPFO released the operational aspects; employers can settle long-pending EPF damages disputes at sharply reduced penalty rates, **window also closing 28 December 2026**.

**Significance:** Part of the operationalisation of the four Labour Codes; simultaneously widens formal social-security coverage and reduces legacy litigation.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-II — Government policies and interventions; GS-III — Indian Economy (employment, social security, labour reforms).

### 5. DAY-NRLM — Lakhpati Didi target doubled + SHG credit roadmap

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | **Ministry of Rural Development** (review chaired by Union Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan, early September 2026) |
| **Parent scheme** | **Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana – National Rural Livelihoods Mission (DAY-NRLM)** — launched as NRLM in **2011**, renamed DAY-NRLM in 2015 |
| **Type** | Centrally Sponsored Scheme |

**The decision:** The **Lakhpati Didi** target has been scaled from **3 crore to 6 crore** women. Roughly **3.5 crore** Lakhpati Didis have been created so far (as stated by the Minister, September 2026).

**Credit roadmap for the next 5 years:**

- **₹10 lakh crore** in bank credit to **SHGs** (collective lending).
- **₹1 lakh crore** in targeted loans to **individual women entrepreneurs**.

**Governance features (4):**

- **Mandatory quarterly virtual and half-yearly physical reviews** with SRLMs, **RBI, NABARD** and commercial banks.
- **Private commercial banks directed to participate** in **District Level Consultative Committee (DLCC)** meetings and take on rural credit targets.
- A dedicated **SHG loan facilitation helpline**.
- **Regional equity mandate** — banks to prioritise underserved, high-poverty districts to remove credit-access imbalances.

**Scale of DAY-NRLM:** over **10 crore rural women** mobilised into roughly **92–93 lakh SHGs**; cumulative bank credit disbursed to women SHGs has crossed **₹12 lakh crore** (Ministry of Rural Development statement, January 2026).

> ⚠️ **Fact-check note:** some secondary current-affairs sites this week reported cumulative SHG disbursals as "₹167 lakh crore." That figure is not credible (India's nominal GDP is roughly ₹330 lakh crore) and appears to be a transcription error. Use **₹12 lakh crore+** as the defensible number, and **3.5 crore** (not 5 crore) for Lakhpati Didis achieved.

**"Lakhpati Didi" definition:** an SHG woman member whose household earns **₹1 lakh or more annually** (sustained over at least four months in a year).

**Criticism:** Income measurement is self-reported and methodology is not published; credit targets set top-down risk pushing loan volume without matching enterprise viability; SHG NPAs and multiple borrowing are under-monitored.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-II — Welfare schemes, SHGs; GS-III — Inclusive growth, rural credit architecture, women's economic empowerment.

### 6. Decentralised Grain Storage Plan in the Cooperative Sector

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | **Ministry of Cooperation** (created **2021**) |
| **Anchor institution** | **PACS** (Primary Agricultural Credit Societies) |
| **Implementing agency** | **NCDC** (National Cooperative Development Corporation) |
| **Launched** | Pilot on **31 May 2023** — 11 PACS across 11 states, 9,750 MT capacity |
| **Type** | **Convergence** of existing central schemes — *not* a new fund |

**Objective:** Build godowns and allied infrastructure **at the PACS level** so that grain is stored close to the farm gate, cutting transport cost and post-harvest wastage. Billed as the **world's largest grain storage plan in the cooperative sector**.

**Progress (as of July 2026):** **313 PACS** have completed godowns, creating over **1.80 lakh MT** of storage capacity.

**Financial architecture (high-yield):**

- **AMI** (Agricultural Marketing Infrastructure) subsidy raised from **25% → 33.33%**.
- **Margin money** requirement cut from **20% → 10%**.
- With the **3% AIF (Agriculture Infrastructure Fund) interest subvention**, the effective interest rate for a PACS falls to about **1%**.
- **FCI provides assured hiring** for eligible godowns — guaranteeing utilisation and revenue.

**Structure to memorise:** PACS is the **lowest tier of the three-tier short-term cooperative credit structure** — **PACS → DCCBs (District Central Cooperative Banks) → StCBs (State Cooperative Banks)**.

**Criticism:** 313 completed godowns against a target framed in "lakhs of PACS" is slow; many PACS are financially weak or dormant; convergence across four schemes means no single accountable budget line.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-III — Food security, buffer stocks, storage; agricultural marketing. GS-II — Cooperatives (97th Constitutional Amendment, Part IXB).

### 7. Goa Ujjwala Scheme, 2026 *(State: Goa)*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Nodal** | Government of Goa; launched by **CM Pramod Sawant** |
| **Launched** | **5 September 2026** |
| **Type** | State scheme, DBT-based cooking-fuel assistance |
| **Outlay** | **₹100 crore** provision for 2026–27 |

**Objective:** Offset household cooking-fuel cost by transferring the cash equivalent of **three domestic LPG cylinders** per year.

**Key features:**

- **₹3,000 per year** per eligible household, paid in a **single instalment** via **DBT** to the **Aadhaar-linked** bank account. (Three cylinders at ~₹956 each ≈ ₹2,868; the State rounded up to ₹3,000.)
- Covers **both LPG and PNG** consumers — notable, since piped gas users are excluded from most cylinder-subsidy designs.
- **No income ceiling** — universal among registered domestic consumers.
- **Phase 1:** approximately **2.5 lakh beneficiaries**; first DBT round expected around 9–10 September 2026.

**Distinguish from the central scheme:** **PM Ujjwala Yojana (PMUY)**, launched **1 May 2016** by the **Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas**, gives *deposit-free LPG connections* to adult women of poor households, plus a per-cylinder subsidy. Goa's scheme is a **State cash transfer to existing consumers**, not a connection scheme — do not conflate the two.

**Criticism:** Universal, income-blind cash transfers to LPG/PNG consumers are regressive at the margin; a fiscal commitment that recurs annually; no linkage to consumption or to clean-fuel switching behaviour.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-II — Government policies and interventions, DBT; GS-III — Energy access, subsidy reform, fiscal federalism.

### 8. Jal Sanchay Jan Bhagidari (JSJB) — 1.58 crore recharge structures *(new development)*

- **Ministry:** **Jal Shakti** (Department of Water Resources, RD & GR). **Launched 6 September 2024.**
- **Milestone announced this week:** over **1.58 crore artificial groundwater recharge structures** built as of September 2026 — flagged at the Ministry's **first Departmental Summit**.
- **Guiding principle — the 3 Cs:** **Community, CSR, Cost.** Approach: *Whole-of-Government and Whole-of-Society*.
- **Trajectory:** JSJB 1.0 → 27 lakh+ structures (May 2025); JSJB 2.0 → 1.5 crore+ (May 2026); **June 2026 — merged with *Jal Shakti Abhiyan: Catch the Rain* to form "JSJB: Catch the Rain."**
- **State models worth quoting in Mains:**
  - **Chhattisgarh — *Aawa Paani Jhoki*:** farmers voluntarily gave **5% of their land** for recharge ponds and terraced pits.
  - **Andhra Pradesh:** *Kalajathas* (folk theatre) for awareness; water budgeting; ridge-to-valley approach.
  - **Odisha — CHHATA:** rooftop rainwater capture. **Odisha — ARUA:** recharging water bodies into underground aquifers.
- **Linked to:** **Jal Jeevan Mission 2.0** (Har Ghar Jal, functional household tap connections).

**Syllabus tag:** GS-III — Water resources, conservation, watershed management; GS-II — Centre-State-community implementation models.

### 9. PM Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana (PMDDKY) — first progress review *(new development)*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare |
| **Announced** | Union Budget **2025–26**; approved by Cabinet in July 2025 |
| **Type** | Convergence scheme — merges **36 existing schemes across 11 departments** |
| **Outlay** | **₹24,000 crore per year for 6 years** from 2025–26 (≈ **₹1.44 lakh crore**) |
| **Coverage** | **100 districts** — selected on low agricultural productivity, low cropping intensity and low access to institutional credit |

**Review outcome (announced ~2 September 2026):**

- Average composite score across the **six objectives** in the 100 districts rose from **22.54 (April 2026) to 38.85 (July 2026)**.
- Objective-wise: agricultural productivity **2.53 → 6.70**; crop diversification **5.60 → 8.65**; post-harvest storage **2.61 → 3.82**; irrigation infrastructure **1.65 → 4.20**; agricultural credit access **2.81 → 5.59**; governance **7.94 → 10.33**.
- **Capacity building (Jan–July 2026):** **6,90,530 farmers** and **88,961 extension workers** trained, including **2,20,683 in natural farming**.

**Note the design parallel:** PMDDKY is explicitly modelled on the **Aspirational Districts Programme** — indicator-based ranking, competitive federalism, convergence rather than fresh money.

**Criticism:** Because it is a convergence of existing schemes, "new" outlay is largely repackaged; the score improvements are on a self-designed index with no external validation; district selection criteria have been contested by some States.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-III — Agriculture (productivity, credit, irrigation, diversification); GS-II — Government interventions, cooperative federalism.

## POLICY / REGULATORY CHANGE OF THE WEEK

### 10. Legal Metrology (Indian Standard Time) Rules, 2026

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Notified by** | **Department of Consumer Affairs**, Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution |
| **Parent Act** | **Legal Metrology Act, 2009** |
| **Scientific custodian of IST** | **CSIR–National Physical Laboratory (CSIR-NPL)**, New Delhi |
| **Comes into force** | **180 days** after publication in the Official Gazette |
| **Vision** | **"One Nation, One Time"** |

**What it does:** Makes **Indian Standard Time the single legally recognised and traceable time reference** for all administrative, commercial and technical systems in India, and creates an enforceable framework for the **generation, maintenance, dissemination and use** of IST.

**Key features (4):**

- **Cybersecurity and financial integrity** — accurate, traceable timestamps underpin digital payments, power-grid coordination, telecom logs and **CERT-In** incident forensics.
- **Distributed regional network** — CSIR-NPL, **ISRO** and Legal Metrology have set up secondary time scales at **5 Regional Reference Standard Laboratories (RRSLs)** — Ahmedabad, Bengaluru, Bhubaneswar, Faridabad, Guwahati — eliminating a single point of failure.
- **Indigenous dissemination stack** — **NavIC** for satellite-based time transfer; **NTP/PTP** protocols; and **"White Rabbit"** sub-nanosecond optical-fibre timing for stock exchanges and critical infrastructure.
- **180-day transition window** for banks, exchanges, utilities and government platforms to align internal clocks.

**Technical anchor:** CSIR-NPL maintains India's primary atomic time scale with **nanosecond-level traceability to UTC** maintained by the **BIPM** (International Bureau of Weights and Measures).

**Significance:** Time is now treated as **critical national infrastructure** and an element of digital sovereignty — India stops depending on foreign GNSS (GPS) time servers for legally significant timestamps.

**Criticism / debate:** Revives the perennial **"single time zone vs. two time zones"** question — the North-East loses over an hour of usable daylight; CSIR-NPL itself has previously studied an **IST-I / IST-II** two-zone proposal, which these Rules do not adopt.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-III — Science & Technology, cybersecurity, digital infrastructure; GS-II — Government policies and regulatory frameworks. *(Also links to GS-I Geography: longitude, 82°30′E standard meridian at Mirzapur.)*

## REPORTS & INDICES

### A. All India Survey on Higher Education (AISHE) — **2022–23 and 2023–24**

- **Publishing body:** **Ministry of Education** (Department of Higher Education). Both reports released together; widely reported in the news in early September 2026.
- **What it measures:** Annual census of higher education — enrolment, Gross Enrolment Ratio (GER), Gender Parity Index, teachers, institutions, infrastructure, by State/discipline/social group.

**Key findings (3):**

1. **Total enrolment: 4.50 crore in 2023–24**, up from 4.46 crore in 2022–23 — an increase of about **31.5%** over 2014–15 (**3.42 crore**).
2. **GER: 30.0% in 2023–24** — against the **NEP 2020 target of 50% by 2035**.
3. **Female GER 31.2% (up from 30.2%) vs male 28.9%** — women's participation in higher education now clearly exceeds men's in ratio terms, though not in absolute STEM or employment outcomes.

**Mains angle:** GER growth is decelerating; the gap between enrolment and *employability* (see the NITI skilling report below) is the sharper policy problem. Note the divergence between rising female GER and India's low female labour force participation.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-II — Education (issues relating to development and management of social sector/services).

### B. NITI Aayog — *Reimagining Skilling for Viksit Bharat@2047*

- **Publishing body:** **NITI Aayog** (released late August 2026).
- **What it measures/does:** Diagnoses India's skilling ecosystem and proposes an overhaul — **five core reforms and four system enablers** — to make skilling flexible, industry-linked and outcome-driven.

**Key findings (3):**

1. **8.7 crore Indians aged 15–29 are NEET** — **N**ot in **E**ducation, **E**mployment or **T**raining — roughly **11%** of that age cohort. Data source: **NSS 78th Round (2020–21)**.
2. The report places the **learner at the centre of a lifelong pathway** spanning education → skilling → employment, and identifies **five segments**: school students from Grade 6 onwards, tertiary learners, formal workers, informal workers, and **NEET youth**, with **women as a cross-cutting segment**.
3. Recommends that skilling be **linked to local demand, self-employment and emerging sectors**, with **affordable/subsidised training and financial support** targeted at NEET youth and women.

**Important caveat (likely to appear as a statement-based Prelims trap):** The government has clarified that the **8.7 crore NEET figure is not an unemployment figure** — the NSS 78th Round was collected in **FY 2020–21**, when education and employment were disrupted by the COVID-19 pandemic.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-II — Human resource development, education; GS-III — Employment, inclusive growth, demographic dividend.

### C. 'RAKSHA' — India's Defence Diplomacy Document

- **Publishing body:** **Ministry of Defence**; released by Defence Minister **Rajnath Singh** (early September 2026).
- **What it is:** India's first consolidated **10-year strategic roadmap for defence diplomacy** and global defence partnerships.

**Four pillars:**

1. **Strategic Partnerships** — deepening bilateral and multilateral security cooperation.
2. **Capacity Building** — more joint exercises, training and best-practice sharing with partner countries.
3. **Indigenous Exports** — promoting Make-in-India defence platforms abroad.
4. **Maritime Security** — India as **Net Security Provider** and **First Responder** in the Indian Ocean Region.

**Linked concepts for Mains:** Joint exercises — **Malabar** (with US, Japan, Australia), **Yudh Abhyas** (US), **Varuna** (France), **Garuda** (France, air). Groupings — **QUAD, BIMSTEC, IONS**. Regional doctrine — **SAGAR** (Security and Growth for All in the Region), now expanded to **MAHASAGAR**.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-II — India and its neighbourhood, bilateral/regional groupings; GS-III — Defence, internal and external security.

### D. e-Shram at Five Years — Ministry of Labour & Employment

- **Milestone:** the **e-Shram portal completed 5 years on 26 August 2026** (launched 2021).
- **What it is:** India's first **Aadhaar-authenticated National Database of Unorganised Workers (NDUW)**; every registrant gets a unique **12-digit Universal Account Number (UAN)**.

**Key data (3):**

1. **31.89 crore registrations** as of **18 August 2026** — among the largest worker databases in the world.
2. **Women are 54.28%** of registered workers (men 45.72%); the **18–40 age group is 55.21%**.
3. The portal has evolved from a registry into a **one-stop platform** integrating welfare schemes, jobs, skilling, apprenticeship and pension services.

**Companion data point — the gig-work coverage gap (GS-III, very examinable):**

- Only about **8.58 lakh gig and platform workers** were registered on e-Shram as of **January 2026** — against **NITI Aayog's estimate of ~1.27 crore gig workers in 2024–25**.
- NITI Aayog estimated **77 lakh gig workers in 2020–21** and projects **2.35 crore by 2029–30**.
- **12 major aggregators** have been onboarded (including Zomato, Swiggy, Uber, Ola, Amazon, Blinkit, Rapido, Urban Company).
- Under the **Code on Social Security, 2020**, aggregators are to contribute **1–2% of turnover** (capped at 5% of payments to workers) to a **Gig Workers' Welfare Fund**; registered gig workers are to get **PM-JAY cover of ₹5 lakh/year** and e-Shram accident cover.

**The exam-ready argument:** this is a **coverage-versus-welfare** problem — the entitlements exist on paper, but the State cannot deliver them without first identifying the beneficiaries. Registration, not legislation, is the binding constraint.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-II — Welfare schemes, mechanisms for vulnerable sections; GS-III — Employment, informalisation of the workforce, social security.

## PRELIMS POINTERS

- **BIRAC** is a **Section 8 company** under **DBT**, established **2012**; it is a **Second-Level Fund Manager** under the **RDI Fund**.
- **PM-AJAY = PMAGY + SCA to SCSP + BJRCY** (three merged schemes). Remember all three expansions.
- **NAMASTE replaced SRMS**; nodal is **MoSJE**, *jointly with MoHUA*; implementing agency **NSKFDC**. Manual scavenging banned by the **2013 Act**.
- **PACS → DCCB → StCB** is the three-tier **short-term** cooperative credit structure. Ministry of Cooperation created in **2021**.
- **AMI subsidy 25% → 33.33%**; margin money **20% → 10%**; with **3% AIF subvention**, effective PACS rate ≈ **1%**.
- **JSJB launched 6 September 2024**; **3 Cs = Community, CSR, Cost**; merged with *Jal Shakti Abhiyan: Catch the Rain* in **June 2026**.
- **PMDDKY:** ₹24,000 crore/year, 6 years, **100 districts**, merges **36 schemes across 11 departments**.
- **IST custodian = CSIR-NPL**; **5 RRSLs** = Ahmedabad, Bengaluru, Bhubaneswar, Faridabad, Guwahati; dissemination via **NavIC**, **NTP/PTP**, **White Rabbit**; **180-day** transition; UTC maintained by **BIPM**.
- **AISHE 2023–24:** enrolment **4.50 crore**, **GER 30.0%**, female GER **31.2%** > male **28.9%**. NEP target **50% by 2035**.
- **NEET youth = 8.7 crore**, aged **15–29**, ~**11%** of the cohort, from **NSS 78th Round (2020–21)** — *not* an unemployment number.
- **e-Shram:** launched **2021**, **NDUW**, **12-digit UAN**, **31.89 crore** registrations, **women 54.28%**.
- **SAGAR → MAHASAGAR** is the expanded regional maritime vision referenced in **RAKSHA**.
- **EOS-05** is India's **first imaging satellite in Geosynchronous Orbit**, launched by **GSLV-F17**.
