## SCHEMES & PROGRAMMES

### 1. 'Samudra Manthan' — National Offshore Exploration Scheme

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas (MoPNG); Directorate General of Hydrocarbons for technical oversight |
| **Launched** | Union Cabinet approval **31 July 2026** |
| **Type** | **Central Sector Scheme** |
| **Outlay / period** | **₹84,084 crore**, implementation up to **FY 2030–31** |
| **GS tag** | **GS3** — Infrastructure (Energy); Resource mobilisation; Growth & development |

**Objective:** Unlock India's under-explored offshore sedimentary basins to raise domestic oil and gas output, cut import dependence, and build a globally competitive offshore energy ecosystem under *Viksit Bharat*.

**Key features**

- Covers the **entire offshore exploration value chain** — seismic surveys, deepwater and ultra-deepwater drilling, frontier-basin exploration, production infrastructure, and large-scale acquisition/processing/interpretation of seismic data.
- **Government bears up to 50% of the cost of drilling deepwater exploration wells** — a risk-sharing instrument to de-risk private and foreign investment in high-cost frontier acreage.
- Provides for an **integrated Oil & Gas Manufacturing and Services Zone** (indigenous equipment and services ecosystem).
- Support for digital programme management, capacity building, technology adoption and international outreach/roadshows.

**Targets / coverage**

- Add **600+ MMTOE** (million metric tonnes of oil equivalent) to hydrocarbon reserves.
- Raise domestic production from about **62 MMTOE to 80 MMTOE** a year.
- Expand the hydrocarbon resource base from **1.6 billion TOE to 2.2 billion TOE**.

**Significance:** India imports over 85% of its crude; offshore basins (KG, Mahanadi, Andaman, Kerala–Konkan) remain largely under-explored relative to their assessed potential. The 50% cost-share directly addresses the exploration-risk problem that OALP/HELP rounds could not fully solve.

**Criticism / issues to note:** Large public subsidy for **fossil-fuel expansion** sits uneasily with India's enhanced 2035 NDC targets and net-zero-by-2070 pledge; risk of stranded assets if global demand peaks earlier; environmental clearance and marine-ecology concerns in ecologically sensitive offshore zones (Andaman, coastal CRZ areas); past deepwater blocks have had poor commercial discovery rates.

### 2. Pradhan Mantri Surya Sarovar Yojana (PM-SSY)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | Ministry of New and Renewable Energy (MNRE) |
| **Launched** | Union Cabinet approval **31 July 2026** |
| **Type** | **Central Sector Scheme** |
| **Outlay / period** | **₹5,070 crore**; projects sanctioned FY 2026–27 to FY 2030–31, disbursement up to FY 2032–33 |
| **GS tag** | **GS3** — Energy; Environment & climate change; Infrastructure |

**Objective:** Develop **Floating Solar Photovoltaic (FSPV)** projects integrated with **Energy Storage Systems (ESS)** on inland water bodies, so that solar capacity addition does not compete for scarce land.

**Key features**

- Target: **5,000 MW (5 GW) of floating solar capacity** with **10,000 MWh of co-located battery energy storage**.
- **Central Financial Assistance of ₹1 crore per MW**, released **after commissioning** (outcome-linked, not upfront).
- Sites: reservoirs, dams, lakes, industrial ponds and other suitable inland water bodies.
- Storage integration addresses solar's evening-peak mismatch — a shift from earlier land-based solar schemes that ignored firm-power delivery.

**Beneficiaries / coverage:** Developers, DISCOMs, state hydro/irrigation corporations owning water bodies; projected **16,000–17,000 jobs** and **~10 million tonnes of CO₂ avoided annually**.

**Significance:** India's non-fossil share of installed electric capacity reached **54.18% (as of 30 June 2026)**; floating solar has lower evaporation loss and higher panel efficiency due to water cooling. Complements PM Surya Ghar and PM-KUSUM in the solar family.

**Criticism / issues:** FSPV capital cost per MW is 15–25% higher than ground-mounted; ecological effects on reservoir aquatic life, dissolved oxygen and fisheries livelihoods are not well studied in Indian conditions; anchoring and mooring in silt-heavy reservoirs is technically hard; ₹5,070 crore for 5 GW is thin support relative to the cost gap.

### 3. Revamped Khelo India Scheme + Enhanced Assistance to National Sports Federations (ANSF)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports (MYAS); Sports Authority of India (SAI) as implementing arm |
| **Launched** | Original Khelo India **2018**; **revamped version approved by Cabinet, July 2026** |
| **Type** | **Central Sector Scheme** |
| **Outlay / period** | **₹36,441 crore** for **2026–27 to 2030–31** — ₹29,054 cr (Khelo India) + ₹7,387 cr (ANSF); nearly **8× the previous outlay** |
| **GS tag** | **GS2** — Government policies & interventions; Social sector/Human resources |

**Objective:** Build a seamless school-to-podium pathway by integrating sport with education, mass participation and high-performance training.

**Key features**

- **Khelo India Feeder Schools** and **Utkrishta Vidyalayas** — sport embedded in school education, addressing the drop-off between school-level talent and elite training.
- **"Emerging Khelo India Athletes"** category — expands the supported athlete pool nearly **ten-fold**.
- Integrated nationwide ecosystem: National Centres of Excellence, Khelo India Centres of Excellence, SAI Training Centres, Khelo India Accredited Academies, Khelo India Centres, and Youth Sports Companies of the Armed Forces.
- **National Coach Accreditation Board** created — first standardised national coaching credential.
- ANSF funds coaching, sports science, equipment, foreign experts, training camps and international exposure.

**Significance:** Largest sports investment since Independence; timed against India's bid ambitions for the **2036 Olympics**. Shifts policy from event-based funding to a standing institutional pipeline.

**Criticism / issues:** Historic problems of **NSF governance** (litigation, ad-hoc committees, non-compliance with the National Sports Development Code) are not solved by more money alone; utilisation rates under earlier Khelo India tranches were uneven; risk of infrastructure being built without maintenance budgets; concentration of Centres of Excellence in a few states.

### 4. PM-KISAN — continuation approved for 2026–27 to 2030–31 *(new development on a previously covered scheme)*

> Covered earlier in the 19 June 2026 tracker (23rd instalment). **What is new:** the Cabinet has approved **continuation of the scheme for a further five years with a ₹3.15 lakh crore outlay** — it was previously an open-ended scheme without a defined five-year financial envelope.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare |
| **Type** | **Central Sector Scheme** (100% Union funding) |
| **New outlay** | **₹3.15 lakh crore**, 2026–27 to 2030–31 |
| **GS tag** | **GS3** — Agriculture, farm subsidies, MSP & DBT; **GS2** — Welfare schemes |

**What's new / key numbers**

- Benefit unchanged: **₹6,000 per year** to eligible landholding farmer families, in **three equal instalments** via **DBT**.
- Coverage: over **9.49 crore farmers**.
- Cumulative transfer so far: **over ₹4.47 lakh crore across 23 instalments**.
- **Women farmers have received over ₹1.06 lakh crore**; roughly **one in four beneficiaries is a woman**.

**Significance:** Locks in India's largest DBT income-support programme through the 16th Finance Commission cycle; Aadhaar-seeded land-record linkage has been the main tool for weeding out ineligible beneficiaries.

**Criticism to remember:** Amount not indexed to inflation since 2019 (real value has eroded materially); **excludes tenant farmers, sharecroppers and landless agricultural labourers** — who are the most vulnerable; exclusion errors from land-record digitisation gaps; recovery drives against ineligible beneficiaries have caused hardship.

### 5. Gyan Bharatam Mission (GBM) — progress review

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | **Ministry of Culture** (National Mission for Manuscripts lineage) |
| **Launched** | Mission announced 2025; **Gyan Bharatam National Manuscript Survey launched 16 March 2026** |
| **Type** | **Central Sector Scheme** (grants-in-aid model) |
| **GS tag** | **GS1** — Indian culture, literature, heritage preservation; **GS2** — Government interventions |

**Objective:** Systematic identification, cataloguing, physical conservation, digitisation, research, publication and dissemination of India's manuscript heritage across all scripts, languages, dialects, traditions and regions.

**Key features**

- **National Digital Repository (NDR)** — centralised platform for global scholarly and public access.
- Technology stack integrates **AI, OCR and blockchain** for smart search, transcription and provenance tracking.
- **Execution model:** grants-in-aid to **Cluster Centres (CCs)** and **Independent Centres (ICs)** — e.g. The Asiatic Society (Kolkata), Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute (Pune), Oriental Research Institute (Mysuru), Central Institute of Buddhist Studies (Leh), Khuda Bakhsh Oriental Public Library (Patna), Nagari Pracharini Sabha (Varanasi).
- Crowdsourced survey via the **Gyan Bharatam mobile app** — citizens, scholars and communities report manuscripts.

**Progress figures (as reported July 2026)**

- **1.19 crore+ manuscripts** identified and reported across all States/UTs.
- **8 lakh+ manuscripts** digitised nationwide.
- **3.90 lakh+ digitised manuscripts** hosted on the Gyan Bharatam portal in view mode.
- Budget: ₹3.50 cr (2024–25) → ₹15 cr (2025–26) → **₹60 cr (2026–27)**, of which only ₹4.48 cr spent till July 2026.

**Criticism / issues:** Sharp under-utilisation in the current year against a 4× budget jump; conservation capacity (trained palaeographers, conservators) is the binding constraint, not funds; questions over **intellectual property and access rights** where manuscripts are held by private/religious institutions; digitisation without transcription limits research value.

### 6. National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme-2 (NAPS-2)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | **Ministry of Skill Development and Entrepreneurship (MSDE)** |
| **Launched** | Original NAPS **August 2016**; **NAPS-2** is the continuing successor scheme |
| **Type** | **Central Sector Scheme**; statutory basis in the **Apprentices Act, 1961** |
| **GS tag** | **GS2** — Human resources & skill development; **GS3** — Employment and inclusive growth |

**Objective:** Expand formal apprenticeship training by part-funding apprentice stipends and lowering the compliance burden on establishments, especially MSMEs.

**Key features**

- **DBT stipend:** the Centre's share of the minimum stipend goes **directly into apprentices' bank accounts** (operational since July 2023), removing employer-intermediary delays.
- **CPI indexation:** minimum stipend rates revised by the **Central Apprenticeship Council (CAC)** and linked to the Consumer Price Index; notified September 2025.
- **Apprenticeship Embedded Degree Programme (AEDP)** — jointly by MSDE, UGC and AICTE under **NEP 2020**; on-the-job apprenticeship counts within a degree/diploma curriculum.
- Emphasis on **sunrise sectors**: IT, semiconductors, drones, renewable energy, logistics, electronics, biotechnology — via Designated and Optional Trades.

**Coverage / performance**

- **52.98 lakh+ apprentices** engaged, FY 2018–19 to June 2026.
- **38,936 of 57,945** registered establishments are **MSMEs**.
- **₹1,573.46 crore** disbursed as government stipend share via DBT (2023–24 to June 2026).
- **7,370 Pradhan Mantri National Apprenticeship Melas (PMNAM)** held, engaging 40.24 lakh+ candidates.
- Third-party study (**AJNIFM**): ~**72% of trained apprentices** secured full-time employment post-completion.

**Criticism / issues:** Apprentice engagement remains a small fraction of India's ~50 crore workforce and far below the statutory potential; low uptake among small firms outside metro clusters; concerns that apprenticeships substitute for regular employment at sub-minimum wages; trade curricula lag industry practice in fast-moving sectors.

### 7. DISHA 2.0 — Designing Innovative Solutions for Holistic Access to Justice

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | **Department of Justice, Ministry of Law and Justice** |
| **Launched** | Original DISHA 2021; **DISHA 2.0 approved/launched July 2026** for 2026–31 |
| **Type** | **Central Sector Scheme** |
| **Outlay** | **₹255 crore** (2026–31) |
| **GS tag** | **GS2** — Judiciary; Governance & transparency; Welfare of vulnerable sections; Access to justice |

**Objective:** Merge India's scattered legal-aid and legal-literacy programmes into a single technology-driven umbrella for pre-litigation advice and awareness.

**Four components**

1. **Tele-Law: Reaching the Unreached** — free **pre-litigation** legal advice through a mobile app, **toll-free 14454**, and video/tele-conferencing at **Common Service Centres (CSCs)** via Village Level Entrepreneurs at the panchayat level.
2. **Nyaya Bandhu (Pro Bono Legal Services)** — advocates volunteer assistance to persons covered under **Section 12 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987**.
3. **Legal Literacy and Legal Awareness Programme**.
4. **VIDHI-Sanjeevani** (Vision for Integrated Delivery of Harmonized Legal Initiatives).

**Beneficiaries / coverage:** Targets **over 3 crore beneficiaries** — women, SC/ST communities, rural populations and others facing economic, social or geographic barriers; explicit accessibility design for **Persons with Disabilities (PwDs)**.

**Related justice-sector numbers this week:** ₹2,010 crore allocated for judicial infrastructure and **eCourts Phase III**; **e-Filing 3.0** fully operational across all district courts in Punjab; **Fast Track Special Courts (FTSC)** scheme extended to **September 2026** with **775 FTSCs** functional across 29 States/UTs.

**Criticism / issues:** Tele-Law is advisory and pre-litigation only — it does not address the ~5 crore case pendency or the shortage of judges; CSC-dependent delivery reproduces the digital divide it aims to bridge; ₹255 crore over five years is modest for a 3-crore beneficiary target; pro-bono participation by advocates has historically been thin.

## POLICY REFORM OF THE WEEK

### 9. Indian Coast Guard — Gender-Neutral Induction & Ex-Gratia Reforms

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Nodal body** | Indian Coast Guard (ICG), **Ministry of Defence** |
| **Announced** | By Raksha Mantri **Rajnath Singh** at Kartavya Bhavan-2, New Delhi (late July 2026) |
| **GS tag** | **GS2** — Government policies; Gender parity & social empowerment; **GS3** — Maritime security |

**Two reforms**

1. **Gender-neutral induction and career progression**
   - Women can now be inducted into **both Short Service Appointments (SSA) and Permanent Appointments (PA)** at par with men; SSA entry simultaneously opened to male candidates, making the stream fully gender-neutral.
   - Equal opportunity from *ab-initio* training through to higher promotions.
   - **Serving women SSA officers get the option to convert to Permanent Appointments.**
2. **Streamlined ex-gratia for personnel missing at sea**
   - Competent authorities may declare personnel missing at sea on duty as **'presumed dead'** for the limited administrative purpose of granting ex-gratia.
   - Removes the prolonged legal wait (ordinarily seven years under the presumption-of-death rule) before next of kin can receive terminal benefits.

**Context:** ICG approaches its **Swarnim Jayanti (Golden Jubilee) in 2027**. Links well to the Supreme Court's permanent-commission jurisprudence for women officers in the armed forces.

### A. Market Distortions Performance Index — *"India's Next Growth Frontier"*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Publishing body** | **Competere Foundation for Trade and Competition Policy**, with the **Centre for Trade and Investment Law (CTIL), IIFT** |
| **Released** | **30 July 2026**, India Habitat Centre, New Delhi |
| **Full title** | *India's Next Growth Frontier: Reducing Anti-Competitive Market Distortions to Build on India's 2010–2023 Reform Progress* |
| **GS tag** | **GS3** — Indian economy, liberalisation, competition policy; **GS2** — Regulatory bodies |

**What it measures:** Anti-competitive market distortions across three pillars — **protection of property rights**, **domestic competition**, and **international competition**.

**India's standing**

- **India rose 25 places, from 82nd (2010) to 57th (2023).**

**Key findings**

- Improvement driven mainly by **GST**, the **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)**, and broader regulatory modernisation and digital-market reforms.
- The report argues India's remaining distortions are concentrated in **state-level regulation, land and factor markets, and selective sectoral protection** rather than in headline trade policy.
- Recommends **evidence-based competition policy** and impact assessment of regulation as the next reform frontier if the reform dividend is to be sustained.

**Caution for answers:** This is a **think-tank index, not an official/multilateral one** — cite it as such, and note the reference year is **2023**, not 2026.

### B. World Investment Report 2026 — UNCTAD

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Publishing body** | **UNCTAD (UN Trade and Development)** |
| **Released** | July 2026 |
| **Theme** | *International investment in a turbulent era* |
| **GS tag** | **GS3** — Indian economy, investment models, FDI; **GS2** — International institutions |

**What it measures:** Global and regional FDI flows, greenfield project announcements, cross-border M&A, and investment policy trends.

**India's standing**

- **India rose from 13th (2024) to 11th (2025)** among the world's largest **inward FDI** recipients.
- **Inward FDI up ~44% to US$38.89 billion** in 2025.
- **Outward FDI up 47% to US$35.66 billion**, taking India up two places to **18th among top home economies**.

**Key findings**

- Global investment remains **highly concentrated** in a small set of economies; developing-country flows are uneven.
- UNCTAD credits India's gain to an "active policy agenda aimed at broadening its investment base beyond services and accelerating advanced manufacturing."
- The narrowing gap between India's inward and outward FDI is itself notable — Indian firms are internationalising at close to the rate foreign capital enters.

## Prelims pointers

- **Central Sector vs Centrally Sponsored:** Samudra Manthan, PM-SSY, Khelo India, PM-KISAN, DISHA 2.0 and NAPS-2 are all **Central Sector** (100% Union funded). Judicial Infrastructure Development is **Centrally Sponsored**.
- **Toll-free 14454** → Tele-Law.
- **AIPA** → Apex Committee for Implementation of the Paris Agreement (MoEFCC).
- **AEDP** → Apprenticeship Embedded Degree Programme (MSDE + UGC + AICTE, under NEP 2020).
- **CAC** → Central Apprenticeship Council, sets minimum apprentice stipend (CPI-indexed).
- **Sarnath UNESCO criteria** → (iii) and (vi). India's **45th** site.
- **FSPV CFA** → ₹1 crore per MW, paid **post-commissioning**.
