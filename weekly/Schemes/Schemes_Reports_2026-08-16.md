## ⭐ HEADLINE OF THE WEEK: 80th Independence Day Announcements (15 August 2026)

**Occasion:** PM's 13th consecutive Red Fort address; 78 minutes. First-ever full rendition of *Vande Mataram* at an Independence Day ceremony.

### The 'Sapta Dhara' framework

A **seven-stream roadmap for Viksit Bharat @2047**, framed by analogy to the Vedic **Sapta Sindhu** (seven rivers). The seven "streams of strength":

| # | Stream | Thrust |
|---|--------|--------|
| 1 | Manufacturing & quality | Global manufacturing hub; quality standards |
| 2 | Agriculture & food processing | Value addition, farm-to-factory linkage |
| 3 | Technology & innovation | AI, semiconductors, deep tech |
| 4 | Connectivity & infrastructure | PM Gati Shakti-led logistics |
| 5 | Defence | Atmanirbharta in arms manufacturing |
| 6 | Green & blue economy | Clean energy + marine/ocean resources |
| 7 | Soft power | Culture, diaspora, diplomacy |

**Framing line to remember:** India must accomplish in the next **5–7 years** what could not be achieved in the previous **5–7 decades**.

## SCHEMES, PROGRAMMES & INITIATIVES

### 1. CBDC-based Direct Benefit Transfer under PMGKAY

*Sector: Digital public infrastructure / Food security*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Nodal ministries** | Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution + Ministry of Finance (with RBI) |
| **Rolled out** | August 2026 (UT saturation phase) |
| **Type** | Central Sector delivery reform layered on PMGKAY (a Central Sector food subsidy scheme) |
| **Pilot phase** | Puducherry and Gujarat |
| **First full-saturation UTs** | **Chandigarh** and **Dadra & Nagar Haveli** |

**Objective:** Deliver the food subsidy as **programmable Digital Rupee (e₹) tokens** credited to beneficiaries' CBDC wallets instead of cash/bank DBT.

**Key features**

- **Programmability** — tokens are coded so the subsidy can only be spent on foodgrains at empanelled merchants, blocking diversion to non-food use.
- **End-to-end traceability** of subsidy utilisation across the supply chain in real time.
- Reduces cash-handling and reconciliation costs versus conventional DBT.
- Designed as a **scalable template** for extending CBDC to other central welfare schemes.

**Significance:** First operational, saturation-level use of a central bank digital currency for welfare delivery in India — moves CBDC from a payments pilot to a governance instrument.
**Criticism/watch:** Raises questions on beneficiary autonomy (earmarked money is paternalistic), digital literacy and smartphone dependence among the poorest, merchant empanelment monopolies, and transaction-level surveillance of consumption.

**Tag:** GS3 (Indian Economy — money & banking, DPI); GS2 (Welfare schemes, PDS).

### 2. E-Samudra — unified digital maritime governance platform

*Sector: Infrastructure / Shipping / e-Governance*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Nodal ministry** | Ministry of Ports, Shipping & Waterways (launched by Sh. Sarbananda Sonowal, Mumbai) |
| **Launched** | **8 August 2026** (flagship rollout; initial version 2025) |
| **Type** | Central Sector initiative / digital platform |
| **Technology partner** | CMS Computers India Ltd |

**Objective:** A single digital window for all seafarer, shipping-company and port-related regulatory services.

**Key features**

- Integrates **72+ services** with real-time application tracking and online payments; fully paperless.
- **e-NAVIK** — a 24×7 grievance-redressal system for seafarers.
- **d-SEA (Digital Seafarers Employment Agreement)** — digitised employment contracts, strengthening seafarer welfare and contract enforceability.
- Administered through the **Directorate General of Maritime Administration**.

**Beneficiaries:** Indian seafarers (India supplies a large share of the global seafarer pool), shipping companies, ports, maritime training institutes.
**Significance:** Aligned to **Maritime India Vision 2030** and **Maritime Amrit Kaal Vision 2047**; complements the earlier 'Seafarer-First' initiative. Reduces the discretionary, paper-heavy interface that made certificate issuance a corruption pressure point.

**Tag:** GS3 (Infrastructure — ports & shipping); GS2 (e-Governance).

### 3. Accreditation Scheme for Mobile Food Testing Laboratories — India's first

*Sector: Health / Food safety / Quality infrastructure*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Nodal body** | **NABL** (National Accreditation Board for Testing and Calibration Laboratories) |
| **Parent** | **Quality Council of India (QCI)** → DPIIT, Ministry of Commerce & Industry |
| **Launched** | August 2026, at the *'Gunvatta Samanvaya'* ceremony, New Delhi (with a multilateral MoU exchange) |
| **Type** | Accreditation scheme (regulatory/quality infrastructure, not a funding scheme) |

**Objective:** Extend internationally benchmarked, quality-assured food testing to mobile units operating at mandis, rural markets and the doorstep of Food Business Operators (FBOs).

**Key features**

- First-of-its-kind accreditation framework for **mobile** (van-based) food labs, aligned to **ISO/IEC 17025**.
- Sits under an **Integrated Assessment Framework/Programme** that merges multiple certifications into one coordinated assessment — removes duplicate audits, improves Ease of Doing Business.
- Enables mutual recognition among **FSSAI**, state food safety authorities and commodity boards (Spices Board, Tea Board).
- Targets on-the-spot adulteration detection in milk, spices, fresh produce and processed foods.

**Significance:** Shifts food-safety enforcement from centralised lab bottlenecks to **last-mile, quality-at-source surveillance** — directly relevant to the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006 enforcement gap.
**Criticism/watch:** Mobile labs typically do screening, not confirmatory, testing; legal admissibility of mobile-lab results in prosecutions remains the weak link. Rollout depends on state FDA funding.

**Tag:** GS2 (Statutory & regulatory bodies — QCI/NABL/FSSAI; health governance); GS3 (Food security & food processing).

### 4. Panchayat NIRNAY + Shashakta Panchayat Netri Abhiyan

*Sector: Local self-government / Women's political empowerment*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Nodal ministry** | Ministry of Panchayati Raj (MoPR) |
| **In news** | 11–12 August 2026, via the National Study Report on Gram Sabha participation (see Report B) |
| **Type** | Central Sector components under the e-Panchayat Mission Mode Project / Revamped RGSA |

**Panchayat NIRNAY** — *National Initiative for Rural India to Navigate, Action & Yield*. A mobile + web application for **end-to-end digital lifecycle management of Gram Sabha meetings**:

- Advance notification of meeting agendas to villagers.
- Photo-proof upload of quorum.
- Digital recording of minutes.
- Public publication and tracking of passed resolutions.

**Shashakta Panchayat Netri Abhiyan** — a targeted capacity-building module for **Women Elected Representatives (WERs)** covering leadership, administration and legal literacy, aimed at ending "Sarpanch-Pati" proxy governance.

**Supporting architecture:** Revamped **RGSA** (Rashtriya Gram Swaraj Abhiyan, FY 2022–23 onwards), **eGramSwaraj** portal for GPDPs, **Model Youth Gram Sabha (MYGS)** in schools, **People's Plan Campaign / "Sabki Yojana Sabka Vikas"** (2 October – 31 January annually), and advisories mandating separate **Ward Sabhas and Mahila Sabhas** before the main Gram Sabha.

**Constitutional hooks:** **Article 243(b)** defines Gram Sabha; **Article 243A** governs its powers and functions; **11th Schedule** (29 subjects).

**Tag:** GS2 (Devolution of powers to local levels; transparency & accountability; women's representation).

### 6. SASCI — Special Assistance to States for Capital Investment (Iconic Tourist Centres)

*Sector: Tourism / Cooperative federalism / Capex*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Nodal ministry** | **Ministry of Tourism**, with Department of Expenditure, Ministry of Finance |
| **Type** | **Central Sector** — 100% central funding as **50-year interest-free loans** to States for capex |
| **In news** | 13 August 2026 (progress review) |

**Objective:** Develop world-class iconic tourist destinations in challenge mode, using capex loans that do not count against normal state borrowing costs.

**Key features**

- **Central Sanctioning & Monitoring Committee (CSMC)** + a **Mission Directorate**.
- Inter-ministerial convergence: Culture, MoHUA, Civil Aviation, MoRTH, Ports, MoEFCC, Rural Development, Railways and **ASI** sit on the monitoring body.
- **Programme Management Information System (PMIS)** — unified digital tracking of physical progress and fund flow.

**Coverage/figures**

- **FY 2024-25:** **₹3,295.76 crore** sanctioned for **40 projects across 23 States**.
- **FY 2025-26:** **₹250 crore each** released to Himachal Pradesh, Mizoram and Nagaland.
- Illustrative projects: Gandikota Fort (AP, ₹77.91 cr); Rang Ghar, Sivasagar (Assam, ₹94.76 cr); Matsyagandha Lake, Saharsa (Bihar, ₹97.61 cr); Orchha + MICE Bhopal (MP, ₹199.30 cr); **INS Guldar underwater museum**, Sindhudurg (Maharashtra, ₹46.91 cr); Amber–Nahargarh & Jal Mahal (Rajasthan, ₹145.92 cr).

**Significance:** A model of **fiscal federalism through conditional capex lending** rather than grants — states get 50-year interest-free money but accept central sanctioning and monitoring.
**Criticism:** Interest-free loans still add to state debt stock; project selection concentrated in politically salient destinations; absorption capacity varies sharply across states.

**Tag:** GS2 (Centre–State financial relations); GS3 (Infrastructure, tourism, capital expenditure).

## POLICY / LEGISLATIVE CHANGE OF THE WEEK

### 8. The Mines and Minerals (Development and Regulation) Amendment Bill, 2026

**Passed:** Lok Sabha 12 August 2026; **Rajya Sabha 13 August 2026**. Awaiting Presidential assent.
**Nodal ministry:** Ministry of Mines.

**Core provisions**

- **New Section 9D:** **No State Government shall impose any tax, cess or other levy on mineral rights or mineral-bearing lands**, by whatever name called.
- **Amended Section 13:** empowers the **Central Government** to make rules prescribing the conditions and restrictions under which States may impose such levies.
- Centre regulates **major minerals** (coal, limestone, iron ore, copper, manganese); States retain powers over **49 minor minerals**.

**Government's justification:** a uniform, predictable mineral taxation regime; points to States' share of mineral revenue rising from **65% (2014-15) to 85%**, and of coal revenue from **51% to 96%**.

**Why this is high-yield:** It is a **legislative response to the Supreme Court's 9-judge Constitution Bench ruling in *Mineral Area Development Authority v. Steel Authority of India* (July 2024)**, which held that royalty is not a tax and that **States have legislative competence to tax mineral rights and mineral-bearing land** (Entry 50, List II), with retrospective recovery permitted from 1 April 2005. The 2026 Amendment effectively **nullifies the fiscal consequence** of that judgment.

**Debate to carry into Mains**

- *For:* prevents cascading state levies that raise input costs for steel, cement and power; avoids a ₹-lakh-crore retrospective demand shock on PSUs and miners; supports critical-mineral investment.
- *Against:* erodes **Entry 50, List II** and State fiscal autonomy; mineral-rich states (Jharkhand, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, West Bengal) lose a constitutionally affirmed revenue head; raises the question whether Parliament can use Entry 54, List I to hollow out a State entry that the Supreme Court has just upheld — a likely fresh round of litigation.

**Tag:** GS2 (Federalism; Centre–State relations; separation of powers; judicial pronouncements); GS3 (Mineral resources, industrial policy).

### B. MoPR — *National Study Report on Low Participation in Gram Sabha*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Publishing body** | Ministry of Panchayati Raj; study conducted by **NIRDPR** (National Institute of Rural Development & Panchayati Raj) |
| **Released** | 11–12 August 2026 |
| **What it measures** | Structural, systemic and socio-economic causes of low Gram Sabha attendance |

**Key findings**

1. **Wage-loss trade-off** — landless labourers and migrant workers cannot attend daytime meetings without losing a day's wage.
2. **Gendered exclusion** — domestic burden, social hesitation, male-dominated deliberation and proxy ("Pradhan-Pati") governance suppress women's substantive participation.
3. **Institutional apathy** — line-department officials (revenue, agriculture, health, education) rarely attend; no feedback loop on earlier resolutions; weak grievance redressal → erosion of trust in the Gram Sabha's efficacy.
4. **Information asymmetry** — inadequate advance notice of agendas and opacity in **GPDP** allocations.

**Remedies proposed:** Panchayat NIRNAY digitisation; mandatory pre-meeting **Ward Sabhas and Mahila Sabhas**; compulsory line-department attendance; WER training under Shashakta Panchayat Netri Abhiyan.

**Tag:** GS2 (Local self-government; devolution; transparency & accountability). Excellent as a **quotable data anchor** for any Panchayati Raj answer.

## PRELIMS POINTERS

1. **Sapta Dhara ≠ Panchamrit.** Sapta Dhara (2026) = 7 growth streams; Panchamrit (COP26, 2021) = 5 climate commitments.
2. **GES is under the Ministry of Culture; ICCR is under the MEA.** Project Mausam is a GES component — not a Ports/Shipping scheme.
3. **SASCI money is a 50-year interest-free loan, not a grant** — 100% central funding, but it is debt on the state's books.
4. **MMDR 2026 inserts Section 9D** (bar on state levies) and **amends Section 13** (Centre's rule-making power). States keep **49 minor minerals**.
5. **NABL is a constituent board of QCI**, which sits under **DPIIT / Ministry of Commerce & Industry** — not under FSSAI or MoHFW.
6. **Article 243(b)** defines Gram Sabha; **Article 243A** deals with its powers. **Article 243W + 12th Schedule** = urban (used in the AMRUT master-plan item).
7. **SACU** — established **1910**, HQ **Windhoek**, five members; world's oldest existing customs union.
8. **DFSM target year = 2030** (zero debris). ISRO has been an **IADC member since 1996**.
9. **India's non-fossil break-up on 31 Jul 2026**: solar 164.59 > wind 58.14 > hydro 57.24 > bio 11.75 > nuclear 8.78 GW.
10. **SHANTI Act, 2025** is the enabling framework cited for the 100 GW-nuclear-by-2047 target.
