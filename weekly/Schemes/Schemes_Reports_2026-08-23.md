## SCHEMES, PROGRAMMES & CAMPAIGNS

### 1. National Campaign on Entrepreneurship–II (NCE-II) ⭐ *scheme of the week*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | Ministry of Rural Development — under **DAY-NRLM** (Deendayal Antyodaya Yojana – National Rural Livelihoods Mission) |
| **Launched** | **21 August 2026** (by Rural Development Secretary Shri Rohit Kansal); NCE-I was launched earlier in 2026 |
| **Type** | Campaign under a **Centrally Sponsored Scheme** (DAY-NRLM) |
| **Duration** | 3 months — 21 August to 21 November 2026 |

**Objective:** Convert the livelihood activities of Self-Help Group (SHG) women into **formal, sustainable enterprises**, and accelerate progress towards the national goal of **6 crore Lakhpati Didis**.

**Key features (4):**

1. **Farm + non-farm focus** — promotes enterprise among SHG women across both agricultural and non-agricultural livelihoods, with a push on **enterprise formalisation** (Udyam registration, GST, licensing).
2. **'Udyam Sakhis'** — CRP-EPs (Community Resource Persons–Enterprise Promotion) and BDSPs (Business Development Service Providers) are formally recognised as *Udyam Sakhis*, giving institutional identity to the community enterprise cadre.
3. **Digital market linkage** — SHG products to be onboarded on **ONDC** and **GeM**; buyer–seller meets and institutional-buyer tie-ups.
4. **'Sankalp Shakti'** — first-ever **Rural Enterprise Technical Bulletin** (monthly), released alongside the campaign, focused on Lakhpati Didi enterprise development.

**Targets / coverage:** 5 lakh SHG women trained · 50,000 enterprises formalised · 25,000 SHG members onboarded on e-commerce · 20 lakh women nudged toward agro-ecological practices.

**Significance:** Marks the shift in NRLM from *credit-and-thrift* to *enterprise-and-market* — the "Lakhpati Didi" target is impossible without formalisation and market access. **Criticism:** campaign-mode (3-month) delivery risks target-chasing over enterprise viability; e-commerce onboarding numbers historically outpace actual transacting sellers; the Lakhpati Didi income threshold (₹1 lakh/year household) is self-reported and not independently audited.

**Tag:** GS-II — Government policies & interventions; welfare schemes for vulnerable sections · GS-III — Inclusive growth, rural economy, SHGs

### 2. Incentive Scheme for Domestic PNG Connections

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas (implemented via **City Gas Distribution (CGD)** entities; gas allocation by GAIL under the APM framework) |
| **Approved / announced** | Third week of August 2026 |
| **Effective from** | **1 September 2026** — in **2 tranches over 6 months** |
| **Type** | **Central Sector** incentive scheme (gas-allocation-based, not budgetary subsidy) |

**Objective:** Accelerate expansion of **active, billed domestic PNG connections** and convert the large stock of "connected but unbilled" households into paying consumers.

**Key features (4):**

1. **The incentive mechanism** — for every **incremental billed domestic PNG connection** above a prescribed threshold in a geographical area, the CGD entity gets an **additional 200 SCM of domestically produced, lower-priced APM gas**.
2. **How it pays** — the extra APM gas **substitutes costlier imported LNG** that CGDs currently buy for the CNG (transport) segment, cutting their blended gas-sourcing cost.
3. **Payback compression** — Government estimate: payback period on domestic PNG capex falls from **~10 years to ~3 years**.
4. **Enabling measures** — Accelerated Approval Framework for PNG infrastructure; **standardised Right-of-Way (RoW) charges** under the Natural Gas & Petroleum Distribution Order, 2026; States urged to cut **VAT on natural gas to 5%**; **National PNG Drive 2.0** (LPG surrender portal, housing-society conversion); proposed **unified single-window PNG registration portal**.

**Coverage:** India currently has about **1.74 crore domestic PNG connections**.

**Significance:** PNG is the cleaner, metered, cylinder-free alternative to LPG and is central to the target of raising **natural gas to ~15% of India's primary energy mix by 2030** (from ~6%). Also cuts the LPG subsidy bill. **Criticism:** the benefit is routed to CGD companies (an implicit cross-subsidy from the APM pool), not directly to households; APM gas is a finite, price-controlled resource — diverting it to CGD raises allocation-equity questions vis-à-vis fertiliser and power; PNG remains an urban/high-rise solution with limited rural reach.

**Tag:** GS-III — Infrastructure (energy); energy security; environment (clean cooking fuel)

### 3. Central Sector Scheme for Development of Makhana + National Makhana Board

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare; **National Makhana Board** (HQ Bihar). Export-promotion angle pushed by Ministry of Commerce & Industry (PIB feature, **19 August 2026**) |
| **Announced / launched** | Announced in **Union Budget 2025–26**; Board launched at **Purnea, Bihar on 15 September 2025** |
| **Type** | **Central Sector Scheme** |
| **Outlay** | **₹476.03 crore** (2025–26 to 2030–31) |

**Objective:** Transform makhana (*Euryale ferox*, fox nut) from a subsistence aquatic harvest in Bihar's Kosi–Mithila belt into a globally competitive premium superfood, through research, quality seed, mechanisation, processing, branding and export promotion.

**Key features (4):**

1. **Commodity-board model** — the National Makhana Board is modelled on the Spices/Coffee/Tea Boards; handles research, quality standards, market development and export promotion.
2. **Seed & research backbone** — **ICAR–National Research Centre for Makhana (NRCM), Darbhanga**; improved varieties **Swarna Vaidehi** and **Sabour Makhana-1**; SAU Sabour and CAU Samastipur designated primary suppliers of quality seed.
3. **Mechanisation & processing** — NRCM-developed seed washer, grader, roasting and popping machines licensed for commercial manufacture; promotion of **field-system cultivation** (paddy-field style) over pond-only cultivation to enable mechanisation and rotation.
4. **Market & GI** — FPO aggregation, e-commerce and export linkages; **"Mithila Makhana" GI tag (2022)**; a dedicated **HSN code introduced in 2025** now allows product-specific export tracking.

**Beneficiaries / coverage:** Bihar produces roughly **74% of India's output and ~80–85% of global supply**; key districts Supaul, Saharsa, Madhepura, Darbhanga, Sitamarhi, Madhubani, Purnea, Katihar. Cultivation is dominated by the traditionally water-dependent **Mallah** community.

**Data points (2025–26, 2nd Advance Estimates):** production **80,590 MT** (up from 63,910 MT in 2024–25); productivity 2.34 MT/ha; exports **7,264.89 MT worth ₹192.96 crore**; top markets USA (~40%), Canada (~20%), UAE (~17%); farm-gate price rose from ~₹500/kg (2020–22) to ~₹1,250/kg (2025).

**Significance:** A textbook case of GI + commodity board + value-chain policy raising farmer incomes in a lagging region. **Criticism:** underwater harvesting has resisted mechanisation — any mechanisation must not displace Mallah livelihoods; **77% export concentration** in three markets is a structural risk; smallholders lack EU-SPS/HACCP/organic certification, locking them out of premium markets (Germany ~$26/kg vs USA ~$19.5/kg); long intermediary chains capture most of the consumer rupee.

**Tag:** GS-III — Agriculture: value chains, food processing, agri-exports, GI · GS-II — Government interventions

### 4. Pradhan Mantri Janjatiya Vikas Mission (PMJVM)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | Ministry of Tribal Affairs; implemented through **TRIFED** and State Implementing Agencies |
| **Launched** | **2021–22** (restructured umbrella of the earlier MFP-MSP scheme and Van Dhan Yojana) |
| **Type** | **Central Sector Scheme** |
| **Why in news** | PIB progress note (21 August 2026): as on **31 July 2026**, **4,172 Van Dhan Vikas Kendras (VDVKs)** are operational, linking **12.48 lakh tribal beneficiaries** |

**Objective:** Promote livelihoods and entrepreneurship among tribal populations by building transparent production–procurement–processing–marketing chains for **Minor Forest Produce (MFP)**, with assured **MSP**.

**Key features (4):**

1. **MSP for MFP** — the Centre has notified Minimum Support Price for **87 MFP items**; TRIFED procures through State agencies **regardless of market price**.
2. **Van Dhan architecture** — **Van Dhan SHGs** (up to 20 members) clustered into **VDVKs** (~300 members), given primary processing equipment (decorticators, dryers, cutting/sieving tools, packaging), training for 30 members, working capital tie-ups (banks, **NSTFDC**) and rental storage/transport.
3. **Haat bazaar modernisation** — over **5,000 tribal haats** being upgraded in phases with permanent structures, storage, drinking water, shade and weighing equipment.
4. **Market & IP support** — geo-tagging of VDVKs, GI facilitation for tribal produce, e-commerce platform upgrades, TRIFED market-intelligence feeds (daily rates), branding via festivals and artisan melas.

**Statutory backdrop (high-yield for Prelims):** The **Forest Rights Act, 2006** defines MFP as *all non-timber forest produce of plant origin* — bamboo, brushwood, stumps, cane, tussar, cocoons, honey, wax, lac, tendu/kendu leaves, medicinal plants, roots, tubers etc. — and gives forest dwellers the right to collect and sell it. Separately, **PESA, 1996** vests **ownership of MFP in tribal Gram Sabhas** in Scheduled Areas.

**Significance:** Links a forest-based, community-owned economy to national markets without alienating land or resource rights. **Criticism:** MSP for MFP is procurement-constrained — actual procurement has historically been a small fraction of notified value; VDVK dormancy after the first grant cycle is widely reported; MSP revision lags market volatility for high-value items like tendu leaf and honey.

**Tag:** GS-II — Welfare schemes for STs; mechanisms and bodies (TRIFED, NSTFDC) · GS-III — Inclusive growth; food processing

### 5. SEED — Scheme for Economic Empowerment of DNTs

| | |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment, via the **Development and Welfare Board for De-notified, Nomadic and Semi-Nomadic Communities (DWBDNC)** |
| **Constituted / launched** | DWBDNC constituted **February 2019**; SEED implemented under it |
| **Type** | **Central Sector Scheme** |
| **Why in news** | Ministry statement (**18 August 2026**) detailing measures for DNT/NT/SNT economic empowerment |

**Objective:** Socio-economic empowerment of **De-notified, Nomadic and Semi-Nomadic Tribes (DNT/NT/SNT)** — communities "de-notified" after repeal of the colonial **Criminal Tribes Act, 1871** (repealed 1952) but still among India's most marginalised and under-documented groups.

**Key features (4 components):**

1. **Education** — free, quality coaching for DNT candidates preparing for competitive examinations.
2. **Health** — health insurance support for DNT households.
3. **Livelihood** — community-level livelihood initiatives and income-generation support.
4. **Housing** — financial assistance for house construction for DNT families.

**Institutional history (Prelims-relevant):** The **National Commission for De-notified, Nomadic and Semi-Nomadic Tribes** was constituted in **January 2015** and submitted its report in **December 2017**; DWBDNC was set up in **February 2019** acting on its recommendations. (Earlier: **Renke Commission, 2008**; **Idate Commission, 2017**.)

**Significance:** DNT communities fall through the SC/ST/OBC classification net — many are listed differently across States, so targeted schemes are the only reliable delivery route. **Criticism:** SEED's utilisation has been low relative to allocation; there is still **no comprehensive national enumeration** of DNT populations, making saturation-type targeting impossible; overlapping State-level classification remains unresolved.

**Tag:** GS-II — Welfare schemes for vulnerable sections; mechanisms, laws and institutions · GS-I — Indian society: marginalised communities

## POLICY / REGULATORY CHANGE OF THE WEEK

### 7. CAQM Directions No. 102 and No. 103 — cleaner LGVs and stone-crusher dust control

**Body:** **Commission for Air Quality Management in NCR and Adjoining Areas (CAQM)** · **29th Full Commission Meeting, 18 August 2026**

- **Direction No. 102** — mandates a **phased shift to cleaner Light Goods Vehicles (LGVs)** in Delhi-NCR, beginning **1 January 2027**.
- **Direction No. 103** — imposes **technology-enabled continuous monitoring** on stone-crushing units, targeting **PM2.5/PM10 fugitive dust**.

**Static backbone (very high-yield):**

- CAQM was created by the **Commission for Air Quality Management in NCR and Adjoining Areas Act, 2021** — a **Parliamentary statute**, replacing the Supreme-Court-created **EPCA (1998)** under the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986. It is the **first statutory authority specifically for Delhi-NCR air quality**.
- **Jurisdiction:** Delhi, Haryana, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab.
- **Overriding powers:** CAQM directions **override CPCB and State PCBs** within its jurisdiction; it is subordinate only to the Supreme Court.
- **GRAP (Graded Response Action Plan):** four-stage (I–IV) emergency framework triggered by AQI thresholds; approved by the Supreme Court, implemented by CAQM.
- **Benchmark:** WHO PM2.5 annual guideline is **5 µg/m³**; Delhi's annual average has repeatedly exceeded 100 µg/m³.

**Tag:** GS-III — Environment: air pollution, pollution-control mechanisms · GS-II — Statutory bodies and their powers

### B. NITI Aayog — *India's Services Sector: Insights on Regulatory Regime in Professional Services*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Publishing body** | **NITI Aayog** (released August 2026) |
| **What it examines** | India's regulatory architecture for **professional services** (law, accountancy/tax, IT and cybersecurity consulting, management consulting, architecture etc.), benchmarked against selected foreign jurisdictions |

**Key findings (3):**

1. **Regulatory fragmentation and opacity** — unclear rules on foreign entry, permissible legal forms of practice, advertising restrictions and recognition of foreign degrees hold back market entry and global competitiveness.
2. **Four-pronged strategy recommended** — (i) harness emerging trends to transform professional services; (ii) embed professional services within the wider services value chain; (iii) adopt global best practices in regulation; (iv) institutionalise **continuous professional development** and skill upgrading.
3. **Why it matters** — professional services contribute roughly **one-fourth of India's total services exports**; reform is projected to raise services exports, create high-skilled jobs and improve cross-border mobility of Indian professionals (relevant to **Mode 4** supply under GATS and to mutual-recognition agreements in India's FTAs).

**Criticism noted in commentary:** the report diagnoses well but stops short of a concrete legislative action plan, timelines, public-interest safeguards or measurable targets.

**Tag:** GS-III — Indian economy: services sector, exports, ease of doing business · GS-II — Regulatory bodies

### C. National Report on Deep-Sea and Distant-Water Fishery Resources of the Indian EEZ

| | |
|---|---|
| **Publishing body** | **Ministry of Earth Sciences (MoES)** — released **21 August 2026** |
| **What it measures** | Consolidates nearly **four decades** of oceanographic research and **400+ scientific cruises** across India's **2.37 million km² Exclusive Economic Zone**, under the **Marine Living Resources Programme (MLRP)**, largely aboard **FORV *Sagar Sampada*** |

**Key findings (3):**

1. **Mesopelagic biomass** — documents the vast, largely unexploited biomass of **lanternfishes (Myctophids)**, whose nightly vertical migration drives the ocean's **"biological carbon pump"**, moving carbon from surface waters to the deep ocean.
2. **Underexploited demersal stocks** — identifies deep-sea shrimps, perches, scombroids, flatfishes and eels along continental slopes **beyond 200 m depth**.
3. **Conservation hotspots mapped** — vulnerable marine ecosystems flagged at **Kollam Bank, Angria Bank**, the off-Mangalore deep-sea slope, the terrace off Trivandrum, **Lakshadweep** and the **Andaman & Nicobar Islands**. Methods integrate fisheries acoustics, **environmental DNA (eDNA)**, autonomous platforms, satellite sensing, AI and ecosystem modelling — a base for **marine spatial planning**.

**Anchors to:** Blue Economy pillar of **Viksit Bharat 2047**, **UN SDG 14 (Life Below Water)**, UN Decade of Ocean Science.

**Tag:** GS-III — Economy: fisheries, blue economy; conservation; science & technology · GS-I — Geography: oceanography

### E. 261st Report of the Parliamentary Standing Committee on Home Affairs — Disaster Management (2026)

**Publishing body:** Department-related Parliamentary Standing Committee on **Home Affairs**, Rajya Sabha (press release issued 7 August 2026; in wider circulation this week)

**What it examines:** India's evolving disaster-vulnerability matrix under accelerating climate change, rapid urbanisation and new hazard-management technologies.

**Key gaps identified:**

- The **National Policy on Disaster Management (2009)** and **NDMP (2016/2019)** predate the **Disaster Management (Amendment) Act, 2025** and contain no provisions for AI-driven forecasting or climate-risk modelling.
- Only **Karnataka** has set up a specialised **Urban Disaster Management Authority (UDMA)**.
- Only **10 States/UTs** have adopted the **Model Bill on Maintenance of Fire and Emergency Services** (circulated 2019).
- Landslide susceptibility mapping and seismic microzonation are rarely mandatory in town-planning or project clearance.
- Financing remains skewed to **post-disaster relief** over **pre-disaster mitigation**.

**Key recommendations:**

- Immediate review of the 2009 Policy and revision of the NDMP to align with the **DM (Amendment) Act, 2025** and the **National Disaster Mitigation Fund (NDMF)**.
- Mandatory **UDMAs** in State capitals and municipal corporations; time-bound adoption of the Fire Services Model Bill; statutory **Disaster Risk Impact Assessment (DRIA)** for major infrastructure and urban projects.
- Shift from district-level to **ward-level hyper-local early warning**; a **unified multi-hazard platform** integrating IMD, CWC, INCOIS and NRSC; quarterly State updates to the **India Disaster Resource Network (IDRN)**.
- Recommend to the **16th Finance Commission** that **heatwaves and lightning** be added to the notified list of disasters eligible under the **SDRF**.
- **Two dedicated Himalayan NDRF Regional Response Centres** (Western and Eastern Himalaya) for landslides, **GLOFs** and flash floods; measure response by **actual site-arrival time**; scale up **Aapda Mitra** and integrate Village DM Plans into District DM Plans.

**Tag:** GS-III — Disaster management · GS-II — Statutory bodies (NDMA), Centre-State finance, Finance Commission

## PRELIMS POINTERS

- **Makhana** = *Euryale ferox*, family **Nymphaeaceae** (water-lily family) — **not** the lotus (*Nelumbo nucifera*). GI: **"Mithila Makhana", 2022**.
- **Minor Forest Produce** is defined in the **Forest Rights Act, 2006**; **ownership** of MFP in Scheduled Areas vests in Gram Sabhas under **PESA, 1996**. MSP notified for **87 MFP items**.
- **CAQM** is **statutory (Act of 2021)**; **EPCA** was **court-created (1998)**. CAQM overrides CPCB/SPCBs; jurisdiction = Delhi + Haryana, Rajasthan, UP, Punjab.
- **CGGI** has **7 pillars**, covers **133 governments**; India **76th** in 2026.
- **UNCCD COP17**: Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, 17–28 Aug 2026; theme *"Restoring Land. Restoring Hope."* (UNCCD is one of the three **Rio Conventions**, alongside UNFCCC and CBD.)
- **FORV *Sagar Sampada*** = Fishery and Oceanographic Research Vessel, Ministry of Earth Sciences. India's **EEZ = 2.37 million km²**.
- **Digital Bharat Nidhi** replaced the **Universal Service Obligation Fund (USOF)** under the **Telecommunications Act, 2023**.
- **HAM (Hybrid Annuity Model)** = 40% government funding during construction + annuity payments; used for NH-22 Bihar approval.
- **DWBDNC** constituted **Feb 2019**; National Commission for DNT/NT/SNT constituted **Jan 2015**, reported **Dec 2017**. Criminal Tribes Act, 1871 → repealed **1952**.
- **SWM Rules, 2026** mandate **four-stream** segregation: **wet, dry, sanitary, special-care**.

1. *"India's welfare architecture has moved from entitlement delivery to enterprise creation."* Examine with reference to DAY-NRLM's Lakhpati Didi and National Campaign on Entrepreneurship-II. **(GS-II, 15 marks)**
2. Commodity boards, GI tags and value-chain schemes are being deployed to raise farmer incomes in lagging regions. Critically evaluate using the makhana sector. **(GS-III, 15 marks)**
3. Statutory authorities with overriding powers, such as CAQM, improve enforcement but raise federalism concerns. Discuss. **(GS-II, 10 marks)**
4. India's disaster-management framework remains relief-heavy and mitigation-light. Analyse in light of the 261st Parliamentary Standing Committee report and the DM (Amendment) Act, 2025. **(GS-III, 15 marks)**
5. Open Natural Ecosystems have been treated as "wastelands" in Indian land policy. Discuss the ecological and livelihood costs of this framing. **(GS-III, 10 marks)**
6. India's rank on global governance indices tells only part of the story. Discuss the interpretive limits of composite indices using CGGI 2026. **(GS-II, 10 marks)**
