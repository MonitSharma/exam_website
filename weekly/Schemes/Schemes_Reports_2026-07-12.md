## SCHEMES & PROGRAMMES

### 1. Regional Connectivity Scheme — Modified UDAN (launched by PM, 4 July 2026)
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Infrastructure (airports/aviation), regional development, employment; GS2 — Centre-State cooperation in connectivity

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Ministry of Civil Aviation** (implemented via Airports Authority of India + airlines); UDAN = *Ude Desh ka Aam Nagrik*.
- **Launched:** UDAN first launched 2017; **Modified UDAN launched by PM Modi on 4 July 2026** at Balotra/Jodhpur, Rajasthan (Cabinet-approved Mar 2026).
- **Type:** Central Sector scheme (Viability Gap Funding based).
- **Objective:** Deepen affordable regional air connectivity and last-mile aviation access for tier-2/tier-3 towns under "Viksit Bharat 2047."
- **Key features:**
  - Total outlay **~₹28,840 crore** over **10 years (FY2026-27 to FY2035-36)**.
  - Target to develop/revive **100 airports, heliports and water aerodromes** from unserved/underserved airstrips.
  - VGF support + concessions to airlines; fare caps on RCS routes to keep seats affordable.
  - Launch paired with inauguration of the new **Jodhpur Airport terminal** (₹480 cr, ~20 lakh passengers/yr capacity).
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** Air travellers in smaller cities; regional carriers; tourism and local economies around revived airstrips.
- **Significance & criticism:** India's flagship air-connectivity push, now in a longer 10-year phase. **Criticism:** several first-phase UDAN routes were discontinued after the 3-year VGF window lapsed (thin traffic, unviable economics); critics question whether new airports will see sustained demand ("planes that may never come").

### 2. Maharashtra Women Farmers Empowerment Bill, 2026
**Syllabus tag:** GS1 — Role of women; GS2 — Welfare of women & vulnerable sections, government policies; GS3 — Agriculture, access to credit & inputs

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Government of Maharashtra** (Agriculture Dept.); Cabinet cleared the draft in late June, **passed by the State Legislative Assembly in the monsoon session (early July 2026)**.
- **Type:** State legislation (a first-of-its-kind law in India).
- **Objective:** **Legally recognise women as independent farmers regardless of land ownership**, unlocking their access to welfare schemes, subsidies, institutional credit and extension services.
- **Key features:**
  - Provision for **"women farmer certificates"** so landless women in farming/dairy/fisheries can access credit, subsidies and extension.
  - Creation of a **Maharashtra State Women Farmers' Fund** and a **digital database** of women farmers.
  - **Women farmer support officers** at district and taluka levels; three-tier framework (governing council, state monitoring committee, empowerment cell).
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** Women engaged in agriculture and allied activities across Maharashtra, especially landless cultivators historically excluded from "farmer" definitions.
- **Significance & criticism:** Addresses the long-standing invisibilisation of women in agriculture (most land is titled to men, so women miss out on schemes). **Criticism/risk:** implementation hinges on certificate issuance not becoming a new bureaucratic hurdle; no automatic land-rights transfer, so structural gender gaps in ownership remain.

### 3. Namo Harit Nagar Yojana (Madhya Pradesh)
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Environment, urban ecology, conservation; GS2 — Urban local bodies, state welfare/development schemes

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Government of Madhya Pradesh** — Urban Development & Housing Department; **approved by the Mohan Yadav Cabinet on ~8 July 2026.**
- **Type:** State scheme.
- **Objective:** Develop **urban forests ("city forests")** to improve urban biodiversity, air quality and green cover in Madhya Pradesh's towns.
- **Key features:**
  - Outlay **₹100 crore** over **five years**.
  - At least **one urban forest in each of 65 urban local bodies**.
  - Focus on native-species plantation, biodiversity and micro-climate improvement (conceptually aligned with the Centre's Nagar Van Yojana).
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** Urban residents of 65 MP municipalities; broader environmental/public-health co-benefits.
- **Significance & criticism:** A state-level response to worsening urban air quality and heat-island effects. **Criticism/risk:** modest per-city funding; success depends on land availability inside congested municipalities and post-plantation survival/maintenance (a recurring weakness of greening drives).

### 4. Himachal Pradesh Agriculture Loan Interest Subvention Scheme
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Agriculture credit, farm distress, cooperative banking; GS2 — State welfare schemes, relief measures

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Government of Himachal Pradesh**; routed through the **HP State Cooperative Agriculture & Rural Development Bank** and the **Kangra Cooperative Agriculture & Rural Development Bank**; announced June 2026, in the news this window.
- **Type:** State scheme (with a one-time-settlement component).
- **Objective:** Prevent auction of farmers' land over defaulted agricultural loans by easing their interest burden.
- **Key features:**
  - Government bears **50% of the outstanding interest liability** on agricultural loans **up to ₹3 lakh per farmer**.
  - One-time settlement route to help regularise overdue loans.
  - Estimated **~6,356 farmers** to benefit; outlay **~₹50 crore**.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** Distressed farmers with overdue cooperative-bank agri loans whose land faces auction.
- **Significance & criticism:** Targets a narrow but acute distress point (land loss), rather than blanket loan waiver. **Criticism:** limited coverage (a few thousand farmers) and a cap of ₹3 lakh; does not address the structural causes of agrarian indebtedness.

### 5. Startup India Fund of Funds 2.0 (DPIIT operating guidelines issued)
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Economy, startups & innovation ecosystem, mobilisation of resources, deep tech/manufacturing; GS2 — Government policies

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Department for Promotion of Industry & Internal Trade (DPIIT)**, Ministry of Commerce & Industry; execution via **SIDBI**; deployed through **SEBI-registered Category I & II Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs)**.
- **Launched/notified:** Notified in the Gazette on **13 April 2026**; **DPIIT released the operating guidelines this window (in the news early July 2026)** — that is the fresh development.
- **Type:** Central Sector scheme (fund-of-funds structure, not direct government equity in startups).
- **Objective:** Mobilise long-term domestic venture capital for the startup ecosystem, with a deliberate tilt toward patient capital for deep tech.
- **Key features:**
  - Corpus **₹10,000 crore**.
  - Priority to **deep tech, AI/ML, semiconductor design and tech-driven manufacturing**.
  - Government does not invest directly in startups — capital flows via SEBI-registered AIFs into DPIIT-recognised startups.
  - Successor to the original ₹10,000 cr Fund of Funds for Startups (FFS, 2016).
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** DPIIT-recognised startups (early-stage and deep-tech focused); domestic VC/AIF ecosystem.
- **Significance & criticism:** Signals a policy shift from consumer-internet startups toward capital-intensive frontier tech. **Criticism/risk:** fund-of-funds disbursal has historically been slow; deep-tech returns are long-gestation, and crowding-in of private capital is not guaranteed.

### 6. Bihar Sugarcane Industries Investment Incentive Policy, 2026
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Industrial policy, agro-processing, food processing, employment; GS2 — State economic policy

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Government of Bihar** (Industries / Sugarcane Industries Dept.); approved early July 2026.
- **Type:** State industrial-incentive policy.
- **Objective:** Revive Bihar's sugar/ethanol sector and attract private investment into sugarcane-based agro-industry.
- **Key features:**
  - **100% reimbursement of registration & stamp duty** on land purchases for eligible projects.
  - **Full reimbursement of State GST (SGST)** on sugar production for **5 years**.
  - Aimed at new sugar mills, distilleries and ethanol/by-product units.
- **Beneficiaries/coverage:** Sugar-industry investors and, indirectly, cane-growing farmers and rural employment in Bihar.
- **Significance & criticism:** Links Bihar's agrarian base to the ethanol-blending push and rural jobs. **Criticism/risk:** tax-reimbursement incentives raise fiscal-forgone concerns; benefits to farmers depend on timely cane-price payments (FRP/SAP arrears are a chronic problem in the sector).

## REPORTS & INDICES

### A. Global Passport Index 2026 — Global Citizen Solutions
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — International relations, global indices; GS3 — economy (mobility & investment migration)

- **Publishing body:** **Global Citizen Solutions (GCS)**, a residency & citizenship advisory firm; **5th edition, published 5 July 2026.**
- **What it measures:** Passport "strength" across three weighted pillars — **Enhanced Mobility (50%), Investment Potential (25%), Quality of Living (25%)** — for **197 countries**.
- **India's standing:** **125th of 197**, slipping one place from 124th (2025), though composite score of **45.1** is India's highest in five years.
- **Key findings:**
  - India's **weakest pillar is mobility (136th)** — Indian passport holders get visa-free access to just **~26 countries**; stronger on Quality of Living (up to 118th) and Investment Index (94th).
  - **Sweden topped** the 2026 edition (96.05/100), followed by Switzerland and Finland; **9 of the top 10 are European** (Singapore the exception).
  - Triggered domestic political sparring over passport strength and revised passport fees.

### B. UN Sustainable Development Report 2026 / SDG Index — SDSN
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — International indices, SDGs, governance; GS3 — sustainable development, hunger/food security

- **Publishing body:** **UN Sustainable Development Solutions Network (SDSN)**; released at the **Hamburg Sustainability Conference 2026** (late June, in the news through early July).
- **What it measures:** Progress of **167 countries** on the 17 SDGs, with an overall 0–100 score.
- **India's standing:** **94th of 167 — India's highest-ever rank**, score **68.3/100** (up from 99th in 2025). Among the world's biggest movers since 2015 (up 18 places from 112th).
- **Key findings:**
  - Despite the jump, India lags most on **SDG 2 (Zero Hunger)** and food-system/nutrition indicators; **hunger remains a major challenge**.
  - Globally, only about **one-third of SDG targets are on track** for 2030.
  - **Nordic countries lead** (Finland 1st, Sweden 2nd, Denmark 3rd).
