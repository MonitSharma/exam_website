## SCHEMES & PROGRAMMES

### 1. Viksit Bharat – Guarantee for Rozgar and Ajeevika Mission (Gramin) — **VB-G RAM G**
*(GS2 — Government policies & interventions; GS3 — Employment, inclusive growth; **very high-yield**)*

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** Ministry of Rural Development
- **Enacted:** VB-G RAM G Act, 2025 — passed by Parliament 18–19 December 2025
- **In force:** **1 July 2026** across all rural areas; **MGNREGA, 2005 stands repealed** from the same date
- **Type:** Centrally Sponsored Scheme — **60:40 Centre–State** funding pattern
- **Objective:** Guarantee wage employment in rural areas while shifting the focus from pure wage-employment to durable asset creation and livelihood generation.

**Key features**

1. **125 days** of guaranteed employment per rural household per financial year (up from 100 days under MGNREGA).
2. Works confined to **four thematic domains** — (i) water security, (ii) rural infrastructure, (iii) livelihood-related infrastructure, (iv) mitigation of extreme weather events.
3. **Viksit Gram Panchayat Plans** — village-level development plans mandated and integrated into a national rural infrastructure planning system.
4. Technology-based monitoring, mandatory social audits and public disclosure at Gram Panchayat level; expanded administrative expenditure limits.
5. **Unemployment allowance** payable after 15 days if work is not provided — **liability rests with States**.

**Coverage:** All rural households in the country (demand-driven, self-selecting).

**Significance:** First major overhaul of India's rural employment guarantee architecture in 20 years; higher day-guarantee and thematic focus aim at climate-resilient rural assets.
**Criticism:** The 60:40 split shifts substantial fiscal burden onto States (MGNREGA wage cost was ~100% Centre-funded); States also bear the unemployment allowance. Critics argue thematic restriction of permissible works narrows the demand-driven character of the original Act, and that repealing a rights-based law of Parliament dilutes the entitlement framing.

### 2. PRAGATI — Promoting Regenerative Agriculture Through Innovative Growth Initiatives
*(GS3 — Agriculture, cropping patterns, climate-resilient farming; GS2 — Role of NGOs/SHGs/philanthropy)*

- **Launched by:** Union Minister of Agriculture & Farmers' Welfare and Rural Development, **Shivraj Singh Chouhan** — early-to-mid **July 2026**
- **Nature:** Multi-stakeholder initiative (Government + corporate foundations + international bodies), not a budgeted Central Sector Scheme
- **Objective:** Create a nationwide ecosystem of agri-entrepreneurs to drive regenerative agriculture, rural entrepreneurship and climate-resilient farming among smallholders.

**Key features**

1. Target of **20,000 new agri-entrepreneurs**, building on an existing network of over **26,000**.
2. Direct impact targeted on **20 lakh smallholder farmers**.
3. Geographic focus: **Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Assam, West Bengal, Jharkhand, Maharashtra, Rajasthan**.
4. Partner consortium includes PepsiCo Foundation, SBI Foundation, Gates Foundation, IDH, Heifer International, Environmental Defense Fund, Agri Entrepreneur Growth Foundation and Transform Rural India Foundation.

**Stated outcome targets:** ≥30% rise in participating farmers' incomes; 15–20% yield improvement in paddy, maize and potato; adoption of regenerative practices by ≥20% of participating farmers.

**Significance:** Operationalises "regenerative agriculture" (soil health, reduced chemical input, climate resilience) at scale through a last-mile entrepreneur cadre rather than departmental extension.
**Criticism:** Heavy reliance on private/philanthropic funding raises questions of continuity and of corporate influence on cropping choices; outcome targets are partner-declared, not independently audited.

### 3. National Investment Policy for Urea-2026 for Atmanirbhar Bharat (**NIPU-2026**)
*(GS3 — Agriculture (subsidies, MSP, fertilisers); Indian economy — investment models)*

- **Ministry:** Department of Fertilizers, Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers
- **Approved:** **Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs (CCEA), 15 July 2026**
- **Type:** Investment policy (replaces **NIP-2012**)
- **Objective:** Attract fresh investment in **gas-based urea** manufacturing, cut import dependence and make India self-reliant in urea.

**Key features**

1. Promotes **new gas-based urea units** using energy-efficient, environment-friendly technology.
2. **Separation of fixed and variable costs** for greater transparency in project economics (key departure from NIP-2012).
3. **Return on Equity (RoE) band — floor 12%, ceiling 16%** — to balance investor viability with subsidy discipline.
4. **Forex risk mitigation** — fixed cost converted into INR after four years at the then-prevailing exchange rate.

**Coverage / target:** ~**10 million tonnes** of additional annual urea capacity via **8–9 new gas-based plants**; estimated savings of over **₹250 crore per plant** relative to NIP-2012 terms.

**Significance:** Directly attacks India's urea import bill and the fiscal drag of the fertiliser subsidy; complements nano-urea and neem-coating measures.
**Criticism:** Locks in further capacity for **nitrogenous** fertiliser at a time when policy elsewhere pushes balanced NPK use and soil-health correction; also deepens dependence on imported natural gas as feedstock.

### 4. Mobile Phone Manufacturing Scheme (**MPMS**)
*(GS3 — Industrial policy, growth & employment; Science & Tech — indigenisation)*

- **Ministry:** Ministry of Electronics & Information Technology (MeitY)
- **Approved:** **Union Cabinet, 15 July 2026**
- **Type:** Central Sector Scheme
- **Outlay:** **₹62,500 crore** | **Tenure:** 5 years, FY 2026-27 to FY 2030-31
- **Objective:** Succeed the PLI for Large Scale Electronics Manufacturing (**PLI-LSEM**, which ended 31 March 2026) — scale up output, deepen domestic value addition and build Indian brands.

**Key features**

1. Incentive on **eligible sales** of mobile phones manufactured in India at **differentiated rates of 2.25%–5%**.
2. **Additional incentive up to 1.5%** linked to **domestic sourcing** of key components and sub-assemblies — an explicit value-addition trigger absent in PLI 1.0.
3. Emphasis on supply-chain resilience, exports, and building **Indian brands** with domestic design/R&D and patents ("technological sovereignty").

**Projected outcomes:** ~**₹39 lakh crore** cumulative mobile phone production; ~**60,000 direct jobs**.

**Significance:** Moves the incentive design from pure assembly volume toward component depth — addressing the standing critique that PLI made India an assembly hub with thin value addition.
**Criticism:** Benefits still concentrate among a few large contract manufacturers; 60,000 direct jobs is modest against the outlay, and import content in components remains high in the near term.

### 5. Semicon 2.0 — *(new development on a previously covered item)*
*(GS3 — Science & Technology, indigenisation; Industrial policy)*

Covered earlier as **India Semiconductor Mission (ISM) 2.0**. **What is new:** the Union Cabinet formally approved **Semicon 2.0 on 15 July 2026** with an outlay of **₹1,27,500 crore** (MeitY).

- Built on **six pillars**: (i) chip **design** deepening — 105 startups already designing chips, focus on developing Indian IPs; (ii) manufacturing; (iii) incentives for **equipment, materials, chemicals and gases** makers and their R&D; (iv) strengthening **ATMP/OSAT** with advanced packaging; (v) **R&D** to move beyond current technology nodes via Indian and foreign research collaborations; (vi) **talent development**.
- Government expectation: ~**₹4 lakh crore** investment attracted and ~**₹2 lakh crore** of semiconductor production during the scheme period.
- Part of a larger Cabinet package on 15 July 2026 totalling **₹2,19,353 crore**, which also included the Paradeep–Haridaspur railway doubling (₹2,542 crore) and the Dangoaposi–Rajkharsawan fourth line (₹1,365 crore).

### 6. Gramodyam — NABARD rural entrepreneurship programme
*(GS3 — Inclusive growth, employment; GS2 — Statutory & regulatory bodies (NABARD))*

- **Nodal bodies:** **NABARD** (launched on its **45th Foundation Day, 13 July 2026**) in partnership with **NSDC**, under the aegis of the Ministry of Skill Development & Entrepreneurship; executed by the Institute for Industrial Development.
- **Type:** Institution-led programme (not a Cabinet-approved scheme)
- **Duration / target:** **3 years**, ~**4,000 rural entrepreneurs** across all States and UTs
- **Objective:** Build a rural enterprise ecosystem covering both farm-based and non-farm enterprises.

**Key features**

1. **Digital-first hybrid delivery** — online platforms, social media outreach, community mobilisation plus on-ground engagement.
2. End-to-end support: psychometric assessment and career counselling → Entrepreneurship Development Programmes (EDPs) → industry-specific skill training → mentoring and handholding.
3. **Credit linkage** — preparation of bankable **DPRs**, digital loan applications, credit through NABARD-supported **Regional Rural Banks** and other financial institutions.
4. Assistance with market linkages and access to other government schemes/incentives.

**Significance:** Addresses the classic gap between rural skilling and actual enterprise formation by bundling training with bankable-project preparation and RRB credit.
**Criticism:** 4,000 entrepreneurs over three years is very small relative to rural under-employment; success depends on RRB appetite for first-generation entrepreneur lending.

### 7. 'Seafarer-First' Initiative
*(GS2 — Welfare schemes for vulnerable sections, India & its neighbourhood / West Asia; GS3 — Internal & external security of sea lanes)*

- **Ministry:** Ministry of Ports, Shipping & Waterways — launched by Union Minister **Sarbananda Sonowal**, **July 2026**
- **Implementing agency:** **Directorate General of Shipping (DGS)**
- **Objective:** Safeguard Indian seafarers operating in high-risk waters — the **Persian Gulf, Strait of Hormuz and Gulf of Oman**.

**Key features**

1. **Real-time, vessel-by-vessel operational dashboard** tracking every Indian seafarer in the designated risk zone.
2. Appointment of **dedicated liaison officers** for coordination and rapid response.
3. Institutionalised welfare and repatriation support in the event of vessel detention or hostilities.

**Coverage:** Indian seafarers — India supplies a large share of the global maritime workforce, making this a significant labour-welfare and diaspora-protection measure.

**Significance:** Links maritime labour welfare to geopolitics of the Hormuz chokepoint; complements India's energy-security exposure in the Gulf.

### 8. Ayushman Bharat PM-JAY — West Bengal rollout
*(GS2 — Health, welfare schemes, Centre–State relations)*

- **Ministry:** Ministry of Health & Family Welfare / National Health Authority
- **Development:** West Bengal signed the MoU on **8 June 2026**, becoming the **36th State/UT** to implement AB PM-JAY; **rollout began July 2026** with integration of the State's **Swasthya Sathi** scheme.
- **Type:** Centrally Sponsored Scheme (60:40 for most States)
- **Coverage change:** Approximately **6 crore Swasthya Sathi cardholders** are brought under the PM-JAY umbrella, gaining **national portability** — cashless treatment at **36,000+ empanelled public and private hospitals** across India, irrespective of home State.

**Significance:** Completes near-universal geographic coverage of PM-JAY; portability is the key gain for West Bengal's large migrant workforce.
**Criticism / issues to watch:** Package-rate harmonisation between Swasthya Sathi (which was universal and State-funded) and PM-JAY (targeted at deprivation-criteria households), and the risk of coverage narrowing for households outside the SECC-based PM-JAY list.

## REPORTS & INDICES

### A. Investment Friendliness Index (IFI) 2026 — **NITI Aayog** *(first edition)*
*(GS2 — Cooperative & competitive federalism; GS3 — Investment models, industrial policy)*

- **Publishing body:** NITI Aayog | **Released:** **17 July 2026**
- **What it measures:** How effectively States and UTs create, enable and sustain a conducive investment environment. Covers **28 States and 8 UTs** across **84 indicators**.

**Eight pillars and weights:** Infrastructure **25%** · Business climate **20%** · Resources **15%** · Regulatory ease **12%** · Government policy **10%** · Financial health **7%** · Institutional environment **6%** · Environmental resilience **5%**.

**Key findings**

1. **Gujarat ranks 1st** among large States (score **56.6**), followed by **Maharashtra (53.7)** and **Tamil Nadu (53.3)**. Overall top five: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Goa, Odisha.
2. Among **hilly and North-Eastern States**, **Uttarakhand** is the best performer, followed by **Assam** and **Himachal Pradesh**. **Lakshadweep** is the weakest performer overall.
3. NITI Aayog positions the IFI as a **reform diagnostic tool** rather than a league table — the objective is to push States to act on identified pillar-level gaps to attract private investment.

**Exam angle:** Compare with the DPIIT **Business Reform Action Plan (BRAP)** rankings and the erstwhile World Bank *Ease of Doing Business*; note the federalism argument that States are now the principal site of investment-climate reform.

### B. Index of Services Production (**ISP**) — **MoSPI** *(new macro indicator)*
*(GS3 — Indian economy, growth & development; data & statistics)*

- **Publishing body:** Ministry of Statistics & Programme Implementation
- **First trial release:** **14 July 2026** (trial monthly indices for 2025-26 and for April 2026)
- **Base year:** **2024-25**
- **What it measures:** Short-term changes in output of the **formal services sector** — India's **first high-frequency indicator dedicated to services**, the counterpart of the IIP for industry.

**Key points**

1. **Coverage:** wholesale & retail trade, transport, banking, insurance, telecommunications, hotels & restaurants, real estate, professional/scientific/technical services, arts, entertainment & recreation.
2. **Methodology:** compiled as a **fixed-weight Laspeyres volume index**, weights derived from sectoral **GVA shares** in National Accounts Statistics; makes substantial use of **GST data**.
3. **Release cycle:** monthly, with a lag of about 60 days, on the 29th of each month (or next working day).
4. **Why it matters:** Services contribute **more than half of India's GDP** but had no monthly output tracker — a long-standing gap in India's statistical system flagged by successive statistical commissions.

**Exam angle:** Pair with the WPI/PPI base-year revision (2022-23) covered earlier — both are part of an ongoing modernisation of India's official statistics.
