## SCHEMES, PROGRAMMES & INITIATIVES

### 1. Coconut Promotion Scheme ⭐ *scheme of the week*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare; implemented by the **Coconut Development Board (CDB)**, HQ Kochi |
| **Announced** | Union Budget **2026–27** |
| **Type** | Central Sector Scheme |
| **Allocation** | Part of a broader **₹350 crore** package for high-value horticulture (coconut + cashew + cocoa). CDB's own outlay in 2025–26 was ₹147.21 crore |

**Objective:** Reverse the structural decline of India's coconut economy — ageing plantations (many palms over 60 years old), root wilt disease, low farm-level value addition — and push India towards global leadership in coconut and coir exports by the 2030s.

**Key features (4):**

- **Quality planting material** — large-scale seedling production through DSP (Demonstration cum Seed Production) Farms; ₹30 lakh for establishment, ₹1 lakh/ha/year maintenance; 2.41 lakh seedlings from 11 farms in 2025–26.
- **Area expansion** — subsidy of **₹56,000/ha** (max 2 ha per beneficiary); 2,175 ha added in 2025–26 across 9 states.
- **Replanting & rejuvenation** — up to **₹54,000/ha** over two years for removing and replacing old/diseased palms; 25,517 ha covered over five years at ₹130.46 crore.
- **PICCS (Productivity Improvement through Coconut-Based Cropping System)** — 100% subsidy at **₹42,000/ha**; 18,800 ha across 266 clusters, 48,696 farmers in 2025–26 (Kerala alone 8,089 ha). Plus integrated processing units and holistic crop-health management.

**Beneficiaries / coverage:** ~**30 million people** dependent on the sector, including ~**10 million farmers**, mostly small and marginal holders in coastal states. CDB operates across 22 States/UTs.

**Sector facts worth memorising:**

- India is **1st in world coconut production** (31.24% of output) and **3rd in area** (17.90%).
- Production 2024–25: **20,301 million nuts** from **2.19 million ha**; productivity **9,249 nuts/ha**.
- Largest area: **Kerala** (7.54 lakh ha) → Karnataka → Tamil Nadu → Andhra Pradesh. Highest productivity: **Andhra Pradesh** → West Bengal → Tamil Nadu. Highest production: **Tamil Nadu**.
- Exports: ₹25.3 crore (2001–02) → ₹3,236.83 crore (2021–22) → **₹7,038.35 crore (US$ 794.70 mn) in 2025–26**, a **62% jump** year-on-year. Top market: **USA**. Top export item: **activated carbon** (from coconut shell).

**Significance:** First dedicated budgetary line for coconut in years; targets the two binding constraints (planting stock and disease) rather than only price support.

**Criticism:**

- **Productivity paradox** — India leads in absolute production but lags badly in yield per hectare behind Sri Lanka and Malaysia; dwarf/hybrid adoption among smallholders is slow.
- **Root wilt** is an incurable phytoplasma-linked disease; replanting on infected soil needs long fallow periods, which the scheme design does not fully address.
- **Export basket skew** — over-dependence on activated carbon (an industrial, not food, product) ties earnings to industrial demand cycles rather than the premium food segment.
- **Copra MSP has historically failed as a price floor** when arrivals are high, because NAFED/NCCF procurement has been inconsistent.

**Companion item — MSP for Copra, 2026 season (Cabinet-approved):** Milling copra **₹12,027/quintal** (↑₹445); ball/edible copra **₹12,500/quintal** (↑₹400). Central Nodal Agencies: **NAFED and NCCF**.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-III — Agriculture (cropping patterns, MSP, food processing & value addition); GS-III Economy (exports).

### 2. Cocomitra & Kera Kaushal Kendra — CDB's new enterprise models

- **Nodal agency:** Coconut Development Board, Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare.
- **Cocomitra:** organises trained coconut tree climbers into **registered service units of 6–10 members** to deliver harvesting/plant-protection services to farmers on demand — converting an individual, informal occupation into a group enterprise.
- **Kera Kaushal Kendra:** **15-member handicraft production groups** working with coconut-based raw material; funding of **₹2.67 lakh per centre from 2026–27**.
- **Related existing CDB programmes:** *Friends of Coconut Tree (FoCT)* — trains unemployed youth in mechanical palm climbing (378 climbers trained in 2024–25); *Kera Suraksha Insurance* — accident cover for tree climbers, Neera technicians and hybridisation workers; *Neera Programme* — training for tapping the unfermented, zero-alcohol coconut inflorescence drink.
- **Significance:** addresses a real bottleneck — the collapse of the traditional climber workforce, which has left large numbers of palms unharvested in Kerala and Tamil Nadu.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-III — Agriculture, skill development, rural livelihoods; GS-II — welfare schemes for vulnerable occupational groups.

### 3. PARIVARTAN — vehicle scrappage and fleet renewal for Delhi-NCR

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Full form** | **P**rogramme for **A**ccelerated **R**enewal and **I**ncentivization of **V**ehicle **A**ssets for Reducing **T**ransport **A**ir Pollution and Network Emissions |
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | Ministry of Road Transport & Highways (MoRTH), implemented through the **National Capital Region Planning Board (NCRPB)** |
| **Approved / launched** | Union Cabinet approval **3 June 2026**; scheme launched **14 July 2026** |
| **Type** | Central Sector Scheme (with State tax concessions layered on) |
| **Outlay** | **₹9,585 crore** total, of which **₹5,041 crore** is central support |

**Objective:** Replace more than **2 lakh** BS-IV and older commercial trucks and buses registered in Delhi and the NCR districts of Haryana, Rajasthan and Uttar Pradesh with BS-VI-compliant diesel/CNG or electric vehicles, to cut transport-sector emissions in the NCR airshed.

**Key features (4):**

- Incentive of up to **₹2.56 lakh** for switching to an electric vehicle.
- Monthly **fuel vouchers of up to ₹4,800** for eligible replacements.
- Roughly **5% interest saving** on vehicle loans via tie-ups with banks/NBFCs.
- **10-year motor vehicle tax concession and registration-fee waiver** notified by Delhi, Haryana, Rajasthan and UP for eligible new vehicles.

**Uptake (the news this week):** As reported around 29 August 2026, the scheme had crossed **~1,300–1,450 registered beneficiaries**, with **42 banks/NBFCs** onboarded and **15 auto OEMs** partnered across Delhi-NCR. MoRTH expects registrations to cross **25,000 by October 2026**.

**Significance:** Moves India's vehicle scrappage policy from voluntary, notification-based (2021) to a **funded, airshed-targeted** replacement programme — the first of its kind concentrated on a single region's commercial fleet.

**Criticism:** Uptake so far is a tiny fraction of the 2 lakh target; the incentive package still leaves a large capital gap for small fleet owners; and NCR air quality is dominated by multiple sources (stubble, construction dust, industry), so freight-fleet renewal alone will not deliver proportionate AQI gains.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-III — Environment (air pollution, urban airsheds); GS-III Infrastructure (transport); GS-II — Centre–State cooperation on a shared airshed.

### 4. PRIP Scheme — second call for applications

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Full form** | **Promotion of Research and Innovation in Pharma-MedTech Sector (PRIP)** |
| **Ministry / nodal agency** | **Department of Pharmaceuticals**, Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers |
| **Launched** | **2023** |
| **Type** | Central Sector Scheme |
| **Outlay** | **₹5,000 crore** |

**Objective:** Move Indian pharma and medical devices up the innovation value chain — from generic volume manufacturing to patented, research-led products — by de-risking R&D for industry, startups and MSMEs.

**Key features (4):**

- Financial support of **up to ₹100 crore per project** for eligible industry players, startups and MSMEs.
- **Priority areas:** new medicines (including for orphan diseases and anti-microbial resistance), complex generics and biosimilars, and novel medical devices — AI/ML-based devices, software as a medical device, genetic-technology diagnostics, robotic surgical devices, telemedicine-enabled devices and innovative in-vitro diagnostics.
- Builds **shared research infrastructure** (Centres of Excellence at NIPERs) and strengthens the trained-talent pool.
- **Facilitation layer:** *MedTech Mitra*, *Patent Mitra* and the online PRIP platform for mentorship, regulatory hand-holding and commercialisation partnerships.

**New this week (announced ~28 August 2026):** The **second call for applications** was opened; the application window opens **by mid-September 2026**.

**Significance:** India is the world's third-largest pharmaceutical producer by volume but spends far below the global average on pharma R&D as a share of sales. PRIP is the first scheme to incentivise **R&D investment** rather than **production volume** — a deliberate contrast with the PLI scheme for bulk drugs and medical devices.

**Criticism:** Disbursement under the first call was slow; industry has flagged that ₹5,000 crore over the scheme period is small relative to what a single novel-drug programme costs, and that IP-sharing and milestone-linked release terms remain a deterrent for larger firms.

**Prelims trap:** **PRIP ≠ PLI.** PRIP = *Promotion of Research and Innovation in Pharma-MedTech*, Department of Pharmaceuticals, R&D-linked. PLI for bulk drugs/medical devices is production/sales-linked.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-III — Economy (industrial policy, innovation ecosystem); GS-II — Health (access to medicines); GS-III S&T.

### 5. PM SVANidhi — one year of the restructured scheme *(new development on a previously noted scheme)*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Full name** | PM Street Vendor's AtmaNirbhar Nidhi |
| **Ministry** | Ministry of Housing & Urban Affairs (MoHUA) |
| **Originally launched** | June 2020 (COVID-19 response) |
| **Restructuring approved** | Union Cabinet, **27 August 2025** — completed one year on **27 August 2026** |
| **Type** | Central Sector Scheme |
| **Restructured outlay** | **₹7,332 crore**; lending period extended to **31 March 2030** |

**Objective of the restructured phase:** Shift the scheme from a pure emergency micro-credit line to a full financial-inclusion and livelihood package for urban street vendors.

**Key features of the restructuring (4):**

- **Higher loan tranches:** 1st tranche raised from ₹10,000 → **₹15,000**; 2nd tranche from ₹20,000 → **₹25,000**; 3rd tranche unchanged at **₹50,000**.
- **UPI-linked RuPay credit card** for vendors who repay the second instalment on time — giving on-demand credit for business and emergency personal needs.
- **Digital-payment cashback of up to ₹1,600** on retail and wholesale transactions.
- **Geographic expansion** beyond statutory towns to **Census Towns and peri-urban areas**; plus social security linkage, capacity building and entrepreneurship support.

**Beneficiaries / coverage:** expected to benefit around **1.15 crore street vendors**, including **50 lakh new beneficiaries**.

**Significance:** One of the few schemes explicitly designed around the **urban informal sector**, a segment largely invisible in both rural welfare architecture and formal labour law. The credit-card link is an attempt to graduate vendors from scheme-dependent lending to mainstream credit.

**Criticism:** Repayment-linked graduation excludes the most precarious vendors; vending-zone identification and Town Vending Committees under the **Street Vendors (Protection of Livelihood and Regulation of Street Vending) Act, 2014** remain patchy, so many beneficiaries still lack legal vending rights even after getting loans.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-II — Welfare schemes, urban governance; GS-III — Financial inclusion, informal economy.

### 6. Bidesh Sikhyabruti Scholarship Scheme *(State: Odisha)*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **State / nodal department** | Government of Odisha (CM Mohan Charan Majhi); implemented under the **Mukhyamantri Medhabi Chhatra Protsahan Yojana (MMCPY)** umbrella |
| **Launched** | **26 August 2026** (applications 18–31 August 2026 for AY 2025–26 and 2026–27) |
| **Type** | State scheme |

**Objective:** Enable meritorious **Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe** students from Odisha to pursue postgraduate and PhD programmes at top-ranked foreign universities.

**Key features (4):**

- Scholarship of **up to ₹25 lakh per student per year**, covering tuition, accommodation and living expenses, international travel, medical insurance and incidentals.
- **50 students supported every year**, across streams and disciplines.
- Eligibility: SC/ST domicile of Odisha, annual family income **up to ₹12 lakh**, and unconditional admission to an institution ranked in the **QS World University Rankings top 200**.
- Applications through the **Odisha State Scholarship Portal** (scholarship.odisha.gov.in).

**Significance:** State-level analogue of the Centre's **National Overseas Scholarship** (Ministry of Social Justice & Empowerment) and the **National Overseas Scholarship for ST students** (Ministry of Tribal Affairs) — an example of competitive federalism in affirmative-action design, and useful as a GS-II example of States supplementing central SC/ST welfare.

**Criticism:** The QS top-200 filter plus the ₹12 lakh income ceiling narrows the pool sharply; 50 slots a year is small against Odisha's SC+ST population share (~40% of the State).

**Syllabus tag:** GS-II — Welfare schemes for vulnerable sections (SC/ST), education; GS-I — Social empowerment.

### 8. DoT–C-DOT End-to-End Optical Network Testbed

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Parties** | Department of Telecommunications (DoT) + **C-DOT (Centre for Development of Telematics)** |
| **Ministry** | Ministry of Communications |
| **Signed** | **27 August 2026**; five-year agreement |
| **Type** | Publicly funded (DoT) shared national facility |
| **Location** | C-DOT campus, New Delhi |

**Objective:** Bridge the gap between lab-stage optical-communication innovation and commercial deployment by giving Indian startups, industry, students and researchers access to a **real-world network testing environment**.

**Key features (3):**

- **Phase 1 (Years 1–2):** infrastructure — optical core, optical access, **quantum-security** components, test & measurement tools.
- **Phase 2 (Years 3–5):** operations, maintenance, technical support, skill development and educational outreach.
- Open as a **shared national resource**, not a captive government lab.

**Significance:** India's telecom infrastructure has historically depended on imported optical equipment; indigenising it (including quantum-secure components) is both an Aatmanirbhar Bharat and a supply-chain-security objective. Complements the PLI scheme for telecom and networking products.

**Criticism:** Government testbeds in India have a track record of **under-utilisation** because of complex access processes; without parallel **demand creation** (e.g. procurement preference for indigenous optical gear), validation alone will not produce commercial scale-up; and India's pipeline of optical-communication engineers is thin.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-III — S&T (indigenisation, quantum technologies), infrastructure (telecom); GS-II — Government policies for R&D.

## POLICY / REGULATORY CHANGE OF THE WEEK

### 9. Defence Export SOP and Open General Export Licence (OGEL) framework simplified

- **Ministry:** Ministry of Defence (Department of Defence Production).
- **What changed:** The Standard Operating Procedure for defence exports and the **OGEL** framework were simplified to reduce procedural burden on Indian defence exporters and widen their access to global markets.
- **What is an OGEL?** A **pre-approved class licence** that lets a company export specified defence items to pre-cleared destination countries **without seeking item-by-item permission** for each shipment — as opposed to the standard case-by-case export authorisation.
- **Context / target:** part of the push towards **₹50,000 crore in annual defence exports**. India's defence exports have risen from under ₹2,000 crore a decade ago to a record level in recent years, with the private sector accounting for a majority share.
- **Significance:** Export competitiveness in defence depends heavily on **delivery predictability**; licensing delays have been a repeatedly cited reason Indian firms lose contracts to competitors from countries with class-licence regimes.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-III — Defence manufacturing, indigenisation, exports; GS-II — India's defence diplomacy.

## REPORTS & INDICES

### A. India's Second Progress Report on Land Restoration — presented at **UNCCD COP17**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Publishing body** | Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (**MoEFCC**), presented at the **17th Conference of the Parties to the UN Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD COP17)**, Ulaanbaatar, **Mongolia**, 24 August 2026 |
| **What it measures** | Area of degraded land brought under restoration in India, 2011–2020, against the **Bonn Challenge** pledge |

**India's standing / headline numbers:**

- **21.76 million hectares** of degraded land restored between **2011 and 2020**.
- Generated approximately **1.22 billion person-days** of employment.
- India's Bonn Challenge pledge: **26 million ha by 2030** (enhanced from the initial 2015 commitment).
- Land under some degree of degradation in India: an estimated **96–120 million ha**.

**Key findings (3):**

1. Restoration is delivered not by a single programme but by **convergence** — Green India Mission (a NAPCC mission), **CAMPA**, **Nagar Van Yojana** (1,000 urban forests), watershed development under **PMKSY + MGNREGS**, and agroforestry under the **National Agroforestry Policy 2014**.
2. The **next reporting cycle (2021–2025)** will be prepared with the **ICFRE Centre of Excellence on Sustainable Land Management** and **IUCN**.
3. In a parallel statement, India pressed for **"diversified, adequate, predictable and sustained"** international finance for land restoration and drought resilience.

**Critical view:** The 21.76 mn ha figure counts area **brought under restoration** — i.e. where interventions were initiated — not area where ecological health has demonstrably recovered; restoration quality and permanence are far harder to verify than area. Restoration is spread across at least eight central ministries with **no single nodal agency**. And India has **not yet submitted a formal Land Degradation Neutrality (LDN) target** to the UNCCD, so the restoration data and the LDN framework remain parallel tracks.

**Prelims hooks:** UNCCD (est. **1994**, secretariat **Bonn**) is one of the three **Rio Conventions** with UNFCCC and CBD. **Bonn Challenge** launched **2011** (Germany + IUCN): 150 mn ha by 2020, 350 mn ha by 2030. **LDN** is the **SDG 15.3** target. COP16 was at **Riyadh, Saudi Arabia (2024)**; **COP17 at Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia (2026)**.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-III — Environment (land degradation, desertification, conservation); GS-II — International Relations (multilateral environmental agreements, climate finance).

### C. PMJDY at 12 years — Department of Financial Services performance data *(28 August 2026)*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Publishing body** | Department of Financial Services, Ministry of Finance |
| **What it measures** | Coverage and deposits under the **Pradhan Mantri Jan-Dhan Yojana**, launched **28 August 2014** |

**Key findings (4):**

- **59.09 crore** PMJDY accounts as on **19 August 2026** — up from 14.72 crore in 2015.
- Total deposits **₹3,16,514 crore** (₹3.17 lakh crore) — roughly a **20-fold rise** from ₹15,670 crore in March 2015.
- **Women hold 55.7%** (32.92 crore) of all Jan Dhan accounts.
- **77.8%** (45.95 crore) of accounts are in **rural and semi-urban** branches or Bank Mitra outlets.

**Significance:** PMJDY is the "J" of the **JAM trinity** (Jan Dhan–Aadhaar–Mobile) and the account layer on which almost all DBT delivery rests. The female-majority account share is one of the strongest single data points on gender gap closure in formal banking.

**Standard critique:** Account *ownership* has outpaced account *usage* — dormancy, zero-balance accounts and low credit uptake remain the measured gaps; the deposit-per-account figure (~₹5,360) is still modest.

**Syllabus tag:** GS-III — Financial inclusion, banking; GS-II — Governance (DBT, welfare delivery).

## MACRO / POLICY DATA POINTS OF THE WEEK

- **UPI completes 10 years (25 August 2026).** Annual transaction volume rose from **1.78 crore (FY 2016–17)** to **24,162 crore (FY 2025–26)** — a ~13,000-fold rise. **P2M = 63% of volume**; **P2P = 37% of volume but 71% of value**. **86% of P2M transactions are below ₹500.** UPI is live in **11 countries**, including Cambodia, Greece and the Maldives' 'Favara' system (linked June–July 2026). *Critique:* PhonePe + Google Pay together exceed **80%** of volume and NPCI's 30% market-share cap remains unenforced; **zero MDR since January 2020** strains smaller PSPs.
- **RBI USD–INR swap facility drew US$ 73 billion** in forex inflows over eleven weeks (reported 24 August 2026). A swap injects rupee liquidity while temporarily boosting reserves.
- **Sugar:** FRP for the **2026–27** season fixed at **₹365/quintal** (from ₹230 in 2016–17). Retail sugar rose from **₹48.18/kg (20 July 2026) to ₹55.70/kg (20 August 2026)** — a **15.6%** jump in a month, driven by **red rot** fungal disease and **top borer** infestation, festive demand and a global deficit of ~**33 lakh MT**.
- **Proposed Strategic Fuel Programme:** ~**28 MT crude + 9 MT LNG + 4 MT LPG** (~41 MT) over a decade, reported cost ~**US$ 42 billion**; **no dedicated cess**. Current position: SPR Phase I **5.33 MT** (Visakhapatnam, Mangaluru, Padur), Phase II **6.5 MT** approved but delayed (Chandikhol, Padur); LPG caverns ~**0.14 MT**; **zero** operational underground natural-gas storage. *Key distinction: regasification capacity ≠ strategic inventory.*
- **Shipping JV:** state-run refiners and the **Shipping Corporation of India** plan a **US$ 1.5–2 billion** JV to acquire **59 ships**, reducing dependence on foreign-flag vessels.

## PRELIMS POINTERS

- **CDB** is a statutory body under the **Coconut Development Board Act, 1979**; established **12 January 1981**; HQ **Kochi**; also the Export Promotion Council for all coconut products **except coir/husk products**.
- **Copra** = dried coconut kernel. **Milling copra** → oil extraction. **Ball/edible copra** → direct consumption and religious use. **Both are MSP crops.** **Neera** = unfermented, zero-alcohol coconut inflorescence sap — legally distinct from **toddy** (fermented).
- **ICC** = International Coconut Community, an inter-governmental organisation of coconut-producing nations; India is a member.
- **PARIVARTAN** is implemented through the **NCRPB**, not directly by MoRTH — a rare use of a regional planning board as an implementing agency.
- **OGEL** = pre-approved **class licence** for defence exports to pre-cleared destinations.
- **C-DOT** was set up under DoT; known for indigenous rural telephony switches in the 1990s; now works on 5G, optical and **quantum** communications.
- **CEIR** is the register behind **Sanchar Saathi**.
- **UNCCD (1994, Bonn)** — third Rio Convention alongside **UNFCCC** and **CBD**. **COP17: Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia, 2026.**
- **NPCI** is a not-for-profit owned by a consortium of banks under RBI guidance; **NIPL** is its international arm; **VPA** = Virtual Payment Address.
- **PMJDY** launch date: **28 August 2014**.
