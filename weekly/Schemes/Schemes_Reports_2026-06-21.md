## SCHEMES & PROGRAMMES

### 1. ECLGS 5.0 — Emergency Credit Line Guarantee Scheme (notably in the news)
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Indian economy, MSMEs, credit/banking, government intervention; GS2 — welfare/relief schemes

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Ministry of Finance** (Department of Financial Services); guarantee administered by the **National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company Ltd (NCGTC).**
- **Launched:** Union Cabinet approved **ECLGS 5.0 on 5 May 2026**; **prominently in the news this week** — RBI on **19 June 2026** relaxed bank capital norms (zero risk-weight on **75%** of the guaranteed portion) and uptake milestones were released.
- **Type:** Central Sector (credit-guarantee support).
- **Objective:** Provide collateral-free, guaranteed additional working capital to MSMEs, non-MSMEs and scheduled passenger airlines to absorb **short-term liquidity stress arising from the West Asia crisis.**
- **Key features:**
  - **Guarantee coverage: 100% for MSMEs; 90% for non-MSMEs and the airline sector.**
  - Additional credit up to **20% of peak Q4 FY26 working-capital utilisation, capped at ₹100 crore.**
  - Eligibility: borrowers with **standard accounts as of 31 March 2026.**
  - Uptake (as of **9 June 2026**): **1,06,549 guarantees** worth **₹48,484 crore**; ~**96% by number** went to MSMEs.
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** MSMEs, larger firms with working-capital limits, and scheduled airlines facing oil-price/forex shocks.
- **Significance:** Revives the COVID-era ECLGS template as a counter-cyclical liquidity backstop; RBI's risk-weight relief frees bank capital and pushes faster disbursal.
- **Criticism / issues:** Critics call it a **short-term lifeline, not structural reform** — adds debt to already-stressed balance sheets; benefits skew to firms with existing credit lines, leaving informal micro-units out.

### 2. Green Hydrogen Certification Scheme of India (GHCI) — Portal launched
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Energy security, renewables, climate commitments, S&T; GS2 — global standards/trade

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Ministry of New & Renewable Energy (MNRE)**, under the **National Green Hydrogen Mission**; certificates issued by the **Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE)** on third-party verification.
- **Launched:** **GHCI Portal launched 19 June 2026** by Union Minister **Pralhad Joshi** at the National Workshop on "Strengthening the National Green Hydrogen Mission." (Parent mission: National Green Hydrogen Mission, approved Jan 2023, outlay **₹19,744 crore**, target **5 MMT/year green hydrogen by 2030**.)
- **Type:** Central Sector (certification/standards framework).
- **Objective:** Create a transparent, traceable, credible certification system so Indian green hydrogen has market and **export** credibility.
- **Key features:**
  - **Strict emissions threshold: ≤ 2 kg CO₂e per kg of H₂** (annual average) — tighter than the EU's RFNBO cap of 3.38 kg.
  - Hydrogen must come from **electrolysis (renewables) or biomass conversion.**
  - **Four certificate types** — Concept, Facility-Level, Provisional, Final — with a standardised **MRV (Monitoring, Reporting, Verification)** framework; producers keep records for **5 years.**
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** Green-hydrogen producers, energy-intensive industries (steel, fertiliser, refining), and exporters.
- **Significance:** Gives "green" a measurable, certifiable definition — essential for premium pricing, carbon markets and EU/Japan/Korea export deals; strengthens India's bid to be a global green-H₂ hub.
- **Criticism / issues:** High compliance/MRV burden for early producers; cost of green hydrogen still far above grey hydrogen; certificate mutual-recognition with the EU/other importers not yet settled.

### 3. PM-JANMAN & Dharti Aaba (DAJGUA) — Tribal welfare drive in focus
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Welfare of STs/PVTGs, vulnerable sections, governance; GS3 — inclusive development, Left-Wing-Extremism context

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Ministry of Tribal Affairs** (multi-ministry convergence).
- **Launched:** **PM-JANMAN — 15 Nov 2023** (Janjatiya Gaurav Divas); **DAJGUA — 2 Oct 2024.** Both **notably back in the news this fortnight** in PIB/coverage of tribal-area saturation and LWE rehabilitation.
- **Type:** PM-JANMAN — Central Sector + Centrally Sponsored mix; DAJGUA — multi-ministry convergence mission.
- **Objective:** Saturate basic services (housing, water, power, roads, connectivity, health, education, livelihoods) in tribal and **Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Group (PVTG)** habitations.
- **Key features:**
  - **PM-JANMAN:** outlay **₹24,104 crore**; **11 interventions across 9 ministries**; targets **75 PVTG communities** in **18 States + 1 UT.**
  - **DAJGUA (Dharti Aaba Janjatiya Gram Utkarsh Abhiyan):** outlay **₹79,156 crore**; **63,843 tribal-majority villages**, **549 districts**, ~**5 crore tribal citizens**; **25 interventions via 17 ministries**; implementation through **March 2029.**
  - Named for **Birsa Munda** ("Dharti Aaba" — Father of the Earth).
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** PVTGs and tribal-majority villages nationwide.
- **Significance:** Largest-ever convergence push for tribal saturation; doubles as a development complement to internal-security/LWE rehabilitation.
- **Criticism / issues:** Saturation targets depend on **inter-ministerial coordination** and last-mile delivery in remote terrain; data on *actual* benefit-saturation (vs. awareness) still patchy; land/forest-rights frictions persist.

### 4. 'Priyadarshini' Free Bus Travel Scheme (Kerala)
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Welfare of women & transgender persons; State welfare schemes; gender & mobility

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Government of Kerala** (KSRTC — Kerala State Road Transport Corporation).
- **Launched:** **15 June 2026.**
- **Type:** State scheme.
- **Objective:** Improve women's and transgender persons' mobility and economic participation via free public-bus travel.
- **Key features:**
  - **Free travel for all women and transgender persons** — irrespective of income/age/status — in **3,125 "ordinary" KSRTC buses** (seven ordinary-service categories).
  - **No registration or documents:** a **zero-value 'Priyadarshini' ticket** is issued via the **Electronic Ticket Machine (ETM)** so usage is tracked and KSRTC is reimbursed.
  - AC, Superfast and City-Fast services are **outside** initial coverage.
  - One of the **five "Indira Guarantees"** of the Congress-led UDF.
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** Women and transgender passengers across Kerala.
- **Significance:** Joins the wave of women's free-transit schemes (e.g., Karnataka Shakti, Delhi, Tamil Nadu, Telangana) — framed as boosting workforce participation and safety.
- **Criticism / issues:** Recurring reimbursement burden on a cash-strapped KSRTC; concerns over crowding on ordinary services and the cost-vs-benefit of universal (non-targeted) free travel.

### 5. Breakfast & Milk Scheme (Telangana)
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Education, child nutrition, social-sector schemes; GS3 — food security/nutrition

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Government of Telangana** (School Education Dept.); executed via **Manna Trust.**
- **Launched:** **15 June 2026** (with school reopening after summer vacation).
- **Type:** State scheme.
- **Objective:** Improve **nutrition, attendance and learning outcomes** by adding a morning meal + milk on top of the midday meal.
- **Key features:**
  - Covers **~29 lakh students** from pre-primary up to **Class 12 / junior colleges** in government institutions (phased rollout).
  - Annual cost **~₹720 crore** (≈₹540 cr breakfast + ₹180 cr milk).
  - Weekly menu: **milk on Mon/Wed/Fri**, **ragi malt on Tue/Thu/Sat**; breakfast items include dosa, poori, millet idli, pongal, bonda.
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** ~29 lakh government-school and junior-college students statewide.
- **Significance:** Tackles morning hunger and classroom inattention; complements the centrally-aided **PM POSHAN** midday-meal scheme; pushes millet-based nutrition.
- **Criticism / issues:** Quality control, hygiene and timely delivery across thousands of schools; fiscal sustainability of a large recurring meal programme; dependence on a single implementing trust.

### 6. I4C — Grievance Redressal Mechanism (GRM) & Money Restoration Module (MRM)
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Internal security, cybercrime, money-laundering; GS2 — e-governance, citizen redressal

- **Ministry / nodal agency:** **Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C)** under the **Ministry of Home Affairs (MHA).**
- **Launched:** **c. 16–17 June 2026**, following a review of the **1930** cyber-fraud helpline chaired by Home Minister Amit Shah.
- **Type:** Central Sector (digital governance platforms).
- **Objective:** Speed up **refund of money lost to cyber fraud** and provide **redress for accounts wrongly frozen** during investigations.
- **Key features:**
  - **MRM (Money Restoration Module):** lets verified fraud victims apply for **refunds of frozen/recovered funds** — ~**94 lakh bank accounts** uploaded into the restoration/redressal framework.
  - **GRM (Grievance Redressal Mechanism):** lets account-holders seek **review of accounts frozen, lien-marked or seized** during cybercrime probes.
  - Establishes **direct coordination among police, banks and the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal (NCRP)**; integrated with helpline **1930.**
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** Victims of financial cyber fraud and holders of accounts caught in investigation freezes.
- **Significance:** Shifts the cybercrime response from *blocking* money to *restoring* it; reduces collateral damage to innocent account-holders.
- **Criticism / issues:** Restoration still hinges on tracing/freezing speed; risk of fresh disputes over who gets returned funds; volumes (lakhs of accounts) may strain verification capacity.

### B. UNICEF — Children & Climate-Risk Report (India findings)
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Vulnerable sections (children), social protection; GS3 — environment, climate vulnerability, disaster risk

- **Publishing body:** **UNICEF** — India findings highlighted **c. 16 June 2026.**
- **What it measures:** Children's exposure to overlapping climate and disaster hazards, and the social-protection cushion available to families.
- **India's standing / key findings:**
  - **~411.62 million children in India face at least two climate/disaster hazards.**
  - **Drought is the most common hazard** — **>410 million children (≈96%)** live in drought-exposed areas.
  - **~48% of under-15s have no access to social-protection** (cash transfers/safety nets) to absorb climate shocks — flagging a child-focused adaptation and social-security gap.
