## SCHEMES, PROGRAMMES & INITIATIVES

### 1. Swachhata Hi Seva (SHS) 2026 ⭐ *campaign of the week*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Nodal agencies** | **Department of Drinking Water & Sanitation (Ministry of Jal Shakti)** + **Department of Urban Development / MoHUA** — jointly |
| **Launched** | **17 September 2026**; runs to **17 October 2026** |
| **Parent mission** | Swachh Bharat Mission (launched 2 October 2014); SHS observed annually since **2017** |
| **Type** | Nationwide *Jan Andolan* campaign under a Centrally Sponsored Mission |
| **Theme 2026** | **"Swachhata Mein Sahbhag; Swachh Bharat, Viksit Bharat"** |

**Objective.** Shift sanitation from a service-delivery target to a shared-responsibility public movement, with emphasis on waste-to-wealth and dignity of sanitation workers.

**Key features**

- **Cleanliness Target Units (CTUs)** — transformation of neglected, high-footfall public spaces identified by citizens.
- **Swachh Paathshala** — structured youth and school engagement.
- **SafaiMitra Suraksha Evam Samman Shivir** — camps for sanitation workers (health screening, entitlement linkage, safety gear).
- **Swachhata se Samriddhi** — circular-economy and waste-to-wealth interventions.
- Nationwide collective *shramdaan* **"Ek Din, Ek Ghanta, Ek Saath"** on **1 October 2026**; special ULB drive on **Gandhi Jayanti, 2 October**.

**Coverage / status data (as of 17 September 2026).** Rural: **5.69 lakh+ ODF Plus villages**, **5.25 lakh+ ODF Plus Model**; **5.38 lakh** villages with solid-waste arrangements and **5.65 lakh** with liquid-waste systems; **12.22 crore** individual household toilets. Urban: **all 4,715 ULBs ODF**; **3,547 ODF+**; **1,191 ODF++**.

**Significance.** SHS is the behaviour-change arm of SBM — the part that statutory targets cannot deliver. **Criticism / caution:** ODF and ODF+ status is largely **self-declared by States with sample verification**, and slippage (toilet non-use, defunct assets) is documented; faecal sludge management (ODF++) still covers only about a quarter of ULBs; sanitation-worker safety remains the mission's weakest link despite NAMASTE.

> **GS tag:** GS2 — Government policies & interventions; Social sector/services. **GS3** — environment, waste management.

### 2. EPFO wage ceiling raised from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 per month

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | **Ministry of Labour & Employment** (proposal); approved by **Union Cabinet, 16 September 2026** |
| **Effective** | **17 September 2026** (Vishwakarma Jayanti) |
| **Statute** | Employees' Provident Funds & Miscellaneous Provisions Act, **1952** |
| **Last revision** | **September 2014** (₹6,500 → ₹15,000) — first revision in 12 years |

**Objective.** Widen mandatory social-security coverage in line with two decades of nominal wage growth and formalisation.

**Key features**

- Threshold for **mandatory EPFO coverage** raised to **₹25,000/month** basic wages.
- Brings **51 lakh+ additional employees** into mandatory PF, pension (EPS) and insurance (EDLI) coverage.
- Estimated **annual government outgo ≈ ₹11,339 crore** (against existing budgetary support of ≈ ₹10,250 crore); **five-year cost ≈ ₹56,696 crore**.
- Recommended by the **Expenditure Finance Committee** on 16 June 2026.

**Significance.** The ceiling had become badly dated — a large share of formal-sector workers earning ₹15,000–25,000 sat outside mandatory coverage. Raising it deepens the contributory social-security base and interacts with the labour-code architecture. **Criticism:** it raises employer wage-bill costs at the margin (potential disincentive to formal hiring in labour-intensive MSMEs); it does nothing for the vast **informal** workforce outside any employer-employee relationship; and the EPS-95 pension adequacy question (minimum ₹1,000/month) is untouched.

> **GS tag:** GS2 — Welfare schemes, mechanisms for social sector. **GS3** — employment, inclusive growth.

### 3. India Semiconductor Mission (ISM) 2.0 — "Semicon 2.0" formally opened at SEMICON India 2026

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | **Ministry of Electronics & IT (MeitY)** — ISM is an Independent Business Division under Digital India Corporation |
| **Cabinet approval** | **15 July 2026** · **formally launched by PM at SEMICON India 2026, 17 September 2026**, Yashobhoomi, New Delhi |
| **Type** | **Central Sector Scheme** |
| **Outlay** | **₹1,27,500 crore** (≈ **$13.5 billion**) — against ≈ $8 billion / ₹76,000 crore in Semicon 1.0 (2021) |
| **FY 2026-27 provision** | ₹1,000 crore |

**Objective.** Move India from assembly-and-packaging entry points to a full-stack semiconductor ecosystem — design, equipment, materials, fabrication, packaging, R&D and talent.

**Key features — the six pillars**

1. **Chip design** (full-stack Indian semiconductor IP)
2. **Equipment, machines and materials** (incl. specialty chemicals and gases)
3. **Fabs** — silicon, compound, memory, display, logic
4. **Advanced packaging / ATMP–OSAT** strengthening
5. **Applied R&D** — moving beyond the 28nm–110nm entry node
6. **Talent development** — scaling from ≈70,000 students trained on EDA tools across 320 universities

**Coverage.** **12 chip projects approved** to date under the Semicon India Programme; SEMICON India 2026 (17–19 September) drew **600+ exhibitors**, ≈300 international firms, delegates from 40+ countries.

**Significance.** Semiconductors are the pivot of India's strategic-autonomy and electronics-export push, and a China+1 / trusted-supply-chain play. **Criticism:** capital subsidies of this scale carry a high fiscal risk if fabs under-deliver; India still lacks the water, ultra-pure chemical and power reliability that leading-edge fabs demand; leading-edge nodes remain far away and the mission is, for now, a mature-node and packaging strategy.

> **GS tag:** GS3 — Indian economy; industrial policy; science & technology; indigenisation.

### 4. PM Vishwakarma — three years completed, 30 lakh registration target met

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Nodal ministry** | **Ministry of MSME**, implemented jointly with **MSDE** and **Department of Financial Services** |
| **Launched** | **17 September 2023** (Vishwakarma Jayanti) |
| **Type** | **Central Sector Scheme** |
| **Outlay / period** | **₹13,000 crore**, FY 2023-24 to FY 2027-28 |
| **Anniversary event** | **17 September 2026**, Dhono Dhanyo Auditorium, **Kolkata** |

**Objective.** End-to-end support to **18 traditional trades** of artisans and craftspeople working with hands and tools — from recognition to credit to market access.

**Key features**

- **PM Vishwakarma certificate and ID** — formal recognition.
- **Skill training** — basic (5–7 days) and advanced, with a **₹500/day stipend**.
- **Toolkit incentive of ₹15,000** (e-voucher).
- **Collateral-free Enterprise Development Loan** up to **₹3 lakh** in two tranches (₹1 lakh / 18 months; ₹2 lakh / 30 months) at a concessional **5% interest**.
- Digital-transaction incentive and marketing support.

**Three-year performance.** **30 lakh** artisans registered (target met); **24.50 lakh** given basic skill training; **18.15 lakh** toolkits delivered to doorsteps via **India Post**; **6.18 lakh** loans worth **₹5,297 crore**; **30,000+** artisans onboarded on e-commerce platforms; **12,000+** trained in AI tools for branding, design, packaging and marketing.

**Significance.** Targets the *kaarigar* economy that sits below the MSME registration threshold and outside PMEGP/MUDRA reach. **Criticism:** loan conversion is thin — **6.18 lakh of 30 lakh** registered (≈21%) have taken credit; trade-list rigidity excludes several artisanal occupations; and the scheme's poverty-reduction claims rest on registration numbers rather than measured income gains.

> **GS tag:** GS2 — Welfare schemes. **GS3** — MSME, employment, skill development.

### 5. Six-Month Certificate (Bridge) Course in Primary Teacher Education (PTE)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | **Ministry of Education** — designed under the **NCTE** framework, delivered through **NIOS** |
| **Launched** | **16 September 2026** by Union Education Minister **Pralhad Joshi** (took charge of Education on 25 July 2026) |
| **Legal trigger** | Supreme Court judgment of **8 April 2024**, *Devesh Sharma v. Union of India* (Civil Appeal No. 5068 of 2023) |

**Objective.** Give **in-service primary teachers holding only a B.Ed.** a certification pathway to lawfully teach Classes I–V, protecting appointments while raising primary-stage pedagogical competence.

**Key features**

- Eligibility: teachers appointed **28 June 2018 – 11 August 2023** to teach Classes I–V with a B.Ed. degree (appointments protected under the NCTE Gazette notification of 28 June 2018).
- Structure: **six theory courses + school-based practicum**; minimum **10 days online contact classes** and **20 days school-based experience**.
- Medium of instruction Hindi and English; the public theory examination may be written in any of the **22 Eighth Schedule languages**.

**Significance.** *Devesh Sharma* held that B.Ed. holders are not qualified for primary-stage teaching (a D.El.Ed. being the appropriate qualification) but allowed NCTE to devise a bridge. The course is the administrative resolution of a large employment-security problem and aligns with NEP 2020's foundational-stage emphasis. **Criticism:** a six-month distance-mode bridge is a thin substitute for a two-year D.El.Ed.; the deeper NCTE regulatory failure that allowed B.Ed. recruitment to primary posts is unaddressed.

> **GS tag:** GS2 — Education; issues relating to development and management of social sector; role of judiciary in policy.

### 6. India's first soil-carbon payments to farmers

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Agencies** | **ICAR / Department of Agricultural Research & Education (DARE)** — technical guidance; programme run by **Grow Indigo** (private) under its **'Aadi'** farmer carbon programme (began 2019) |
| **Event** | DBT released **16–17 September 2026** at **Punjab Agricultural University, Ludhiana**, by **Dr M. L. Jat**, Secretary (DARE) & Director General, ICAR |
| **Type** | Voluntary carbon-market payment (not a budgetary scheme) |

**Objective.** Convert verified soil-carbon sequestration and emission reduction on smallholder fields into a direct income stream.

**Key features**

- **₹2.9 crore+** disbursed by **Direct Benefit Transfer** to **2,550 smallholder farmers** in **Punjab and Haryana**.
- Covers ≈**30,000 acres** and **50,000+ carbon credits**; individual payouts **₹3,000–₹15,000** (average ≈ **₹11,478**), linked to *measured outcomes*, not acreage.
- Qualifying practices adopted 2019–2022: **Direct Seeded Rice (DSR)**, **reduced tillage**, **crop-residue management** (i.e., no stubble burning).

**Significance.** First operational demonstration in India that regenerative agriculture can be monetised at the smallholder level — directly relevant to the **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS), 2023** offset mechanism, to stubble-burning control in the NCR airshed, and to India's agriculture NDC. **Criticism:** voluntary-market credits face persistent **additionality, permanence and MRV** doubts; soil carbon is reversible if practices lapse; payouts are small relative to input costs; and aggregator-led models raise questions about how much of the credit value reaches the farmer.

> **GS tag:** GS3 — Agriculture, cropping patterns; environment & climate change; carbon markets.

### 7. Pradhan Mantri Bhartiya Janaushadhi Pariyojana (PMBJP) — basket widened beyond generics

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | **Department of Pharmaceuticals, Ministry of Chemicals & Fertilizers** |
| **Implementing agency** | **Pharmaceuticals & Medical Devices Bureau of India (PMBI)** (formerly BPPI) |
| **Launched** | Jan Aushadhi campaign **2008**; renamed **PMBJP in November 2016** |
| **Type** | **Central Sector Scheme** |
| **New development** | PIB backgrounder (**19 September 2026**) confirms expansion from medicines into **surgicals, consumables and medical devices** |

**Objective.** Quality generic medicines and now supportive-care products at prices far below branded equivalents, through a dedicated retail network.

**Key features**

- Product basket: **2,110 medicines + 315 surgicals, consumables and devices** across **29 therapeutic categories** (anti-infectives, anti-diabetics, cardiovascular, anti-cancer and others).
- **19,502 Jan Aushadhi Kendras (JAKs)** operational (as of April 2026).
- Prices typically **~50%** and in some cases **80–90%** below branded market prices.
- New coverage arcs: infancy, post-partum recovery, routine monitoring and elderly care.

**Significance.** Out-of-pocket expenditure remains the dominant mode of health financing in India, and medicines are its largest component; the device/consumable expansion addresses chronic-care and elder-care costs that PM-JAY (hospitalisation-focused) does not touch. **Criticism:** quality-assurance capacity and consumer trust in generics remain the binding constraints; Kendra viability depends on margins and supply reliability; district-level distribution is uneven.

> **GS tag:** GS2 — Health; issues relating to poverty and hunger; government policies.

### 8. GOBARdhan — operational guidelines notified *(new development on a previously covered scheme)*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | Guidelines issued by the **Ministry of Petroleum & Natural Gas**, dated **15 September 2026** |
| **Scheme** | **GOBARdhan — National Unified Scheme for Compressed Biogas**, Cabinet-approved with **₹23,731 crore** outlay (covered in the 9 August 2026 tracker) |
| **Period** | **2026-27 to 2035-36** |

**What is new this week**

- **Assured offtake of up to 100%** of a producer's CBG output.
- A **government-backed administered price** guaranteed for **at least 10 years**.
- **Capital assistance** reimbursing up to **50% of eligible expenditure** on feedstock-aggregation and organic-manure value-addition machinery, capped at **₹0.75 crore per TPD**, released against milestones (approvals, first commercial sale of CBG, subsequent plant performance).
- **SATAT** (Sustainable Alternative Towards Affordable Transportation, 2018) is **subsumed from 1 September 2026**; committed liabilities under SATAT will continue to be honoured.

**Significance.** Offtake and price risk — not capital cost — were the binding constraints that stalled SATAT's CBG pipeline; a 10-year administered price makes projects bankable. **Criticism:** an administered price creates a long-dated contingent liability and, if set above market, a subsidy the OMCs ultimately carry; feedstock aggregation logistics remain the hardest unsolved problem.

> **GS tag:** GS3 — Energy; environment; waste-to-wealth; circular economy.

## STATE SCHEMES & POLICY

### 9. Maharashtra Women Farmers Empowerment Act, 2026 — administrative machinery operationalised

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **State** | **Maharashtra** — India's **first** dedicated law recognising women as farmers in their own right |
| **Status** | Governor's assent; published in the Maharashtra Government Gazette **22 July 2026**; implementation machinery notified **mid-September 2026** |
| **Department** | Department of Agriculture, Government of Maharashtra |

**Key features**

- Recognises women engaged in agricultural work **irrespective of land ownership** — decoupling "farmer" status from land title.
- **Women Farmer Certificate** as a single-window enabler for subsidies, institutional credit, insurance and market access.
- **Digital registry** of women farmers; a dedicated **fund**; a **State Women Farmers Empowerment Cell**; designated officers at district and sub-district level.
- **State-level Monitoring Committee** chaired by the **Chief Secretary**; **Governing Council** chaired by the **Chief Minister**.
- Minimum **30% share reserved for women** across all Agriculture Department schemes.

**Significance.** Roughly two-thirds of agricultural labour in India is performed by women, but land titles — the gateway to PM-KISAN, KCC, crop insurance and procurement — sit overwhelmingly with men. Statutory decoupling of farmer identity from land title is a structural, not cosmetic, reform. **Criticism:** certificate issuance depends on district revenue and agriculture machinery; without inheritance-law enforcement (Hindu Succession Act, 2005 amendment) the underlying asset gap remains.

> **GS tag:** GS1 — Role of women. **GS2** — Government policies; vulnerable sections. **GS3** — Agriculture, land reforms.

### A. India Electric Mobility Index (IEMI) 2025 — **NITI Aayog** ⭐ *report of the week*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Publisher** | **NITI Aayog** |
| **Released** | **17 September 2026** — **second edition** (first edition: IEMI 2024, released August 2025) |
| **What it measures** | Progress of States and UTs on road-transport electrification — **EV adoption, charging infrastructure, and research & innovation** |

**Key findings**

- **Delhi tops with a score of 84** (up from 77 in the previous edition), followed by **Maharashtra (78)**, **Karnataka (73)**, **Chandigarh (71)** and **Goa (65)**.
- Among **Large States**, **Maharashtra** and **Karnataka** are the top performers.
- Among **Hilly and North-Eastern States**, **Manipur leads with 46**.
- **Median score across States/UTs rose from 36 (2024) to 40 (2025)** — the distribution is improving, but the gap between leaders and laggards widened (top score rose 77 → 84).

**Significance.** A competitive-federalism instrument for the transport-sector decarbonisation leg of the net-zero-2070 pathway; complements PM E-DRIVE and the FAME successor architecture. **Caution:** small UTs with compact geographies score structurally well on charging density; the index does not capture grid-emission intensity, so a high EV score in a coal-heavy grid State overstates actual emission gains.

> **GS tag:** GS3 — Environment; energy; infrastructure. **GS2** — Cooperative and competitive federalism.

### B. Global Gender Gap Report 2026 — **World Economic Forum** · India **131st of 145**

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Publisher** | **World Economic Forum (WEF)**, Geneva |
| **Released** | **16 September 2026** — **20th edition**; the longest-standing index of its kind (since 2006) |
| **What it measures** | Share of the gender gap **closed** (100% = full parity) across **four sub-indices**: Economic Participation & Opportunity, Educational Attainment, Health & Survival, Political Empowerment |
| **Coverage** | **145 economies**; long-term trends tracked on a constant sample of **97 economies** |

**India's standing**

- **Rank 131 of 145** — unchanged from the previous edition; only **14 countries rank below India** (bottom three: **Chad, Iran, Pakistan**).
- **Overall parity: 64.5%** (score improved slightly; rank static). Gain of **+4.3 percentage points since 2006**, driven mainly by education.
- **Economic Participation & Opportunity: 41.2%** — up 0.5 pp year-on-year but **3.5 pp below India's 2013 peak**. Professional & technical workers parity doubled from 26.6% (2006) to **49.9%**; legislators/senior officials/managers **13.1%**; labour-force participation parity **44.1%**.
- **Educational Attainment: 96.6%** (down 0.5 pp).
- **Health & Survival: 95.6%** (improved); sex ratio at birth still ≈1 pp below its 2006 level.
- **Political Empowerment: 24.5%** — India's **best-ranked** sub-index at **67th globally**. Parliament gap closed 16.1%; ministerial parity **5.9%** (against a 2019 peak of 30%); head-of-state indicator fell from 72.3% to **40.7%** since 2021.

**Global findings**

- Global gap **69.2% closed**, up 0.4 pp; **full parity is 120 years away**.
- **Iceland first for the 17th consecutive year (93%)** — the only country above 90% — then **Finland (87.2%)** and **Norway (85.7%)**; **Namibia 4th**; **Australia enters the top 10 for the first time**.
- **Political empowerment is the clearest reversal**: now below its 2016 level; the share of economies led by a woman peaked at 27% in 2022 and has fallen back to 2016 levels.
- Women are **19.1% of CEOs**; only **1 in 10** ministerial portfolios held by women is high-influence; women are **19.3% of AI engineers**.
- **Southern Asia ranks 7th of 8 regions (64.5%)** and is the **lowest-scoring region on economic participation (40.9%)**.

**Caution for answers.** The GGGI measures **gaps between men and women, not absolute levels** — a poor country with low male and female outcomes can outrank a richer one. India has formally disputed some parameters in the past. Use alongside NFHS-5, PLFS female LFPR and the Gender Social Norms Index.

> **GS tag:** GS1 — Role of women, social empowerment. **GS2** — Issues relating to women; international indices. **GS3** — Female labour-force participation.

### C. Project Cheetah — Four-Year Status Review (Kuno National Park)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Publisher** | **Kuno National Park / MoEFCC — NTCA** (Project Cheetah) |
| **Released** | **17 September 2026** — four years since the first translocation (**17 September 2022**, Namibia → Kuno, Madhya Pradesh) |

**Key findings**

- Total cheetah population in India: **52** — **49 at Kuno National Park**, **3 at Gandhi Sagar Sanctuary** (MP). **32 are India-born**.
- At Kuno: **17 free-ranging** (10 males, 7 females); **32 in Soft Release Bomas** (5 males, 8 females, 19 cubs under one year).
- Of the original **8 Namibian** cheetahs, 3 survive, plus 22 India-born offspring; of the **12 South African**, 8 survive plus 10 India-born offspring. A further **9 arrived from Botswana on 28 February 2026**.
- The review frames the programme as entering a **"phase of establishment"** — explicitly describing learning, setbacks, adaptation and recovery rather than unbroken success.
- Post-review: India-born female **KGP12** gave birth to **four cubs** at Kuno (18 September 2026).

**Significance.** The world's first intercontinental large-carnivore translocation. Second-generation India-born births are the key biological marker of a self-sustaining population. **Criticism:** high early adult mortality; a large share of animals still in **soft-release bomas** rather than truly wild; Kuno's prey base and carrying capacity remain contested; the long-pending **Kuno lion translocation** order of the Supreme Court remains unimplemented.

> **GS tag:** GS3 — Conservation, biodiversity, environment. **GS2** — Judiciary and environmental governance.

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|

| **In news** | **19 September 2026** |
