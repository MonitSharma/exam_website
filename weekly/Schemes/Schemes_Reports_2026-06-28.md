## SCHEMES & PROGRAMMES

### 1. Delhi Building & Construction Workers Health Scheme
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Welfare schemes for vulnerable sections; mechanisms for welfare of labour · GS3 — Unorganised-sector labour, social security

- **Nodal agency:** **Government of NCT of Delhi** — Labour Department, via the **Delhi Building & Other Construction Workers (BOCW) Welfare Board**; cleared by the State Cabinet chaired by CM **Rekha Gupta**.
- **Launched:** Cabinet approval **23 June 2026**.
- **Type:** State scheme (BOCW cess-funded welfare).
- **Objective:** Provide **cashless health cover** to registered construction workers — among the most accident-prone, uninsured segments of the unorganised workforce — and their families.
- **Key features:**
  - **Cashless treatment** at empanelled hospitals — up to **₹2 lakh per individual worker** and up to **₹10 lakh per family**.
  - Covers **spouse, children and parents**; free OPD/IPD, diagnostics, lab, emergency and referral services.
  - **Annual health check-ups** for registered workers and spouses; **mobile medical units** deployed at construction sites and worker-dense areas.
  - Estimated annual spend **~₹200 crore**.
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** ~**2.70 lakh registered construction workers**; nearly **10 lakh** beneficiaries including families.
- **Significance + criticism:** Operationalises BOCW cess funds — long criticised for under-utilisation — into a concrete health entitlement; complements AB-PMJAY. **Criticism:** depends on workers being *registered* with the Board (a large share of the migrant/informal construction workforce is not), so coverage hinges on enrolment drives; sustainability of the cess corpus and quality of empanelled hospitals will be tested.

### 2. Thaaimaaman Thanga Mothira Thittam (Gold Ring Scheme) — Tamil Nadu
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Welfare of women & children; State health schemes; issues relating to development of social sector

- **Nodal agency:** **Government of Tamil Nadu** (TVK-led govt) — Health & Family Welfare Department.
- **Launched:** Government Order issued **23 June 2026**; benefit applies to babies born from **22 June 2026**; formal launch slated for **15 September** (C.N. Annadurai birth anniversary).
- **Type:** State scheme.
- **Objective:** Encourage **institutional deliveries in government hospitals** by gifting a **1-gram gold ring** to every child born there — rooted in the Tamil custom of *"Thaai Maaman Seer"* (a maternal uncle's gift to a newborn).
- **Key features:**
  - **One-gram gold ring** to every newborn in a government hospital; parents must be **permanent residents of Tamil Nadu**.
  - Annual outlay earmarked at **₹755.83 crore**.
  - Positioned as both a welfare gift and a nudge to shift deliveries from private to public facilities.
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** All eligible newborns in TN government hospitals.
- **Significance + criticism:** Joins a wave of high-visibility maternal/child cash-and-kind transfers; aimed at boosting institutional-delivery share and public-hospital footfall. **Criticism:** large recurring fiscal commitment tied to volatile gold prices; questions over whether a one-time gift meaningfully improves maternal/neonatal *outcomes* versus strengthening hospital infrastructure; risk of being read as a poll-promise giveaway.

### 3. Nirbhay Chetna — Grassroots Gender-Sensitisation of Male Elected Representatives
**Syllabus tag:** GS1 — Role of women & women's organisations · GS2 — Issues relating to women; local self-government; government policies for vulnerable sections

- **Nodal agency:** **Ministry of Panchayati Raj**, under the **Nirbhaya Fund**; training module developed by **Transform Rural India**.
- **Launched:** Three-day national **Training of Trainers (ToT)** held **17–19 June 2026**, New Delhi (rollout of the module). Part of the broader **Nirbhay Raho** initiative launched **11 March 2026**.
- **Type:** Central Sector initiative (Nirbhaya Fund-supported).
- **Objective:** Sensitise **male elected representatives** in Panchayati Raj Institutions on women's safety, rights, gender equality and gender-responsive governance at the grassroots.
- **Key features:**
  - Builds a cadre of **~28,500 Master Trainers** at State/district/block levels.
  - Targets outreach to **over 17.5 lakh male elected representatives** nationwide — described as the **world's largest** such gender-sensitisation drive.
  - Sits alongside the other two **Nirbhay Raho** components — **Nirbhay Netri** (capacity-building/legal awareness for elected *women* representatives) and **Nirbhay Drishti** (CCTV in strategic rural locations).
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** Elected male PRI representatives across India; indirectly, rural women.
- **Significance + criticism:** Targets the *demand-side* of gender norms by working with male power-holders, not just women — an unusual design. **Criticism:** sensitisation programmes are notoriously hard to convert into behaviour change; outcome measurement and follow-through beyond a single training are the key risks.

### 4. National AI Skilling Initiative for the Creative ("Orange") Economy
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Skill development; IT & digital economy; growth & employment · GS2 — Government policies (PPP in skilling)

- **Nodal agency:** **Ministry of Information & Broadcasting**, implemented through the **Indian Institute of Creative Technologies (IICT)**, in partnership with **Google & YouTube**; announced by Union Minister **Ashwini Vaishnaw** (notably in the news **26 June 2026**).
- **Launched:** Phase 1 running **23 March – 30 June 2026**; Phase 2 **July–December 2026**.
- **Type:** Central scheme via **public–private partnership** (free of cost to learners).
- **Objective:** Build AI skills across media, animation, VFX, gaming, comics and digital content — strengthening India's **"Orange Economy"** (creative economy).
- **Key features:**
  - **15,000 scholarships** for students, creators, media professionals and developers.
  - Phase 1 — foundational AI (AI Essentials, Prompting Essentials, Intro to Generative AI, Generative AI Leader) via Google Career Certificates / Google Cloud paths.
  - Phase 2 — advanced, hands-on creative-industry training (storytelling, YouTube best practices, tools like **Gemini, Veo, Vertex AI**).
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** 15,000 trainees from the creative & media sectors.
- **Significance + criticism:** Frames the creative economy as a formal skilling and jobs frontier and embeds AI tooling early. **Criticism:** heavy reliance on a single Big-Tech stack raises vendor lock-in and curriculum-neutrality questions; 15,000 is small relative to the sector; durability of "free" access after the pilot is unclear.

### 5. Jewar Electronics / PCB Manufacturing Projects (Uttar Pradesh)
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Industrial policy; electronics manufacturing; import substitution; infrastructure & investment

- **Nodal agency:** **Ministry of Electronics & IT (MeitY)** + **Government of Uttar Pradesh**; foundation stones laid jointly by Union Minister **Ashwini Vaishnaw** and CM **Yogi Adityanath**.
- **Launched:** Foundation-stone ceremony **27 June 2026**, Yamuna City, **Jewar** (Gautam Buddha Nagar).
- **Type:** Industrial investment under the electronics-manufacturing push (ESDM ecosystem; complements ISM 2.0).
- **Objective:** Build domestic capacity in **advanced printed circuit boards (PCBs)** and components, cutting import dependence.
- **Key features:**
  - **Two projects, ~₹6,750 crore** combined — **ASCENT-K Circuit** (JV with Korea's KCC, **₹3,250 cr**, high-density multi-layer PCBs of up to ~20–22 layers) and **AMBER Enterprises** (**₹3,500 cr**, critical HVAC components & PCB assemblies).
  - Pitched as making Jewar a **"Silicon Valley of North India"**; ~**3,000 jobs** expected.
  - Targets the **~₹40,000 crore/yr** PCB import bill for substitution, aiding the Balance of Payments.
- **Beneficiaries / coverage:** Regional employment; domestic electronics supply chain.
- **Significance + criticism:** Deepens electronics value-chain localisation beyond assembly into components/PCBs. **Criticism:** PCBs and components still depend on imported raw materials and IP; concentration of incentives and land in a few hubs; jobs created are modest relative to the headline investment.

## REPORTS & INDICES

### A. World Bank — *Global Economic Prospects*, June 2026
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Indian economy, growth, mobilisation of resources

- **Publishing body:** **World Bank Group** (flagship half-yearly report).
- **What it measures:** Global and country-wise growth outlook and risks.
- **India's standing:** **Fastest-growing major economy in 2026.**
- **Key findings:**
  - India's growth projected to **moderate from ~7.4% (2025) to 6.6% (2026)** — driven by resilient household consumption, strong public investment, tax-reform effects and lower interest rates.
  - **South Asia** growth seen easing to **5.6% in 2026** (from 5.9% in 2025), with India the regional bright spot.
  - Exports face headwinds from higher US tariffs, but key export segments largely exempt; strong demand from other markets to partly offset.

### B. *Realising Rights: A Handbook of Welfare in India* — Azim Premji University
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Welfare schemes; governance; issues relating to development & management of the social sector

- **Publishing body:** **Centre for the Study of the Indian Economy (CSIE), Azim Premji University**; released **26 June 2026**, India Habitat Centre, New Delhi.
- **What it measures:** A stock-take of India's welfare architecture — achievements, gaps and the future of the welfare model (27 authors, 18 chapters).
- **India's standing / framing:** Flags a conceptual shift in Indian welfare **from "rights-holders" to "beneficiaries"** — i.e., from entitlement-based design (NREGA, NFSA, RTE-type) toward discretionary, scheme/transfer-based delivery.
- **Key findings:**
  - Welfare coverage has expanded, but **design is drifting from rights-based guarantees to targeted benefit transfers**, raising accountability questions.
  - Highlights persistent **gaps in delivery, exclusion errors and quality** across social-sector programmes.
  - Useful GS2 ammunition on the **rights vs. benefits** debate in welfare design.
