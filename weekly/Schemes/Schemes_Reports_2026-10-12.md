## SCHEMES, PROGRAMMES & INITIATIVES

### 1. Green Energy Corridor Phase-III (GEC-III) ⭐ *scheme of the week*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | **Ministry of New & Renewable Energy (MNRE)**, the ministry that runs the GEC scheme |
| **Approved** | **Union Cabinet, 30 September 2026** |
| **Parent scheme** | Green Energy Corridor, first approved by the **CCEA in 2015** |
| **Type** | Central scheme with **Central Financial Assistance (CFA)** to States. Intra-State Transmission Systems (InSTS) are built by State Transmission Utilities |
| **Outlay** | **₹1,86,405 crore**: InSTS **₹1,36,378 crore** + Battery Energy Storage **₹50,000 crore** |
| **Central support** | **₹54,082 crore** CFA |
| **Timeline** | Completion by **FY 2032-33** |

**Objective.** Strengthen intra-state grids so that up to **135 GW** of renewable energy can be evacuated without curtailment. This supports the target of **900 GW non-fossil installed capacity by 2035**.

**Key features**

- **First GEC phase with storage built in**: **50 GWh BESS**, placed at RE-generator ends or other key nodes. It tackles intermittency, congestion, peak-hour curtailment and demand in non-solar hours.
- **CFA offsets intra-state transmission charges**, so the cost of the new grid does not land fully on State consumer tariffs.
- **Procurement**: greenfield InSTS through **Tariff-Based Competitive Bidding (TBCB)** on a **Build-Own-Operate-Maintain (BOOM)** model. Brownfield upgrades are done on a **cost-plus** basis.
- **Track record**: about **9,856 ckm** of intra-state lines and **24,300 MVA** of substation capacity have been commissioned under GEC so far.

**Significance.** Transmission, not generation, is now India's binding constraint on renewables. Curtailment and stranded solar parks (for example in Rajasthan) come from the grid lagging behind capacity addition. Putting BESS inside a transmission scheme is a structural shift: the scheme now pays for firming power as well as moving it. **Criticism / caution:** GEC-I and II ran years behind schedule because of land and right-of-way problems and weak STU finances. TBCB at State level is still new. The large BESS component depends on domestic cell manufacturing (PLI-ACC), which has lagged.

> **GS tag:** GS3: Infrastructure (energy); Conservation & climate (renewables). Prelims: GEC, BESS, TBCB/BOOM, 900 GW-by-2035 target.

### 2. Integrated Transport & Logistics Authority (ITLA)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Approved** | **Union Cabinet, 6 October 2026** |
| **Legal form** | **Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)**. It is **not a statutory authority** |
| **Type** | Institutional reform, the apex body for transport and logistics planning |
| **Linked frameworks** | **PM GatiShakti National Master Plan (2021)**, **National Logistics Policy (2022)** |

**Objective.** Replace siloed, ministry-by-ministry transport planning with one integrated, data-driven planning and appraisal body, in order to cut logistics costs and raise competitiveness.

**Key features**

- Prepares a **National Transport Master Plan** with a horizon of **10+ years**. It covers **8 domains**: roads, railways, ports and shipping, civil aviation, inland waterways, coastal shipping, urban mobility and logistics.
- Reviews the ~5-year sectoral plans and annual plans of the transport ministries to check they fit the master plan.
- Does **technical appraisal, monitoring and post-implementation impact assessment** of central infrastructure projects worth **₹500 crore or more**. **Financial appraisal stays with existing bodies** (EFC/PIB).
- Builds a **National Transport Data Repository (NTDR)** that pools **GSTN e-way bill, FASTag, VAHAN, GPS and urban-traffic data** for freight-flow and origin-destination analytics. It will also advise on updating the NLP 2022.

**Significance.** It implements, after more than a decade, a central idea of the **Rakesh Mohan-led National Transport Development Policy Committee (2014)**: integrated multimodal planning. For context, **NCAER-DPIIT (2023)** put logistics costs at **7.8–8.9% of GDP**, and India ranked **38th of 139** on the World Bank **LPI 2023**. **Criticism:** an SPV without statutory backing may not be able to bind powerful line ministries. Its role overlaps with GatiShakti's Network Planning Group, PRAGATI and MoSPI's project monitoring. Pooling e-way-bill, FASTag and GPS data raises **DPDP Act, 2023** privacy and purpose-limitation questions.

> **GS tag:** GS3: Infrastructure; Investment models; Economic planning. GS2: Government policies & institutions.

### 3. SME Growth Fund (SGF)

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | **Ministry of Finance** (as reported under the PIB header) |
| **Approved** | **Union Cabinet, 6 October 2026** |
| **Origin** | **Para 28, Union Budget 2026-27** |
| **Type** | Government commitment of **₹10,000 crore** to an **Alternative Investment Fund (AIF)** under **SEBI (AIF) Regulations, 2012** |

**Objective.** Fill the **growth-stage equity gap** for small and medium firms, the "missing middle". Existing state-backed funds mostly target start-ups and micro units.

**Key features**

- **Direct growth-equity** investment in high-potential SMEs with proven viability and room to scale.
- **Most of the money goes to manufacturing-focused small and medium enterprises**, with attention to **industrial clusters in Tier-II and Tier-III cities**.
- Eligible uses: capacity expansion, technology upgrades, exports, **global value chain integration** and **acquisitions**.
- Sits alongside the **Fund of Funds for Startups (2016, SIDBI, ₹10,000 crore)** and the **Self-Reliant India Fund (2020, NSIC Venture Capital, ₹10,000 crore corpus)**.

**Context numbers.** MSMEs account for **~31% of GDP**, **~35% of manufacturing output** and **~49% of exports** (Economic Survey 2025-26). The formal credit gap was estimated at **₹20–25 lakh crore** by the **U.K. Sinha Committee (RBI, 2019)**.

**Significance.** It responds to the Economic Survey 2018-19 diagnosis ("nourishing dwarfs to become giants") that Indian firms stay small. **Criticism:** the release gives no details on fund manager, tenure, ticket size or private co-investment. The SRI Fund's slow deployment is a warning. Equity does not fix the bigger MSME problems of **delayed payments** and working-capital credit.

> **GS tag:** GS3: Indian economy (MSMEs, mobilisation of resources, industrial policy).

### 4. Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses: one-year review *(new scheme for this tracker)*

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | **Ministry of Agriculture & Farmers Welfare** |
| **Approved / launched** | Cabinet **1 October 2025**; launched **11 October 2025** (with PMDDKY) |
| **Type** | Centrally Sponsored Mission |
| **Outlay / tenure** | **₹11,440 crore**, **2025-26 to 2030-31** |
| **Focus crops** | **Tur (arhar), urad, masoor** |

**Objective.** Make India self-reliant in pulses by **2030-31**: about **350 lakh tonnes** production and **1,130 kg/ha** yield (government targets).

**Key features**

- **Cluster approach in ~489 districts**, adding **35 lakh ha** of pulse area, mainly **rice-fallow** land, to reach **310 lakh ha**.
- **Seeds:** **87.5 lakh free seed kits** and **126 lakh quintals** of certified seed over six years.
- **Assured procurement:** **100% of tur, urad and masoor** offered by registered farmers bought at MSP by **NAFED and NCCF** for 2025-26 to 2028-29.
- **Processing:** **1,000 pulse-processing units (dal mills)**.

**Year-one status (as reported in this week's PIB-based coverage).** Production in 2025-26 was **274.09 lakh tonnes** on **286.46 lakh ha** at **957 kg/ha** (3rd Advance Estimates). Procurement rose from **18.40 to 31.41 lakh tonnes**. **528 of 1,000 dal mills** have been allocated. Only about **10.6%** of the rice-fallow seed-kit target was distributed in Rabi 2025-26. The **value of pulse imports fell ~31.5%**.

**Significance.** Pulses are India's main source of plant protein, and import dependence makes food inflation volatile. Assured MSP procurement deals with the price risk that kept farmers on wheat and paddy. **Criticism:** yield is still far below the target, and rice-fallow expansion, the main lever, is the slowest to deliver. Procurement depends on States registering farmers. Stock limits and import liberalisation under the **Essential Commodities Act, 1955** keep sending mixed price signals.

> **GS tag:** GS3: Agriculture (cropping patterns, MSP, food security, food processing). GS2: Government interventions.

### 5. JOSH Teams (Joint Squad for Health) under TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | **Ministry of Health & Family Welfare**, with **Youth Affairs & Sports** and **Defence** |
| **Launched** | **8 October 2026**, Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi, by Union Health Minister **J.P. Nadda** |
| **Parent programme** | **TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan** / National TB Elimination Programme |
| **Type** | Youth-volunteer community mobilisation campaign |

**Objective.** Make TB elimination a youth-led movement for awareness, active case-finding and treatment adherence. Slogan: **"TB Haarega, Desh Jeetega"**.

**Key features**

- **6,347 JOSH Squads**: **31,429 MY Bharat volunteers** and **26,794 NCC cadets**.
- **Phase 1:** **55 districts in 11 States/UTs**. All **804 districts** joined the launch virtually and will be oriented later.
- Squads work at district and block level on **active case-finding** (including people with TB but no symptoms), adherence counselling, stigma reduction and tobacco/substance-use awareness.
- Digital tools: **JOSH feature in the TB Mukt Bharat App**, and **"Khushi Avatar"** on **e-Sangini**, which gives TB information and medicine reminders in **12 languages**.

**Context numbers (as cited by the Minister from WHO data).** TB incidence decline: **India 21% vs 12% globally**. Treatment coverage: **India 92% vs 78% globally**. **67,933 panchayats in 697 districts** were declared TB Mukt in 2026. India aims to eliminate TB **before the SDG year 2030**.

**Significance.** Most missing TB cases are in the community. Peer volunteers widen case-finding at low cost and draw on the MY Bharat and NCC networks. **Criticism:** volunteer campaigns tend to fade after the launch. The real bottlenecks are **molecular diagnostics in rural areas, drug-resistant TB, and nutrition support (Ni-kshay Poshan)**, not awareness. Self-reported "TB Mukt Panchayat" status needs independent verification.

> **GS tag:** GS2: Health; Government interventions; Role of volunteers/civil society.

## POLICY / REGULATORY CHANGE OF THE WEEK

### 6. India–Japan Joint Crediting Mechanism (JCM): Operational Manual launched

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Ministry** | **MoEFCC**. India's Article 6 authority is the **National Designated Authority for the Implementation of the Paris Agreement (NDAIAPA, 2022)** |
| **Launched** | **30 September 2026**, New Delhi |
| **Legal basis** | **Article 6.2, Paris Agreement** (bilateral "cooperative approaches") |
| **Milestones** | Memorandum of Cooperation **7 Aug 2025** → Rules of Implementation **8 Jun 2026** → Operational Manual **30 Sep 2026** |

**What changes.** The manual sets out the full project cycle, from the **Project Idea Note** through methodology approval, registration, third-party verification, and **issuance and authorisation of credits**. This moves the JCM from rules to actual rollout.

**Key features**

- Eligible sectors: **renewable energy, energy efficiency, waste management, compressed biogas, renewable hydrogen/ammonia, CCS**.
- A bilateral **Joint Committee** oversees methodologies, approvals and issuance. **National registries** prevent **double counting** (corresponding adjustments).
- **Crediting period capped at 10 years** including renewals. Any extension needs approval from India's authority.

**Significance.** It is India's first operational Article 6.2 bilateral. It brings Japanese finance and technology into hard-to-abate sectors, and it sits alongside the domestic **Carbon Credit Trading Scheme, 2023** (BEE). **Caution:** each credit authorised for transfer must be **subtracted from India's own NDC accounting**. Selling cheap abatement abroad could make India's later targets more expensive, which is why India restricts eligibility to a 2023 list of advanced technologies.

> **GS tag:** GS3: Environment (climate finance, carbon markets). GS2: Bilateral relations (India–Japan).

## REPORTS, SURVEYS & INDICES

### A. Global Innovation Index (GII) 2026: **WIPO** · India **38th of 139** ⭐ *index of the week*

- **Publishing body:** **World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO)**, released on **29 September 2026** as scheduled.
- **What it measures:** innovation performance of 139 economies across **innovation inputs** (institutions, human capital and research, infrastructure, market and business sophistication) and **outputs** (knowledge and technology outputs, creative outputs).
- **India's standing:** **38th overall, the same as GII 2025** (up from 81st in 2015). **1st among 36 lower-middle-income economies** and **1st in Central & Southern Asia**.
- **Key findings for India**
  1. **Outputs rank (25th) is well ahead of inputs (49th).** India turns its limited resources into results efficiently, but the base of R&D spending, skills and infrastructure is thin.
  2. Global highs: **1st in ICT services exports**, **4th in late-stage venture-capital deals**, **13th in intangible asset intensity**, **15th in knowledge and technology outputs**.
  3. **Bengaluru (25th)** and **Delhi (27th)** are among the world's top science and technology clusters.
- **Top 5:** Switzerland (1st, **16th year running**), Sweden, USA, Republic of Korea, Singapore. **China is 10th.**

> **GS tag:** GS3: Science & technology; Indian economy (R&D, IPR). Prelims: rank, publisher, India's stagnation at 38.

### B. Annual Survey of Industries (ASI) 2024-25: **NSO, MoSPI**

- **Released:** **30 September 2026**. Reference period April 2024 to March 2025.
- **What it measures:** the main source of statistics on the **organised (registered) manufacturing sector**: factories under the Factories Act, 1948, and others.
- **Key findings**
  1. **GVA ₹26.94 lakh crore, +9.59%**. Output **+7.81%**. Factories **2.67 lakh (+2.64%)**.
  2. **Persons engaged 2.10 crore (+7.19%)**, crossing **2 crore for the first time**, about 14 lakh more jobs. Emoluments **+12.08%**, fixed capital **+10.54%**.
  3. **Concentration:** Maharashtra, Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and UP together produce **>54% of manufacturing GVA**. **Tamil Nadu has the most factories (41,221)**, followed by Gujarat (33,084) and Maharashtra (27,379). Five industries (basic metals, motor vehicles, chemicals, pharma, food products) make up **>45% of GVA**.
- **Mains use:** evidence that formal manufacturing is creating jobs, though it also shows regional concentration and only a modest manufacturing share of GDP compared with the National Manufacturing Policy 2011 goal of 25%.

> **GS tag:** GS3: Industry, employment, growth.

### C. Strategy for Environmental-Economic Accounts in India 2026–2030: **NSO, MoSPI**

- **Released:** **7 October 2026**. It succeeds the **2022–2026** strategy.
- **What it is:** a non-statutory, "self-evolving" roadmap to build **natural-capital accounts** using the **UN System of Environmental-Economic Accounting (SEEA)**: SEEA Central Framework (2012) and SEEA Ecosystem Accounting (2021).
- **Key points**
  1. Covers **forests, minerals, soil, land, water, carbon stock, biodiversity** and environment statistics.
  2. Two strategic areas: **(A) quality improvement and filling data gaps**, and **(B) communication and outreach**. It also serves as a **guide for States and UTs** building their own accounts.
  3. Complements **SNA 2025**, which treats **depletion of natural resources as a cost of production**. It also supports **KMGBF Target 14** (mainstreaming biodiversity values) and SDG indicator **15.9.1(b)**.
- **Lineage for Prelims:** **Partha Dasgupta-led Expert Group on Green National Accounts (2011-13)**; the EU-funded **NCAVES** project (2017-21, UNSD and UNEP); annual **EnviStats India** since 2018.

> **GS tag:** GS3: Environment; Indian economy (measuring growth, green GDP).

### D. India Development Update (October 2026): **World Bank**

- **Released:** **6 October 2026**. Special focus: **"India and AI: Seizing the Development Opportunity."**
- **Forecast:** India's **FY27 GDP growth at 7.1%**, driven by strong domestic demand and exports. South Asia is projected at **6.9%** for 2026.
- **Key findings**
  1. Risks: **high crude oil prices, El Niño, and global equity corrections** that could cause capital-flow volatility.
  2. India is well placed on AI because of its tech workforce, integrated IT sector and **DPI**. **Private AI investment roughly tripled from $1.2 bn (2024) to $4.1 bn (2025)**. **Global Capability Centre employment rose from 1.9 million to 2.36 million** (2024 to 2025).
  3. Recommendations: AI-enabling infrastructure, wider access, a better business environment, and **workforce upskilling** so the gains are widely shared.
- **Pair with:** the **RBI MPC (October 2026)**, which **raised the repo rate 25 bps to 5.50%**, its first hike since February 2023, and also put **FY27 growth at 7.1%**.

> **GS tag:** GS3: Indian economy (growth, employment, technology). GS2: International institutions.

## PRELIMS POINTERS

- **GII** is published by **WIPO**, not WEF or UNDP. India: **38th** in both 2025 and 2026. Switzerland is 1st for the 16th straight year.
- **GEC-III** is the first GEC phase with **battery storage (50 GWh)**. Greenfield lines use **TBCB on a BOOM model**. Brownfield works use **cost-plus**.
- **ITLA is an SPV, not a statutory body.** It does **technical** appraisal only; financial appraisal stays with EFC/PIB.
- **SME Growth Fund** is an **AIF under SEBI (AIF) Regulations, 2012**. Don't confuse it with the **FFS (SIDBI, 2016)** or the **SRI Fund (NSIC VCF, 2020)**.
- **JCM** operates under **Article 6.2** (bilateral). **Article 6.4** is the UN-supervised **Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism**. **Article 6.8** covers non-market approaches.
- **ASI** covers the **registered/organised** sector. **ASUSE** covers the **unincorporated** sector. Both are run by NSO.
- **SEEA** is a UN statistical standard. The **Dasgupta Review (UK, 2021)** is a separate document from India's **Dasgupta-led Expert Group (2011-13)**.
- Pulses mission focus crops: **tur, urad, masoor** (not chana or moong).
