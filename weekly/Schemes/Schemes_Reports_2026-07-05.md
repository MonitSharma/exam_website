## SCHEMES & PROGRAMMES

### 1. Employees' Provident Funds Scheme, 2026
**Ministry/nodal agency:** Ministry of Labour & Employment / EPFO
**Notified:** Gazetted 29 June 2026; came into force same day
**Type:** Statutory scheme under the Code on Social Security, 2020 (replaces EPF Scheme, 1952)
**Objective:** Modernise provident fund administration while retaining core retirement-benefit guarantees, aligning EPF with the four labour codes.
**Key features:**
- Employer and employee contributions remain at 12% of wages each (10% for notified categories); mandatory contribution still computed on the ₹15,000 wage ceiling, with contributions above ₹1,800/month now formally "voluntary" top-ups.
- Withdrawal categories collapsed from 13 heads to 3 broad heads (illness, education/marriage, housing); up to 100% of eligible balance withdrawable after 12 months' membership, subject to a minimum balance.
- Push for digital-first administration — e-passbooks, online claims, UAN-linked processing; EPFO must settle or flag deficiencies in pension claims within 20 days.
- Central Government empowered to temporarily reduce/defer employer or employee contribution (up to 3 months) during a pandemic, epidemic or national disaster.
**Beneficiaries/coverage:** Applies to all existing and future EPF subscribers in establishments covered under the EPF & MP Act (organised-sector formal workforce, ~7+ crore subscribers).
**Significance & criticism:** First comprehensive EPF rules overhaul since 1952; seen as procedural modernisation rather than a benefit expansion. Criticised because the wage ceiling (₹15,000) remains unrevised and now sits well below government's own notified minimum wage benchmarks in several states; employee federations (e.g. AINPSEF) are demanding the ceiling be raised to ₹55,000–₹69,000 to reflect inflation.
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Government policies/social security codes; GS3 — Labour reforms, unorganised vs organised sector welfare

### 2. SARAS Shakti Collection (under DAY-NRLM)
**Ministry/nodal agency:** Ministry of Rural Development
**Launched:** 29 June 2026, at the Rashtriya Gramin Vikas Sammelan (RGVS) 2026
**Type:** Central Sector initiative (branding/marketing vertical under DAY-NRLM)
**Objective:** Create a premium branding and market-linkage platform for products made by rural women-led Self-Help Groups (SHGs), bridging the gap between grassroots producers and corporate/institutional buyers.
**Key features:**
- Curated line of hand-woven textiles, organic wellness products and traditional crafts produced by SHGs, sold under a unified "SARAS Shakti" premium label.
- Accompanying "SARAS Shakti Coffee Table Book" documenting SHG success stories and market potential.
- Structured pathway for SHG enterprises to access corporate gifting, government procurement and institutional markets directly (as opposed to only mela/exhibition sales).
- Complements the existing eSARAS e-commerce platform for SHG products.
**Beneficiaries/coverage:** Rural women SHG members under DAY-NRLM (mission covers ~9 crore+ women in ~90 lakh SHGs nationally).
**Significance & criticism:** Positioned as a concrete step toward the "Lakhpati Didi" target of 3 crore rural women earning ₹1 lakh+ annually. Success will hinge on actual offtake by corporates beyond the launch event — branding initiatives of this kind have previously struggled to scale past pilot/showcase stage.
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Government policies for women/rural welfare; GS3 — Rural livelihoods, SHG-based inclusive growth

### 3. Delhi Electric Vehicle Policy 2026 (EV Policy 2.0)
**Ministry/nodal agency:** Government of NCT of Delhi (Transport Department)
**Rolled out:** 1 July 2026; valid till 31 March 2030
**Type:** State scheme
**Objective:** Accelerate Delhi's shift to electric mobility via a mix of fiscal incentives and phased bans on new ICE-vehicle registrations.
**Key features:**
- ~₹15,000 crore outlay over four years for incentives and charging infrastructure (target: 32,000 charging stations by 2030).
- Tiered, annually-declining purchase subsidies: two-wheelers up to ₹30,000 in year 1 (tapering to ₹10,000 by year 3); e-autos ₹50,000 in year 1; electric cars/goods vehicles up to ₹1 lakh for the first 1 lakh applicants (cars priced ≤₹30 lakh).
- 100% road tax and registration fee waiver for eligible battery-electric vehicles till March 2030.
- Phased ICE-registration bans: new petrol/diesel three-wheelers barred from 1 Jan 2027; non-electric two-wheelers barred from 1 April 2028. All new Delhi government/DTC vehicles must be electric immediately; school bus fleets face a phased electrification target (30% by 2030).
**Beneficiaries/coverage:** All Delhi vehicle buyers and transport operators; direct fiscal benefit capped at first 1 lakh electric car applicants.
**Significance & criticism:** One of India's most aggressive state EV transition roadmaps, moving from "incentivise" to "mandate/ban." Criticised by auto-industry stakeholders over limited EV model choice, high upfront cost, import-dependence for batteries/EV tech, inadequate home-charging access, and the complete absence of a direct purchase subsidy for private cars in the base policy.
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Environment/urban air pollution, electric mobility, energy transition; GS2 — State government policy & federalism (subject overlaps with central FAME scheme)

### 4. Mukh Mantri Mawan Dheeyan Satkar Yojana (Punjab)
**Ministry/nodal agency:** Government of Punjab
**Announced:** 8 March 2026; launched 14 April 2026; first DBT payments from 1 July 2026
**Type:** State scheme (women's cash-transfer)
**Objective:** Direct income support to adult women in Punjab as a welfare/dignity measure.
**Key features:**
- ₹1,000/month to eligible adult women; ₹1,500/month to Scheduled Caste women, via Direct Benefit Transfer.
- Near-universal eligibility for adult women voters of Punjab, excluding government employees, income-taxpayers, and current/former MPs/MLAs.
- Government estimates ~97% of adult women in the state may eventually qualify; roughly 52 lakh women covered at rollout.
**Beneficiaries/coverage:** ~52 lakh women initially, scaling toward near-universal adult female coverage in Punjab.
**Significance & criticism:** Among the larger state cash-transfer-to-women schemes rolled out in 2026; estimated annual cost of ~₹9,300 crore has drawn criticism over fiscal sustainability given Punjab's existing debt burden and possible crowding-out of health/education/road spending. Some political opponents have also questioned the adequacy of the transfer amount, drawing pushback from welfare advocates.
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Welfare schemes for women, DBT-based income support; GS3 — State fiscal federalism and subsidy design

### 5. MoTA clarification on wildlife clearance under the Forest Rights Act, 2006
**Ministry/nodal agency:** Ministry of Tribal Affairs (office memorandum to MoEFCC)
**Issued:** 2 July 2026
**Type:** Regulatory/policy clarification (not a funded scheme)
**Objective:** Remove ambiguity that was delaying construction of basic public infrastructure (schools, anganwadis, roads, health centres, irrigation works) on forest land meant for Scheduled Tribes and Other Traditional Forest Dwellers (OTFDs).
**Key features:**
- Clarifies that under Section 3(2) of the FRA, 2006, wildlife clearance is not automatically mandatory for such facilities if recommended by the Gram Sabha.
- Directly addresses a 2020 MoEFCC letter that forest officials had been using to block such infrastructure in forest villages, despite no formal order to states.
- Restores primacy of Gram Sabha recommendation in the clearance process for small public-facility land diversion.
**Beneficiaries/coverage:** Forest-dwelling Scheduled Tribes and OTFDs in villages awaiting basic public infrastructure under pending/approved FRA claims.
**Significance & criticism:** A long-pending administrative logjam between MoTA and MoEFCC that had stalled anganwadi/school/road construction in many forest villages; welcomed by tribal-rights researchers, though implementation depends on state forest departments actually updating field-level practice.
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Welfare of Scheduled Tribes, Forest Rights Act, Centre-Centre inter-ministerial coordination; GS3 — Forest governance, tribal land rights

### 6. National Drone Cooperative (launched at National Drone Summit 2026)
**Ministry/nodal agency:** Ministry of Cooperation, with Drone Tech Multi-State Co-operative Society Ltd. and the National Skill Development Corporation (NSDC)
**Launched:** 4 July 2026, New Delhi (theme: "From Policy to Prosperity")
**Type:** Central Sector initiative / cooperative-sector platform
**Objective:** Build India's first cooperative-led drone entrepreneurship platform, linking rural drone service providers to skilling and market access, marking the culmination of the Ministry of Cooperation's five-year "Sahkar Se Samriddhi" push (2021–2026).
**Key features:**
- National platform organised around cooperative (not purely private) ownership of drone-service enterprises.
- Parallel launch of Advanced Skill Centres across universities, engineering colleges, polytechnics and Government ITIs, training in drone technology, AI, robotics, GIS/remote sensing and electric mobility, in partnership with NSDC and aligned with NEP 2020/Skill India Mission.
- Positioned to serve agriculture (crop-spraying, surveying), rural logistics and disaster response.
**Beneficiaries/coverage:** Rural entrepreneurs, cooperative societies and ITI/polytechnic/engineering students being skilled in drone-related trades.
**Significance & criticism:** Novel attempt to fuse the cooperative model with emerging drone-tech entrepreneurship; still a launch-stage initiative — concrete funding outlay, beneficiary targets and regulatory (DGCA) integration details are yet to be fully published.
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Emerging technology (drones), skill development, agri-tech; GS2 — Cooperative federalism, Ministry of Cooperation's mandate

## REPORTS & INDICES

### A. NITI Aayog — "Strategic Roadmap for Making Ayurveda Global"
**Publishing body:** NITI Aayog (Health Division), with Ministry of Ayush and PwC
**Released:** 2 July 2026
**What it measures/covers:** A phased strategy (to 2047, aligned with Viksit Bharat@2047) for globalising Ayurveda, built on a three-pillar framework — Availability (workforce, exports, manufacturing, R&D, standardised education), Acceptability (regulatory compliance, international collaborations, insurance coverage, localisation), and Propagation (brand positioning, global visibility, medical value travel).
**Key findings/highlights:**
- India's Ayurveda ecosystem still lacks standardised global education and regulatory harmonisation, limiting export and insurance-coverage potential abroad.
- Recommends stronger integration with international research bodies and global health institutions to build clinical evidence acceptance.
- Sets out a long-horizon (2047) roadmap rather than immediate targets, reflecting the scale of regulatory/standardisation work needed.
**Syllabus tag:** GS2 — Health governance, AYUSH sector, government policy for traditional medicine; GS3 — Science & health diplomacy, soft power

### B. India MSME Report 2026 — "Enterprise Security and Resilience Framework"
**Publishing body:** Ministry of Micro, Small & Medium Enterprises (released by Joint Secretary Mercy Epao)
**Released:** ~1 July 2026 (around MSME Day 2026)
**What it measures/covers:** Proposes a shift in MSME policy from subsidy-based "promotion" to systematic "de-risking," introducing the concept of enterprise security as core to MSME survival and resilience against global shocks (COVID-19, West Asia tensions cited as trigger events).
**Key findings/highlights:**
- MSMEs remain disproportionately exposed to global shocks due to limited access to finance/technology, thin margins and market uncertainty.
- Calls for a coordinated Enterprise Security and Resilience Framework involving government, banks, insurers and promotional agencies to manage cascading business risks.
- Notes ONDC has onboarded 3 lakh+ sellers across 400+ cities, with rural seller (farmer/artisan) orders up 11-fold in FY26 — cited as evidence of digital-channel gains, alongside the MSME-TEAM scheme funding ONDC onboarding for 5 lakh small enterprises.
**Syllabus tag:** GS3 — Indian economy, MSMEs, industrial policy, employment; GS2 — Government policy interventions for the small-enterprise sector
